## Goal

You decide whether a mark is ready for US federal trademark filing, prepare the core application information, and submit through the USPTO only when the filing basis and goods or services are accurate.

## Preconditions

- The word, logo, slogan, or design you want to protect.
- A clear list of goods or services sold or planned under the mark.
- Evidence of real use in commerce if filing based on current use, such as product labels, website purchase pages, or service advertising.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the mark is actually a trademark.** Identify how the name, logo, or slogan tells customers the source of goods or services. → *Expect:* the mark functions as a brand, not just a product description or decoration.
2. **Search for conflicts.** Search USPTO trademark records, web results, app stores, domain names, and marketplaces for similar marks on related goods or services. → *Expect:* you have a conflict list and can explain why your mark is distinguishable or risky.
3. **Assess distinctiveness.** Avoid generic terms and be cautious with merely descriptive names unless you have legal advice. → *Expect:* the mark appears distinctive enough to examine.
4. **Choose the filing basis.** [BRANCH: already using the mark in commerce, prepare a use-based application | not using yet, prepare an intent-to-use application] → *Expect:* the filing basis matches reality.
5. **Identify goods and services precisely.** Use the USPTO ID Manual style and select the correct international classes. → *Expect:* the description is specific, accurate, and not broader than actual business plans.
6. **Prepare the specimen if using the mark now.** Show the mark as customers encounter it in sales or advertising, not just a mockup. → *Expect:* the specimen connects the mark to the listed goods or services.
7. **Verify identity and applicant ownership.** Decide whether the owner is an individual or legal entity and use the exact legal name. → *Expect:* the applicant listed is the true owner of the mark.
8. **File in USPTO Trademark Center.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm owner, mark image, goods/services, filing basis, and fee before submitting because mistakes can be expensive or impossible to fix. → *Expect:* the USPTO issues a serial number and filing receipt.
9. **Monitor the application.** Watch for office actions, publication, opposition deadlines, allowance notices, and maintenance deadlines. → *Expect:* the application status is trackable and calendar reminders are set.

## Decision points

- A similar mark appears in related goods or services → consult a trademark attorney before filing.
- You need protection outside the US → investigate foreign national filings or Madrid Protocol strategy.
- You are not yet selling → intent-to-use can reserve a place, but registration still requires later proof of use.
- The mark includes a living person's name, geographic term, or government symbol → expect extra consent, refusal risk, or limits.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Likelihood-of-confusion refusal:** detect a USPTO office action citing another mark → respond with legal arguments, amend if allowed, or seek counsel.
- **F2 Specimen refused:** detect that USPTO says the specimen is mockup, ornamental, or not tied to sales → submit an acceptable substitute specimen if available.
- **F3 Wrong owner named:** detect ownership mismatch after filing → review whether correction is legally possible; consult counsel before assignment or amendment.
- **F4 Missed deadline:** detect abandoned status → check whether a petition to revive is available and file promptly if eligible.
- **F5 Scam invoice:** detect private mail demanding payment after filing → verify all payments through USPTO systems before paying anything.

## Verification

The USPTO filing receipt shows the correct applicant, mark, goods or services, filing basis, classes, fees paid, and serial number in Trademark Center.

## Variations

- `us`: USPTO Trademark Center is the current federal online filing system for trademark applications.
- `state trademark`: state registration may be cheaper but protects only within that state and does not replace federal registration.
- `attorney-required applicants`: foreign-domiciled applicants generally need a US-licensed attorney for USPTO trademark matters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because trademark filings are public, fees are usually nonrefundable, and errors can harm legal rights. Confirm ownership and conflict risk before submission, and treat unsolicited trademark invoices as suspicious.
