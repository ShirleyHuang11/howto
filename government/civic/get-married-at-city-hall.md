## Goal

You obtain a marriage license, complete a civil ceremony at city hall or a local clerk's office, and receive proof that the marriage was recorded.

## Preconditions

- Both partners' legal names, dates of birth, identification, and any required proof of divorce, annulment, or death of a prior spouse.
- Knowledge of the jurisdiction where you will apply and marry.
- Appointment, witnesses, and fees if required locally.

## Steps

1. **Check local marriage-license rules.** Review the city, county, or state clerk website for residency, waiting period, expiration, witness, age, and fee requirements. → *Expect:* you know where to apply and when the license will be valid.
2. **Gather identity and prior-marriage documents.** Bring government photo ID and certified divorce decrees or death certificates if either partner was previously married. → *Expect:* both partners can prove eligibility.
3. **Apply for the marriage license.** Complete the application online or in person; both partners usually must appear or sign as required. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* the application is a legal statement; confirm names, dates, and prior-marriage information before signing. → *Expect:* the clerk issues or schedules issuance of the license.
4. **Wait if the jurisdiction requires it.** Do not hold the ceremony before the license is valid. → *Expect:* the ceremony date falls inside the valid license window.
5. **Book the civil ceremony.** Reserve a city hall, courthouse, clerk, judge, or authorized officiant appointment and confirm witnesses. → *Expect:* you have a ceremony time, location, fee, and witness plan.
6. **Attend the ceremony with required people and documents.** Bring the license, IDs, witnesses, rings if desired, and payment. → *Expect:* the officiant solemnizes the marriage and signs the license.
7. **Ensure the signed license is returned for recording.** Confirm whether the officiant, clerk, or couple files it. → *Expect:* the vital records office receives the signed license.
8. **Order certified marriage certificates.** Request certified copies after recording for name changes, benefits, taxes, or immigration. → *Expect:* certified copies arrive or are available for pickup.

## Decision points

- Either partner is under 18 → rules vary and may prohibit marriage or require court approval.
- You need an urgent certificate → ask about same-day recording or expedited certified copies.
- You plan to change a name → order multiple certified certificates for Social Security, DMV, passport, banks, and employers.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 License expired:** ceremony occurs too late → apply for a new license before marrying.
- **F2 Waiting period missed:** ceremony happens too soon → ask the clerk whether the marriage can be recorded or must be redone.
- **F3 Missing witness:** ceremony cannot proceed → bring an eligible witness or use a jurisdiction that does not require one.
- **F4 Certificate not recorded:** no record appears after the ceremony → contact the officiant and clerk to locate or refile the signed license.

## Verification

The clerk or vital records office shows the marriage license recorded, and you have at least one certified marriage certificate with correct names and date.

## Variations

- `us-state-county`: license waiting periods, expiration periods, proxy marriage, self-solemnization, and witness rules vary widely.
- `international-use`: some countries require an apostille or additional authentication of the marriage certificate.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because marriage changes legal, tax, benefits, debt, inheritance, and immigration status. Confirm eligibility, names, and prior-marriage records before signing, and store certified certificates securely.
