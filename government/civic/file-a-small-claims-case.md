## Goal

You file a small claims case in the correct court, serve the defendant properly, and prepare enough evidence for the hearing.

## Preconditions

- Defendant's correct legal name and service address.
- Amount claimed within your jurisdiction's small claims limit.
- Evidence such as contracts, invoices, photos, messages, receipts, and a demand letter if required.

## Steps

1. **Confirm small claims is the right forum.** Check the court limit, allowed case types, venue rules, and whether businesses need a registered-agent address. → *Expect:* your claim fits the court's small claims rules.
2. **Identify the correct defendant.** Use contracts, business registrations, vehicle records, or property records to name the person or legal entity accurately. → *Expect:* the caption names the party that can be held responsible.
3. **Calculate the claim.** Add principal damages, allowed fees, and interest only if permitted; keep proof for each amount. → *Expect:* the amount requested is supported by documents.
4. **Complete court forms.** Fill in parties, addresses, claim facts, requested amount, and any required pre-suit demand information. → *Expect:* the clerk can accept the filing without missing fields.
5. **File and pay the fee.** Submit online or at the clerk's office and request a fee waiver if eligible. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* filing starts a legal case and may create public records; confirm names, amounts, and venue before submitting. → *Expect:* the clerk issues a case number and hearing or service instructions.
6. **Arrange legal service.** Use sheriff, marshal, process server, certified mail, or clerk service allowed by the court; do not serve the papers yourself unless rules allow it. → *Expect:* proof of service is filed or ready to file.
7. **Organize evidence for court.** Make labeled copies for the judge, defendant, and yourself; prepare a one-page timeline and damages summary. → *Expect:* you can explain the case in a few minutes with documents in order.
8. **Attend the hearing or mediation.** Arrive early, check in, answer the judge directly, and ask for a payment plan or judgment if you win. → *Expect:* the court enters a judgment, dismissal, continuance, or settlement.

## Decision points

- Defendant is in another county or state → confirm venue and service before filing.
- Claim exceeds the small claims limit → waive the excess only if you are willing to give it up, or file in a higher court.
- Defendant offers settlement → get the agreement in writing and know whether to dismiss now or after payment clears.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Clerk rejects filing:** missing form, fee, or venue → correct the packet and refile in the proper court.
- **F2 Service fails:** defendant moved or evades service → find a better address or request alternate service if allowed.
- **F3 Hearing continued:** court postpones for service or scheduling → calendar the new date and preserve evidence.
- **F4 You win but are not paid:** judgment entered but no payment → use lawful collection tools such as payment hearing, lien, garnishment, or sheriff execution if available.

## Verification

The court docket shows the case filed with a case number, the defendant has been served or service is scheduled, and the hearing date or next required action is recorded.

## Variations

- `us-state-county`: small claims limits, service methods, appeal rights, and attorney rules vary by court.
- `landlord-tenant`: many courts have separate housing procedures; do not use ordinary small claims if a special docket applies.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because filing creates legal deadlines and public records. Confirm jurisdiction, defendant identity, and service rules before filing, and redact unnecessary account numbers or private information from exhibits when court rules allow.
