## Goal

You report a broken, flickering, damaged, or always-on streetlight to the utility or local government so it can be repaired.

## Preconditions

- Location of the streetlight, ideally nearest address, cross street, pole number, or map pin.
- Description of the problem and whether wires, sparks, or crash damage are present.
- Phone camera or notes if you can safely document it.

## Steps

1. **Observe from a safe place.** Note whether the light is out, flickering, cycling, leaning, damaged, or on during daylight. → *Expect:* you can describe the defect without approaching hazards.
2. **Record the exact location.** Capture nearest address, intersection, side of street, pole number, or GPS/map pin. → *Expect:* a repair crew can find the specific pole.
3. **Identify who owns the light.** Check the city 311 page, local public works site, or electric utility outage form. → *Expect:* you have the correct reporting channel.
4. **Submit the report.** Enter location, problem type, pole number, photo if safe, and contact details if you want updates. → *Expect:* the system accepts the outage report.
5. **Escalate dangerous conditions.** If there are downed wires, sparks, fire, collision damage, or immediate traffic danger, call emergency services or the utility emergency line instead of only filing a web form. → *Expect:* urgent hazards are routed to emergency response.
6. **Save the ticket number.** Keep confirmation, date, and any promised repair window. → *Expect:* you can follow up if the repair is delayed.
7. **Check after the repair window.** Revisit only from a safe public place. → *Expect:* the light works normally or you have evidence for follow-up.

## Decision points

- Pole has exposed wires or is smoking → call emergency services or utility emergency number.
- Light is on private property → report to the property owner or homeowners association instead of city public works.
- Multiple lights are out → report each pole or describe the affected block clearly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong owner:** city says utility owns it → resubmit through the utility form with the same location details.
- **F2 Crew cannot locate pole:** ticket closes unresolved → provide pole number, photo, and map pin.
- **F3 Intermittent flicker:** light works during inspection → include time of day and pattern, then reopen if needed.
- **F4 No response:** repair window passes → follow up with the ticket number or call 311/public works.

## Verification

You have a confirmation or ticket number, and the streetlight later operates normally at night without flickering or visible damage.

## Variations

- `city-311`: many cities accept reports through 311 apps or websites.
- `utility-owned`: electric utilities often maintain lights outside city limits or on certain roads.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary reports, but damaged poles and electrical wires can be dangerous. Do not touch poles, wires, or metal objects nearby; call emergency services for immediate hazards.
