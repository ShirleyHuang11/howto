## Goal

You file a clear complaint with the correct state consumer protection office and keep enough proof to follow up or escalate if the business does not respond.

## Preconditions

- The business name, address, website, phone number, and any account or order number.
- Receipts, contracts, screenshots, emails, photos, tracking records, and notes from phone calls.
- A concise statement of what happened, when it happened, and the remedy you want.

## Steps

1. **Try a direct written resolution first.** Contact the business by email, portal, or letter and ask for the specific fix: refund, repair, cancellation, correction, or delivery. → *Expect:* a dated copy of your request and either a response or a missed response deadline.
2. **Identify the correct state office.** Use USA.gov's state consumer protection directory or your state attorney general's consumer complaint page. → *Expect:* the complaint form belongs to your state or the state where the business operates.
3. **Check whether a specialized regulator is better.** [BRANCH: bank, credit, mortgage, or debt collection, consider CFPB | insurance, use the state insurance department | utilities, use the public utility commission | licensed contractor, use the licensing board] → *Expect:* you know whether the state consumer office is the main filing destination or a backup.
4. **Prepare a factual timeline.** List dates, amounts, promises made, names of representatives, and what documentation proves each point. → *Expect:* a short chronology that can be pasted into the complaint form.
5. **State the requested remedy plainly.** Ask for an exact action such as "$214.36 refund to the original payment method" or "cancel the contract with no further billing." → *Expect:* the agency and business can tell what outcome would resolve the complaint.
6. **Upload only relevant evidence.** Attach receipts, contract pages, correspondence, photos, and screenshots; redact unrelated account numbers and sensitive data. → *Expect:* the upload list supports the timeline without exposing extra private information.
7. **Review and submit the complaint.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* once submitted, the complaint may be shared with the business, so confirm names, dates, accusations, and attachments are accurate. → *Expect:* the portal accepts the complaint and displays a confirmation number or sends a confirmation email.
8. **Save the record.** Download or print the confirmation page and store copies of every attachment you sent. → *Expect:* you can produce the complaint number, submission date, and evidence packet later.
9. **Monitor the response window.** Watch email and mail for agency or business replies and answer requests for more information by the stated deadline. → *Expect:* the complaint status changes or you receive a written response.

## Decision points

- The complaint involves identity theft or a scam → also report to the FTC at IdentityTheft.gov or ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- The amount is large or a deadline is imminent → consider legal advice or small claims court instead of waiting for informal mediation.
- The business is in another state → file with your state, the business's state, or both if each portal allows it.
- You signed an arbitration agreement → a state complaint may still help, but it may not preserve court rights.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong agency:** detect a rejection or referral notice → refile with the named regulator and include the first complaint number.
- **F2 Missing evidence:** detect an agency request for documents → send only the requested documents by the deadline and keep the upload receipt.
- **F3 Business ignores complaint:** detect no response after the stated window → ask the agency about escalation and consider a demand letter or small claims filing.
- **F4 Complaint too broad:** detect a request to clarify the remedy → rewrite the complaint around dates, amounts, and one requested outcome.
- **F5 Portal upload fails:** detect errors or stalled submission → compress PDFs, use supported file types, or mail the complaint packet if the agency permits.

## Verification

The state agency has issued a complaint confirmation number, your evidence packet is saved, and the complaint status or confirmation email shows it was submitted to the agency.

## Variations

- `us`: state attorney general or consumer protection offices handle general complaints; specialized agencies handle banking, insurance, utilities, professional licensing, and some housing issues.
- `paper filing`: some offices accept mail-in forms; send copies, not originals, and use tracked mail for high-value disputes.
- `cross-border online purchase`: for an overseas seller, use econsumer.gov in addition to any card chargeback or platform dispute.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because complaints can affect money, contracts, and reputation. Submit only truthful statements you can support, redact unrelated private data, and confirm before sending anything that may be forwarded to the business.
