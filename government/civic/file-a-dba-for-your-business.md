## Goal

You register a "doing business as" name, also called a trade name, assumed name, or fictitious business name, with the correct state, county, or city office.

## Preconditions

- The legal owner name: individual, partnership, LLC, corporation, or other entity.
- Proposed DBA name and business address.
- Access to the state secretary of state, county clerk, city clerk, or local licensing office rules for your business location.

## Steps

1. **Determine whether a DBA is required.** Check whether you will operate under a name different from your legal name or registered entity name. → *Expect:* you know whether the business activity needs a DBA filing.
2. **Find the filing office.** Use your state and local government pages because DBA filing may be handled by the state, county, city, or a combination. → *Expect:* you have the exact office, form name, fee, and renewal term.
3. **Search name availability.** Search the DBA database, secretary of state entity database, and local records if required. → *Expect:* the proposed name is available or you choose a different name.
4. **Check separate trademark risk.** Search USPTO and web usage for similar brands even if the DBA database allows the name. → *Expect:* you understand that DBA registration does not create trademark protection.
5. **Complete the application.** Enter owner legal name, entity ID if applicable, address, business type, DBA name, and contact information exactly. → *Expect:* the form is complete and internally consistent.
6. **Handle notarization or publication if required.** [BRANCH: notarization required, sign before a notary | publication required, arrange newspaper publication and proof of publication] → *Expect:* required local formalities are satisfied.
7. **Submit and pay.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm owner name, DBA spelling, jurisdiction, and address before filing because public records and fees may not be easily corrected. → *Expect:* the office accepts the filing and issues a receipt or registration number.
8. **Store the certificate and calendar renewal.** Keep the stamped DBA certificate and record expiration or renewal deadlines. → *Expect:* the business can present proof of DBA registration to banks, vendors, or licensing agencies.

## Decision points

- You are forming an LLC or corporation → complete entity formation first if the DBA will belong to that entity.
- You need a bank account under the DBA → ask the bank what certificate and EIN documentation it requires.
- Your jurisdiction requires newspaper publication → do not assume the filing is complete until proof of publication is accepted.
- The name is rejected → adjust the name and search again before refiling.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Filed in wrong jurisdiction:** detect bank or agency refusal → file in the correct county, city, or state and keep both records if needed.
- **F2 Name conflict:** detect a rejection or legal demand from another business → stop using the name until legal risk is reviewed.
- **F3 Publication missed:** detect a notice that proof was not filed → complete publication within the allowed window or refile.
- **F4 Owner mismatch:** detect that the DBA lists you personally instead of your LLC → ask the filing office about amendment or cancellation and refile correctly.

## Verification

You have an accepted DBA certificate, receipt, or registration record showing the exact DBA name, legal owner, jurisdiction, filing date, and renewal or expiration date.

## Variations

- `us`: DBA rules vary by state and locality; the SBA notes that registration may be with state, county, or city government.
- `sole proprietor`: a DBA may be needed when the business name does not include the owner's legal name.
- `LLC/corporation`: the DBA should usually be registered by the entity, not by an owner personally.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because DBA filings are public and affect contracts, banking, and licensing. Confirm the legal owner before filing and remember that DBA registration does not protect the brand like a trademark.
