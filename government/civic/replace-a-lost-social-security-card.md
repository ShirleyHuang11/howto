## Goal

You request a replacement Social Security card from the Social Security Administration and reduce identity-theft exposure if the lost card may be misused.

## Preconditions

- You know your Social Security number and legal name.
- A my Social Security account or ability to visit a Social Security office if online replacement is not available.
- Current proof of identity if SSA requires an in-person visit.

## Steps

1. **Decide whether you need the physical card.** Many tasks only require knowing the number; avoid ordering a card if you do not need one. → *Expect:* you know whether replacement is necessary.
2. **Start at SSA's official replacement-card page.** Sign in to my Social Security or begin the online screening. → *Expect:* SSA tells you whether you can complete the request online.
3. **Answer eligibility questions accurately.** Confirm citizenship or immigration status, age, mailing address, and whether you need a name or information change. → *Expect:* the system routes you to online completion or an office appointment.
4. **Submit the replacement request.** Provide only the information SSA asks for and review the mailing address. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* a card mailed to the wrong address exposes your SSN; confirm the address before submitting. → *Expect:* SSA accepts the request or gives office instructions.
5. **Schedule or attend an office visit if required.** Bring original identity and immigration documents listed by SSA; photocopies and notarized copies usually are not accepted. → *Expect:* the office accepts your documents and processes the replacement.
6. **Monitor the mail.** SSA says replacement cards are mailed after processing; keep an eye on the delivery window. → *Expect:* the card arrives by mail, typically within the stated timeframe.
7. **Secure the card.** Store it in a locked place, not in a wallet, unless you need it that day. → *Expect:* the card is protected from routine loss.
8. **Add fraud protections if the card was stolen.** Place a fraud alert or credit freeze if you suspect identity theft, and report misuse at IdentityTheft.gov. → *Expect:* credit bureaus and agencies have a record of the risk.

## Decision points

- You need a name change or corrected information → this is not a simple replacement; follow SSA's evidence requirements.
- You are not a U.S. citizen → SSA may require immigration documents and an office visit.
- You suspect theft rather than simple loss → treat it as identity-theft risk, not just a document replacement.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Online request unavailable:** SSA says you cannot finish online → schedule an appointment and bring required originals.
- **F2 Identity verification fails:** login or ID proofing is rejected → use SSA phone or office options.
- **F3 Card never arrives:** mail window passes → contact SSA and verify the address before requesting another card.
- **F4 SSN misuse appears:** unfamiliar accounts or IRS notices arrive → file an identity-theft report and freeze credit.

## Verification

SSA has accepted the replacement request, and the new card arrives by mail with the correct legal name and Social Security number.

## Variations

- `us-state`: online replacement availability can depend on state ID systems and whether you have a current driver's license or state ID.
- `identity-theft`: replacement does not change your SSN; changing an SSN is rare and requires strong evidence of ongoing harm.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a Social Security card enables identity fraud. Use only SSA.gov, avoid carrying the card, shred old copies, and confirm the mailing address before submission.
