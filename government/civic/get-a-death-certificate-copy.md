## Goal

You order a certified copy of a death certificate from the correct vital records office so it can be used for estate, insurance, benefits, banking, or property matters.

## Preconditions

- Deceased person's full legal name, date of death, and place of death.
- Your relationship to the person and reason for the request.
- Government ID and payment method accepted by the vital records office.

## Steps

1. **Identify the vital records office.** Use the state or territory where the death occurred, not necessarily where the person lived. → *Expect:* you have the correct state, county, or city ordering page.
2. **Check eligibility and copy type.** Determine whether you need a certified long form, short form, informational copy, or cause-of-death copy. → *Expect:* the requested version meets the receiving agency's requirement.
3. **Gather required information.** Prepare full name, date and place of death, spouse or parent names if requested, funeral home if known, and your ID. → *Expect:* the application can be completed without guessing.
4. **Choose the ordering method.** [BRANCH: online | mail | in person | funeral home] Use only the official vital records office or its named authorized vendor. → *Expect:* you know fees, delivery time, and ID upload or notarization requirements.
5. **Submit the application and fee.** Review the mailing address and number of copies before paying. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* death certificates expose identity and family information; order only the copies needed and send them to a secure address. → *Expect:* you receive an order number or receipt.
6. **Track and receive the certificates.** Monitor email, vendor status, or mail tracking. → *Expect:* certificates arrive with seal, registrar signature, and correct details.
7. **Inspect each copy.** Check spelling, dates, place, and certification. → *Expect:* copies are accepted for the intended business or agency task.
8. **Request amendments if errors matter.** Follow the vital records correction process if legal names, dates, or demographic fields are wrong. → *Expect:* an amendment case or corrected certificate process begins.

## Decision points

- Death occurred abroad → contact the U.S. State Department for a Consular Report of Death Abroad or the foreign civil registry as applicable.
- You are not immediate family or legal representative → access may be limited to informational copies.
- Estate has many accounts → order multiple certified copies to avoid delays.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong jurisdiction:** no record found → apply in the state or county where death occurred.
- **F2 ID rejected:** upload or notarization fails → resubmit with accepted government ID and clear images.
- **F3 Receiving agency rejects copy:** informational copy is not enough → order a certified copy with seal.
- **F4 Record has an error:** name or date is wrong → request an amendment with supporting documents.

## Verification

You have the requested number of certified death certificate copies with the correct decedent information, official seal, registrar signature, and usable certification date.

## Variations

- `us-state`: access rules, cause-of-death restrictions, fees, and authorized vendors vary by state.
- `estate`: banks, insurers, probate courts, and retirement plans often require certified copies, not photocopies.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because certificates can be used to access estates, benefits, and accounts. Mail them to a secure address, track originals, and provide copies only to verified institutions.
