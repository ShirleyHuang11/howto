## Goal

You complete the birth registration process so the newborn has an official birth certificate record and, where requested, a Social Security number.

## Preconditions

- The child's full legal name, date and place of birth, sex as recorded by the birth facility, and parents' legal names.
- Parent identification and any parentage, marriage, surrogacy, adoption, or court documents required by the jurisdiction.
- Hospital birth worksheet or instructions from the local vital records office.

## Steps

1. **Ask who files the birth record.** [BRANCH: hospital or birthing center, staff usually submit the birth certificate worksheet | home birth, contact the local registrar or vital records office promptly] → *Expect:* you know the filing authority and deadline.
2. **Complete the birth worksheet carefully.** Enter the newborn's name, parent information, birth details, and mailing address exactly as intended for the legal record. → *Expect:* all names and dates are spelled correctly before submission.
3. **Resolve parentage fields before signing.** Confirm how the jurisdiction records married parents, unmarried parents, assisted reproduction, surrogacy, adoption, or second-parent information. → *Expect:* the record reflects the legally correct parent or parents.
4. **Choose Social Security number issuance if offered.** Many US birth registration workflows can request the child's SSN during the hospital birth registration. → *Expect:* the worksheet or electronic form shows whether an SSN was requested.
5. **Sign the certification.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm the child's name, parents' names, and birth date before signing because later corrections can require affidavits, fees, or court orders. → *Expect:* the registrar or hospital accepts the signed worksheet.
6. **Request certified birth certificate copies.** Order at least one certified copy from the state or local vital records office after the record is filed. → *Expect:* an order receipt lists the number of certified copies, fee, and delivery method.
7. **Track SSN and certificate delivery separately.** The birth certificate comes from vital records; the Social Security card comes from SSA if requested. → *Expect:* you have separate expected delivery windows.
8. **Check the certificate immediately on arrival.** Review spelling, dates, parent names, and place of birth. → *Expect:* the certified copy matches the intended legal information.

## Decision points

- The baby was born outside a hospital → contact the county or municipal registrar quickly; witness, medical, or proof-of-pregnancy documents may be required.
- Parentage is legally complex → get legal or hospital social-work guidance before signing the record.
- You need proof urgently for insurance or travel → ask vital records about expedited certified copies.
- A typo appears on the certificate → request a correction through vital records before using the document widely.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed filing deadline:** detect that the birth was not registered in the normal window → contact vital records for delayed birth registration requirements.
- **F2 Name spelled wrong:** detect an error on the certified copy → file a correction request with supporting IDs and hospital records.
- **F3 SSN never arrives:** detect no Social Security card after the stated wait → contact SSA with the child's birth details and parent ID.
- **F4 Parent listed incorrectly:** detect a parentage error → ask vital records what amendment, acknowledgment, court order, or legal process is required.

## Verification

The vital records office has accepted the birth record, you have an order receipt or certified birth certificate copy, and any requested Social Security card is received or traceable with SSA.

## Variations

- `us`: birth registration is handled by state or local vital records offices, usually initiated by the hospital for facility births.
- `home birth`: documentation requirements vary widely and may require a midwife statement, medical exam, witness affidavit, or proof of residence.
- `outside-us birth to US citizen parent`: use the nearest US embassy or consulate process for a Consular Report of Birth Abroad instead.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because the record establishes legal identity. Confirm every name and date before signing, keep certified copies secure, and share the newborn's SSN only when required for benefits, insurance, or tax records.
