## Goal

You apply for a U.S. passport card for land and sea travel to eligible nearby destinations, or as an additional wallet-size federal ID.

## Preconditions

- Proof of U.S. citizenship, government photo ID, compliant passport photo, and payment.
- Your current passport book or card if renewing or adding a card by mail.
- Access to the State Department passport form tools and, for first-time applications, a passport acceptance facility.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the card fits your travel need.** Passport cards are not valid for international air travel; use a passport book for flights abroad. → *Expect:* your itinerary is compatible with a passport card or you decide to apply for a book too.
2. **Choose the correct form.** Use DS-11 if applying for the first time, under 16, replacing a lost/damaged passport, or not eligible for renewal; use DS-82 if eligible to renew by mail. → *Expect:* you have the right form for your situation.
3. **Prepare citizenship and identity evidence.** Gather a certified birth certificate, naturalization certificate, prior passport, or other accepted proof, plus photo ID and photocopies if required. → *Expect:* documents match the form instructions.
4. **Get a compliant photo.** Use a recent color passport photo that meets State Department size and background rules. → *Expect:* the photo is accepted by the facility or form checker.
5. **Calculate fees and payment methods.** Use the State Department fee calculator and note that acceptance facilities may require a separate execution fee for DS-11. → *Expect:* you have the correct check, money order, card, or facility-accepted payment.
6. **Submit the application.** [BRANCH: DS-11, apply in person at an acceptance facility and sign only when instructed | DS-82, mail the signed form, old passport, photo, and fee to the listed address] ⚠️ *Irreversible:* signing and submitting certifies the information under penalty of law; verify every field first. → *Expect:* the facility accepts the packet or your mailing has tracking.
7. **Track the application.** Use the State Department status page after the application enters the system. → *Expect:* the application shows received, in process, approved, or mailed.
8. **Inspect the card on arrival.** Check spelling, date of birth, sex marker, and expiration date. → *Expect:* the card is accurate and stored separately from other ID.

## Decision points

- You may fly internationally → apply for a passport book instead of, or in addition to, the card.
- You need it soon → check current expedited and urgent-travel rules before applying.
- Applicant is under 16 → both parents or guardians generally must consent in person or provide accepted consent documents.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong form:** application returned or delayed → redo with DS-11 or DS-82 as instructed.
- **F2 Bad photo:** facility or agency rejects it → obtain a new compliant photo and resubmit quickly.
- **F3 Missing citizenship document:** packet cannot be accepted → order a certified copy before reapplying.
- **F4 Delivery problem:** status says mailed but card never arrives → contact the National Passport Information Center promptly and follow lost-in-mail instructions.

## Verification

The State Department status shows the passport card issued or mailed, and the physical card arrives with correct personal information.

## Variations

- `us`: passport cards are issued by the U.S. Department of State and are valid only for specific land and sea border travel, not international air travel.
- `renewal`: eligible adults can generally renew by mail with DS-82; first-time applicants must apply in person with DS-11.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because citizenship evidence and identity documents are valuable. Use official State Department forms, mail with tracking when allowed, and never send passport documents to a private expediting company unless you understand the fees and limitations.
