## Goal

You file the correct USCIS replacement application for a lost, stolen, damaged, or incorrect Permanent Resident Card and preserve proof of lawful permanent resident status while waiting.

## Preconditions

- Your USCIS online account or ability to file Form I-90 by mail.
- Alien Registration Number, biographic information, mailing address, and evidence of identity.
- A copy or photo of the lost card if available, or other government ID.

## Steps

1. **Confirm Form I-90 is the right form.** Use Form I-90 for replacing a Permanent Resident Card; do not use it for conditional resident status removal or naturalization. → *Expect:* your situation matches a replacement reason listed by USCIS.
2. **Protect against misuse if stolen.** If the card was stolen, consider a police report and monitor mail and accounts for identity misuse. → *Expect:* you have any report number or notes needed for your records.
3. **Create or sign in to USCIS online.** Start Form I-90 online when eligible, or download current USCIS paper instructions if filing by mail. → *Expect:* you are using the current USCIS Form I-90 flow.
4. **Select the exact replacement reason.** Choose lost, stolen, destroyed, mutilated, never received, incorrect data, or other applicable reason. → *Expect:* the fee and evidence checklist match that reason.
5. **Enter identity and immigration details.** Provide name, A-Number, class of admission if known, addresses, and biographic details exactly. → *Expect:* the draft form is complete with no missing required fields.
6. **Upload evidence.** Attach available card copies, government ID, police report if useful, or USCIS notices requested by the form instructions. → *Expect:* each upload is readable and tied to the requested evidence category.
7. **Review and submit.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm the mailing address, replacement reason, and A-Number before paying and submitting because errors can delay identity documents. → *Expect:* USCIS accepts the filing and issues a Form I-797 receipt notice.
8. **Attend biometrics if scheduled.** Follow the appointment notice instructions exactly and bring required ID. → *Expect:* biometrics are taken or the notice says USCIS will reuse prior biometrics.
9. **Track the case and save notices.** Use the receipt number in your USCIS account and keep all I-797 notices. → *Expect:* case status is visible and notices are stored.

## Decision points

- Card expired or expiring soon → this may be a renewal rather than a lost-card replacement, but Form I-90 is still commonly used for both.
- Conditional resident card expired or expiring → do not file I-90 to remove conditions; review Form I-751 or Form I-829 requirements.
- Need to travel urgently → contact USCIS for evidence of status options before leaving the United States.
- Card never arrived → check USCIS instructions because fee treatment may differ if USCIS or postal delivery caused the issue.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong form for conditional resident:** detect that the card category is conditional and near expiration → stop and review removal-of-conditions filing instead.
- **F2 Receipt notice missing:** detect no I-797 after expected processing → check USCIS account, mail address, and e-request options.
- **F3 Biometrics missed:** detect a missed appointment notice → follow USCIS rescheduling instructions immediately.
- **F4 Evidence rejected:** detect a request for evidence → respond by the deadline with the exact requested documents.
- **F5 Address changes during case:** detect a move before the card arrives → update USCIS address promptly and keep proof of submission.

## Verification

USCIS has issued a Form I-797 receipt for Form I-90, the case is trackable by receipt number, and the replacement Green Card is approved, produced, or delivered.

## Variations

- `us`: USCIS Form I-90 is used to replace a Permanent Resident Card; online filing is available for many applicants.
- `fee waiver`: some applicants may request a fee waiver if eligible, often requiring paper filing and supporting evidence.
- `outside-us`: contact a US embassy, consulate, airline, or USCIS guidance before travel; a lost card abroad may require carrier documentation.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because this involves immigration status and identity documents. Use only USCIS.gov, keep copies of every notice, and confirm the form is not replacing a removal-of-conditions or naturalization requirement.
