## Goal

You obtain an official certified copy of a public or vital record that will be accepted for legal, identity, benefits, school, travel, or court purposes.

## Preconditions

- The document type: birth certificate, death certificate, marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order, deed, naturalization record, or other official record.
- The issuing office or likely jurisdiction where the original record was created.
- Proof of identity, relationship, authorization, or case information required by the issuing office.

## Steps

1. **Identify the record custodian.** Determine whether the record is held by vital records, a court clerk, county recorder, state archive, federal agency, school, or licensing office. → *Expect:* you have the official office name and request page.
2. **Confirm you need a certified copy.** Ask the receiving agency whether it requires a raised seal, wet signature, electronic certification, apostille, or plain copy. → *Expect:* you know the exact certification level needed.
3. **Gather search details.** Collect names, dates, locations, case numbers, book/page numbers, certificate numbers, or parcel numbers. → *Expect:* the office can locate the record without guesswork.
4. **Check eligibility and ID requirements.** Vital and court records may restrict who can order certified copies. → *Expect:* you know what ID or authorization must be submitted.
5. **Submit the request.** [BRANCH: online portal, upload ID and pay | in person, bring ID and payment | mail, send signed application, ID copy, and payment as instructed] → *Expect:* the office accepts the request or gives a specific deficiency.
6. **Choose delivery and copies.** Select standard mail, pickup, expedited shipping, or electronic certified copy if accepted by the receiving party. → *Expect:* the receipt shows copy count, delivery method, and fees.
7. **Review the certified copy on arrival.** Check names, dates, seals, signatures, certification language, and page completeness. → *Expect:* the copy is complete and visibly certified.
8. **Store and transmit safely.** Send certified copies only to the requesting organization and keep at least one secure copy for future use. → *Expect:* the recipient gets the document in the required format.

## Decision points

- The document is for international use → ask whether an apostille or embassy legalization is required.
- The record has confidential information → confirm you are authorized before requesting or sharing it.
- The receiving agency accepts electronic certified copies → use the official digital delivery method instead of scanning a paper copy.
- The record contains an error → request an amendment or corrected order before ordering many copies.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong custodian:** detect a "no record found" response → verify jurisdiction, date range, name variants, and forwarding office.
- **F2 ID rejected:** detect a rejected order → resubmit with an accepted government ID or notarized proof if allowed.
- **F3 Plain copy sent instead of certified:** detect missing seal or certification statement → request a certified copy specifically and ask for replacement if the order was wrong.
- **F4 Recipient refuses document:** detect rejection for age, format, or certification type → ask the recipient for exact requirements and reorder accordingly.
- **F5 Mail lost:** detect no delivery after the stated window → contact the issuing office with order number and shipping details.

## Verification

The issuing office provides a certified copy with the required seal, signature, certification statement, or secure electronic certificate, and the receiving agency accepts it or confirms it is acceptable.

## Variations

- `us`: vital records are usually state or local; deeds and many court records are usually county-level; federal records use the relevant federal agency or archive.
- `apostille`: for Hague Convention countries, an apostille is usually issued by a designated state or federal authority after the certified copy is obtained.
- `court records`: some courts certify each page or attach a clerk certificate; ask before ordering for immigration or international use.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because certified documents can establish identity, ownership, family status, or legal rights. Order only from official custodians, verify recipient requirements, and keep certified copies secure.
