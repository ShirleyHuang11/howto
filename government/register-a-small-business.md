## Goal

Register a small business with the appropriate government office and keep proof of legal formation or trade-name registration.

## Preconditions

- You know the business name, owner names, mailing address, physical address, business activity, and ownership structure.
- You have chosen a structure such as sole proprietorship, LLC, corporation, partnership, or nonprofit.
- You have a registered agent or service address if your structure requires one.
- You can pay filing fees and receive official mail.

## Steps

1. **Choose the legal structure.** Compare liability, tax, ownership, reporting, and cost for sole proprietor, LLC, corporation, partnership, or nonprofit. → *Expect:* one structure is chosen and documented.
2. **Search the business name.** Check the state registry, local fictitious-name database, and obvious trademark/domain conflicts. → *Expect:* the name is available or you have a backup.
3. **Identify required registrations.** Determine whether formation, DBA/trade name, local business license, sales tax permit, professional license, or zoning approval applies. → *Expect:* you have a checklist by agency.
4. **Prepare filing information.** Gather organizer, owner/member, registered agent, principal office, mailing address, purpose, management, and duration details. → *Expect:* the online form can be completed in one session.
5. **File with the official registry.** Submit articles, certificate, DBA, or local license application through the secretary of state, county clerk, or city portal. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* filing fees are usually nonrefundable and public records may reveal addresses, so review names and addresses first. → *Expect:* you receive a filing receipt, entity number, or pending status.
6. **Save formation documents.** Download stamped articles, certificate of organization/incorporation, DBA certificate, and payment receipt. → *Expect:* you can prove the business exists or the filing is pending.
7. **Apply for tax accounts if needed.** Register for employer withholding, sales tax, excise tax, or local gross receipts accounts after formation. → *Expect:* tax account numbers or portal access are issued.
8. **Create internal records.** Prepare operating agreement, bylaws, initial resolutions, ownership ledger, and business record folder as appropriate. → *Expect:* ownership and authority are documented before banking or contracts.
9. **Calendar renewals and reports.** Record annual report, franchise tax, DBA renewal, local license, and registered-agent deadlines. → *Expect:* compliance deadlines are on a calendar with reminders.

## Decision points

- You need liability separation or investors → consider LLC or corporation before filing as a sole proprietor.
- You operate from home → check zoning, landlord/HOA rules, and local business-license requirements.
- You sell taxable goods or hire employees → tax registrations may be required before sales or payroll.
- Name is available in state registry but conflicts with a trademark → get advice before investing in branding.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Name rejected:** detect filing denial for name conflict → recover by amending the name and resubmitting.
- **F2 Wrong structure chosen:** detect tax, liability, or ownership mismatch → recover by converting, dissolving, or forming a new entity with professional guidance.
- **F3 Address privacy problem:** detect personal home address on public record → recover by updating to an allowed commercial/registered-agent address where permitted.
- **F4 Missing local license:** detect city/county notice or blocked permit → recover by applying locally and paying any late fees.
- **F5 Annual report missed:** detect inactive, delinquent, or administratively dissolved status → recover by filing reinstatement and overdue reports.

## Verification

You have a state or local confirmation showing the business name, registration/entity number, filing date, and current active or pending status.

## Variations

- `us`: formation is state-level, local licensing is city/county-level, and tax accounts may be state and federal.
- `sole-proprietor`: may need only DBA, local license, and tax permits, not entity formation.
- `professional-service`: licensing boards may restrict names, owners, and services.
- `nonprofit`: formation is separate from tax-exempt recognition and charitable solicitation registration.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from public identity exposure, tax obligations, and liability assumptions. Do not use a personal address if privacy matters and an allowed alternative exists; keep formation records separate from marketing copies.
