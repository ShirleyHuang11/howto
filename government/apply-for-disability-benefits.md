## Goal

Submit a disability benefits application with medical, work, income, and daily-function evidence, then track decisions and appeals.

## Preconditions

- You know which program may apply, such as Social Security disability, veterans disability, state disability insurance, workers' compensation, or local income support.
- You have identity, address, work history, income/resources if required, medical diagnoses, provider contacts, medications, test results, hospital visits, and functional limitations.
- You can sign releases or request medical records.
- You can respond to agency mail, exams, calls, and appeal deadlines.

## Steps

1. **Identify the correct disability program.** Match the benefit to your work history, military service, injury source, income/resources, and state coverage. → *Expect:* you know the agency, form, and basic eligibility theory.
2. **Read medical and nonmedical rules.** Check required duration, severity, work limits, insured status, income/resource limits, and application date rules. → *Expect:* you know what evidence must prove.
3. **Build a medical timeline.** List diagnoses, onset date, symptoms, treatments, providers, tests, medications, side effects, hospitalizations, and assistive devices. → *Expect:* the application can identify every relevant source.
4. **Document work and daily limitations.** Describe job duties, why work stopped or changed, lifting, standing, sitting, concentration, attendance, personal care, transportation, and household tasks. → *Expect:* limitations are concrete and tied to real activities.
5. **Gather records and releases.** Collect medical records you have, provider addresses, patient portal downloads, therapy notes, imaging reports, and signed release forms if requested. → *Expect:* the agency can request or review the evidence.
6. **Complete the application.** [BRANCH: online | phone | paper | in-person] Answer identity, work, medical, income, and household questions fully. → *Expect:* the application summary has no unexplained gaps.
7. **Submit and save proof.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* statements about onset, work, income, and medical limitations shape the case, so correct errors before submitting. → *Expect:* you receive a confirmation, claim number, or stamped copy.
8. **Attend consultative exams and respond to requests.** Keep appointments, update address/phone, and send new records by deadlines. → *Expect:* the claim remains active and evidence is current.
9. **Act on the decision.** [BRANCH: approved | denied | partially approved | more evidence requested] Review benefit amount, onset date, back pay, continuing reviews, or appeal deadline. → *Expect:* you know whether to accept, correct, or appeal.

## Decision points

- Condition is expected to improve quickly → short-term disability, state disability, or leave benefits may fit better than long-term disability.
- You are still working → report earnings accurately; work activity can affect eligibility.
- Denial arrives → file the appeal before gathering perfect evidence if the deadline is short.
- You need representation → compare legal aid, accredited representatives, contingency-fee rules, and fee caps.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing medical records:** detect agency notice that records were not received → recover by contacting providers, uploading copies, or signing new releases.
- **F2 Missed exam:** detect missed consultative exam notice → recover by calling immediately to reschedule and explaining good cause.
- **F3 Work history unclear:** detect request for job duties or earnings → recover with pay records, job descriptions, supervisor statements, or tax forms.
- **F4 Denied for insufficient severity:** detect decision rationale → recover by appealing with functional evidence, provider opinions, and updated tests.
- **F5 Overpayment risk:** detect benefits paid after work or income changes → recover by reporting changes promptly and saving proof.

## Verification

You have a disability claim confirmation and later a written decision, payment notice, or appeal filing receipt tied to the correct program and claim number.

## Variations

- `us-social-security`: SSDI depends on insured work credits; SSI depends on income/resources; many claims require reconsideration or hearing appeals.
- `us-veterans`: VA disability uses service connection, ratings, and effective dates.
- `state-disability`: some states cover short-term wage replacement through payroll-funded programs.
- `workers-compensation`: job-related injury claims use employer/insurer processes and medical-provider rules.

## Safety & privacy

Disability applications expose medical, financial, employment, and household data. Use official portals, keep copies of every submission, and do not exaggerate or minimize limitations; accuracy matters for benefits and future reviews.
