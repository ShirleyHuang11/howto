## Goal

Challenge a property tax assessment with evidence and preserve appeal rights before the local deadline.

## Preconditions

- You have the assessment notice, parcel/account number, property address, assessed value, tax year, and appeal deadline.
- You can access comparable sales, property record cards, appraisal reports, photos, repair estimates, or exemption records.
- You understand that taxes may still need to be paid while an appeal is pending.
- You can attend a hearing or submit written evidence.

## Steps

1. **Read the assessment notice.** Identify assessed market value, taxable value, exemptions, classification, deadline, and appeal office. → *Expect:* you know exactly what can be challenged and by when.
2. **Review the property record card.** Check square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size, construction type, condition, year built, improvements, and exemptions. → *Expect:* you have a list of factual errors or know the dispute is about value.
3. **Find comparable evidence.** Gather recent sales, appraisals, listings, photos of defects, repair bids, flood/fire damage records, or income data for rental property. → *Expect:* evidence supports a specific lower value or correction.
4. **Calculate the requested value.** Write a clear proposed assessed value and explain the method, not just the desired tax bill. → *Expect:* the assessor can see the adjustment requested.
5. **Try informal review if available.** Contact the assessor before the formal deadline and provide concise evidence. → *Expect:* the office either corrects the record or tells you to file a formal appeal.
6. **File the formal appeal.** Complete the board, assessor, or tribunal form with parcel number, owner name, disputed value, grounds, and evidence list. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* missed appeal deadlines usually cannot be revived, so submit before attempting more negotiation. → *Expect:* you receive a filing receipt, case number, or hearing notice.
7. **Pay required taxes or escrow amounts.** Follow local rules on whether payment is required to avoid penalties while appealing. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* tax payments and late penalties affect liens and deadlines, so confirm payment rules before withholding anything. → *Expect:* taxes are current or a lawful appeal-payment arrangement is documented.
8. **Prepare for hearing.** Organize evidence in the requested format, label comparable sales, and make copies for the board and assessor. → *Expect:* your packet proves factual error or overvaluation.
9. **Attend and follow up.** Present facts briefly, answer questions, and wait for the written decision. → *Expect:* you receive a decision, revised notice, refund, credit, or further appeal deadline.

## Decision points

- Notice contains a factual property error → ask for correction even if you also file a value appeal.
- Comparable sales are higher than assessed value → appeal may increase scrutiny; consider stopping unless an exemption/classification error exists.
- You cannot attend hearing → request written submission, remote appearance, or continuance before the deadline.
- You need hardship relief → assessment appeal is separate from tax deferral, exemption, abatement, or payment-plan programs.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Deadline missed:** detect refusal to accept appeal → recover by asking about clerical-error correction, exemption correction, or next tax year's appeal window.
- **F2 Weak comparables:** detect assessor rejects distant or dissimilar sales → recover with closer sales, appraisal, or record-card errors.
- **F3 Exemption omitted:** detect missing homestead, senior, veteran, disability, or agricultural exemption → recover by filing the exemption application or correction path.
- **F4 Tax bill still due:** detect penalty or lien warning → recover by paying under protest if allowed and documenting the appeal.
- **F5 Decision unfavorable:** detect written denial → recover by filing the next-level appeal before its deadline or accepting the value.

## Verification

You have a filed appeal receipt and later a written decision showing corrected assessment, denied appeal with reasons, or next-level appeal deadline.

## Variations

- `us`: deadlines, boards, exemptions, and whether taxes must be paid during appeal vary by county and state.
- `recent-purchase`: sale price may be strong evidence, but reassessment rules differ.
- `condo`: use same-building unit sales and check shared-area assessment treatment.
- `commercial-rental`: income, expense, vacancy, and capitalization evidence may matter more than comparable sales.

## Safety & privacy

Property appeals expose ownership, home condition, financial hardship, and sometimes income data. Submit only needed evidence, redact account numbers, and do not miss tax-payment deadlines while disputing value.
