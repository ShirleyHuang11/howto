## Goal

Apply for a building permit with plans, contractor details, fees, and inspection requirements understood before work starts.

## Preconditions

- You know the property address, parcel number, owner details, project scope, estimated cost, and whether work affects structure, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, zoning, or historic rules.
- You have drawings, site plan, product specifications, contractor license and insurance, and owner authorization if you are not the owner.
- You can pay permit and plan-review fees.
- No construction has started unless emergency work rules allow it.

## Steps

1. **Confirm permit type.** Check the local building department site for whether the project needs building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, zoning, demolition, right-of-way, or historic approval. → *Expect:* you know every permit or review required.
2. **Check zoning and property limits.** Review setbacks, height, lot coverage, easements, floodplain, HOA, historic district, and utility clearance rules. → *Expect:* the proposed work is allowed or needs a variance/extra approval.
3. **Prepare plans and documents.** Gather site plan, floor plans, elevations, structural details, energy forms, product approvals, contractor licenses, insurance, and owner affidavit. → *Expect:* the submission packet matches the checklist.
4. **Create the permit application.** Enter property, owner, contractor, scope, valuation, occupancy, and trade information in the official portal or form. → *Expect:* the draft permit shows the correct project address and permit category.
5. **Upload plans and pay intake fees.** Attach files using required names, sheet sizes, seals, and formats. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* application and plan-review fees are often nonrefundable once review begins, so confirm scope and valuation first. → *Expect:* the portal issues an application number and fee receipt.
6. **Respond to plan review comments.** Revise drawings, answer code comments, and resubmit with changes clouded or listed. → *Expect:* each review cycle shows approved, pending, or comments returned.
7. **Receive and post the permit.** Download the approved permit card, stamped plans, conditions, and inspection list. → *Expect:* the permit is active and visible at the job site before work starts.
8. **Schedule required inspections.** Call or use the portal for footing, framing, rough electrical/plumbing/mechanical, insulation, final, or specialty inspections at the required stages. → *Expect:* inspection results show passed, failed with corrections, or scheduled.
9. **Close the permit.** Complete final inspection and obtain certificate of completion or occupancy if required. → *Expect:* the permit status is finaled/closed, not expired or open.

## Decision points

- Work changes structure, egress, load-bearing walls, gas, electrical panel, or plumbing → assume licensed plans and inspections may be needed.
- Contractor says no permit is needed → verify with the building department before work starts.
- Property is in floodplain, coastal, historic, or fire zone → expect extra reviews and longer timelines.
- Permit expires before final inspection → request extension before expiration.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Incomplete application:** detect intake rejection → recover by using the checklist and resubmitting missing plans, licenses, or owner authorization.
- **F2 Plan review denied:** detect code comments → recover with corrected plans and a response letter addressing each comment.
- **F3 Unpermitted work started:** detect stop-work order or inspector warning → recover by stopping work, applying for after-the-fact permit, and exposing work for inspection if required.
- **F4 Failed inspection:** detect correction notice → recover by fixing listed items and rescheduling after the work is visible.
- **F5 Permit left open:** detect open/expired status during sale or refinance → recover by scheduling final inspection or asking for closure procedure.

## Verification

The permit is issued, posted, inspected at required stages, and finaled with written closure or certificate of completion/occupancy.

## Variations

- `us`: permit thresholds, owner-builder rules, contractor licensing, inspection names, and fees are local.
- `minor-repair`: cosmetic work may be exempt, but electrical, plumbing, structural, and exterior changes often are not.
- `condo-hoa`: association approval may be separate from government permit approval.
- `emergency-repair`: some jurisdictions allow immediate safety work with permit application within a short period.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because unpermitted or unsafe construction can injure people, void insurance, create fines, or block sale. Do not conceal work from inspectors, and share plans only through official portals or licensed professionals.
