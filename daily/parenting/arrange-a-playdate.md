## Goal

You arrange a playdate with clear supervision, timing, contact information, and safety expectations so children can play comfortably.

## Preconditions

- The child's friend and caregiver contact information.
- Your child's allergies, medical needs, behavior triggers, and pickup constraints.
- A proposed date, time, location, and activity.

## Steps

1. **Contact the other caregiver directly.** Confirm they are the responsible adult and ask whether the child is available. → *Expect:* communication is adult-to-adult, not only child-to-child.
2. **Set the basic plan.** Agree on date, start time, end time, location, and whether food will be served. → *Expect:* both caregivers have the same schedule.
3. **Discuss supervision.** Ask which adult will be present, whether siblings or other children will be there, and what areas are off-limits. → *Expect:* you know who is supervising and the play area boundaries.
4. **Share health and safety needs.** Mention allergies, medication, pets, swimming, trampoline, firearms storage, or other household risks relevant to the visit. ⚠️ *Hazard:* confirm high-risk items such as pools, weapons, and loose pets are secured before drop-off. → *Expect:* safety concerns have explicit answers.
5. **Choose a manageable length.** Start with 60-90 minutes for younger children or new friends. → *Expect:* the visit is short enough to end before children are overtired.
6. **Prepare the child.** Review manners, bathroom asking, food rules, screen rules, and what to do if uncomfortable. → *Expect:* the child knows how to ask for help or call you.
7. **Exchange emergency details.** Share phone numbers, pickup person, allergy information, and any needed permission for snacks or outings. → *Expect:* the supervising adult can reach you quickly.
8. **Do a clear handoff.** At drop-off, confirm pickup time and any changed details. → *Expect:* both adults restate the plan.
9. **Debrief afterward.** Ask what they played, whether anything felt uncomfortable, and whether they want another playdate. → *Expect:* you know whether to repeat, adjust, or avoid the arrangement.

## Decision points

- You do not know the family well → meet in a public place or stay for the first visit.
- Activity involves swimming, driving, or leaving the home → require explicit permission and supervision details.
- Child is reluctant → do not force the visit; offer a shorter public meetup.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pickup confusion:** detect mismatched end time or location → call immediately and confirm in writing for next time.
- **F2 Unsafe household risk:** detect unsecured pool, weapons, aggressive pet, or no adult supervision → end the playdate and explain the boundary calmly.
- **F3 Children argue repeatedly:** detect escalating conflict → shorten the visit and plan a structured activity next time.
- **F4 Food allergy exposure:** detect uncertain ingredients or symptoms → stop the food, follow the allergy plan, and seek medical help if needed.

## Verification

Both caregivers have agreed to the time, location, supervising adult, emergency contact information, food permissions, and pickup plan, and the child knows how to get help.

## Variations

- Public playdate: meet at a park, library, or community center and keep both caregivers present.
- Sleepover request: treat as a higher-risk event requiring stronger familiarity, household safety questions, and child readiness.
- Group playdate: identify one lead supervising adult and set boundaries for the group.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because another household may supervise your child. Confirm supervision, transport, water, pets, weapons storage, allergies, and emergency contact details before leaving.
