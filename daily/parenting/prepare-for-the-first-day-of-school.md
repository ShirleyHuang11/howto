## Goal

You make the first day of school predictable by preparing supplies, routines, transportation, paperwork, and emotional support before the morning arrives.

## Preconditions

- School calendar, start time, teacher or classroom assignment if available, and supply list.
- Transportation plan and emergency contact information.
- Any medication, allergy, IEP, 504, custody, or pickup paperwork the school requires.

## Steps

1. **Confirm logistics with the school.** Check start date, arrival window, entrance, dismissal method, bus route, lunch process, and after-school care. → *Expect:* you know exactly where and when the child should arrive and leave.
2. **Complete required forms.** Submit emergency contacts, health forms, medication authorizations, lunch account setup, technology agreements, and pickup permissions. → *Expect:* the school portal or office shows forms complete.
3. **Prepare supplies and labels.** Buy or gather required items and label backpack, lunchbox, water bottle, jacket, and supplies as appropriate. → *Expect:* the packed bag matches the school list.
4. **Practice the morning routine.** Rehearse wake-up, dressing, breakfast, shoes, backpack, and leaving at the target time. → *Expect:* the family knows where delays happen.
5. **Practice transportation.** Walk the route, visit the bus stop, or drive the drop-off loop before school starts. → *Expect:* the child recognizes the route and pickup plan.
6. **Set up sleep earlier.** Move bedtime and wake time toward the school schedule several days ahead if possible. → *Expect:* the first morning does not require a sudden schedule shift.
7. **Talk through feelings.** Name excitement, nervousness, and questions; avoid promising everything will be perfect. → *Expect:* the child can identify one trusted adult or plan if they need help.
8. **Pack the night before.** Put backpack, lunch, clothes, shoes, forms, and weather gear in fixed locations. → *Expect:* the morning requires fewer decisions.
9. **Use a brief goodbye.** At drop-off, be warm, confident, and short; remind the child who will pick them up. → *Expect:* the child enters school with the adult's calm support.

## Decision points

- Child has medical needs → confirm the nurse or office has medication and written orders before the first day.
- Child is very anxious → request a teacher note, school visit, or transition support if offered.
- Transportation changes → notify the school in writing using the official channel.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing form blocks attendance or medication:** detect office call or portal warning → submit the form immediately and confirm receipt.
- **F2 Morning meltdown:** detect refusal to dress or leave → reduce choices, use a visual checklist, and keep the goodbye routine predictable.
- **F3 Wrong dismissal plan:** detect uncertainty about bus, pickup, or aftercare → call the office before dismissal and update written permissions.
- **F4 Lost belongings:** detect missing jacket, lunchbox, or water bottle → check lost-and-found and improve labels.

## Verification

The child arrives on time with needed supplies, the school has required forms and pickup information, and the family knows the dismissal plan.

## Variations

- Kindergarten or preschool: add a comfort item if allowed and practice opening lunch containers.
- Middle school: practice locker, schedule changes, device charging, and class transitions.
- New school: visit the building and identify the office, classroom, bathroom, cafeteria, and pickup area.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but school records and custody instructions are sensitive. Share medical, custody, and emergency-contact information only through official school channels, and confirm pickup changes before dismissal.
