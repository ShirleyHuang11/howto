## Goal

You reduce sibling conflict by improving fairness, safety, repair, and predictable adult responses without expecting siblings to like each other all the time.

## Preconditions

- A recent example of the conflict pattern.
- Basic house rules about hitting, insults, property, and privacy.
- Willingness to avoid comparing children.

## Steps

1. **Identify the trigger pattern.** Note whether fights happen over attention, toys, turns, space, chores, screens, or tiredness. → *Expect:* you know the most common rivalry trigger.
2. **Set a few safety rules.** State rules such as no hitting, no threats, no name-calling, and stop when someone says stop. → *Expect:* children know which behaviors always require adult intervention.
3. **Avoid comparison language.** Replace "Why can't you be like your sister?" with specific expectations for each child. → *Expect:* correction does not rank siblings against each other.
4. **Give each child protected attention.** Schedule small, regular one-on-one moments that are not earned by competing. → *Expect:* attention is less scarce.
5. **Teach turn and property systems.** Use timers, ownership labels, shared-item rules, and private storage for special items. → *Expect:* fewer disputes depend on adult judgment in the moment.
6. **Intervene early and calmly.** Step in when voices rise or bodies crowd, before the conflict becomes physical. → *Expect:* children separate or lower intensity before harm occurs.
7. **Coach problem-solving.** Ask each child what they want, restate the rule, and offer limited choices for repair or taking turns. → *Expect:* the solution is concrete and enforceable.
8. **Require repair after harm.** Repair may be apology, replacing, helping, or giving space; do not force performative affection. → *Expect:* the harmed child receives a meaningful repair.
9. **Review patterns weekly.** Adjust routines, sleep, chores, or screen limits if the same fight keeps repeating. → *Expect:* the plan addresses causes, not only incidents.

## Decision points

- Conflict becomes physical or one child is afraid → separate immediately and increase supervision.
- One child is consistently targeted → treat it as bullying, not normal rivalry.
- Fights spike during transitions → add structure before school, meals, bedtime, or screen changes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adult takes sides too quickly:** detect one child always labeled the problem → investigate the full pattern and enforce rules evenly.
- **F2 Forced apologies fail:** detect sarcastic apologies and repeated harm → require concrete repair and changed behavior.
- **F3 Scarce resources drive fights:** detect conflict over one tablet or toy → create a schedule or duplicates where realistic.
- **F4 Younger child gets excused:** detect older child expected to tolerate harm → protect both children with age-appropriate accountability.

## Verification

The household has clear safety rules, predictable sharing systems, one-on-one attention for each child, and a repair process used after conflicts.

## Variations

- Toddler and older child: protect the older child's belongings and give the toddler simpler boundaries.
- Teens: emphasize privacy, space, shared responsibilities, and respectful speech.
- Blended family: move slowly and avoid forcing sibling labels before relationships develop.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary rivalry, but physical aggression, threats, coercion, or fear require immediate adult action and possibly professional support.
