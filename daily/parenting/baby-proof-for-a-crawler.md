## Goal

You make the home safer for a crawling baby by removing reachable hazards, securing furniture, controlling access, and creating supervised play zones.

## Preconditions

- The baby is beginning to roll, scoot, crawl, pull, or reach beyond previous limits.
- You have outlet covers, cabinet locks, furniture anchors, gates where needed, cord controls, and basic tools.
- You can inspect every room from the baby's height.

## Steps

1. **Crawl the space yourself.** Move through each room at floor level and list reachable cords, small objects, sharp edges, unstable furniture, plants, pet items, and chemicals. → *Expect:* hazards are visible from the baby's perspective.
2. **Create safe zones and blocked zones.** Use hardware-mounted gates for stairs and close or lock rooms that cannot be made safe. ⚠️ *Hazard:* pressure gates can fail at stair tops; confirm stair gates are installed according to instructions. → *Expect:* the baby cannot access stairs or unsafe rooms.
3. **Anchor tip-over risks.** Secure dressers, bookcases, TVs, mirrors, and freestanding cabinets to wall studs or approved anchors. → *Expect:* heavy furniture cannot topple if pulled.
4. **Control electrical hazards.** Cover outlets, hide power strips, shorten cords, and keep chargers unplugged when not in use. → *Expect:* no accessible live slots or chewable cords remain.
5. **Lock chemicals, medicines, and sharp items.** Move cleaners, pods, alcohol, cannabis products, medications, knives, razors, batteries, and tools to locked or high storage. → *Expect:* poisons and blades are inaccessible.
6. **Remove choking hazards.** Pick up coins, button batteries, magnets, beads, pen caps, small toys, and older siblings' small parts. ⚠️ *Hazard:* button batteries and high-powered magnets are emergencies if swallowed; confirm they are locked away. → *Expect:* floors and low surfaces pass a small-object sweep.
7. **Make windows and blinds safer.** Lock windows, use stops/guards where appropriate, move furniture away from windows, and eliminate looped blind cords. → *Expect:* no climbable window access or strangulation cord is reachable.
8. **Secure kitchen and bathroom risks.** Add cabinet locks, toilet locks if needed, anti-scald settings, appliance locks, and keep hot drinks and pan handles out of reach. → *Expect:* water, heat, and appliance hazards are controlled.
9. **Recheck daily high-traffic areas.** Before floor play, scan for dropped pills, small food, cords, and sibling toys. → *Expect:* the safe zone remains safe after normal household use.

## Decision points

- Baby starts pulling to stand → reassess higher shelves, tablecloths, drawer pulls, and furniture anchors.
- Home has stairs → prioritize gates before free crawling.
- Older siblings live in the home → create small-toy storage zones behind gates or closed doors.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Furniture anchor missed:** detect wobbling or climbable drawers → anchor immediately and remove tempting objects from top.
- **F2 Gate installed wrong:** detect looseness, wrong gate type, or trip hazard → reinstall per manual or replace with a hardware-mounted gate.
- **F3 Poison left in bag:** detect purses, backpacks, or visitors' medications on the floor → move bags to locked/high storage.
- **F4 Small objects reappear:** detect coins, batteries, or toy parts after cleaning → add a daily sweep and sibling rules.

## Verification

From floor level, the baby cannot reach stairs, cords, poisons, medicines, button batteries, magnets, choking hazards, unsecured furniture, standing water, hot surfaces, or blind cords.

## Variations

- Rental home: use removable products where possible, but still use proper anchors and gates for serious hazards with landlord permission if required.
- Multi-story home: install gates before crawling practice near stairs.
- Grandparent or travel home: create one fully controlled room if whole-home proofing is not possible.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because crawling babies can choke, fall, drown, be poisoned, be burned, or be crushed by furniture. Confirm anchors, gates, locks, and small-object sweeps before allowing floor access.
