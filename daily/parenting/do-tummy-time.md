## Goal

You give a baby supervised awake tummy time that builds neck, shoulder, and trunk strength while avoiding unsafe sleep or airway positions.

## Preconditions

- The baby is awake, breathing normally, and not immediately uncomfortable after a large feed.
- You have a firm, clean surface such as a floor mat, blanket on the floor, or your chest while you are awake.
- An adult can supervise continuously.

## Steps

1. **Choose a safe surface.** Use the floor or your awake chest, not a soft bed, sofa, pillow pile, or elevated unattended spot. → *Expect:* the baby cannot roll or slide into a dangerous gap.
2. **Start when the baby is calm.** Try after a diaper change or short wake period, not when hungry or exhausted. → *Expect:* the baby is alert enough to participate.
3. **Place the baby belly-down while awake.** Turn the head to one side if needed and keep the nose and mouth clear. ⚠️ *Hazard:* tummy time is only for awake, supervised practice; confirm the baby is not being placed belly-down to sleep. → *Expect:* airway is visible and unobstructed.
4. **Engage at eye level.** Use your face, a high-contrast toy, mirror, or gentle voice to encourage lifting and turning. → *Expect:* baby attempts small head or shoulder movements.
5. **Keep sessions short at first.** Begin with 1-3 minutes if needed and repeat several times daily. → *Expect:* practice ends before the baby is overwhelmed.
6. **Increase total daily time gradually.** Add minutes as strength and tolerance improve. → *Expect:* the baby spends more comfortable awake time on the tummy over weeks.
7. **Use alternatives if frustrated.** Try tummy-to-tummy on your chest, across your lap, or with a small rolled towel under the chest while supervised. → *Expect:* the baby can practice with less distress.
8. **Stop for distress or sleepiness.** Pick up the baby if crying escalates, breathing seems obstructed, or the baby falls asleep. → *Expect:* unsafe or unproductive sessions end promptly.

## Decision points

- Baby has reflux or spits up easily → wait a bit after feeding and use chest or lap tummy time.
- Baby strongly dislikes floor time → use shorter, more frequent sessions with caregiver interaction.
- Baby has torticollis, flat head, prematurity, or motor concerns → ask the clinician or physical therapist for tailored exercises.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Baby falls asleep on tummy:** detect drowsing or sleep → move baby to back on a safe sleep surface immediately.
- **F2 Face presses into surface:** detect muffled breathing or hidden nose → reposition or stop the session.
- **F3 Excessive distress:** detect escalating crying every session → shorten sessions and try chest-based practice.
- **F4 Uneven head turning:** detect preference for one side → mention it at the next pediatric visit or sooner if severe.

## Verification

The baby completes supervised awake tummy time on a firm safe surface, with face visible, no sleep, and gradual improvement in tolerance or head control over repeated sessions.

## Variations

- Newborn: very short sessions on caregiver chest count when the caregiver is awake and alert.
- Older infant: use toys placed slightly to the side to encourage reaching and pivoting.
- Medical needs: follow clinician restrictions after surgery, respiratory illness, or prematurity.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because tummy positioning is unsafe for sleep and airway obstruction can occur on soft surfaces. Confirm the baby is awake, supervised, and on a firm surface every time.
