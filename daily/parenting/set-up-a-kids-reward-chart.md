## Goal

You create a reward chart that builds one desired behavior through clear expectations, immediate tracking, and rewards the child actually values.

## Preconditions

- One behavior to encourage, not a whole personality trait.
- A child old enough to understand a simple visual goal.
- Stickers, checkboxes, tokens, magnets, or an app.

## Steps

1. **Choose one target behavior.** Use observable wording such as "brush teeth before stories" or "put shoes on after breakfast." → *Expect:* the child and adult can both tell when it happened.
2. **Set a realistic time frame.** Pick daily tracking for young children and weekly goals for older children. → *Expect:* the child can earn progress quickly enough to care.
3. **Choose a simple visual chart.** Make spaces for stickers, checks, or tokens with the behavior written or pictured. → *Expect:* the chart is easy to read at a glance.
4. **Pick meaningful rewards.** Use extra story time, choosing dinner, park trip, small privilege, or modest item; avoid rewards that are too expensive to sustain. → *Expect:* the reward is motivating and realistic.
5. **Explain the rule before starting.** State exactly what earns a mark and when the reward happens. → *Expect:* the child can repeat the basic deal.
6. **Mark success immediately.** Add the sticker or token right after the behavior whenever possible. → *Expect:* the child connects the mark to the action.
7. **Keep misses neutral.** Say "No sticker this time; try again tomorrow" without lectures or shame. → *Expect:* a miss does not become a long conflict.
8. **Deliver earned rewards on time.** Follow through when the chart is complete. → *Expect:* the child trusts the system.
9. **Fade or change the chart.** After the behavior is stable, reduce rewards or choose a new target. → *Expect:* the chart remains useful instead of permanent bribery.

## Decision points

- Child never earns marks → make the behavior easier or reward smaller steps.
- Child demands rewards for everything → limit the chart to one or two priority behaviors.
- Behavior involves anxiety or skill deficits → teach the skill first instead of only rewarding outcomes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Goal too vague:** detect arguments over whether the child was "good" → rewrite as a visible action.
- **F2 Reward too delayed:** detect loss of interest → shorten the path to reward.
- **F3 Parent forgets marks:** detect blank chart despite effort → place the chart where the behavior happens.
- **F4 Sibling unfairness:** detect comparison or resentment → give each child individualized goals.

## Verification

The chart shows one observable behavior, a clear earning rule, immediate tracking, a sustainable reward, and a plan to fade or revise it.

## Variations

- Toddler: use pictures and immediate small rewards.
- Elementary child: use points toward a weekly privilege.
- Classroom or therapy plan: coordinate language and goals with the teacher or clinician.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not use food restriction, humiliation, or public shaming as reward-chart consequences; keep sensitive behaviors private.
