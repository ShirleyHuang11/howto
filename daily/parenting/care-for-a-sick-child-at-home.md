## Goal

You keep a mildly sick child comfortable, hydrated, monitored, and separated enough to reduce spread while knowing when medical help is needed.

## Preconditions

- A thermometer, fluids, tissues, soap, and any medicine already recommended for this child.
- The child's age, weight, allergies, medical conditions, and usual medications.
- Emergency numbers and the pediatrician or local nurse line available.

## Steps

1. **Check for emergency signs first.** Look for trouble breathing, bluish lips, severe dehydration, stiff neck, seizure, confusion, a non-blanching rash, or an infant under 3 months with fever. ⚠️ *Hazard:* if any severe sign is present, seek urgent medical care before continuing home care. → *Expect:* you have ruled out obvious emergency symptoms or escalated care.
2. **Measure and record symptoms.** Take temperature, note cough, vomiting, diarrhea, pain, rash, urine output, and when symptoms began. → *Expect:* a dated symptom log exists.
3. **Set up a rest area.** Keep the child comfortable, supervised, and near fluids, tissues, a lined trash bin, and washable bedding. → *Expect:* the child can rest without searching for supplies.
4. **Offer frequent fluids.** Give small sips often; for vomiting or diarrhea, use an oral rehydration solution if available. → *Expect:* the child is urinating at reasonable intervals and mouth/lips are not very dry.
5. **Use fever or pain medicine correctly.** Give only age-appropriate medicine at the label or clinician dose based on weight; avoid aspirin for children unless specifically prescribed. → *Expect:* dose, time, and medication name are written down.
6. **Keep food gentle and optional.** Offer bland foods when the child wants them; do not force meals if fluids are staying down. → *Expect:* eating does not trigger repeated vomiting or distress.
7. **Reduce spread.** Wash hands, clean high-touch surfaces, avoid sharing cups, and keep the child home according to school or daycare illness rules. → *Expect:* household exposure is reduced and return rules are known.
8. **Reassess on a schedule.** Check breathing, alertness, hydration, fever, and pain every few hours or sooner if symptoms change. → *Expect:* the log shows whether the child is improving, stable, or worsening.
9. **Call for medical guidance when thresholds are met.** Contact a clinician for persistent fever, worsening pain, dehydration signs, breathing concerns, symptoms in a medically fragile child, or anything that feels unsafe. → *Expect:* you have documented the advice and next action.

## Decision points

- Child is under 3 months and has a rectal temperature of 100.4 F / 38 C or higher → call urgent medical care promptly.
- Symptoms are mild and improving → continue fluids, rest, and monitoring.
- Symptoms worsen, last longer than expected, or the child has chronic conditions → call the pediatrician or local nurse line.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Accidental overdose risk:** detect overlapping cold medicines or unclear dosing → stop, read active ingredients, and call a pharmacist or clinician before another dose.
- **F2 Dehydration:** detect very little urine, dry mouth, no tears, dizziness, or unusual sleepiness → give small frequent oral rehydration sips and seek medical advice.
- **F3 Fever anxiety:** detect treating the number only while the child is otherwise comfortable → focus on behavior, hydration, and clinician thresholds, not fever alone.
- **F4 Illness spreads through the home:** detect multiple sick household members → increase handwashing, ventilation, separate cups/towels, and cleaning.

## Verification

The child is breathing comfortably, drinking enough to urinate, resting, symptoms are logged, medication doses are tracked, and you know the specific signs that would trigger a call or urgent visit.

## Variations

- `us`: many pediatric practices have after-hours nurse lines; poison control is available at 1-800-222-1222 for medication mistakes.
- Stomach illness: prioritize oral rehydration solution and small sips; avoid anti-diarrhea medicines unless a clinician recommends them.
- Respiratory illness: use humidified air, fluids, and saline as appropriate; avoid OTC cough medicines in young children unless advised.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because illness can worsen and medication errors can harm children. Confirm age, weight, ingredients, and timing before any dose, and seek urgent care for breathing trouble, severe lethargy, dehydration, or infant fever.
