## Goal

You teach a child practical personal-safety rules without making them fear all unfamiliar people or ignore helpful adults.

## Preconditions

- The child's age, independence level, routes, school pickup rules, and online access.
- A list of trusted adults and safe places the child can approach for help.
- Calm time for a short conversation and practice.

## Steps

1. **Replace "stranger danger" with behavior rules.** Explain that unsafe behavior matters more than whether someone is known or unknown. → *Expect:* the child understands that familiar people also must respect safety rules.
2. **Name trusted adults and helpers.** Identify caregivers, teachers, police, store employees at a register, librarians, and other safe helpers in context. → *Expect:* the child can name at least three people or places for help.
3. **Teach body-boundary rules.** Explain that private parts are private, secrets about touching are not okay, and they can say no to unwanted touch. → *Expect:* the child can state that safety secrets should be told to a trusted adult.
4. **Set pickup and leaving rules.** The child should never leave with anyone unless the caregiver has approved it using the agreed process. → *Expect:* the child knows the pickup password or confirmation rule if you use one.
5. **Practice refusal phrases.** Role-play saying "No," "I need to ask my grown-up," and moving toward a safe adult. → *Expect:* the child can speak loudly and move away in practice.
6. **Teach what to do if lost.** Have the child stay put or go to a safe helper depending on location, give caregiver name and phone number, and avoid leaving the place. → *Expect:* the child can recite or access contact information.
7. **Cover tricks and lures calmly.** Discuss adults asking kids for help, gifts, pets, rides, or secrets. → *Expect:* the child knows adults should ask other adults for help.
8. **Include online contact.** Explain that people online may not be who they say they are and that private information, photos, and meetups require caregiver involvement. → *Expect:* the child agrees to report uncomfortable messages.
9. **Repeat in small refreshers.** Practice before malls, parks, school events, and independent walks. → *Expect:* safety language stays familiar without one frightening lecture.

## Decision points

- Child becomes very anxious → shorten the talk, focus on helpers, and practice confidence.
- Child has more independence → add route rules, check-in times, and phone procedures.
- Someone violates boundaries → believe the child, protect them from contact, and seek professional guidance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Child thinks all strangers are dangerous:** detect fear of asking for help → teach uniforms, counters, and families as context-specific helpers.
- **F2 Child obeys politeness over safety:** detect reluctance to say no → practice loud refusal and explain safety rules outrank manners.
- **F3 Pickup confusion:** detect child willing to leave with a familiar adult not on the list → reinforce approved pickup process.
- **F4 Online secrecy:** detect hidden chats or requests for photos → pause access, preserve evidence if needed, and get help.

## Verification

The child can name trusted helpers, state the leaving-with-others rule, demonstrate a refusal phrase, explain what to do if lost, and promise to tell about unsafe secrets or messages.

## Variations

- Preschool child: use simple rules and repetition; avoid detailed scary scenarios.
- Older child: include independent walking, public transit, online gaming, and phone location rules.
- Neurodivergent child: use visual scripts, direct language, and repeated practice in real settings.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because personal safety and online privacy are involved. Confirm the child knows how to reach you, avoid fear-based threats, and escalate any boundary violation immediately.
