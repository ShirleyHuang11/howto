## Goal

You teach a child to brush all tooth surfaces twice a day while using an age-appropriate amount of fluoride toothpaste and adult supervision until they have the skill to do it well.

## Preconditions

- The child has a soft child-size toothbrush and toothpaste appropriate for their age and dental guidance.
- A caregiver can supervise and finish brushing for young children.
- The child can stand or sit safely at the sink, or you have a safe lap position for a younger child.

## Steps

1. **Set up the brush and toothpaste.** Use a smear for children under 3 or a pea-size amount for ages 3-6 unless a dentist gives different instructions. → *Expect:* toothpaste amount is controlled.
2. **Show the pattern.** Explain that brushing covers outside, inside, and chewing surfaces, top and bottom. → *Expect:* the child understands brushing is more than front teeth.
3. **Brush together in the mirror.** Model small circles or gentle back-and-forth motions at the gumline. → *Expect:* the child copies the motion instead of scrubbing harshly.
4. **Use a two-minute target.** Use a timer, song, or visual cue to keep brushing long enough. → *Expect:* brushing lasts close to two minutes.
5. **Coach spitting, not swallowing.** Have the child spit excess toothpaste and avoid rinsing heavily if your dentist recommends leaving fluoride on teeth. → *Expect:* swallowing is minimized.
6. **Let the child try, then finish.** For young children, let them practice first and have an adult brush again to clean missed areas. → *Expect:* teeth are actually cleaned while independence grows.
7. **Add floss when teeth touch.** Use floss picks or string floss where toothbrush bristles cannot reach. → *Expect:* tight contacts are cleaned.
8. **Make it routine, not a negotiation.** Brush morning and bedtime in the same order, with small choices like brush color or song. → *Expect:* brushing becomes expected daily care.

## Decision points

- Child resists toothpaste flavor → try another fluoride toothpaste flavor while keeping the correct amount.
- Child chews the brush → give a separate chew item if needed and replace damaged brushes.
- Gums bleed repeatedly → improve gentle brushing/flossing and schedule dental advice if it persists.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Child only brushes front teeth:** detect plaque or skipped molars → use a mirror and name each zone.
- **F2 Too much toothpaste:** detect a full ribbon on the brush → wipe off and reload the correct amount.
- **F3 Brushing is painful:** detect flinching or bleeding from hard pressure → switch to gentler pressure and check for dental issues.
- **F4 Caregiver stops supervising too early:** detect bad breath, visible plaque, or cavities → resume adult finishing until skill improves.

## Verification

The child brushes twice daily with the correct toothpaste amount, covers all surfaces for about two minutes, spits excess toothpaste, and has adult help until brushing is thorough.

## Variations

- Toddler: caregiver does most of the cleaning; independence is practice.
- School-age child: use disclosing tablets occasionally to show missed plaque if age-appropriate.
- Braces or appliances: follow orthodontist instructions for interdental brushes, floss threaders, or water flossers.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with the main concerns being excess toothpaste swallowing, slips at the sink, and missed dental problems. Supervise young children, store toothpaste out of reach, and ask a dentist about fluoride or dental pain.
