## Goal

You pack a diaper bag that covers feeding, changing, cleanup, clothing, and small emergencies without becoming too heavy to carry.

## Preconditions

- The child's age, feeding method, diaper size, and expected outing length.
- A clean bag with compartments or small pouches.
- Weather-appropriate clothing and any required medicines or allergy supplies.

## Steps

1. **Estimate the outing window.** Count travel time, appointment time, and a buffer for delays. → *Expect:* you know whether this is a short errand, half-day, or full-day bag.
2. **Pack diapers by time away.** Use one diaper for every 2-3 hours away plus two extras. → *Expect:* diapers match the child's current size and expected duration.
3. **Add changing supplies.** Include wipes, diaper cream, a portable changing pad, and disposable or washable bags for soiled items. → *Expect:* one pouch contains everything needed for a change.
4. **Pack feeding supplies.** [BRANCH: bottle-fed, pack prepared bottles or formula supplies according to safe storage rules | breastfed, pack nursing cover or pump supplies if used | solids, pack age-safe snacks, spoon, bib, and water if appropriate] → *Expect:* feeding items match the child's current diet.
5. **Add clothing backups.** Pack at least one full outfit, extra socks, and a layer for weather or indoor air conditioning. → *Expect:* a blowout, spit-up, or spill can be handled.
6. **Add cleanup basics.** Include hand sanitizer, tissues, burp cloth, small towel, and a spare shirt for the caregiver if the child is very young. → *Expect:* messes can be cleaned without leaving the location immediately.
7. **Include comfort and distraction.** Pack a pacifier, teether, small toy, or book that can be cleaned. → *Expect:* the child has one or two familiar calming items.
8. **Pack health and ID essentials.** Add any prescribed emergency medication, allergy plan, sunscreen if age-appropriate, and a card with caregiver contact information. → *Expect:* urgent information is reachable without searching your phone.
9. **Restock after returning.** Remove trash and soiled items, replace used diapers and clothes, and wash bottles or snack containers. → *Expect:* the bag is ready for the next outing.

## Decision points

- Outing overlaps nap → add sleep aid items such as sleep sack, pacifier, or stroller shade.
- Child has food allergies or medicine needs → pack labeled medicine and instructions every time.
- Bag is too heavy → remove duplicate toys and excess clothes before reducing diapers, wipes, or feeding supplies.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong diaper size:** detect leaks or red marks → replace the stash immediately with the current size.
- **F2 Spoiled milk or formula:** detect storage time exceeded or temperature unsafe → discard and use a safe backup.
- **F3 Missing change of clothes:** detect a soiled outfit with no spare → use a blanket or buy a basic outfit, then restock at home.
- **F4 Disorganized bag:** detect rummaging during a messy change → use labeled pouches for changing, feeding, clothes, and health items.

## Verification

The bag contains diapers, wipes, changing pad, feeding supplies, spare clothes, cleanup items, comfort item, and any required health supplies for the planned outing length.

## Variations

- Newborn: pack more diapers, burp cloths, and an extra caregiver shirt.
- Toddler: add snacks, water, small activities, and fewer feeding supplies.
- Daycare handoff: label bottles, containers, pacifiers, and clothes according to the daycare policy.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but feeding and medicine safety matter. Keep medicines out of the child's reach, follow breast milk or formula storage rules, and include only necessary personal information on ID cards.
