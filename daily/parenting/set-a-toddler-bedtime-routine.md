## Goal

You create a predictable toddler bedtime routine that supports sleep, reduces bedtime conflict, and keeps the sleep space safe.

## Preconditions

- The toddler has a safe crib or bed appropriate for their climbing and development stage.
- Caregivers agree on the routine, bedtime window, and response to stalling.
- Screens, rough play, and sugary snacks can be limited before bedtime.

## Steps

1. **Choose a realistic bedtime window.** Base it on wake time, nap timing, daycare schedule, and sleepy cues. → *Expect:* bedtime is not so early or late that the toddler fights from under- or over-tiredness.
2. **Set a short fixed sequence.** Use the same order: cleanup, bath or wash, pajamas, toothbrushing, bathroom or diaper, two books, song, lights out. → *Expect:* the toddler can predict what comes next.
3. **Prepare the room.** Dim lights, set comfortable temperature, remove unsafe cords or climbable hazards, and keep favorite safe comfort items available if age-appropriate. → *Expect:* the room is calm and safe for sleep.
4. **Give limited choices.** Offer two acceptable pajama, book, or stuffed-toy options. → *Expect:* the toddler gets control without delaying bedtime.
5. **Use a clear final cue.** Say the same bedtime phrase, turn off lights, and leave or sit according to your plan. → *Expect:* the routine has a consistent endpoint.
6. **Respond boringly to stalling.** Return the child to bed calmly, use few words, and avoid restarting books, snacks, or play. → *Expect:* delay tactics get less rewarding.
7. **Handle night waking consistently.** Check safety, illness, diaper, or bathroom needs, then return to the sleep plan. → *Expect:* wakes do not become long play sessions.
8. **Adjust gradually.** If shifting bedtime, move by 10-15 minutes every few nights and protect wake time and nap timing. → *Expect:* the toddler adapts without a sudden schedule shock.
9. **Track for two weeks.** Note bedtime start, lights out, sleep onset, night wakes, naps, and morning mood. → *Expect:* you can see whether the routine is working.

## Decision points

- Toddler climbs out of the crib → assess crib safety, mattress height, sleep sack use, and whether a toddler bed transition is safer.
- Bedtime takes more than an hour nightly → shorten the routine and check nap timing, separation anxiety, or inconsistent limits.
- Snoring, pauses in breathing, reflux, pain, or frequent night terrors appear → discuss with the pediatric clinician.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Routine becomes too long:** detect extra books, snacks, and repeated negotiations → write a shorter sequence and enforce one final choice.
- **F2 Caregivers respond differently:** detect one adult restarting play while another sets limits → agree on the exact script and handoff.
- **F3 Bed escape loops:** detect repeated leaving the room → calmly return with minimal attention and reinforce safety gates or door strategy as appropriate.
- **F4 Sleep problem is medical:** detect snoring, breathing pauses, pain, or chronic coughing → seek medical evaluation instead of treating it as behavior only.

## Verification

For most nights over two weeks, the toddler completes the same routine, gets into a safe sleep space, and falls asleep with fewer conflicts and predictable caregiver responses.

## Variations

- Crib sleeper: keep the crib free of climb aids, bulky bedding, and cord hazards.
- Toddler bed: use room-level safety proofing because the child can move freely.
- Shared room: stagger bedtime or use white noise to reduce sibling disruption.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from climbing, cords, unsafe sleep setup, and missed medical sleep problems. Confirm the room is safe if the child can get out of bed, and avoid locking strategies that would prevent emergency access.
