## Goal

You teach a child to balance, start, pedal, steer, and stop a bicycle in a controlled area while reducing crash and traffic risk.

## Preconditions

- The child has a properly fitting helmet and a bike that fits their height and skill.
- You have a flat, open, low-traffic practice area such as an empty court, quiet path, or paved schoolyard.
- Brakes, tires, chain, seat, and handlebars are in working condition.

## Steps

1. **Fit the helmet.** Place it level, low on the forehead, straps forming a V under the ears, buckle snug under the chin. → *Expect:* helmet stays still when the child shakes their head.
2. **Check the bike fit and function.** Set the seat so the child can reach the ground during learning, squeeze brakes, and spin wheels to check rubbing. → *Expect:* the bike is controllable and mechanically safe.
3. **Start with balance practice.** Remove pedals or have the child coast with feet pushing like a balance bike. → *Expect:* the child can glide briefly with feet lifted.
4. **Teach braking before speed.** Practice stopping with hand brakes or coaster brake while walking the bike, then while gliding. → *Expect:* the child can stop on command.
5. **Add steering targets.** Use cones, chalk, or landmarks for gentle turns. → *Expect:* the child looks where they want to go and avoids oversteering.
6. **Introduce pedaling.** Start with one pedal in the power position, push down, and begin pedaling while you steady the torso or seat briefly. → *Expect:* the child starts moving without you carrying their weight.
7. **Let go in short intervals.** Release when balanced and jog nearby, giving simple cues like "look ahead" and "pedal." → *Expect:* the child rides a few independent seconds.
8. **Practice controlled stops and restarts.** Repeat starting, riding straight, turning, and braking. ⚠️ *Hazard:* do not practice near cars, steep hills, water, or crowds until control is reliable. → *Expect:* the child can start and stop predictably.
9. **Teach basic road/path rules.** Stop at driveways, look both ways, ride with an adult, signal intentions when age-appropriate, and yield to pedestrians. → *Expect:* the child knows riding is not just balance.

## Decision points

- Child fears falling → return to balance glides and lower the seat temporarily.
- Bike is too large → switch to a smaller bike; oversized bikes slow learning and increase crashes.
- Coaster brakes confuse the child → practice braking separately before pedaling again.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Helmet fits poorly:** detect wobble, exposed forehead, or loose buckle → refit or replace before riding.
- **F2 Adult holds too tightly:** detect child leaning on you instead of balancing → support lightly and release sooner.
- **F3 Practice area is unsafe:** detect cars, slopes, broken pavement, or crowds → move locations.
- **F4 Child panics at speed:** detect freezing or steering toward hazards → teach braking and restart with shorter glides.

## Verification

The child can wear a fitted helmet, start independently, ride straight, make gentle turns, brake on command, and stop safely in a traffic-free area.

## Variations

- Balance bike path: skip training wheels and focus on gliding, then add pedals.
- Training wheels: useful for pedaling practice but do not teach balance; remove or raise them when ready.
- Hand-brake bike: ensure the child can reach and squeeze levers with enough force.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from falls, traffic, head injury, and unsafe equipment. Confirm helmet fit, bike function, and a protected practice area before each session; traffic riding requires separate instruction and adult judgment.
