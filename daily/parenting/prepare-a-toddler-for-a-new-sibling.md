## Goal

You help a toddler understand that a new baby is coming and protect their sense of security before, during, and after the birth.

## Preconditions

- A rough timeline for the baby's arrival.
- A plan for who will care for the toddler during labor, hospital stay, or adoption placement.
- Familiar routines you can preserve after the baby arrives.

## Steps

1. **Explain the change simply.** Use concrete language such as "The baby will live with us after they are born" and avoid promising an instant playmate. → *Expect:* the toddler has a basic phrase for what is happening.
2. **Read sibling books together.** Choose books showing babies crying, sleeping, feeding, and needing help. → *Expect:* the toddler has seen normal baby behavior in a low-pressure way.
3. **Keep major transitions early or delayed.** Move beds, rooms, toilet training, or childcare changes well before the baby or wait until routines settle. → *Expect:* the toddler does not feel displaced by the baby.
4. **Practice gentle touch.** Use a doll or stuffed toy to practice touching feet, using soft hands, and asking an adult before holding. → *Expect:* the toddler can demonstrate safe touch with reminders.
5. **Create a care plan for birth day.** Pack toddler essentials and introduce the caregiver who will stay with them. → *Expect:* the toddler knows who will be with them when the parent is away.
6. **Let the toddler help in small ways.** Invite them to put diapers in a basket, choose a baby outfit, or place books near the chair. → *Expect:* the toddler has a role without responsibility for safety.
7. **Plan the first meeting.** Have another adult hold the baby at first if possible so the parent can greet the toddler with open arms. → *Expect:* the toddler receives focused attention before being introduced to the baby.
8. **Protect daily one-on-one time.** Schedule even 10 minutes of child-led play with the toddler after the baby arrives. → *Expect:* the toddler has predictable parent attention each day.
9. **Name mixed feelings calmly.** Say jealousy, anger, curiosity, and love can all happen, while keeping firm safety limits. → *Expect:* the toddler hears feelings accepted and unsafe actions stopped.

## Decision points

- Toddler becomes aggressive near the baby → increase supervision, reduce access when adults are distracted, and reinforce gentle-touch practice.
- Toddler regresses in sleep, toileting, or clinginess → treat it as stress communication and preserve routines.
- Toddler shows no interest → do not force bonding; offer small invitations and time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Toddler feels replaced:** detect more tantrums when baby care starts → add predictable one-on-one time before feeding or bedtime.
- **F2 Unsafe helping:** detect attempts to pick up, feed, or cover the baby → give safe helper jobs and keep the baby out of reach when unsupervised.
- **F3 Overpromising:** detect disappointment that the baby cannot play → reframe the baby as someone who grows slowly.
- **F4 Birth-day confusion:** detect distress when parents leave suddenly → rehearse the caregiver plan and keep a packed toddler bag ready.

## Verification

The toddler has heard a simple explanation, practiced gentle touch, knows the temporary caregiver plan, and continues to receive predictable routines and one-on-one attention after the baby arrives.

## Variations

- Adoption or foster placement: use arrival language that fits the actual process and avoid uncertain promises.
- Twins or medically complex baby: prepare the toddler for extra adult help and more time at appointments.
- Older toddler: offer simple choices, such as which book to read to the baby.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but baby safety is nonnegotiable. Never leave a toddler alone with an infant, even if the toddler is loving; confirm another adult is supervising before stepping away.
