## Goal

You host a manageable children's party with clear timing, food, supervision, and activities without overbuilding the event.

## Preconditions

- Child's age, guest count, budget, location, date, and any allergy or accessibility needs.
- Contact information for invited caregivers.
- A cleanup and supervision plan.

## Steps

1. **Set the scope.** Choose guest count, budget, length, and location before picking decorations or activities. → *Expect:* the party has limits that fit your capacity.
2. **Pick a simple schedule.** Plan arrival, one activity, food or cake, optional gifts, and pickup. → *Expect:* the event fits in 90-120 minutes for most young children.
3. **Send clear invitations.** Include date, time, address, RSVP deadline, caregiver stay/drop-off expectation, allergy question, and pickup time. → *Expect:* guests know what kind of party it is.
4. **Plan easy food.** Choose foods children can eat quickly and ask about allergies before finalizing. → *Expect:* the menu is simple and allergy-aware.
5. **Prepare one main activity plus backups.** Use crafts, playground time, scavenger hunt, movie, simple games, or free play with a rain plan. → *Expect:* there is enough structure without a minute-by-minute program.
6. **Assign supervision.** Make sure an adult watches doors, outdoor areas, water, pets, and bathrooms as needed. → *Expect:* every area children can access is covered.
7. **Childproof the party area.** Remove breakables, medicines, choking hazards, weapons, cleaning chemicals, and private items. ⚠️ *Hazard:* confirm unsafe items are inaccessible before guests arrive. → *Expect:* the accessible space is safe for the youngest guest.
8. **Run the party by the clock.** Give transition warnings before food, cake, gifts, and pickup. → *Expect:* children move between activities with less chaos.
9. **Close with organized pickup.** Match children to authorized caregivers and note any incident or allergy exposure. → *Expect:* every child leaves with the correct adult.

## Decision points

- Guest is young or anxious → ask caregivers to stay or keep the party shorter.
- Party includes swimming, trampoline, or bounce house → increase supervision and permission requirements.
- Budget is tight → prioritize food, activity, and safety over favors or decorations.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too many guests:** detect noise and supervision gaps → restrict accessible areas and add adult helpers.
- **F2 Allergy surprise:** detect unreported allergy at arrival → set aside safe packaged food or ask caregiver guidance.
- **F3 Activity ends early:** detect boredom or roughhousing → switch to backup free play, outdoor time, or a simple group game.
- **F4 Pickup uncertainty:** detect adult you do not recognize → call the child's caregiver before releasing them.

## Verification

Invitations are clear, food and activities are ready, hazards are removed, adults are assigned to supervision, and pickup matches each child to the correct caregiver.

## Variations

- Home party: reduce clutter and limit children to specific rooms.
- Park party: bring permits if required, shade, water, first-aid supplies, and a bad-weather plan.
- Drop-off party: collect emergency contacts and allergy information before caregivers leave.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with supervision and allergy concerns. Confirm caregiver permission for food, photos, swimming, trampolines, or transport, and do not post identifiable child photos without permission.
