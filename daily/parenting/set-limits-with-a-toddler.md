## Goal

You set firm, calm boundaries that a toddler can understand while reducing power struggles and unsafe behavior.

## Preconditions

- A specific behavior to address.
- A safe environment with hazards reduced where possible.
- Agreement among caregivers on the limit and response.

## Steps

1. **Choose one clear limit.** Pick a behavior that matters, such as hitting, throwing food, running into the street, or bedtime leaving. → *Expect:* the limit is specific enough to enforce.
2. **State the rule positively and briefly.** Use words like "Blocks stay on the floor" or "Hands are for gentle touch." → *Expect:* the toddler hears a short rule they can repeat or recognize.
3. **Get close and calm.** Move to the toddler's level, use a steady voice, and reduce shouting across the room. → *Expect:* the child is more likely to notice the instruction.
4. **Offer a limited acceptable choice.** Say "You can hold my hand or ride in the stroller" instead of asking open-ended questions. → *Expect:* the toddler has control inside the boundary.
5. **Follow through immediately.** If the unsafe or unwanted behavior continues, calmly stop it or remove the object. → *Expect:* the limit becomes real through action.
6. **Name the feeling without changing the rule.** Say "You're mad. I won't let you hit." → *Expect:* the child hears empathy and the same boundary.
7. **Redirect to what they can do.** Offer a safe alternative such as throwing soft balls into a basket or stomping feet. → *Expect:* energy moves to an allowed behavior.
8. **Repeat consistently.** Use the same phrase and consequence each time for several days. → *Expect:* the toddler begins to anticipate the boundary.
9. **Repair after the storm.** Once calm, reconnect with a hug, simple apology practice, or cleanup help. → *Expect:* the relationship resets without removing accountability.

## Decision points

- Behavior is dangerous → physically block or remove the child first, then explain.
- Toddler is hungry, tired, or overstimulated → meet the need and lower expectations temporarily.
- Limit is enforced differently by caregivers → agree on one script and consequence.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too many words:** detect the toddler escalating during long explanations → shorten to one rule and one action.
- **F2 Empty threats:** detect repeated warnings with no follow-through → choose a consequence you can calmly do every time.
- **F3 Limit changes during tantrum:** detect giving in to stop crying → hold the boundary and offer comfort.
- **F4 Environment invites failure:** detect repeated climbing, grabbing, or throwing → childproof or remove tempting items.

## Verification

The toddler hears a short rule, receives a consistent follow-through action, has an acceptable alternative, and reconnects after calming.

## Variations

- Public place: use fewer words, move the child to safety, and debrief later.
- Bedtime: use a predictable return-to-bed script and minimal engagement.
- Aggression: block immediately, attend to the hurt child, then guide repair.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary limits, but physical safety comes first. Confirm roads, stairs, hot surfaces, water, cords, and choking hazards are blocked before relying on verbal limits.
