## Goal

You teach a child basic water safety habits: ask permission, enter safely, keep an adult watcher, float or reach for help, and avoid risky water behavior.

## Preconditions

- A sober, attentive adult is assigned to supervise without phone distraction.
- Practice happens in a controlled pool or shallow calm water appropriate for the child's age and ability.
- Proper flotation devices are available when needed, and swim lessons are used when accessible.

## Steps

1. **Assign a water watcher.** Name the adult responsible for constant visual supervision before the child approaches water. ⚠️ *Hazard:* drowning can be quick and quiet; confirm one adult is watching only the water. → *Expect:* supervision responsibility is explicit.
2. **Teach permission first.** The child must ask and wait for "yes" before entering any pool, tub, beach, pond, or splash area. → *Expect:* the child pauses at the edge and looks for an adult.
3. **Show safe entry and exit.** Practice steps, ladder, or seated entry; identify exits before play starts. → *Expect:* the child knows how to get in and out without jumping unexpectedly.
4. **Practice face comfort gradually.** Blow bubbles, wet face, and submerge only when calm and supervised; never force dunking. → *Expect:* water confidence grows without panic.
5. **Teach float and call.** Practice rolling or leaning back with support, keeping mouth/nose clear, and calling for help. → *Expect:* the child can attempt a help position with assistance.
6. **Teach reach or throw, do not go.** For helping someone else, use a towel, noodle, branch, or flotation item from land instead of jumping in. → *Expect:* the child does not enter water to rescue.
7. **Set pool and open-water rules.** No running, pushing, breath-holding contests, drain play, swimming alone, or entering unknown water. → *Expect:* the child can repeat the rules.
8. **Use life jackets correctly.** In boats or open water, use a properly fitted approved life jacket, not inflatable toys as safety gear. → *Expect:* jacket is snug and cannot ride over the chin or ears.
9. **Repeat in real contexts.** Review rules at every pool, beach, bath, or dock, and keep barriers/gates closed. → *Expect:* safety habits transfer beyond lessons.

## Decision points

- Child cannot follow instructions near water → keep them within touch supervision and leave if limits fail.
- Open water has currents, waves, cold temperature, poor visibility, or boats → use life jackets and stricter boundaries.
- Home has a pool, hot tub, pond, or drainage feature → install barriers, alarms, and locked access; teaching rules is not enough.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Assumed supervision:** detect adults chatting while everyone thinks someone else is watching → assign one named watcher and rotate formally.
- **F2 Floaties create false confidence:** detect reliance on arm bands or toys → use approved life jackets where required and continue skill practice.
- **F3 Child runs back to water after leaving:** detect repeated return attempts → secure barriers, change clothes, and maintain supervision until away.
- **F4 Panic during practice:** detect coughing, crying, or clinging → stop, comfort, and restart with shallower, simpler skills later.

## Verification

The child asks permission before water entry, identifies the water watcher and exit, follows no-running/no-pushing rules, practices floating or calling for help with supervision, and wears a correctly fitted life jacket when conditions require it.

## Variations

- Toddler: use touch supervision, barriers, and simple permission language; do not rely on the toddler remembering rules.
- Older child: add treading water, safe diving rules, current awareness, and buddy checks.
- Beach or lake: teach currents, drop-offs, cold shock, water quality warnings, and staying within marked areas.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because drowning can occur silently and quickly. Confirm active adult supervision, physical barriers, appropriate flotation, and emergency readiness before any water access.
