## Goal

You clean and sterilize baby bottles, nipples, and feeding parts when needed so milk is prepared in equipment with reduced germ risk.

## Preconditions

- Bottles and parts are heat-safe or approved for the sterilizing method you will use.
- You have clean water, dish soap, a bottle brush used only for infant feeding items, and a clean drying area.
- You know whether the baby is premature, under 2 months, immunocompromised, or medically fragile, because sterilizing is more important then.

## Steps

1. **Wash hands and separate parts.** Disassemble bottles, nipples, collars, valves, caps, and pump parts. → *Expect:* every surface that contacts milk is exposed for cleaning.
2. **Rinse milk residue promptly.** Rinse each part under running water before washing. → *Expect:* visible milk film is removed.
3. **Wash with soap and hot water.** Scrub with a clean bottle brush, squeezing water through nipples and valves. → *Expect:* parts look and feel clean with no residue.
4. **Rinse thoroughly.** Use clean running water so no soap remains. → *Expect:* parts have no suds or slippery film.
5. **Choose a sterilizing method.** [BRANCH: boiling | steam sterilizer | microwave steam bag | dishwasher sanitize cycle | chemical sterilizing solution] Follow product instructions exactly for time, water amount, and cooling. ⚠️ *Hazard:* steam and boiling water cause burns; confirm parts are cool before handling. → *Expect:* the full sterilizing cycle completes.
6. **Air-dry on a clean surface.** Use a clean towel or drying rack reserved for feeding items; do not wipe inside parts with a used dish towel. → *Expect:* parts dry without recontamination.
7. **Store assembled or covered when dry.** Put dry parts in a clean covered bin or cabinet; if assembling, wash hands first. → *Expect:* sterilized parts stay clean until use.
8. **Repeat at the right frequency.** Sterilize daily for higher-risk infants or as clinician/product guidance requires; otherwise thorough washing may be enough after the early newborn period. → *Expect:* cleaning intensity matches the baby's risk.
9. **Discard damaged parts.** Replace cracked bottles, cloudy plastic, torn nipples, sticky valves, or parts with trapped residue. → *Expect:* feeding equipment is intact and cleanable.

## Decision points

- Baby is premature, immunocompromised, or under medical instruction → sterilize more often and follow clinician guidance.
- Water supply is unsafe or under boil-water advisory → use boiled/cooled or bottled water as local health guidance directs.
- Product cannot be boiled or steamed → use the manufacturer's approved method only.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Parts melt or warp:** detect changed shape or poor fit → discard damaged parts and use the correct method next time.
- **F2 Milk residue remains:** detect cloudy film or odor → rewash before sterilizing; sterilizing does not remove debris.
- **F3 Recontamination after sterilizing:** detect parts placed in a dirty sink or wiped with a used towel → wash and sterilize again.
- **F4 Burn injury:** detect contact with steam or boiling water → cool the burn under running water and seek medical care for serious burns.

## Verification

All bottle and nipple parts are visibly clean, completed an approved sterilizing cycle, dried on a clean surface, and are stored covered or assembled with clean hands.

## Variations

- Dishwasher: use hot water and heated dry or sanitize cycle if parts are dishwasher-safe and secured in a basket.
- Travel: use ready-to-feed formula and pre-sterilized bottles when safe washing is not available.
- Pump parts: follow pump-maker and clinician guidance; some parts require special handling.

## Safety & privacy

High risk for very young or medically fragile infants because contaminated feeding equipment can cause serious infection. Confirm cleaning before sterilizing, follow time/temperature instructions, prevent burns, and keep feeding items away from general sink contamination.
