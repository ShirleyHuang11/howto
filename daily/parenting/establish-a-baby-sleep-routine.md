## Goal

You create a predictable, developmentally appropriate sleep routine that helps a baby wind down while preserving safe sleep practices every time.

## Preconditions

- The baby has a safe sleep space: firm, flat mattress; fitted sheet; no loose blankets, pillows, bumpers, toys, or positioners.
- You understand that newborn sleep is irregular and frequent night waking is normal.
- Feeding, diapering, medical, reflux, or prematurity instructions from the clinician are available.

## Steps

1. **Set the sleep environment.** Dim lights, reduce noise, use a comfortable room temperature, and prepare sleep clothing or sleep sack. → *Expect:* the room supports sleep without unsafe bedding.
2. **Anchor the routine to the baby's age.** Use short wake windows for newborns and gradually longer ones as the baby grows; avoid keeping an overtired baby awake for a schedule. → *Expect:* bedtime timing matches sleepy cues and age.
3. **Do care tasks in the same order.** Feed, burp, diaper, pajamas, brief song or book, then sleep space. → *Expect:* the baby receives consistent cues that sleep is next.
4. **Place the baby on their back.** Put the baby down on their back for every sleep, including naps. ⚠️ *Hazard:* side or stomach sleep increases risk for infants; confirm back placement and an empty sleep surface before leaving. → *Expect:* baby starts sleep on their back in a clear crib, bassinet, or play yard.
5. **Separate feeding from fully asleep when practical.** For older babies, try putting down drowsy but awake; for newborns, prioritize safe transfer after feeding. → *Expect:* the routine supports settling without compromising feeding or safety.
6. **Respond consistently to wakes.** Check hunger, diaper, temperature, illness, and comfort; keep night care quiet and boring. → *Expect:* needs are met without turning every wake into playtime.
7. **Track patterns for a week.** Note bedtime, naps, wakes, feeds, and unusual events. → *Expect:* patterns and realistic adjustment points become visible.
8. **Adjust one variable at a time.** Shift bedtime, nap timing, light exposure, or routine length gradually rather than changing everything at once. → *Expect:* you can tell which change helped or hurt.
9. **Escalate medical concerns.** Call the clinician for breathing concerns, poor feeding, fever, unusual lethargy, or sleep changes tied to illness. → *Expect:* unsafe or medical sleep issues are handled clinically.

## Decision points

- Baby is under 4 months → use gentle routine cues, not rigid sleep training.
- Baby rolls independently → still place on back initially, but stop swaddling and use a safe sleep sack.
- Caregivers are dangerously sleep-deprived → create shifts or ask for help so no one falls asleep holding the baby in an unsafe place.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unsafe sleep surface creeps in:** detect pillows, blankets, loungers, or toys in sleep area → remove them before every sleep.
- **F2 Routine is too long:** detect baby gets overtired before crib → shorten to essential care and one calming cue.
- **F3 Feed-sleep association is unmanageable:** detect older baby needs feeding at every brief wake → adjust gradually with clinician guidance if growth is normal.
- **F4 Caregiver falls asleep during feeding:** detect repeated drowsing on sofa or chair → feed in a safer setup and transfer baby promptly.

## Verification

The baby follows a repeatable wind-down pattern and is placed for every sleep on their back on a firm, flat, empty sleep surface, with caregivers able to describe the plan.

## Variations

- Newborn: routine may be only diaper, feed, burp, swaddle if safe, and crib.
- Older infant: add a short book, consistent phrase, and sleep sack.
- Room sharing: many families keep the baby in the caregiver's room on a separate sleep surface during early infancy.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because infant sleep setup affects suffocation and injury risk. Confirm back placement, empty crib, no swaddle after rolling signs, and caregiver alertness before each sleep.
