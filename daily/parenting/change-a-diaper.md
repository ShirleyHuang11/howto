## Goal

You change a baby's diaper cleanly and safely, prevent falls and skin irritation, and notice diaper findings that need medical attention.

## Preconditions

- You have a clean diaper, wipes or warm water/cloth, diaper cream if needed, disposal bag or pail, and clean clothes if soiled.
- The changing surface is stable, cleanable, and within arm's reach of supplies.
- The baby is not left alone on any elevated surface.

## Steps

1. **Prepare before placing the baby down.** Open the clean diaper, wipes, cream, and disposal method. → *Expect:* no need to step away mid-change.
2. **Secure the baby with one hand.** Use a changing pad strap if present, but keep one hand on the baby. ⚠️ *Hazard:* babies can roll unexpectedly; confirm supplies are reachable before releasing support. → *Expect:* the baby remains safely on the surface.
3. **Open the dirty diaper and assess.** Note stool, urine, rash, blood, diarrhea, constipation signs, or unusual color. → *Expect:* you know whether this is a normal change or something to monitor.
4. **Clean front to back.** Wipe gently from cleanest to dirtiest areas; for vulvas, wipe front to back; for penises, clean folds without forcefully retracting foreskin. → *Expect:* skin is clean without aggressive rubbing.
5. **Let skin dry briefly.** Air-dry or pat dry before cream and diaper closure. → *Expect:* moisture is reduced under the new diaper.
6. **Apply barrier cream if needed.** Use a thin protective layer for mild redness or a thicker layer for rash as directed. → *Expect:* irritated areas are protected from urine and stool.
7. **Fasten the clean diaper.** Slide it under the baby, bring front up, fasten snugly below the belly button, and check leg cuffs. → *Expect:* diaper fits with no tight marks and no gaps.
8. **Dispose and disinfect.** Seal the dirty diaper, wash or sanitize hands, and clean the changing surface. → *Expect:* waste is contained and germs are not spread.
9. **Escalate concerning findings.** Call the clinician for fever, blood in stool, black stool after the newborn meconium period, dehydration signs, severe rash, or persistent diarrhea. → *Expect:* abnormal findings are not ignored.

## Decision points

- Newborn umbilical stump is present → fold diaper below it or use a newborn cutout.
- Diaper rash is mild → increase changes, dry skin, and barrier cream; severe, blistering, or persistent rash needs medical advice.
- Baby starts rolling → move diaper changes to the floor if an elevated surface is hard to manage.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Baby nearly rolls off:** detect loss of hand contact or sudden rolling → stop, secure baby, and move future changes to the floor.
- **F2 Leaks continue:** detect wet clothes after changes → check size, fastening, absorbency, and leg cuffs.
- **F3 Rash worsens:** detect open sores, yeast-like bright red rash with satellite spots, or pain → call the clinician.
- **F4 Contamination spreads:** detect dirty hands touching clean supplies → pause, clean hands, disinfect surfaces, and replace contaminated items.

## Verification

The baby is in a clean, correctly fitted diaper, the skin is clean and protected, waste is disposed of, hands are washed, and any concerning stool, urine, or rash has been documented or escalated.

## Variations

- Cloth diapers: use the laundering and liner system recommended by the diaper maker and avoid creams that damage absorbency unless liner-protected.
- Newborns: expect frequent changes and track wet/dirty diapers if the clinician asks.
- Daycare handoff: label diapers and creams, and provide written rash-care instructions if required.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from falls, infection, and missed health signs. Keep one hand on the baby, never leave them on an elevated surface, wash hands, and share diaper photos only with caregivers or clinicians when medically necessary.
