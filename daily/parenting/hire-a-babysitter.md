## Goal

You hire a babysitter who is capable, trustworthy, clear on expectations, and prepared to keep the child safe while you are away.

## Preconditions

- Child's schedule, allergies, medications, emergency contacts, and house rules.
- A job date, hours, location, pay range, and transportation expectations.
- Permission to check references or background information where appropriate.

## Steps

1. **Define the job.** Write down date, hours, number and ages of children, bedtime, meals, pets, driving, and pay. → *Expect:* the sitter can tell what work is required before accepting.
2. **Find candidates through trusted sources.** Ask known families, schools, community groups, or vetted services. → *Expect:* candidates have some traceable connection or profile.
3. **Screen for experience and fit.** Ask about age groups handled, infant care if relevant, first aid/CPR, discipline approach, emergencies, and comfort with pets or driving. → *Expect:* unsuitable candidates are filtered out.
4. **Check references.** Contact at least one prior family and ask about reliability, safety, communication, and whether they would rehire. → *Expect:* references support the sitter's claims.
5. **Confirm safety credentials for higher-risk care.** For infants, swimming, driving, or medical needs, require relevant experience and current training. → *Expect:* the sitter can explain how they would handle realistic situations.
6. **Agree on pay and logistics.** Confirm hourly rate, minimum pay, cancellation expectations, arrival time, transport, and payment method. → *Expect:* both sides know the financial terms.
7. **Do an orientation.** Walk through exits, alarms, bedtime, food, diapers/toileting, medicines, screens, visitors, and forbidden areas. → *Expect:* the sitter can repeat the key rules.
8. **Leave written emergency instructions.** Include your location, return time, phone numbers, pediatrician, nearest urgent care, allergies, medication rules, and authorized contacts. → *Expect:* the sitter has written instructions, not only verbal memory.
9. **Check in and debrief.** Ask for a short update during the job if appropriate, then review bedtime, meals, incidents, and pay before the sitter leaves. → *Expect:* the job closes with no unresolved safety or payment questions.

## Decision points

- Infant, special needs, or overnight care → raise the experience threshold and consider a trial period while you are nearby.
- Sitter will drive the child → verify license, insurance, car seat competence, and written permission.
- Candidate resists references or safety rules → choose someone else.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Late or no-show sitter:** detect missed arrival time → use backup care and do not rebook without a credible explanation.
- **F2 Rule mismatch:** detect screens, visitors, food, or bedtime ignored → clarify once for minor issues; stop using for safety breaches.
- **F3 Child reports discomfort:** detect fear, secrecy, or inappropriate behavior → take the report seriously, ensure safety, and seek professional or legal guidance if needed.
- **F4 Medication confusion:** detect sitter unsure about dosing → do not delegate medication unless written instructions and permission are clear.

## Verification

The sitter has accepted clear terms, references are checked, emergency instructions are written, house rules are understood, and payment and return logistics are confirmed.

## Variations

- Agency or app sitter: review platform screening limits; still interview and check references when possible.
- Mother's helper: parent stays home, so requirements can be lighter but safety orientation still matters.
- Overnight sitter: include sleep arrangements, night waking rules, alarms, and morning routine.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a non-household adult supervises children and enters your home. Confirm identity, references, emergency instructions, visitor rules, medication rules, and payment before leaving.
