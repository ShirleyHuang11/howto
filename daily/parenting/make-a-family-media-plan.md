## Goal

You set clear, realistic rules for screens, games, social media, and devices so everyone in the household knows when, where, and how media is used.

## Preconditions

- A list of the devices, apps, games, streaming services, and school platforms your child uses.
- A basic sense of your child's sleep schedule, school workload, activities, and family routines.
- Agreement that adults will model the rules they expect children to follow.

## Steps

1. **List current media use.** Write down each child's regular screen activities, approximate daily time, and where devices are used. → *Expect:* a plain inventory of devices, apps, games, and high-use times.
2. **Choose device-free anchors.** Pick nonnegotiable times and places such as meals, bedrooms overnight, homework start time, car rides, or the first hour after school. → *Expect:* a short list of device-free zones and periods.
3. **Set sleep-protecting rules.** Move charging out of bedrooms or set a nightly dock time at least 30-60 minutes before sleep. → *Expect:* devices have a visible overnight home outside the bed area.
4. **Define allowed content and contacts.** Name which apps, ratings, privacy settings, multiplayer features, and messaging contacts are allowed by age. → *Expect:* the child can say what is allowed, what needs permission, and what is off-limits.
5. **Build in positive media.** Include video calls with relatives, educational tools, creative apps, audiobooks, or co-viewed shows so the plan is not only restriction. → *Expect:* the plan includes healthy uses, not just time limits.
6. **Write the plan in child-readable language.** Keep it to one page with rules, consequences, and review dates. → *Expect:* the plan can be read aloud in under five minutes.
7. **Review it together.** Ask the child what feels unclear, what will be hard, and what privilege they can earn with responsible use. → *Expect:* at least one child concern is addressed without removing the core safety rules.
8. **Turn on matching controls.** Set app limits, content filters, router schedules, store purchase approvals, or device downtime to support the written rules. → *Expect:* device settings match the plan instead of relying only on reminders.
9. **Schedule a check-in.** Put a review date on the calendar after 2-4 weeks. → *Expect:* the family has a specific date to adjust what is not working.

## Decision points

- Child needs a device for school → separate school access from entertainment access and whitelist required sites.
- Rules are constantly argued → make limits more visible and predictable, then apply consequences consistently.
- Teen has a phone outside the home → emphasize privacy, location-sharing expectations, driving safety, and how to ask for help.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rules too vague:** detect repeated arguments over "a little longer" → rewrite limits as exact times, places, or conditions.
- **F2 Adults undermine the plan:** detect parents using phones during device-free times → revise adult habits or exempt only true emergencies.
- **F3 Filters block schoolwork:** detect blocked assignments or research sites → create a supervised exception and document it.
- **F4 Secret accounts appear:** detect hidden apps, alternate logins, or deleted history → pause privileges, discuss trust, and rebuild access gradually.

## Verification

The household has a written plan, device settings support it, devices are out of bedrooms overnight if that is part of the rule, and everyone knows the next review date.

## Variations

- `young-child`: use picture rules, short time windows, and co-viewing.
- `teen`: include social media privacy, group chats, late-night messaging, driving, sextortion risk, and school-device boundaries.
- Shared custody: keep the plan focused on rules in your home and coordinate with the other caregiver where possible.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but privacy and online contact matter. Confirm before turning on location sharing, reading private messages, or changing passwords; use monitoring to protect safety, not to shame the child.
