## Goal

You introduce solid foods when the baby is developmentally ready, using safe textures, supervised feeding, and allergen introduction guidance that reduces choking and reaction risk.

## Preconditions

- The baby shows readiness signs: sits with support, has good head/neck control, opens mouth for food, and can move food back to swallow.
- The baby still receives breast milk or formula as the primary nutrition source unless a clinician says otherwise.
- You have a safe high chair, clean utensils, and age-appropriate foods prepared in safe shapes and textures.

## Steps

1. **Confirm readiness and timing.** Most babies start around 6 months; do not start before developmental readiness. → *Expect:* the baby can sit supported and control head position during feeding.
2. **Seat the baby upright and supervised.** Buckle the high chair and keep the baby sitting, awake, and watched. ⚠️ *Hazard:* choking can happen silently; confirm an attentive adult is present for the entire meal. → *Expect:* the baby's airway and posture are protected.
3. **Start with soft, simple foods.** Offer iron-rich purees, mashed foods, or safely prepared soft finger foods depending on your approach. → *Expect:* food texture can be swallowed or mashed with gums.
4. **Avoid choking shapes and unsafe foods.** No whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, hard raw vegetables, chunks of meat/cheese, spoonfuls of nut butter, or round coin-shaped pieces. → *Expect:* foods are soft, thinly spread, mashed, shredded, or cut lengthwise as appropriate.
5. **Offer tiny amounts without forcing.** Let the baby taste and explore; stop when they turn away, close mouth, gag repeatedly, or get upset. → *Expect:* feeding stays responsive and low pressure.
6. **Introduce common allergens thoughtfully.** Peanut, egg, dairy, wheat, soy, fish, shellfish, sesame, and tree nuts should be introduced in safe forms; ask the clinician first for severe eczema or known allergy. → *Expect:* allergens are introduced as age-safe textures, not choking hazards.
7. **Watch for reactions.** Monitor for hives, vomiting, swelling, cough, wheeze, pale limpness, or breathing trouble after new allergens. → *Expect:* mild versus urgent symptoms are recognized.
8. **Add water only in small practice amounts.** Use an open cup or straw cup with meals if age-appropriate; keep milk/formula primary. → *Expect:* cup practice does not replace needed feeds.
9. **Build variety gradually.** Rotate vegetables, fruits, grains, proteins, and healthy fats while repeating exposures many times. → *Expect:* the baby experiences different tastes and textures over weeks.

## Decision points

- Baby has severe eczema, egg allergy, or prior reaction → ask the clinician or allergist before allergen introduction.
- Baby gags but recovers quickly → stay calm; gagging is common while learning, but review texture and size.
- Baby chokes, cannot cough, turns blue, or has breathing trouble → start infant choking first aid and call emergency services.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Food is a choking hazard:** detect round, hard, sticky, or large pieces → remove and prepare in a safer texture or shape.
- **F2 Allergic reaction:** detect hives, swelling, vomiting, cough, wheeze, or lethargy → follow emergency plan and seek urgent care for breathing or multi-system symptoms.
- **F3 Constipation after solids:** detect hard stools or pain → offer appropriate fiber foods and discuss fluids with the clinician.
- **F4 Feeding becomes pressured:** detect crying, forced spooning, or battles → pause and return to responsive feeding.

## Verification

The baby sits upright, tries safe-textured foods under direct supervision, has no choking event, and caregivers know which foods were introduced and any reaction observed.

## Variations

- Puree-led: gradually thicken textures and add soft lumps as skills improve.
- Baby-led feeding: use soft, graspable pieces prepared to minimize choking risk and supervise closely.
- Vegetarian family: prioritize iron and zinc sources such as fortified cereals, beans, lentils, tofu, eggs if used, and nut butters thinned safely.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because choking and allergic reactions can be life-threatening. Confirm developmental readiness, upright supervision, safe food shapes, and emergency response knowledge before each meal.
