## Goal

You support a child with nighttime wetting using low-shame routines, practical protection, and medical escalation when signs suggest a health issue.

## Preconditions

- The child is old enough that nighttime dryness is a realistic goal, usually after daytime toilet training is established.
- Waterproof mattress protection, spare bedding, and easy laundry access.
- No punishment or shaming as part of the plan.

## Steps

1. **Normalize and remove blame.** Tell the child bedwetting is common and not something they are doing on purpose. → *Expect:* the child understands the plan is help, not punishment.
2. **Screen for medical flags.** Note pain with urination, excessive thirst, daytime accidents, constipation, snoring, new wetting after long dryness, or urinary infections. → *Expect:* you know whether to call a clinician before training.
3. **Set a simple evening routine.** Encourage regular fluids earlier in the day, bathroom before bed, and avoiding large drinks right before sleep without restricting hydration harshly. → *Expect:* bedtime includes one calm bathroom trip.
4. **Protect the sleep setup.** Use a waterproof mattress cover, absorbent underwear if needed, and spare sheets nearby. → *Expect:* cleanup can happen quickly with less distress.
5. **Give the child an age-appropriate cleanup role.** They can place pajamas in a hamper or help pull off sheets without being scolded. → *Expect:* responsibility is practical, not punitive.
6. **Track dry and wet nights.** Record patterns, constipation, late drinks, and wake times. → *Expect:* you have objective information instead of guesses.
7. **Consider an alarm if the child is ready.** Bedwetting alarms work best when the child wants to participate and adults can help them wake. → *Expect:* the family can commit to consistent alarm response.
8. **Reward process, not dryness only.** Praise bathroom routine, helping with cleanup, and responding to the alarm. → *Expect:* progress is reinforced even when wet nights continue.
9. **Consult a clinician when needed.** Call for new onset, pain, daytime symptoms, constipation, excessive thirst, sleep breathing concerns, or major distress. → *Expect:* medical causes are not missed.

## Decision points

- Child is under about 5-6 years or not bothered → focus on protection and reassurance, not training pressure.
- Child has constipation → address it with a clinician because it can contribute to wetting.
- Sleepovers or camps are coming → ask the clinician about short-term options and plan discreet supplies.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Shame cycle:** detect hiding sheets or lying → remove punishment and make cleanup neutral.
- **F2 Alarm abandoned too soon:** detect inconsistent waking or adult support → pause or restart when the household can respond nightly.
- **F3 Missed health issue:** detect new wetting after dryness, pain, thirst, or daytime accidents → seek medical evaluation.
- **F4 Sleep disruption overload:** detect exhausted child or parent → simplify to protection and revisit later.

## Verification

The child has a shame-free plan, protected bedding, a bedtime bathroom routine, pattern tracking, and clear criteria for when to involve a clinician.

## Variations

- Younger child: use pull-ups or absorbent underwear without calling them babyish.
- Older child: include privacy, laundry independence, and sleepover strategies.
- Medication option: use only with clinician guidance, often for specific events rather than permanent training.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because urinary symptoms can signal medical issues and shame can harm. Do not punish bedwetting; confirm medical red flags and protect the child's privacy.
