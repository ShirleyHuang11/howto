## Goal

You feed a newborn safely, whether breast/chest feeding, expressed milk, formula, or a medically directed combination, and confirm the baby is transferring enough milk for their age and condition.

## Preconditions

- The baby is awake enough to feed, breathing normally, and has no urgent distress signs such as blue color, repeated choking, limpness, or fever.
- Feeding supplies are clean, formula is prepared exactly as directed, and expressed milk has been stored safely.
- You have any clinician instructions for prematurity, jaundice, weight loss, tongue-tie, reflux, or low blood sugar.

## Steps

1. **Wash hands and prepare supplies.** Clean hands, gather burp cloths, bottle or nursing pillow if used, and verify milk or formula temperature is lukewarm or room temperature, not hot. → *Expect:* everything needed is within reach before the baby is positioned.
2. **Position the baby upright enough to protect breathing.** Hold the newborn close with head and neck supported, nose clear, and body aligned. ⚠️ *Hazard:* never prop a bottle or feed a baby lying flat; confirm an adult is holding and watching the baby. → *Expect:* the baby can breathe, suck, pause, and swallow without obstruction.
3. **Offer the breast/chest or bottle calmly.** For breast/chest feeding, bring baby to the nipple with a wide latch; for bottle feeding, use paced feeding with the bottle more horizontal and pauses. → *Expect:* rhythmic sucking with audible or visible swallowing and relaxed hands after letdown or flow begins.
4. **Watch hunger and stress cues.** Continue while the baby roots, sucks, and stays engaged; pause for coughing, gulping, grimacing, milk spilling, arching, or turning away. → *Expect:* feeding pace matches the baby's ability to coordinate suck-swallow-breathe.
5. **Burp as needed.** Pause midway or when the baby slows, then hold upright and gently pat or rub the back. → *Expect:* a burp, relaxed posture, or enough comfort to resume or finish.
6. **Stop based on satiety and plan.** End when the baby releases, relaxes, sleeps with a satisfied body, or takes the clinician-recommended volume. Do not force the last drops. → *Expect:* the baby appears settled and is not being pressured to continue.
7. **Record intake and diapers when needed.** In the early days or if medically advised, note time, side or volume, spit-up, and wet/dirty diapers. → *Expect:* a trackable pattern for weight and hydration checks.
8. **Clean up safely.** Discard leftover formula from the bottle after the recommended time, refrigerate usable expressed milk according to guidance, and wash feeding parts. → *Expect:* no questionable milk is saved for later feeding.
9. **Seek help for warning signs.** Contact the baby's clinician urgently for poor feeding, fewer wet diapers than expected, fever, persistent vomiting, dehydration signs, lethargy, breathing trouble, or worsening jaundice. → *Expect:* medical concerns are escalated promptly.

## Decision points

- Baby is sleepy because of jaundice, prematurity, illness, or medication exposure → follow clinician feeding intervals and wake-up instructions rather than waiting for cues only.
- Breast/chest feeding hurts after latch adjustment or nipples are damaged → contact a lactation professional and pediatric clinician for assessment.
- Formula is needed or chosen → use the exact powder-to-water ratio and safe water guidance for your location.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Shallow latch:** detect clicking, nipple pain, dimpling cheeks, or poor transfer → break suction gently, reposition belly-to-belly, and relatch wider.
- **F2 Bottle flow too fast:** detect coughing, gulping, milk leaking, or panic → pause, sit baby more upright, use paced feeding, and consider a slower nipple.
- **F3 Inadequate intake:** detect persistent hunger, lethargy, weight concerns, or too few wet diapers → call the clinician the same day for a feeding plan.
- **F4 Unsafe formula mixing:** detect guessed scoops, diluted formula, or concentrated formula → remake exactly per label or medical instructions.

## Verification

The newborn completes a feed without choking or distress, has age-appropriate wet and dirty diapers, settles after feeding, and is following the weight and feeding plan set by the clinician.

## Variations

- Breast/chest feeding: early feeds may be frequent and cluster; latch quality and diaper output matter more than minutes alone.
- Bottle feeding: paced feeding helps the baby control flow and reduces overfeeding.
- Medically fragile newborn: follow the written neonatal or pediatric feeding plan exactly, including fortification or timed feeds.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because newborn feeding affects hydration, blood sugar, breathing, and weight gain. Confirm formula ratios, milk storage, and baby positioning before each feed; seek medical care for fever, lethargy, breathing trouble, dehydration, or repeated choking.
