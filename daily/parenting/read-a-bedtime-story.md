## Goal

You use a bedtime story to help a child wind down, connect with you, and transition toward sleep.

## Preconditions

- A bedtime routine already underway: bathroom, pajamas, teeth, and water if needed.
- One or two books appropriate to the child's age and attention span.
- A sleep space that is safe for the child's age.

## Steps

1. **Set the story boundary.** Decide before starting whether you will read one book, two short books, or a set number of pages. → *Expect:* the child knows when reading will end.
2. **Choose a calming book.** Pick familiar, gentle, or rhythmic stories instead of highly exciting or scary content. → *Expect:* the book supports winding down.
3. **Reduce stimulation.** Dim lights enough for sleep while keeping the page readable, and put screens away. → *Expect:* the room feels quieter and slower.
4. **Invite small participation.** Let the child turn pages, point to pictures, predict what comes next, or repeat a phrase. → *Expect:* the child is engaged without becoming wound up.
5. **Read slowly and warmly.** Use expression but keep volume and pace lower than daytime reading. → *Expect:* the child's body and voice begin to settle.
6. **Handle interruptions briefly.** Answer simple questions, then return to the story without opening a long conversation. → *Expect:* the routine stays on track.
7. **Close the story consistently.** Use the same phrase, bookmark, or final page cue each night. → *Expect:* the ending is predictable.
8. **Transition directly to sleep.** Put the book away, turn off or lower the light, and move to your normal goodnight routine. → *Expect:* reading does not restart negotiations.
9. **Keep books accessible for tomorrow.** Return library books or favorites to a consistent spot. → *Expect:* the next bedtime can start smoothly.

## Decision points

- Child asks for one more book every night → set the number before reading and offer a choice for tomorrow.
- Child becomes scared or upset → stop the book, comfort briefly, and choose calmer material next time.
- Child cannot sit through a book → use shorter board books, picture walks, or one poem.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Story turns into delay tactic:** detect repeated water, questions, or book requests → move those needs before reading and keep the end firm.
- **F2 Book is overstimulating:** detect jumping, shouting, or fear → switch to calmer books.
- **F3 Parent rushes angrily:** detect conflict during reading → shorten the story rather than making it tense.
- **F4 Screens replace reading:** detect videos delaying sleep → keep screens out of the bedtime story slot.

## Verification

The child hears the agreed story, participates calmly, accepts the consistent ending cue, and moves into the next sleep step without restarting the routine.

## Variations

- Baby: read short board books, label pictures, and keep safe sleep rules afterward.
- Early reader: alternate pages or let the child read one familiar sentence.
- Multiple children: choose one shared book plus occasional individual turns by night.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. For infants, remove books, pillows, blankets, and loose items from the crib before sleep; do not use screens as the bedtime story routine.
