## Goal

You help a child build friendship skills and create low-pressure opportunities to connect with peers.

## Preconditions

- A sense of the child's interests, temperament, school situation, and social challenges.
- Contact with teachers, coaches, caregivers, or other parents if needed.
- Willingness to coach without taking over the child's relationships.

## Steps

1. **Observe the pattern.** Notice whether the child struggles to start play, join groups, handle conflict, share interests, or recover from rejection. → *Expect:* you can describe the actual skill gap.
2. **Ask gently about their experience.** Use open questions such as "Who did you enjoy being near today?" rather than "Why don't you have friends?" → *Expect:* the child shares names, worries, or preferences without feeling blamed.
3. **Choose one friendship skill to practice.** Pick greeting, asking to join, taking turns, noticing boredom, or inviting someone. → *Expect:* the goal is small enough to rehearse.
4. **Role-play exact words.** Practice short scripts: "Can I play too?" "Do you want to trade turns?" or "Want to sit with me?" → *Expect:* the child can say one usable phrase.
5. **Create repeated exposure.** Choose clubs, playground times, library groups, sports, art classes, or neighborhood routines where the same children appear. → *Expect:* the child has recurring chances, not one-off pressure.
6. **Arrange a low-pressure meetup.** Invite one compatible child for a short, structured activity. → *Expect:* the playdate has a clear activity and end time.
7. **Coach after, not during, when possible.** Debrief privately about what went well and one thing to try next time. → *Expect:* feedback preserves dignity.
8. **Coordinate with adults if needed.** Ask teachers where the child seems comfortable and whether seating or group pairing could help. → *Expect:* supportive adults know the child is working on connection.
9. **Watch for bullying or exclusion.** Separate ordinary friendship bumps from repeated targeting, humiliation, or threats. → *Expect:* serious social harm is escalated appropriately.

## Decision points

- Child wants friends but avoids groups → start with structured interest-based activities.
- Child is content with one or two friends → do not force popularity; support healthy connections.
- Bullying is present → document incidents and involve school leadership.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Parent overmanages:** detect the child relying on you to speak for them → step back and coach privately.
- **F2 Poor match:** detect repeated awkward or one-sided playdates → try a different peer or activity.
- **F3 Skill goal too broad:** detect "be nicer" or "join in" failing → teach one observable behavior.
- **F4 Hidden anxiety or neurodevelopmental needs:** detect intense distress, rigidity, or missed cues → consider teacher input or professional evaluation.

## Verification

The child has practiced one specific friendship skill, has recurring peer opportunities, and can identify at least one peer or activity where connection feels possible.

## Variations

- Shy child: use parallel activities and one-on-one meetups before groups.
- Neurodivergent child: use explicit scripts, visual expectations, and interest-based peers.
- New school: ask the teacher for compatible buddy suggestions and clubs with stable attendance.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but protect the child's dignity. Do not share sensitive social struggles widely, and escalate bullying, threats, coercion, or online harassment.
