## Goal

You clean a newborn safely with a sponge bath or shallow bath while preventing drowning, burns, chilling, and skin irritation.

## Preconditions

- The baby is stable, not ill, and not immediately after a large feed.
- You have a towel, washcloths, clean diaper, clean clothes, mild baby cleanser if needed, and a safe bathing surface.
- Until the umbilical stump falls off and heals, sponge bathing is usually preferred unless your clinician says otherwise.

## Steps

1. **Prepare everything first.** Place towel, washcloth, diaper, clothes, and cleanser within arm's reach. → *Expect:* you never need to leave the baby during bathing.
2. **Set safe water temperature.** Use warm, not hot, water; test with wrist or elbow and adjust the water heater if scald risk is high. → *Expect:* water feels comfortably warm and not hot.
3. **Use only a small amount of water.** For tub bathing, keep water shallow; for sponge bathing, use a basin and keep most of the baby wrapped. ⚠️ *Hazard:* babies can drown in very little water; confirm constant hands-on supervision before starting. → *Expect:* water level and setup are controlled.
4. **Undress the baby last.** Keep the room warm and cover body parts not being washed. → *Expect:* the baby is not chilled.
5. **Wash face first with plain water.** Clean eyes from inner to outer corner with separate cloth areas, then face and scalp. → *Expect:* face is clean without soap in eyes.
6. **Wash body gently.** Clean neck folds, underarms, hands, diaper area, and creases; avoid scrubbing and avoid pulling on the umbilical stump. → *Expect:* skin folds are clean and the stump stays dry and undisturbed.
7. **Keep one hand supporting the baby.** In a tub, support head and shoulders at all times and keep the face above water. → *Expect:* the baby cannot slip or submerge.
8. **Lift out and dry thoroughly.** Wrap in a towel, pat dry skin folds, and put on a clean diaper and clothes promptly. → *Expect:* baby is warm, dry, and dressed.
9. **Empty water and clean supplies immediately.** Drain the tub or basin, rinse washcloths, and store products safely. → *Expect:* no standing water or slippery setup remains.

## Decision points

- Umbilical stump is present or circumcision is healing → follow clinician instructions and avoid soaking unless cleared.
- Baby cries intensely or gets cold → stop, dry, dress, and try a shorter sponge bath later.
- Skin is very dry or eczema-prone → use minimal cleanser and ask the clinician about moisturizers.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Water too hot:** detect redness, crying on contact, or wrist test feels hot → remove baby, cool water, and seek care for any burn.
- **F2 Baby slips:** detect unstable grip or sliding → lift baby securely, reset position, and use a non-slip infant bath support if appropriate.
- **F3 Chilling:** detect shivering, mottled skin, or prolonged undressed time → stop and warm/dress the baby.
- **F4 Umbilical irritation:** detect redness spreading, foul odor, pus, or fever → call the clinician.

## Verification

The newborn is clean, warm, dry, dressed in a clean diaper and clothes, breathing normally, and was never left unattended near water.

## Variations

- Sponge bath: best for the earliest days; wash one area at a time while the baby stays mostly wrapped.
- Infant tub: use only a stable tub designed for babies and keep one hand on the baby.
- Cultural oils or products: avoid anything scented, harsh, or unverified on newborn skin unless the clinician approves.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because of drowning, scalding, falls, and newborn temperature instability. Confirm all supplies are ready, water is warm not hot, and an adult maintains touch supervision from start to finish.
