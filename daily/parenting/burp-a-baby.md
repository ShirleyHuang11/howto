## Goal

You help a baby release swallowed air during or after feeding while keeping their airway supported and avoiding unsafe pressure.

## Preconditions

- The baby has just fed or is pausing during a feed and is breathing normally.
- You have a burp cloth and a stable seated or standing position.
- The baby is not showing emergency signs such as blue color, limpness, repeated choking, or breathing trouble.

## Steps

1. **Place a cloth where spit-up may land.** Cover your shoulder, lap, or hand depending on the burping position. → *Expect:* clothing and furniture are protected.
2. **Support the head and neck.** Hold the baby close with the chin clear of the chest and nose/mouth unobstructed. ⚠️ *Hazard:* young babies cannot reliably protect their airway; confirm the face is visible and breathing is easy. → *Expect:* the baby is stable and airway is open.
3. **Choose a burping position.** [BRANCH: over shoulder | sitting on lap | tummy-down across lap] Use the one that keeps the baby calm and well supported. → *Expect:* the baby is secure against your body or hand.
4. **Pat or rub gently.** Use light upward pats or slow back rubs, avoiding hard thumps. → *Expect:* a burp, softened belly, relaxed body, or no change after a reasonable attempt.
5. **Pause if the baby spits up.** Tilt slightly forward or upright, wipe the mouth, and wait until breathing and comfort are normal. → *Expect:* spit-up clears without gagging or distress.
6. **Resume feeding or settle the baby.** If mid-feed, offer the feed again when calm; if finished, hold upright briefly before laying down on the back. → *Expect:* the baby is comfortable enough to continue or rest.
7. **Stop after a reasonable try.** If no burp comes after several minutes and the baby is comfortable, stop forcing it. → *Expect:* burping does not become rough or prolonged.

## Decision points

- Baby is uncomfortable but will not burp → try a different position, bicycle legs gently, or pause before feeding again.
- Spit-up is forceful, green, bloody, or frequent with poor weight gain → call the clinician.
- Baby falls asleep after feeding → place on their back on a firm, flat, empty sleep surface.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Airway slumps closed:** detect chin pressed to chest or face buried → reposition immediately with head and neck aligned.
- **F2 Patting too hard:** detect crying, startle, or red marks → stop and switch to gentle rubbing.
- **F3 Frequent large spit-up:** detect soaked cloths or discomfort after most feeds → document timing and ask the clinician.
- **F4 Caregiver falls asleep holding baby:** detect drowsiness during night feeds → move to a safer seated setup and place baby in crib when finished.

## Verification

The baby is breathing comfortably, appears settled or ready to feed again, and is placed back to sleep on their back if they fall asleep.

## Variations

- Breast/chest fed babies: some need little burping if they swallow less air.
- Bottle fed babies: burp midway, especially with fast flow, gulping, or fussing.
- Reflux-prone babies: the clinician may recommend longer upright holding after feeds.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from airway positioning, falls, and misreading serious vomiting. Keep one hand supporting the baby at all times, never leave the baby unattended on a sofa or bed, and escalate abnormal vomiting or breathing changes.
