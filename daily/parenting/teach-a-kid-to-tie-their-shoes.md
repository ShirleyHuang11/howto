## Goal

You teach a child a repeatable shoe-tying method and practice until they can tie secure bows without adult help.

## Preconditions

- The child has shoes with laces long enough to tie, or a practice shoe/card with two contrasting laces.
- The child can sit comfortably with the shoe stable in front of them.
- You can practice in short sessions without rushing to leave the house.

## Steps

1. **Choose one method.** [BRANCH: bunny-ear method | loop-swoop-pull method] Pick one and use the same words every time. → *Expect:* the child hears a consistent sequence.
2. **Use contrasting laces if possible.** Put two different colored laces in a practice shoe or card. → *Expect:* the child can tell which lace moves where.
3. **Demonstrate slowly from the child's viewpoint.** Sit beside or behind the child so left/right matches their perspective. → *Expect:* the child sees the movement they need to copy.
4. **Teach the starting knot.** Cross laces, tuck one under, and pull both ends sideways to tighten. → *Expect:* the shoe has a firm base knot.
5. **Build the bow step by step.** For bunny ears, make two loops, cross, tuck, and pull; for loop-swoop-pull, make one loop, wrap the other lace, push a loop through, and pull. → *Expect:* a bow forms even if loose.
6. **Practice pulling the loops, not the ends.** Show that pulling lace ends can untie the knot. → *Expect:* the child tightens the bow correctly.
7. **Repeat in short sessions.** Practice 5-10 minutes, then stop before frustration builds. → *Expect:* accuracy improves over days.
8. **Move from lap practice to real shoes.** Practice with the shoe on the foot once the child can tie on a table or floor. → *Expect:* the skill transfers to daily use.
9. **Check security before walking.** Have the child tug gently and tuck long loops if needed. → *Expect:* laces stay tied and do not drag.

## Decision points

- Child is left-handed → sit on the same side and let them mirror naturally instead of forcing right-handed motions.
- Fine motor skills are not ready → use elastic laces or hook-and-loop shoes while practicing.
- Laces keep coming undone → try a double knot after the bow is learned.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Child reverses steps:** detect a knot without a bow or crossed loops in the wrong order → return to the same verbal cue and slow demonstration.
- **F2 Loops are huge:** detect dragging laces → start with shorter lace ends before tying.
- **F3 Frustration stops learning:** detect tears or refusal → pause and resume later with a practice card.
- **F4 Bow collapses:** detect loose base knot → practice tightening the first knot before making loops.

## Verification

The child can tie both shoes into secure bows, tighten by pulling loops, walk without laces dragging, and repeat the skill on another day.

## Variations

- Practice board: easier for early learning because the shoe stays still.
- Double knot: useful for sports or slippery laces after the basic bow is mastered.
- Adaptive needs: use elastic laces, toggles, or occupational therapy strategies if hand strength or coordination makes tying inaccessible.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. The main safety concern is tripping on loose laces, so check length and security before running, stairs, or playground use.
