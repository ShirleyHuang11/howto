## Goal

You soothe a frequently crying baby as safely as possible, check for medical concerns, and protect caregivers from exhaustion and unsafe reactions.

## Preconditions

- Baby has been fed, changed, and held safely.
- You know the baby's age, feeding pattern, temperature, wet diapers, and recent symptoms.
- A safe sleep surface is available: firm, flat, empty, and separate from adult bedding.

## Steps

1. **Check for illness or injury.** Look for fever, poor feeding, vomiting, breathing trouble, limpness, swelling, hair tourniquet, rash, or fewer wet diapers. ⚠️ *Hazard:* if the baby seems ill, injured, or under 3 months with fever, seek medical care promptly. → *Expect:* obvious medical red flags are ruled out or escalated.
2. **Confirm basic needs.** Offer feeding if due, burp, change diaper, and adjust clothing temperature. → *Expect:* hunger, gas, wet diaper, and overheating are addressed.
3. **Use safe holding.** Hold the baby securely against your chest, across your forearm, or upright after feeds. → *Expect:* the baby's head and airway are supported at all times.
4. **Try rhythmic soothing.** Use gentle rocking, walking, swaying, white noise, singing, or a pacifier. → *Expect:* crying decreases, pauses, or at least does not escalate.
5. **Reduce stimulation.** Dim lights, lower noise, and move to a quieter room. → *Expect:* the environment is calmer and less visually busy.
6. **Try motion safely.** Use a stroller walk, baby carrier with airway visible, or car ride only when the driver is alert. → *Expect:* the baby is secured according to the product instructions.
7. **Put the baby down if you are overwhelmed.** Place the baby on their back in an empty crib or bassinet and step away for a few minutes. ⚠️ *Hazard:* never shake, hit, or throw a baby; safe crying in a crib is safer than an overwhelmed caregiver holding them. → *Expect:* the baby is in a safe place while you regain control.
8. **Rotate caregivers.** Ask another trusted adult to take over or call someone for support. → *Expect:* no single caregiver is pushed past their limit.
9. **Track patterns and call the clinician.** Record crying times, feeds, stools, spit-up, sleep, and soothing attempts; ask about reflux, allergy, feeding problems, or other causes if crying is severe or persistent. → *Expect:* you have useful data for medical guidance.

## Decision points

- Crying is sudden, high-pitched, or unlike normal → seek medical advice urgently.
- Baby feeds poorly or has fewer wet diapers → call a clinician the same day.
- Caregiver feels anger or panic rising → put baby safely down and get help immediately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed medical cause:** detect fever, lethargy, vomiting, blood in stool, or poor feeding → call the pediatrician or emergency services as appropriate.
- **F2 Unsafe sleep during exhaustion:** detect baby placed on couch, adult bed, swing, or with blankets → move baby to back, firm flat surface, empty crib/bassinet.
- **F3 Caregiver burnout:** detect dread, rage, or intrusive thoughts → hand off care, call support, and seek urgent help if safety feels uncertain.
- **F4 Overfeeding to stop crying:** detect repeated feeds with discomfort or vomiting → pause, burp, hold upright, and ask a clinician about feeding.

## Verification

The baby has no urgent red flags, basic needs are addressed, soothing attempts are safe, the baby is placed only on a safe sleep surface when unattended, and caregiver backup is available.

## Variations

- Breastfed baby: consider latch, supply, oversupply, or food sensitivity only with clinician or lactation support.
- Formula-fed baby: do not change formulas repeatedly without medical guidance.
- Premature or medically fragile baby: use the clinician's threshold for calling, which may be lower.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because infants are vulnerable and caregiver exhaustion can become dangerous. Confirm safe sleep, airway position, temperature, feeding, and caregiver self-control before continuing home soothing.
