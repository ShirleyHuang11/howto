## Goal

You help a child stop using a pacifier with a plan that protects sleep, comfort, dental development, and caregiver consistency.

## Preconditions

- The child is healthy enough for a routine change and not in the middle of a major transition if avoidable.
- Caregivers agree on timing, method, and language.
- You have alternate comfort tools such as a stuffed animal, blanket if age-safe, bedtime routine, songs, or extra caregiver comfort.

## Steps

1. **Choose gradual or cold-turkey weaning.** [BRANCH: gradual limits | planned goodbye] Pick based on age, temperament, daycare rules, and caregiver capacity. → *Expect:* one method is selected before the first limit.
2. **Set clear pacifier boundaries.** For gradual weaning, limit to crib/bed or naps first; for goodbye, choose a date and remove extras. → *Expect:* pacifier access is no longer random.
3. **Use simple, consistent language.** Say "pacifier is for bedtime" or "pacifiers are all done" without long negotiations. → *Expect:* the child hears the same message from every adult.
4. **Add replacement comfort.** Offer cuddles, a safe comfort item, back rubs, water, or a predictable bedtime phrase. → *Expect:* the child has another way to settle.
5. **Tell daycare or other caregivers.** Share the exact rule and backup plan. → *Expect:* the child does not get mixed signals.
6. **Expect a rough patch.** Plan for several harder sleeps or tantrums and respond calmly without restarting the old pattern. → *Expect:* protest is anticipated, not treated as failure.
7. **Remove hidden pacifiers.** Check bags, car seats, crib corners, and toy bins. → *Expect:* accidental reintroduction is unlikely.
8. **Monitor mouth, sleep, and stress.** If thumb sucking, severe sleep loss, or distress escalates, adjust pace or ask the pediatrician/dentist. → *Expect:* the plan stays humane and medically reasonable.

## Decision points

- Child is under 6 months → ask the clinician before removing a pacifier used for soothing or sleep.
- Dental or speech concerns are present → ask a pediatric dentist, pediatrician, or speech-language professional about timing.
- A major move, new sibling, illness, or daycare start is happening → delay if the pacifier is not causing urgent problems.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Caregivers give in inconsistently:** detect pacifier returning after tantrums → reset the plan and align adults before trying again.
- **F2 Sleep collapses for many nights:** detect severe overtiredness → consider a slower limit or clinician advice.
- **F3 Child substitutes thumb sucking:** detect frequent thumb use → offer other comfort and discuss dental guidance if persistent.
- **F4 Pacifiers reappear:** detect hidden extras → remove all backups except any intentionally kept emergency item.

## Verification

The child no longer uses a pacifier outside the chosen limit or has fully stopped, caregivers apply the same rule, and sleep and comfort are stabilizing.

## Variations

- Gradual method: restrict to sleep, then naps only or bedtime only, then remove.
- Goodbye ritual: trade pacifiers for a small comfort item or mark a final day.
- Older preschooler: use a calendar and dentist explanation if that motivates without shame.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but avoid cutting pacifier nipples because damaged pieces can become choking hazards. Do not shame the child; coordinate with caregivers and consult a clinician for severe distress or dental concerns.
