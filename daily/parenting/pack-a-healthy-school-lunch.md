## Goal

You pack a school lunch the child can safely eat, open independently, and finish within the school's lunch period.

## Preconditions

- School food allergy rules, lunch duration, and whether refrigeration or reheating is available.
- Child's dietary needs, chewing ability, and reliable foods.
- Clean lunchbox, water bottle, containers, and ice pack or thermos if needed.

## Steps

1. **Check school restrictions.** Confirm nut rules, classroom allergy policies, microwave access, and whether candy or glass containers are allowed. → *Expect:* the lunch follows school rules.
2. **Choose a balanced template.** Include a protein, grain or starch, fruit or vegetable, drink, and optional small snack. → *Expect:* the lunch has enough variety without being oversized.
3. **Make foods easy to eat.** Cut fruit, peel oranges if needed, use small containers, and avoid packaging the child cannot open. → *Expect:* the child can open and eat the lunch independently.
4. **Control temperature.** Use an ice pack for perishable cold foods or a preheated insulated container for hot foods. → *Expect:* food will stay in a safer temperature range until lunch.
5. **Pack water.** Fill a leakproof bottle and label it. → *Expect:* the child has a drink even if they skip milk or juice.
6. **Limit choking hazards for younger children.** Cut grapes, cherry tomatoes, hot dogs, and firm round foods lengthwise and into age-appropriate pieces. ⚠️ *Hazard:* confirm food size and texture are safe for the child's age before packing. → *Expect:* round, hard, or sticky foods are modified or omitted.
7. **Do a leak check.** Close containers, turn them briefly sideways over the sink, and pack wet items separately. → *Expect:* the lunchbox will not soak papers or backpack items.
8. **Add any required note or utensil.** Include spoon, fork, napkin, or cafeteria card if needed. → *Expect:* the child has every tool needed to eat.
9. **Review what came back.** After school, note untouched foods, time issues, or containers that were hard to open. → *Expect:* the next lunch improves based on real feedback.

## Decision points

- Child returns most food uneaten → pack smaller portions and more reliable foods, then ask about lunch timing.
- School bans a common ingredient → create a substitute list for protein and snacks.
- Child has diabetes, allergies, or feeding therapy needs → follow the clinician or school care plan exactly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Food spoils:** detect warm dairy, meat, or leftovers at return → improve ice packs, use shelf-stable choices, or switch containers.
- **F2 Child cannot open containers:** detect full lunch and complaints → practice at home and change packaging.
- **F3 Lunch violates allergy policy:** detect school warning → remove the food and label safe alternatives.
- **F4 Overpacking:** detect heavy lunchbox and repeated waste → reduce portions and keep one backup snack.

## Verification

The lunch follows school rules, includes balanced foods the child can open and eat, is temperature-controlled when needed, and returns with usable feedback for the next day.

## Variations

- Young child: pack bite-size foods, avoid choking hazards, and use fewer containers.
- Teen: involve them in planning and packing so the lunch is realistic.
- No refrigeration: choose shelf-stable proteins, whole fruit, crackers, seed butter if allowed, or insulated cold storage.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with food safety concerns. Confirm allergy rules, keep perishables cold or hot as appropriate, and modify choking hazards for younger children.
