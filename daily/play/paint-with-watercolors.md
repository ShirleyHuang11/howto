## Goal

Make a small watercolor painting using controlled washes, layered color, and preserved highlights.

## Preconditions

- Watercolor paper or thick mixed-media paper.
- Watercolor paint, brush, two water cups, and a cloth or paper towel.
- A simple subject such as fruit, a leaf, a cup, or a sky shape.

## Steps

1. **Tape or steady the paper.** Secure the sheet to a board or table if it buckles easily. → *Expect:* the paper stays mostly flat while wet.
2. **Sketch lightly.** Draw only the major shapes and mark any highlight areas to keep white. → *Expect:* pencil lines guide the painting without dominating it.
3. **Mix a pale wash.** Add water to a color puddle until it is lighter than you think you need. → *Expect:* the first stroke is transparent, not opaque.
4. **Paint the lightest layer.** Cover broad areas and leave highlights unpainted. → *Expect:* the subject has a light color base and visible white highlights.
5. **Let the layer dry.** Wait until the shine leaves the paper before adding sharper details. → *Expect:* new strokes sit on top instead of spreading unpredictably.
6. **Build shadows gradually.** Add darker mixtures in shadow areas with fewer strokes. → *Expect:* form appears without muddying the whole painting.
7. **Add final accents.** Use a smaller brush or drier paint for edges, texture, and darkest details. → *Expect:* the image has focus points and still looks luminous.

## Decision points

- You want soft edges → paint into damp paper before the surface dries.
- You want crisp edges → wait until the previous layer is fully dry.
- Color looks too strong → blot while wet or glaze surrounding areas after dry.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Muddy color:** detect by dull brown or gray where colors were overworked → let it dry and glaze clean color over selected areas.
- **F2 Lost highlights:** detect by white areas accidentally painted over → lift wet paint with a clean damp brush or use opaque white only as a last resort.
- **F3 Buckled paper:** detect by puddles pooling in warped areas → tape thicker paper next time and use less water.
- **F4 Cauliflower blooms:** detect by hard-edged backwash marks → avoid dropping wet paint into half-dry washes unless you want that texture.

## Verification

The finished painting has transparent color layers, at least one preserved or recovered highlight, and darker accents that define the subject.

## Variations

- Wet-on-wet sky: wet the paper first and drop in blue and purple washes.
- Botanical study: paint one leaf and layer veins after it dries.
- Limited palette: use only two colors plus water to learn mixing.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use non-toxic paints when possible, do not drink paint water, and protect tables and clothing from stains.
