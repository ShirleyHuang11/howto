## Goal

Start a small vinyl record collection with playable records, basic care habits, and spending limits.

## Preconditions

- A working turntable with speakers or a plan to buy records before playback gear.
- A budget for records and protective sleeves.
- A place to store records upright away from heat and moisture.

## Steps

1. **Set a collecting focus.** Choose a genre, artist, era, local scene, cover-art theme, or personal favorite list. → *Expect:* browsing has a clear filter.
2. **Set a spending limit.** Decide the maximum price for one record and for the first month. → *Expect:* impulse purchases have a boundary.
3. **Inspect condition.** Check vinyl under light for scratches, warps, grime, and sleeve damage. → *Expect:* the record looks playable for the price.
4. **Confirm the pressing basics.** Read the artist, title, format speed, and track list before buying. → *Expect:* the record is the album or single you actually want.
5. **Clean before playing.** Brush dust off the record and check the stylus area is clean. → *Expect:* playback starts with less crackle and lower wear.
6. **Store records upright.** Place records vertically in sleeves, not stacked flat. → *Expect:* the collection stands without bending or heat exposure.
7. **Log the first records.** Write down artist, title, condition, price, and where you found it. → *Expect:* the collection has a simple inventory from the start.

## Decision points

- Budget is small → buy used common records in good condition before rare pressings.
- You care about sound quality → prioritize clean vinyl over perfect jacket art.
- You care about artwork → accept cosmetic vinyl flaws only if playback still matters less.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Warped record:** detect by visible waves or needle movement → return it if possible or avoid buying severe warps.
- **F2 Overpriced impulse:** detect by price exceeding your limit → pause and compare typical prices before purchasing.
- **F3 Dirty playback:** detect by crackle and visible dust → clean the record and stylus before judging condition.
- **F4 Poor storage:** detect by leaning, heat, or stacked piles → move records upright into a cooler stable space.

## Verification

You own or have selected at least one record that matches your focus, fits your budget, is stored upright, and has condition notes in an inventory.

## Variations

- Thrift-store start: inspect more carefully because returns may be limited.
- New-record start: budget for higher prices and inner sleeves.
- Listening-first collection: buy only records you will play within the next month.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because spending can escalate. Set a budget before shopping, protect payment information, and avoid storing records in hot cars or damp rooms.
