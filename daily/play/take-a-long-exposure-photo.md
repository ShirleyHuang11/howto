## Goal

Take a long-exposure photo that keeps static objects sharp while motion turns into blur, trails, or smooth texture.

## Preconditions

- A camera or phone app with long exposure, night mode, or manual shutter control.
- A tripod, stable surface, or clamp.
- A scene with motion, such as traffic, water, clouds, or moving lights.

## Steps

1. **Stabilize the camera.** Mount it on a tripod or place it on a firm surface. → *Expect:* the frame stays fixed when you release your hands.
2. **Compose with static anchors.** Include buildings, rocks, rails, or trees along with the moving subject. → *Expect:* the photo will show contrast between sharp and blurred elements.
3. **Set a slow shutter.** Start around 1 to 4 seconds for traffic or water, longer if the scene is dark. → *Expect:* moving subjects have time to streak or soften.
4. **Lower sensitivity.** Use low ISO and a small aperture if available to avoid overexposure. → *Expect:* the preview or meter stays within a usable brightness range.
5. **Use a timer or remote.** Trigger the shot without tapping during the exposure. → *Expect:* the camera starts after vibrations settle.
6. **Review sharpness and blur.** Zoom into static objects and inspect the moving areas. → *Expect:* fixed objects are sharp while motion is visibly blurred.
7. **Adjust and repeat.** Lengthen shutter for more blur or shorten it if the frame is too bright or chaotic. → *Expect:* the next frame moves closer to the intended effect.

## Decision points

- Daylight is too bright → use a neutral density filter or choose dusk, shade, or night.
- Static objects are blurry → improve support and use a timer.
- Motion barely shows → lengthen the shutter or choose faster-moving subjects.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overexposed frame:** detect by large white areas with no detail → lower ISO, close aperture, shorten shutter, or add an ND filter.
- **F2 Camera vibration:** detect by everything looking doubled or smeared → use a firmer support and delayed shutter.
- **F3 Weak motion effect:** detect by moving subjects looking nearly frozen → use a slower shutter speed.
- **F4 Unsafe position:** detect by needing to stand near traffic, water edges, or unstable ground → move to a safer viewpoint before shooting.

## Verification

The final image contains at least one sharp static element and one intentionally blurred moving element, with exposure bright enough to read detail.

## Variations

- Light trails: photograph vehicles or handheld lights after dark.
- Silky water: use 1 second or longer with a stable tripod.
- Phone night mode: hold the phone still until the capture indicator finishes.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk to medium in hazardous locations. Stay clear of roads, ledges, and water edges, and avoid capturing identifiable people in private settings.
