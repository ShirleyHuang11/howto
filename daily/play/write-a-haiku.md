## Goal

Write a concise three-line haiku that captures an observed moment through concrete images.

## Preconditions

- Paper or a notes app and something to write with.
- A real scene, memory, or object to observe.
- Willingness to revise for image and sound rather than explanation.

## Steps

1. **Observe one moment.** Look at a small scene for a full minute without writing. → *Expect:* one sensory detail stands out.
2. **List concrete images.** Write five nouns or phrases from sight, sound, touch, smell, or movement. → *Expect:* the raw material is specific rather than abstract.
3. **Choose a seasonal or time clue.** Add a word such as frost, cicadas, lunch light, rain, or late train. → *Expect:* the poem has a felt setting.
4. **Draft three lines.** Write a short first line, a longer second line, and a short third line. → *Expect:* the poem has the recognizable haiku shape.
5. **Cut explanation.** Remove words that tell the reader what to feel. → *Expect:* images carry the meaning by themselves.
6. **Read it aloud.** Listen for a natural pause or turn between two images. → *Expect:* the poem has a small shift instead of one flat sentence.
7. **Make a final copy.** Rewrite the best version cleanly with deliberate line breaks. → *Expect:* the haiku is readable and complete.

## Decision points

- You want strict syllables → revise toward 5, 7, and 5 syllables without forcing awkward words.
- The poem feels vague → replace emotion words with observed details.
- The ending explains too much → end on an image instead of a conclusion.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Abstract draft:** detect by words like beauty, sadness, or peace doing all the work → replace them with things seen or heard.
- **F2 Forced syllables:** detect by unnatural filler words → loosen the count and keep the image alive.
- **F3 No turn:** detect by three lines saying the same thing → place two images beside each other with a pause.
- **F4 Cliche image:** detect by generic moon, rose, or sunset language → add one unusual observed detail.

## Verification

The final poem has three lines, concrete sensory images, a seasonal or time clue, and no sentence that directly explains the intended emotion.

## Variations

- Strict 5-7-5: count syllables after drafting, not before.
- Urban haiku: use traffic, light, weather, and overheard sound as nature-adjacent material.
- Photo prompt: write from one image but include only details visible in it.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid exposing private names, locations, or identifiable moments if the poem will be shared.
