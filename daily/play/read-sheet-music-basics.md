## Goal

Read a simple treble-clef melody by identifying note names, basic rhythms, and measure boundaries.

## Preconditions

- A printed or digital beginner melody in treble clef.
- A pencil and an instrument, keyboard app, or voice for checking pitches.
- Enough light to see staff lines and note stems.

## Steps

1. **Find the clef and staff.** Look for the treble clef and count the five lines from bottom to top. → *Expect:* the staff is oriented and the clef is known.
2. **Name the line notes.** Use E, G, B, D, F from bottom line to top line. → *Expect:* notes sitting on lines can be named.
3. **Name the space notes.** Use F, A, C, E from bottom space to top space. → *Expect:* notes sitting between lines can be named.
4. **Mark difficult notes lightly.** Pencil names only above notes you cannot recognize quickly. → *Expect:* the page remains readable without every note being labeled.
5. **Read rhythm shapes.** Identify whole, half, quarter, and eighth notes or rests in the piece. → *Expect:* each symbol has an approximate count value.
6. **Tap the rhythm.** Clap or tap through one measure at a steady slow pulse. → *Expect:* the notes and rests fit inside the measure.
7. **Play or sing slowly.** Move through the melody one measure at a time, saying note names if needed. → *Expect:* pitch order and rhythm begin to match the printed page.

## Decision points

- Notes go above or below the staff → count ledger lines from the nearest known line or space.
- Rhythm feels confusing → ignore pitch briefly and clap only the rhythm.
- Key signature has sharps or flats → mark affected note names before playing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Line-space mixup:** detect by naming a space note as a line note → point to whether the note head is pierced by a line.
- **F2 Lost measure count:** detect by too many or too few beats before a bar line → tap a steady pulse and count aloud.
- **F3 Ignored rests:** detect by playing through silence symbols → circle rests and count them like notes.
- **F4 Wrong octave:** detect by correct letter name but too high or low → compare the note's staff position to middle C or another anchor.

## Verification

You can name the notes and clap the rhythm of at least four consecutive measures, then play or sing them slowly in the printed order.

## Variations

- Bass clef: learn its line and space note names separately.
- Piano practice: point to each printed note before pressing the key.
- Guitar practice: read rhythm first, then choose fretboard positions.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Take breaks from small print and keep volume considerate when practicing repeated passages.
