## Goal

Knit a simple garter-stitch scarf with even edges, consistent tension, and secured ends.

## Preconditions

- Medium or bulky yarn and knitting needles matched to the yarn label.
- Scissors, a yarn needle, and a comfortable place to sit.
- Ability to make a basic knit stitch, or willingness to practice slowly.

## Steps

1. **Cast on the width.** Cast on 18 to 30 stitches depending on yarn thickness and desired scarf width. → *Expect:* the needle holds a flexible row of loops.
2. **Knit the first row.** Insert, wrap, pull through, and slide off each stitch across the row. → *Expect:* all stitches move to the other needle without dropping.
3. **Turn and repeat.** Turn the work and knit every stitch again for garter stitch. → *Expect:* ridges begin forming on both sides of the fabric.
4. **Check edge tension.** Keep the first and last stitch snug but not tight on each row. → *Expect:* the scarf edges stay straight enough to follow.
5. **Measure as you go.** Lay the scarf flat after several inches and check width and length. → *Expect:* the fabric grows evenly without narrowing or widening.
6. **Bind off loosely.** Knit two stitches, pass the first over the second, and repeat across the row. → *Expect:* the final edge is secure and not cinched tight.
7. **Weave in ends.** Thread yarn tails through nearby stitches on the back and trim after a gentle tug. → *Expect:* tails disappear and the scarf does not unravel.

## Decision points

- Scarf is too narrow → restart early with more stitches before investing hours.
- Edges are messy → keep practicing garter stitch; scarves tolerate small irregularities well.
- Yarn runs low → make a shorter scarf or add a new ball at the edge.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Dropped stitch:** detect by a loose vertical ladder → catch the loop with a hook or needle and work it back up.
- **F2 Added stitch:** detect by the row count increasing → knit two stitches together near the edge to return to count.
- **F3 Tight bind-off:** detect by the final edge puckering → undo the bind-off and redo it with looser loops.
- **F4 Uneven tension:** detect by tight and loose patches → block gently after finishing and practice steady hand position.

## Verification

The scarf is one continuous knitted rectangle with stable stitch count, secure cast-on and bind-off edges, and woven-in ends that stay put after a gentle tug.

## Variations

- Chunky scarf: use bulky yarn and larger needles for faster progress.
- Striped scarf: change colors at row ends and weave tails into matching color sections.
- Practice scarf: make a short neck warmer before committing to full length.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Take hand breaks, keep needles capped or stored when not in use, and keep yarn away from small children and pets.
