## Goal

Create a hand-lettered word or short phrase with consistent spacing, controlled stroke weight, and a finished layout.

## Preconditions

- Smooth paper, pencil, eraser, and pen or brush pen.
- A short word or phrase of one to five words.
- A ruler or straightedge if you want clean guides.

## Steps

1. **Choose the phrase.** Pick a short phrase and decide which word should be most important. → *Expect:* the lettering has a clear focal word.
2. **Draw light guides.** Pencil a baseline, x-height, and cap height for the main word. → *Expect:* letters have a shared structure to sit on.
3. **Block the layout.** Sketch each word as a simple rectangle or loose shape before drawing letters. → *Expect:* the phrase fits the page with balanced margins.
4. **Pencil the letters.** Draw letter skeletons first, keeping spacing visually even. → *Expect:* the phrase is readable before ink or decoration.
5. **Add weight deliberately.** Thicken downstrokes or shadow one side of each letter consistently. → *Expect:* stroke weight follows one rule across the piece.
6. **Ink the final lines.** Trace slowly and let ink dry before erasing pencil. → *Expect:* clean dark letters remain without smears.
7. **Add small finishing details.** Use dots, lines, flourishes, or color only where they support the words. → *Expect:* decoration frames the lettering without reducing readability.

## Decision points

- Phrase is too long → reduce words or use plain small lettering for secondary text.
- Brush pen feels hard → fake calligraphy by drawing monoline letters and thickening downstrokes.
- Layout feels unbalanced → move word blocks before inking, not after.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Crowded letters:** detect by letters touching unintentionally → redraw with wider spacing or a smaller pen.
- **F2 Inconsistent weight:** detect by thick strokes appearing on random sides → mark all downstrokes before inking.
- **F3 Smudged ink:** detect by gray streaks after erasing → wait longer or use a drier pen.
- **F4 Illegible flourish:** detect by decoration mistaken for letters → erase or simplify the flourish.

## Verification

The finished lettering is readable at arm's length, has even visual spacing, follows one stroke-weight rule, and shows no major ink smears.

## Variations

- Envelope lettering: leave clear address space and keep decoration away from postal marks.
- Quote card: emphasize one word and keep the rest simple.
- Chalk style: use white pencil or gel pen on dark paper.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Ventilate strong markers, protect tables from ink, and avoid sharing private names or addresses in public examples.
