## Goal

Start a daily meditation habit using short sessions that train attention without requiring a blank mind.

## Preconditions

- A quiet enough place to sit or stand for 5 to 10 minutes.
- Timer, app, or clock.
- Willingness to stop and ground yourself if practice becomes distressing.

## Steps

1. **Choose a daily anchor.** Attach meditation to waking, lunch, or bedtime. → *Expect:* the session has a predictable trigger.
2. **Set a short timer.** Begin with 5 minutes for the first week. → *Expect:* the duration feels small enough to repeat daily.
3. **Take a stable posture.** Sit or stand upright with shoulders relaxed and hands supported. → *Expect:* breathing is easy and the body does not strain.
4. **Pick one anchor.** Feel the breath at the nose, chest, belly, or feet on the floor. → *Expect:* attention has one simple place to return.
5. **Notice and return.** When thoughts, sounds, or feelings pull attention away, label them lightly and return to the anchor. → *Expect:* wandering happens many times and each return counts as practice.
6. **End deliberately.** At the timer, take one breath, notice the room, and stand slowly. → *Expect:* the session closes by choice, not by frustration.
7. **Mark completion.** Check off the day or write one word about the session. → *Expect:* the habit streak is visible without judging performance.

## Decision points

- Five minutes feels too long -> use two minutes daily rather than quitting.
- Sleepiness dominates -> meditate sitting upright with eyes slightly open.
- Distress, panic, or traumatic memories intensify -> stop, ground in the room, and consider trauma-informed support.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Chasing a blank mind:** detect by frustration that thoughts continue -> recover by treating noticing as the repetition.
- **F2 Inconsistent timing:** detect by repeatedly forgetting sessions -> recover by tying it to a stronger daily anchor.
- **F3 Practice feels unsafe:** detect by escalating distress or dissociation -> recover by opening eyes, naming objects in the room, stopping, and seeking professional support if needed.

## Verification

You complete at least five sessions in seven days, each ended by a timer, and you can describe returning attention to the chosen anchor at least once per session.

## Variations

- Guided meditation: use a short audio guide until the structure feels familiar.
- Walking meditation: anchor attention on footfalls when sitting feels agitating.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for brief practice, but meditation can surface distress for some people. It is not a substitute for mental-health care, and app use may expose mood or routine data.
