## Goal

Improve daily posture by building awareness, strength, mobility, and movement breaks instead of forcing a rigid pose.

## Preconditions

- Space to stand, sit, and move arms freely.
- A mirror or phone camera is optional.
- No new numbness, weakness, severe pain, or injury; consult a professional for those.

## Steps

1. **Check your baseline.** Stand normally and notice head, ribs, pelvis, shoulders, and weight distribution. → *Expect:* you see the posture you actually use, not a forced correction.
2. **Stack gently.** Bring ears over shoulders, ribs over pelvis, and weight balanced over mid-foot. → *Expect:* posture feels taller but not stiff.
3. **Open the front.** Do 5 slow chest-opening breaths with shoulders relaxed. → *Expect:* chest and upper back feel less compressed.
4. **Strengthen the back line.** Do 8 to 12 rows with a band or squeeze shoulder blades gently without shrugging. → *Expect:* upper back muscles engage.
5. **Train hips and core.** Do 8 slow hip hinges or glute bridges and a short plank variation. → *Expect:* posture support comes from hips and trunk, not neck tension.
6. **Set movement cues.** Change position, stand, or walk for 1 minute every 30 to 60 minutes. → *Expect:* stiffness is interrupted before it becomes pain.
7. **Review one daily trigger.** Pick one situation, such as phone use or dishwashing, and apply the gentle stack there. → *Expect:* posture practice enters real life.

## Decision points

- Sitting drives symptoms -> adjust desk setup and break frequency.
- Phone use drives symptoms -> raise the phone and relax shoulders.
- Pain persists or radiates -> consult a clinician or physical therapist.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overcorrecting:** detect by rigid military posture or back tension -> recover by softening ribs, jaw, and knees.
- **F2 Only stretching:** detect by temporary relief with no strength work -> recover by adding rows, hinges, or bridges.
- **F3 Ignoring pain signals:** detect by numbness, weakness, or radiating pain -> recover by stopping and getting professional guidance.

## Verification

For seven days, you complete a 10-minute posture routine, take movement breaks, and can demonstrate a relaxed stacked posture during one daily activity without pain increase.

## Variations

- Desk worker: pair posture checks with calendar transitions.
- Driver: adjust seat and mirrors so slumping changes the mirror view.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Good posture is variable and relaxed, not held rigidly all day. Persistent pain, neurological symptoms, or post-injury symptoms warrant professional assessment.
