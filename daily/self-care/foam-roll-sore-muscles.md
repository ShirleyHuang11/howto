## Goal

Use a foam roller to reduce muscle tightness temporarily without irritating joints, nerves, or injuries.

## Preconditions

- A foam roller or firm massage ball and floor space.
- Soreness is muscular and mild to moderate, not severe injury pain.
- No suspected fracture, blood clot, open wound, numbness, unexplained swelling, or severe pain; seek medical care for those.

## Steps

1. **Choose one muscle area.** Pick calves, quads, glutes, hamstrings, or upper back, avoiding joints and the low back spine. → *Expect:* the target area is muscle, not bone or a joint line.
2. **Apply light pressure first.** Support body weight with hands or the opposite leg so pressure is tolerable. → *Expect:* sensation feels like firm pressure, not sharp pain.
3. **Roll slowly.** Move 1 to 2 inches per second over the muscle for 30 to 60 seconds. → *Expect:* tenderness decreases slightly or stays tolerable.
4. **Pause on tender spots.** Hold gentle pressure on a sore spot for 10 to 20 seconds while breathing normally. → *Expect:* the spot softens or becomes less reactive.
5. **Move after rolling.** Walk, squat, or stretch lightly through the new range. → *Expect:* movement feels a little easier than before rolling.
6. **Limit total dose.** Spend no more than 1 to 2 minutes per muscle area. → *Expect:* skin is not bruised and soreness is not worse afterward.

## Decision points

- Pain is sharp, burning, numb, or electric -> stop that area and avoid nerve pressure.
- Bruising happens easily or you take blood thinners -> ask a clinician before firm rolling.
- Soreness follows a new workout -> combine gentle rolling with sleep, food, hydration, and easy movement.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rolling a joint:** detect by pressure on kneecap, ankle bone, hip point, or spine -> recover by moving back onto surrounding muscle.
- **F2 Pressing too hard:** detect by breath holding, flinching, or bruising -> recover by reducing body weight or using a softer roller.
- **F3 Treating injury as soreness:** detect by swelling, weakness, numbness, or pain that worsens -> recover by stopping and getting professional assessment.

## Verification

After rolling, the target muscle moves at least as well as before, pain is not sharper, and no bruising, numbness, or swelling has appeared.

## Variations

- Massage ball: use for small areas like glutes or feet with lighter pressure.
- Upper back: keep the roller across the shoulder blade area and avoid rolling directly on the neck.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if pressure stays moderate. Do not foam-roll suspected injuries, varicose veins, numb areas, wounds, or unexplained swelling; consult a professional for persistent or severe symptoms.
