## Goal

New shoes are eased into use while preventing blisters, numbness, and avoidable foot pain.

## Preconditions

- Shoes are the correct approximate size and can be returned if unworn outdoors.
- Socks, blister bandages or moleskin, and optional shoe stretcher are available.
- No existing open foot wound or medical condition requiring special footwear advice is present.

## Steps

1. **Check fit indoors.** Wear the shoes with normal socks and stand, walk, and bend toes. → *Expect:* toes have room and heels do not slip excessively.
2. **Identify pressure points.** Wear the shoes indoors for 10-15 minutes and note rubbing, pinching, or numbness. → *Expect:* specific tight spots are known.
3. **Protect skin first.** Apply moleskin or blister bandages to likely rub areas before longer wear. → *Expect:* high-friction skin is covered.
4. **Wear in short sessions.** Use the shoes indoors for 30-60 minutes, then remove them. → *Expect:* feet remain comfortable without hot spots becoming blisters.
5. **Increase gradually.** Add time over several days before using them for a full day. → *Expect:* upper material flexes and pressure decreases.
6. **Adjust lacing or straps.** Loosen tight zones and secure the heel using alternate eyelets if available. → *Expect:* foot is held without numbness.
7. **Use stretching only for tight spots.** Apply a shoe stretcher or cobbler stretch for width pressure, not length. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* overstretching can deform shoes; stretch gradually and check often. → *Expect:* targeted pressure eases without misshaping the shoe.
8. **Stop if skin breaks.** Remove shoes if pain, numbness, or an open blister develops. → *Expect:* injury does not worsen from continued wear.

## Decision points

- Toes hit the front while standing → the shoes are too short; return or exchange.
- Numbness or tingling occurs → loosen immediately or stop wearing.
- Shoes are leather dress shoes → a cobbler can stretch width and specific pressure points.
- Shoes are athletic shoes for running → test with short walks before running mileage.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Blister forms:** skin is hot, raised, or fluid-filled → stop wearing, protect the blister, and resume only after healing.
- **F2 Heel slips:** back of shoe rubs → try heel grips or different lacing; return if fit remains poor.
- **F3 Toe pain persists:** shoe length or toe box shape is wrong → exchange rather than forcing break-in.
- **F4 Shoe deforms:** stretching was too aggressive → stop stretching and use a cobbler if salvageable.

## Verification

After several short sessions, the shoes can be worn for at least 2 hours without new blisters, numbness, or worsening pressure points.

## Variations

- Leather shoes: condition lightly and use shoe trees between wears.
- Heels: add ball-of-foot cushions and limit first outings.
- Hiking boots: test on stairs and short walks with loaded socks before trails.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for healthy feet, but stop for numbness, sharp pain, or broken skin. People with diabetes, poor circulation, or neuropathy should use clinician footwear guidance.
