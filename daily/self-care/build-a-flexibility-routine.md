## Goal

Build a flexibility routine that improves comfortable range of motion through consistent, gentle stretching.

## Preconditions

- Floor space and optional mat, towel, or strap.
- Body is warm from light movement or a workout.
- No acute injury, severe pain, joint instability, or unexplained neurological symptoms.

## Steps

1. **Choose target areas.** Pick three to five areas that affect daily movement, such as calves, hamstrings, hips, chest, and shoulders. → *Expect:* the routine has a clear scope.
2. **Warm up first.** Walk, cycle, or move lightly for 3 to 5 minutes. → *Expect:* muscles feel warmer before stretching.
3. **Stretch gently.** Move into each stretch until mild tension, not pain, and hold 20 to 45 seconds. → *Expect:* breathing stays normal and the stretch is tolerable.
4. **Repeat consistently.** Do 1 to 3 rounds per area on most days. → *Expect:* total routine stays short enough to repeat.
5. **Use relaxed breathing.** Exhale slowly and avoid bouncing or forcing range. → *Expect:* tension eases without guarding.
6. **Track one measure.** Note reach distance, squat comfort, shoulder range, or how the movement feels. → *Expect:* progress is measured by function, not only sensation.
7. **Adjust after soreness.** If stretching leaves pain later, reduce range, hold time, or frequency. → *Expect:* the next session feels manageable.

## Decision points

- Goal is sport performance -> combine flexibility with strength through the new range.
- Hypermobility or frequent joint instability -> consult a professional before aggressive stretching.
- Pain is sharp or nerve-like -> stop that stretch and seek guidance if it repeats.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stretching cold:** detect by stiffness and discomfort at the start -> recover by adding light movement first.
- **F2 Forcing range:** detect by pain, breath holding, or bouncing -> recover by backing off to mild tension.
- **F3 No consistency:** detect by stretching only when very sore -> recover by scheduling a short daily or post-workout routine.

## Verification

After two weeks, the routine was completed at least eight times, stretches stayed pain-free, and one chosen movement measure is the same or improved.

## Variations

- Morning routine: use more dynamic motion and shorter holds.
- Post-workout routine: use longer relaxed holds once muscles are warm.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when gentle. Flexibility work should not produce sharp pain, numbness, or joint instability; consult a clinician or physical therapist for those signs or after injury.
