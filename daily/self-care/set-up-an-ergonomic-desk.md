## Goal

Arrange a desk so common work happens with supported feet, neutral wrists, relaxed shoulders, and regular movement.

## Preconditions

- Chair, work surface, screen, keyboard, and pointing device.
- Permission to move equipment or add supports such as books, boxes, or cushions.
- No new numbness, weakness, severe pain, or pain after trauma; seek care for those.

## Steps

1. **Set the chair height.** Raise or lower the chair so elbows are near 90 degrees at the keyboard. → *Expect:* shoulders can drop while typing.
2. **Support the feet.** Put feet flat on the floor or on a stable footrest. → *Expect:* thighs are supported and feet do not dangle.
3. **Support the low back.** Sit back and add a small cushion or rolled towel if needed. → *Expect:* the back feels supported without rigid bracing.
4. **Place the screen.** Put the main screen directly ahead, about arm's length away, with the top near eye level. → *Expect:* neck stays mostly neutral.
5. **Place keyboard and mouse.** Keep both close enough that elbows stay near the body and wrists remain straight. → *Expect:* reaching and wrist bending are minimal.
6. **Clear pressure points.** Move hard desk edges away from wrists and forearms. → *Expect:* no edge presses into soft tissue while working.
7. **Add movement reminders.** Set a cue to stand, walk, or change position every 30 minutes. → *Expect:* posture resets before stiffness builds.

## Decision points

- Laptop only -> raise the laptop and use an external keyboard and mouse when possible.
- Standing desk -> keep elbows near 90 degrees and shift weight instead of locking knees.
- Symptoms persist after changes -> consult occupational health, a clinician, or a physical therapist.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Screen too low:** detect by chin jutting or neck bending down -> recover by raising the screen on a stable support.
- **F2 Chair too high:** detect by dangling feet or thigh pressure -> recover by lowering the chair or adding a footrest.
- **F3 Mouse too far away:** detect by reaching from the shoulder -> recover by placing it beside the keyboard.

## Verification

Feet are supported, screen is centered near eye level, wrists are neutral, shoulders are relaxed, and at least one movement break occurs every 30 minutes.

## Variations

- Small desk: prioritize keyboard and mouse closeness over decorative objects.
- Shared workstation: mark preferred chair and monitor positions with small removable cues.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Ergonomics can reduce strain but cannot rule out medical problems; numbness, weakness, radiating pain, or symptoms after injury warrant professional evaluation.
