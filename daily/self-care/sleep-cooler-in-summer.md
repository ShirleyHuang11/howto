## Goal

Make a hot bedroom safer and more sleep-friendly using ventilation, bedding, hydration, and heat-risk checks.

## Preconditions

- Access to the sleeping area before bedtime.
- Fan, window, air conditioning, lighter bedding, or cooling options if available.
- Awareness that infants, older adults, pregnant people, and people with some illnesses or medications face higher heat risk.

## Steps

1. **Block daytime heat.** Close curtains or blinds on sun-facing windows during the hottest hours. → *Expect:* the room gains less heat before bedtime.
2. **Vent when outside air cools.** Open windows or use fans only when outdoor air is cooler or airflow helps evaporation. → *Expect:* air movement improves without pulling in hotter air.
3. **Lighten the bed.** Use breathable sheets, lighter sleepwear, and fewer blankets. → *Expect:* heat can escape from the body.
4. **Pre-cool the body.** Take a lukewarm shower or use a cool cloth before bed. → *Expect:* skin feels cooler without shivering.
5. **Hydrate sensibly.** Drink water earlier in the evening and keep water nearby. → *Expect:* thirst is reduced without excessive overnight bathroom trips.
6. **Move if needed.** Sleep in the coolest safe room, lower floor, or temporary cooling center if home remains dangerously hot. → *Expect:* the sleep location is cooler than the original room.
7. **Watch for heat illness.** Check for dizziness, confusion, nausea, rapid pulse, fainting, or inability to cool down. → *Expect:* red flags trigger immediate cooling and medical help.

## Decision points

- Indoor temperature remains unsafe -> use air conditioning elsewhere, a cooling center, or contact local heat resources.
- Fan use feels hotter or dehydration worsens -> stop relying on the fan alone and seek cooler air.
- Heat illness symptoms appear -> cool the person and seek urgent medical care.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Trapping heat all day:** detect by room hotter than outside at night -> recover by blocking sun earlier and venting after outdoor temperature drops.
- **F2 Overcooling shower:** detect by shivering after a cold shower -> recover by using lukewarm water and light drying.
- **F3 Heat illness missed:** detect by confusion, fainting, vomiting, or inability to cool -> recover by calling emergency services or seeking urgent care.

## Verification

Before sleep, bedding is lighter, airflow or cooling is set, water is available, the room is the coolest safe option, and no heat-illness red flags are present.

## Variations

- No air conditioning: prioritize shade, night ventilation, cool cloths, lower floors, and public cooling spaces during extreme heat.
- Shared housing: agree on fan and window positions before bedtime.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk during extreme heat. Heat illness can become dangerous quickly, especially for higher-risk people. Cooling centers or assistance programs may involve sharing location or contact information.
