## Goal

A likely stye is cared for with warm compresses and hygiene while monitoring for signs that need medical care.

## Preconditions

- Small tender bump is on the eyelid margin or near an eyelash.
- Clean washcloth, warm water, mild soap, and clean towel are available.
- No vision loss, severe eye pain, spreading facial redness, fever, or eye injury is present.

## Steps

1. **Check for urgent signs.** Look for vision changes, severe pain, fever, spreading redness, or swelling closing the eye. → *Expect:* none of those urgent signs are present.
2. **Wash hands.** Clean hands with soap and water before touching near the eye. → *Expect:* hands are clean and dry.
3. **Warm a compress.** Wet a clean cloth with warm, not hot, water and wring it out. → *Expect:* cloth feels comfortably warm on the inner wrist.
4. **Apply the compress.** Hold it on the closed eyelid for 10-15 minutes, rewarming as needed. → *Expect:* the lid feels warmed and softened without burning.
5. **Clean the lid gently.** Wipe crust from the lash line with clean water or diluted mild cleanser. → *Expect:* crust loosens without rubbing the eye.
6. **Avoid squeezing.** Leave the bump alone between compresses. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* squeezing can worsen infection or injure the eyelid; do not try to pop it. → *Expect:* the stye drains only on its own, if at all.
7. **Pause eye makeup and contacts.** Use glasses and skip eye cosmetics until healed. → *Expect:* fewer irritants contact the eyelid.
8. **Repeat daily.** Use warm compresses 3-4 times per day until improving. → *Expect:* tenderness and swelling trend down over several days.

## Decision points

- Symptoms worsen or do not improve after 48 hours of compresses → contact a clinician.
- Vision changes, fever, or spreading redness appears → seek urgent medical care.
- Bump is painless and persistent for weeks → ask an eye clinician about chalazion or other causes.
- You wear contacts → avoid lenses until the eyelid is comfortable and clean.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Compress burns:** cloth is too hot → cool it and test on wrist before the eyelid.
- **F2 Swelling spreads:** redness moves beyond the lid → seek medical care promptly.
- **F3 Stye recurs:** lid oil glands may be chronically irritated → discuss lid hygiene with an eye clinician.
- **F4 Eye makeup contaminates:** old cosmetics touch the area → discard contaminated products and pause makeup.

## Verification

The eyelid is being warmed with clean compresses, the bump is not squeezed, and pain, swelling, and redness are stable or improving without vision changes.

## Variations

- Child: supervise compress temperature and prevent rubbing.
- Blepharitis history: use clinician-recommended lid hygiene after the acute tenderness improves.
- External drainage: wipe gently with clean gauze and continue warm compresses.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because eyelid infections can spread. Do not squeeze a stye, share towels, or wear eye makeup or contacts during active symptoms.
