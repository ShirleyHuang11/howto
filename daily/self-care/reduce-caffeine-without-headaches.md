## Goal

Lower caffeine intake gradually while minimizing withdrawal headaches and sleep disruption.

## Preconditions

- Current caffeine sources are known, including coffee, tea, energy drinks, soda, pre-workout, and pills.
- Access to water and lower-caffeine substitutes.
- No pregnancy, heart rhythm concerns, severe anxiety, or medication interaction concerns without clinician guidance.

## Steps

1. **Measure the baseline.** Write down all caffeine sources for two normal days. → *Expect:* average daily servings are visible.
2. **Set a reduction target.** Reduce total caffeine by about 10 to 25 percent for the first step. → *Expect:* the first cut is small enough to be sustainable.
3. **Cut the latest dose first.** Move or remove caffeine taken within 8 hours of bedtime. → *Expect:* evening sleep pressure is less disrupted.
4. **Blend down gradually.** Mix regular with decaf, choose smaller servings, or swap one drink for tea. → *Expect:* caffeine drops without an abrupt cliff.
5. **Hydrate and eat normally.** Pair the reduction with water and regular meals. → *Expect:* headache triggers from dehydration or skipped food are reduced.
6. **Hold each level several days.** Stay at the new amount until headaches and irritability settle. → *Expect:* symptoms remain mild or fade before the next cut.
7. **Track sleep and headaches.** Note caffeine amount, cutoff time, headache, and bedtime for one week. → *Expect:* patterns show whether the taper speed is working.

## Decision points

- Headache is severe or unusual -> seek medical advice rather than assuming withdrawal.
- Withdrawal is disruptive -> slow the taper and hold the current level longer.
- Caffeine is used to manage exhaustion -> assess sleep, workload, or health causes with a professional if persistent.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Abrupt stop:** detect by strong headache, fatigue, and irritability within a day -> recover by restoring a smaller controlled dose and tapering slower.
- **F2 Hidden caffeine:** detect by symptoms not matching the log -> recover by checking labels on energy drinks, supplements, chocolate, and medications.
- **F3 Late replacement habit:** detect by switching to sugary drinks or late snacks -> recover by choosing water, herbal tea, or decaf earlier in the day.

## Verification

After one to three weeks, daily caffeine is at or below the target, the last dose is at least 8 hours before bedtime, and headaches are absent or mild for three consecutive days.

## Variations

- Coffee drinker: replace part of each cup with decaf before reducing cup count.
- Energy drink user: step down serving size and avoid replacing caffeine with high sugar intake.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most adults, but caffeine can affect anxiety, sleep, pregnancy, blood pressure, heart rhythm, and medications. Consult a clinician or pharmacist when those factors apply.
