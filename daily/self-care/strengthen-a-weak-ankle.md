## Goal

Build ankle strength and balance gradually after mild weakness or old sprains while avoiding signs that need clinical care.

## Preconditions

- Ability to bear weight without severe pain.
- A wall, chair, or counter for balance support.
- No acute fracture concern, major swelling, inability to walk four steps, numbness, or severe instability; seek medical care for those.

## Steps

1. **Check baseline safely.** Stand near support and compare ankle pain, swelling, and balance side to side. → *Expect:* you know the starting difference without provoking pain.
2. **Restore range of motion.** Trace the alphabet with the foot or make slow ankle circles. → *Expect:* movement is smooth and pain stays mild.
3. **Strengthen four directions.** Use a band or gentle hand resistance for up, down, inward, and outward ankle motions. → *Expect:* muscles work around the ankle without sharp pain.
4. **Add calf raises.** Rise onto both toes slowly, then lower with control. → *Expect:* calf and ankle load feels symmetrical or only mildly harder on one side.
5. **Train balance.** Stand on one foot near support for 20 to 30 seconds. → *Expect:* wobbling decreases across attempts.
6. **Progress carefully.** Add single-leg calf raises, uneven surfaces, or light hops only when pain-free basics are easy. → *Expect:* challenge increases without swelling afterward.
7. **Recheck next day.** Note pain, swelling, and walking quality. → *Expect:* the ankle is no worse the day after training.

## Decision points

- Recent sprain with swelling or bruising -> get assessed before strengthening hard.
- Balance drills cause pain or giving way -> return to supported range and consult a physical therapist.
- Sport return is the goal -> add sport-specific progression with professional guidance if instability repeats.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Loading too early:** detect by increased swelling or limping after exercises -> recover by reducing difficulty and seeking assessment if symptoms persist.
- **F2 Skipping balance:** detect by strength improving but wobbling remains -> recover by adding supported single-leg balance daily.
- **F3 Ignoring red flags:** detect by inability to bear weight, numbness, or deformity -> recover by stopping and seeking medical care.

## Verification

For two weeks, exercises are completed at least four days per week, walking is not worsened the next day, and single-leg balance time improves or becomes more controlled.

## Variations

- Very weak ankle: begin seated with range of motion and band work only.
- Athletic return: progress to hopping, cutting, and landing drills only after pain-free strength and balance.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because ankle weakness may hide an untreated sprain, tendon injury, or fracture. Consult a clinician for acute injury, swelling, instability, numbness, or pain that does not improve.
