## Goal

Create a short daily gratitude practice that notices specific good moments without denying real problems.

## Preconditions

- Notebook, notes app, or index card.
- A daily anchor such as breakfast, bedtime, or commute arrival.
- Willingness to keep entries small and specific.

## Steps

1. **Choose the anchor.** Tie the practice to one existing daily moment. → *Expect:* you know exactly when the entry will happen.
2. **Write three specific items.** Name concrete things, people, comforts, or moments from the last 24 hours. → *Expect:* each line points to an actual event or detail.
3. **Add why one item mattered.** Pick one line and write a short reason it affected you. → *Expect:* the entry feels connected rather than generic.
4. **Keep the tone honest.** Allow hard days to include small gratitudes like clean water, a text reply, or five quiet minutes. → *Expect:* the practice does not require pretending everything is fine.
5. **Review weekly.** Once a week, reread entries and mark any repeated themes. → *Expect:* patterns in support, values, or routines become visible.

## Decision points

- Writing feels forced -> use one sentence or a voice note instead of three lines.
- Gratitude increases guilt or self-criticism -> pause and consider support from a mental-health professional.
- Privacy matters -> use paper stored privately or a locked digital note.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Generic lists:** detect by repeating vague words like family or health with no detail -> recover by naming a specific moment.
- **F2 Toxic positivity:** detect by using gratitude to dismiss pain or avoid action -> recover by writing both the hard fact and one thing that helped.
- **F3 Habit breaks:** detect by missing several days -> recover by restarting with one line at the next anchor.

## Verification

At least five entries exist after one week, each includes a specific gratitude, and one weekly review identifies at least one repeated theme.

## Variations

- Shared practice: exchange one item with a trusted person.
- Photo version: take one daily photo of something appreciated and caption it privately.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. This is a reflection habit, not treatment for depression, trauma, or anxiety; seek professional help if distress is severe or persistent. Private entries can reveal relationships and routines, so store them accordingly.
