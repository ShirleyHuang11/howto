## Goal

Create a repeatable strength-training habit that builds consistency before heavy loading.

## Preconditions

- Two or three weekly time slots of 20 to 45 minutes.
- Basic equipment such as body weight, bands, dumbbells, machines, or a gym.
- Medical clearance when advised for heart disease, pregnancy, recent surgery, uncontrolled blood pressure, or significant pain.

## Steps

1. **Schedule fixed sessions.** Choose two nonconsecutive days and one optional third day. → *Expect:* sessions appear on the calendar with recovery between them.
2. **Pick five movement patterns.** Include squat, hinge, push, pull, and carry or core. → *Expect:* the plan trains the whole body without guessing each day.
3. **Start with easy sets.** Do 1 to 2 sets of 8 to 12 controlled reps, stopping with 2 to 4 reps left in reserve. → *Expect:* muscles work but form stays clean.
4. **Track the minimum.** Record exercise, load or variation, reps, and how hard it felt. → *Expect:* the next session has a clear starting point.
5. **Progress slowly.** Add one rep, one set, or a small amount of weight only when all reps are controlled. → *Expect:* improvement is visible without sudden jumps.
6. **Protect recovery.** Leave at least one day between harder sessions and sleep enough to adapt. → *Expect:* normal soreness fades before the next hard effort.
7. **Keep the habit floor tiny.** On low-energy days, do one set of each pattern rather than skipping entirely. → *Expect:* the routine survives imperfect weeks.

## Decision points

- No equipment -> use bodyweight squats, hip hinges, wall or incline pushups, towel rows, and planks.
- Technique feels uncertain -> use machines, lighter loads, or coaching before adding weight.
- Pain changes movement -> stop that exercise and get qualified advice if it repeats.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much too soon:** detect by soreness lasting more than 72 hours or declining performance -> recover by cutting volume in half for a week.
- **F2 Random workouts:** detect by choosing exercises only at the moment -> recover by writing the five-pattern template before training.
- **F3 Form breaks under load:** detect by compensating, bouncing, or losing control -> recover by reducing load and range until form returns.

## Verification

Across four weeks, you complete at least eight strength sessions, log every session, and progress at least one movement without pain or form breakdown.

## Variations

- Gym machines: useful for beginners because setup controls balance demands.
- Home training: bands and dumbbells can cover the same movement patterns with less setup time.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from poor technique, excess load, and ignored pain. Stop for chest pain, faintness, sharp pain, or neurological symptoms, and consult a health professional or qualified trainer when unsure.
