## Goal

Reduce seasonal allergy symptoms by limiting pollen exposure and using appropriate over-the-counter care when suitable.

## Preconditions

- Symptoms fit allergies, such as sneezing, itchy eyes, runny nose, and seasonal pattern.
- Access to weather or pollen information, laundry, and basic cleaning supplies.
- No trouble breathing, facial swelling, high fever, severe sinus pain, or symptoms in a very young child; seek medical care for those.

## Steps

1. **Check pollen timing.** Look up local pollen level or note whether symptoms spike after outdoor exposure. → *Expect:* you know the highest-risk part of the day or week.
2. **Reduce indoor pollen.** Keep windows closed during high pollen, use ventilation or filtration if available, and wipe visible dust. → *Expect:* sleeping and work areas have less outdoor air and dust.
3. **Decontaminate after outdoor time.** Change clothes, wash hands and face, and shower before bed after high exposure. → *Expect:* pollen is not carried onto bedding.
4. **Rinse the nose safely.** Use sterile, distilled, or previously boiled and cooled water for saline rinses if you choose rinsing. → *Expect:* nasal passages feel clearer without unsafe tap-water exposure.
5. **Use medicines responsibly.** Follow the label for antihistamines, nasal steroid sprays, or eye drops, checking interactions and drowsiness warnings. → *Expect:* doses match the package and side effects are understood.
6. **Track response for one week.** Note symptoms, exposure, and any medicine used. → *Expect:* triggers and effective measures are clearer.

## Decision points

- Symptoms include wheezing or asthma flare -> follow your asthma plan and contact a clinician if not controlled.
- Over-the-counter medicine is needed most days or does not work -> consult a clinician or pharmacist.
- Symptoms look infectious, with fever or thick worsening discharge -> seek medical advice.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pollen enters bedding:** detect by worse symptoms at night or morning -> recover by showering before bed and washing pillowcases more often.
- **F2 Medication side effects:** detect by drowsiness, racing heart, nosebleeds, or irritation -> recover by stopping or changing only with pharmacist or clinician guidance.
- **F3 Unsafe nasal rinse:** detect by using plain tap water -> recover by switching to sterile, distilled, or boiled-and-cooled water.

## Verification

After one week, symptoms are milder or better timed, bedding exposure is controlled, any medicine is used according to label, and red-flag symptoms are absent.

## Variations

- Contact lenses: switch to glasses during severe eye symptoms and ask an eye-care professional about drops safe for contacts.
- Outdoor work: add wraparound glasses, mask use, and immediate clothing changes after shifts.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because allergies can overlap with asthma, infection, or medication interactions. Consult a professional for breathing trouble, severe symptoms, pregnancy, chronic illness, or regular medication use.
