## Goal

Return to exercise after time off with a conservative plan that rebuilds tolerance without triggering injury or burnout.

## Preconditions

- Reason for the break is understood, such as schedule, illness, injury, travel, or motivation.
- Comfortable clothing, safe space, and water are available.
- Medical clearance if returning after significant illness, surgery, pregnancy complications, chest symptoms, fainting, or injury.

## Steps

1. **Pick the starting level.** Choose about half of the time, load, or intensity you last handled comfortably. → *Expect:* the first week looks easier than your memory of peak fitness.
2. **Schedule two or three sessions.** Leave rest days between harder efforts. → *Expect:* recovery is built into the calendar.
3. **Warm up thoroughly.** Spend 5 to 10 minutes on easy movement and practice reps. → *Expect:* joints and breathing are ready before intensity rises.
4. **Keep effort conversational.** For cardio, use the talk test; for strength, stop with several reps in reserve. → *Expect:* you finish knowing you could have done more.
5. **Progress weekly, not daily.** Add small amounts of time, load, or frequency only if the previous week felt good. → *Expect:* increases are boring and measurable.
6. **Monitor the 24-hour response.** Check soreness, energy, sleep, and pain the next day. → *Expect:* normal soreness improves and daily function remains intact.
7. **Protect consistency.** Use short fallback sessions when life gets busy. → *Expect:* the routine continues even when the ideal workout does not happen.

## Decision points

- Returning after illness -> wait until fever and acute symptoms are gone, then start very easy.
- Returning after injury -> follow clinician or physical therapist limits before general training advice.
- Motivation is the barrier -> make sessions shorter and more scheduled before increasing ambition.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Ego pacing:** detect by matching old personal bests in week one -> recover by cutting volume and intensity back to the planned start.
- **F2 Next-day flare:** detect by pain, exhaustion, or soreness that disrupts normal activity -> recover with rest and a smaller next session.
- **F3 Inconsistent restart:** detect by one hard workout followed by many missed days -> recover by scheduling smaller repeatable sessions.

## Verification

After two weeks, at least four sessions are completed, next-day symptoms are stable or improving, and exercise volume has increased only when recovery stayed acceptable.

## Variations

- Strength return: begin with fewer sets and lighter loads before adding intensity.
- Cardio return: start with walk-run, bike, or swim intervals at easy effort.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from returning too hard after illness, injury, or long inactivity. Stop for chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, sharp pain, or neurological symptoms, and consult a professional when medical factors are involved.
