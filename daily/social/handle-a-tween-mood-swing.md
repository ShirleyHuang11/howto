## Goal

Help a tween move through a mood swing while preserving connection, boundaries, and growing independence.

## Preconditions

- The tween is physically safe and not in immediate crisis.
- The adult can stay calm and avoid mocking or escalating.
- A private or quieter space is available if needed.

## Steps

1. **Check safety and privacy.** Move the conversation away from siblings, peers, or public attention. → *Expect:* the tween is less exposed and easier to reach.
2. **Name the shift gently.** Say you notice a hard moment without accusing them of being dramatic. → *Expect:* the tween feels observed rather than attacked.
3. **Offer space or support.** Ask whether they want quiet, a walk, food, help, or a few minutes alone. → *Expect:* the tween gets some control over regulation.
4. **Hold basic limits.** Keep rules against insults, threats, slamming near people, or unsafe behavior. → *Expect:* feelings are allowed and harm is not.
5. **Return when calmer.** Discuss the trigger, repair, or plan after intensity drops. → *Expect:* problem-solving happens when the tween can think.
6. **Watch patterns.** Notice sleep, hunger, screens, school stress, friendship issues, puberty changes, and frequency. → *Expect:* repeated triggers become visible.

## Decision points

- If the tween mentions self-harm, hopelessness, abuse, or not wanting to live → seek urgent professional help immediately.
- If mood swings are frequent, extreme, or impairing school or relationships → contact a pediatrician or mental health professional.
- If the trigger is an adult boundary → stay kind while holding the reasonable limit.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Public correction:** detect embarrassment making the mood worse → recover by moving private and reducing words.
- **F2 Boundary collapse:** detect rude behavior getting rewarded to end conflict → recover by revisiting expectations after calm.
- **F3 Missed crisis:** detect self-harm talk or severe withdrawal → recover by getting immediate professional support.

## Verification

The tween is safe, intensity has decreased, the boundary remains clear, and a later repair or trigger discussion is planned or completed.

## Variations

- `hunger-or-sleep`: meet the body need before a long conversation.
- `friend-drama`: listen first and avoid taking over the social problem.
- `puberty`: normalize body and mood changes while tracking severe symptoms.

## Safety & privacy

Low to medium risk depending on severity. Protect privacy, avoid ridicule, and seek urgent help for self-harm, abuse, severe depression, or unsafe aggression.
