## Goal

Build a child's basic water comfort and swimming skills while treating water as a serious safety environment.

## Preconditions

- A safe pool or supervised swimming area is available.
- A sober, attentive adult can stay within arm's reach of weak swimmers.
- The child has appropriate swimwear and any needed approved flotation device.

## Steps

1. **Set water rules.** Explain walk near water, ask before entering, stay where the adult can reach, and stop on cue. → *Expect:* the child can repeat the rules.
2. **Enter gradually.** Start on steps or shallow water and let the child adjust to temperature and depth. → *Expect:* the child remains calm enough to listen.
3. **Practice breath control.** Blow bubbles, hum into water, and wet the face without forcing submersion. → *Expect:* the child learns to manage water near the face.
4. **Practice floating with support.** Hold the child's back or hands while they relax into front and back floats. → *Expect:* the child feels buoyancy with adult support.
5. **Add kicks and reaches.** Move short distances between adult and wall with close spotting. → *Expect:* the child coordinates movement without panic.
6. **End with safe exit practice.** Have the child reach the wall, climb out, and wait before reentering. → *Expect:* exiting becomes part of the skill, not an afterthought.

## Decision points

- If the child panics, shivers, coughs repeatedly, or refuses → stop, warm up, and try another day.
- If the child is near open water, deep water, or a crowded pool → use trained swim instruction and stricter supervision.
- If there are motor, seizure, respiratory, or developmental concerns → ask a pediatrician and qualified instructor for adaptations.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Forced submersion:** detect crying, breath-holding panic, or fear of returning → recover by stopping and rebuilding comfort slowly.
- **F2 False security:** detect adults relying on floaties instead of supervision → recover by assigning an active water watcher.
- **F3 Unsafe environment:** detect unclear depth, currents, crowding, or no exit route → recover by leaving or moving to a safer area.

## Verification

The child follows water rules, exits safely, and practices one supported float or short assisted swim without distress.

## Variations

- `infant-or-toddler`: focus on water comfort and caregiver safety, not independent swimming.
- `older-beginner`: start privately to reduce embarrassment.
- `open-water`: add life jackets, current awareness, and local hazard rules.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because drowning can happen quickly and quietly. Maintain constant supervision, use qualified instruction when possible, and seek medical help after any serious coughing, submersion scare, or breathing concern.
