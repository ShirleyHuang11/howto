## Goal

Support a child in developing two languages through consistent exposure, positive identity, and realistic expectations.

## Preconditions

- At least one caregiver, family member, school, community, or media source can provide regular input in each language.
- Caregivers agree that bilingual development is valuable and not a punishment or test.
- Hearing, speech, or developmental concerns will be discussed with a pediatrician or speech-language professional.

## Steps

1. **Define the language plan.** Choose patterns such as one person one language, home language outside language, or time-and-place routines. → *Expect:* caregivers know when each language will be used.
2. **Build daily exposure.** Use conversation, books, songs, calls with relatives, playgroups, school, or media in both languages. → *Expect:* each language appears predictably in real life.
3. **Keep it warm.** Respond to meaning first and model the target language without constant correction. → *Expect:* the child keeps wanting to communicate.
4. **Support the weaker language.** Add stories, peers, trips, classes, or family routines where that language is useful. → *Expect:* the child has a reason to use it.
5. **Accept mixing.** Treat code-switching as normal while gently restating phrases when helpful. → *Expect:* mixing does not become shame.
6. **Monitor development.** Watch comprehension, hearing, social communication, and progress in both languages over time. → *Expect:* concerns are noticed early without blaming bilingualism.

## Decision points

- If the child resists one language → increase enjoyable need-based exposure rather than forcing drills.
- If speech seems delayed in both languages or comprehension is weak → seek pediatric or speech-language evaluation.
- If school pressures dropping a home language → ask about support while preserving family language and identity.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Correction overload:** detect the child avoiding the language → recover by prioritizing conversation and modeling.
- **F2 Uneven exposure:** detect one language disappearing from daily life → recover by adding regular people, books, or routines.
- **F3 Misread delay:** detect concerns dismissed as bilingualism → recover by evaluating skills across both languages with a professional.

## Verification

The child receives regular meaningful exposure to both languages, caregivers follow a clear plan, and any communication concerns are tracked or referred.

## Variations

- `heritage-language`: prioritize family stories, relatives, and community use.
- `school-immersion`: support homework language while maintaining the home language.
- `late-start`: set practical goals and increase exposure gradually.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, centered on identity and communication dignity. Do not shame accents or mixing, and seek professional guidance for hearing, speech, comprehension, or social communication concerns.
