## Goal

Help a child address a school conflict with safety, fairness, documentation, and appropriate adult support.

## Preconditions

- The child is currently safe or can be made safe.
- You can listen before contacting the school.
- School communication channels, teacher names, or administrator contacts are available.

## Steps

1. **Listen privately.** Ask what happened, who was involved, where it happened, and what the child needs now. → *Expect:* the child feels heard and basic facts are clearer.
2. **Assess safety.** Check for threats, injury, bullying, discrimination, harassment, or self-harm concerns. → *Expect:* urgent issues are separated from ordinary conflict.
3. **Document facts.** Write dates, locations, messages, screenshots, witnesses, and prior attempts to solve it. → *Expect:* details are preserved accurately.
4. **Choose the first contact.** Email or speak with the teacher, counselor, coach, or administrator closest to the issue. → *Expect:* the school has a clear concise report.
5. **Coach the child.** Practice a respectful statement, boundary, or request if direct repair is safe. → *Expect:* the child has words for their part of the solution.
6. **Follow up.** Ask what action was taken and watch whether the conflict stops. → *Expect:* the plan is monitored instead of assumed.

## Decision points

- If there is violence, credible threat, sexual harassment, discrimination, or self-harm risk → escalate immediately to school leadership and appropriate emergency or professional help.
- If the child contributed to the conflict → support accountability while still addressing safety.
- If the school response is unclear → request a written plan and timeline.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Minimizing harm:** detect the child remains afraid or targeted → recover by escalating and documenting each incident.
- **F2 Overreacting publicly:** detect angry posts or group chats about the conflict → recover by moving communication to official channels.
- **F3 Missing child's voice:** detect adults solving around the child → recover by asking what outcome would help them feel safe and respected.

## Verification

The conflict has a documented summary, the appropriate school adult has been contacted, and the child knows the current safety or repair plan.

## Variations

- `bullying`: document repeated power imbalance and request a safety plan.
- `teacher-conflict`: ask for clarification and focus on specific classroom events.
- `friend-conflict`: coach repair first unless safety or harassment is involved.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from bullying, retaliation, and exposure of children's private information. Avoid public accusations, preserve evidence, and seek professional help for threats, trauma, or self-harm concerns.
