## Goal

Help a baby learn a predictable sleep pattern while keeping feeding, safety, and attachment needs protected.

## Preconditions

- The baby is old enough for sleep training guidance from their pediatrician, commonly after early newborn months.
- Safe sleep conditions are available: firm flat surface, no loose bedding, and a smoke-free sleep space.
- Caregivers agree on one calm response plan for night waking.

## Steps

1. **Confirm readiness.** Check growth, feeding, reflux, medication, and developmental concerns with the pediatrician before reducing night help. → *Expect:* you know whether sleep training is appropriate now.
2. **Set a repeatable bedtime.** Use feeding, diaper, sleepwear, dim light, short song, and crib in the same order each night. → *Expect:* the baby gets consistent cues that sleep is next.
3. **Place the baby down drowsy.** Put the baby in the sleep space calm but awake enough to notice the crib. → *Expect:* the baby has a chance to practice falling asleep there.
4. **Respond on a schedule.** [BRANCH: gradual checks | caregiver presence] use brief checks or quiet presence without restarting full play or feeding unless needed. → *Expect:* comfort is offered without making waking more stimulating.
5. **Track patterns.** Record bedtime, wakeups, feeds, naps, illness, and soothing response for one week. → *Expect:* changes are based on patterns rather than one hard night.
6. **Protect daytime needs.** Keep age-appropriate naps, feeds, daylight, and connection during the day. → *Expect:* the baby is not overtired, hungry, or undercomforted by bedtime.

## Decision points

- If the baby is ill, teething hard, traveling, or losing weight → pause training and return to responsive care.
- If night feeding is still medically needed → keep the feed and train only the sleep transitions around it.
- If crying feels extreme or caregivers feel unsafe or overwhelmed → stop and seek pediatric or professional support.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unsafe sleep setup:** detect loose blankets, pillows, inclined sleepers, or adult-bed sleep → recover by moving the baby to a safe sleep surface.
- **F2 Mixed responses:** detect different caregivers changing rules nightly → recover by writing one simple plan and following it for several nights.
- **F3 Missed medical need:** detect poor weight gain, breathing trouble, vomiting, fever, or unusual lethargy → recover by calling the pediatrician promptly.

## Verification

The baby sleeps in a safe sleep space, bedtime follows the same routine most nights, and wake responses match the chosen plan without ignoring medical or feeding needs.

## Variations

- `gentle-method`: stay in the room and gradually reduce touch or voice support.
- `shared-room`: use the same sleep surface and response plan while minimizing lights and talking.
- `older-baby`: include a comfort object only if age and safety guidance allow it.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from sleep-surface hazards, missed feeding needs, and caregiver exhaustion. Never use unsafe restraints or sedating products; ask a pediatrician about age, feeding, growth, reflux, breathing, or persistent distress.
