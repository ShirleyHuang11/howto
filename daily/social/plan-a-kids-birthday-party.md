## Goal

Plan a child-friendly birthday party with a realistic guest list, safe activities, food details, and clear parent communication.

## Preconditions

- You know the child's age, preferred activity, budget, and any venue limits.
- You have contact information for invited families.
- You can account for allergies, supervision, bathrooms, and pickup.

## Steps

1. **Set the party frame.** Choose date, time, duration, budget, and whether the party is at home, a park, or a venue. → *Expect:* the basic plan fits naps, meals, weather, and family capacity.
2. **Match activities to age.** [BRANCH: toddlers | school-age kids] use short sensory or free-play stations for toddlers; use games, crafts, or structured activities for older kids. → *Expect:* no activity requires skills the guests are unlikely to have.
3. **Build the guest and supervision plan.** Decide whether caregivers stay, how many adults are needed, and where siblings fit. → *Expect:* every child will have enough adult attention.
4. **Send clear invitations.** Include date, time, location, RSVP deadline, parking, whether caregivers stay, and allergy or food questions. → *Expect:* parents can answer without follow-up guesswork.
5. **Plan food and cake safely.** Choose simple snacks, drinks, and cake or cupcakes with allergy alternatives labeled. → *Expect:* food matches the time of day and known restrictions.
6. **Prepare the party flow.** Schedule arrival play, main activity, food, cake, and pickup, with one backup activity. → *Expect:* there is a loose timeline without overpacking.
7. **Set up safety basics.** Put away breakables, secure pets, mark bathrooms, and keep first-aid supplies visible to adults. → *Expect:* the party space is safe for excited kids.
8. **Confirm final details.** Recheck RSVPs, weather, venue rules, and helper roles the day before. → *Expect:* supplies, headcount, and responsibilities are ready.

## Decision points

- Weather affects an outdoor party → reserve a covered option or communicate a rain plan.
- Allergies are reported → adjust food or ask parents what is safe.
- The child gets overwhelmed → build in a quiet break space and shorter spotlight moments.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too many guests:** detect supervision or budget strain → reduce scope, choose a venue package, or invite by clear group boundaries.
- **F2 Activity collapses:** detect boredom or chaos → switch to free play, music, bubbles, or a simple group game.
- **F3 Allergy confusion:** detect unlabeled food or unclear ingredients → remove questionable food and contact the parent.
- **F4 Pickup confusion:** detect adults arriving at different places or times → send one clear message with pickup location and end time.

## Verification

The party has a confirmed date, location, guest list, supervision plan, food plan with allergy handling, activity flow, and parent communication before the event.

## Variations

- `park-party`: add permits, shade, water, bathrooms, and wind-proof decorations.
- `winter-birthday`: plan indoor movement activities and coat storage.
- `low-budget`: use home games, homemade cake, and a small guest list.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, mainly child supervision, allergies, photos, and private contact information. Ask before posting photos of other children and keep guest addresses or phone numbers private.
