## Goal

Stop a sibling fight safely, help each child regulate, and guide repair without taking sides too quickly.

## Preconditions

- The children can be separated or supervised safely.
- The adult can intervene calmly enough to prevent harm.
- Any immediate medical injury or danger has priority over teaching.

## Steps

1. **Stop harm first.** Move bodies apart, remove thrown objects, and block hitting or biting. → *Expect:* no one can keep hurting another person.
2. **Use neutral language.** Say what you see without deciding the whole story immediately. → *Expect:* both children hear that safety comes before blame.
3. **Separate to calm.** Give each child space, water, breathing, or quiet time while staying nearby as needed. → *Expect:* volume and body tension start to drop.
4. **Hear each child briefly.** Ask each child what happened and what they needed, one at a time. → *Expect:* the adult has enough information to guide repair.
5. **Set the boundary.** State the rule about bodies, belongings, words, or shared space. → *Expect:* the limit is clear and applies to everyone.
6. **Repair and reset.** Help return an item, apologize, make a plan, or separate activities for now. → *Expect:* the conflict has a next step instead of a winner.

## Decision points

- If one child is repeatedly targeted or afraid → increase supervision and treat it as a safety pattern, not normal rivalry.
- If the fight is about a scarce item → use turns, duplication, or put the item away.
- If aggression is severe, frequent, or causing injuries → seek pediatric, school, or family therapy support.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Instant blame:** detect one child always labeled the problem → recover by focusing on behavior and patterns.
- **F2 Forced apology:** detect a resentful scripted apology with no repair → recover by asking for a concrete repair action.
- **F3 Unsafe repeat:** detect renewed hitting after reset → recover by separating activities and increasing supervision.

## Verification

The children are physically safe, the immediate conflict has stopped, and each child knows the repair or separation plan.

## Variations

- `toddlers`: use fewer words and more physical separation.
- `school-age`: teach turn-taking, trade offers, and problem-solving scripts.
- `teen-siblings`: respect privacy while setting firm limits on threats, harassment, or property damage.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from injury and emotional harm. Do not ignore intimidation, humiliation, or repeated targeting; seek professional help if one child seems unsafe at home.
