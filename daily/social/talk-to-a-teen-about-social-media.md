## Goal

Have a respectful conversation with a teen about social media safety, privacy, mental health, and boundaries.

## Preconditions

- The teen has or wants access to social media, messaging, or online communities.
- The adult can listen before lecturing.
- Household expectations about devices, sleep, school, and privacy can be discussed.

## Steps

1. **Start with curiosity.** Ask what apps they use, what they like, and what feels stressful. → *Expect:* the teen is more likely to talk honestly.
2. **Name real risks.** Discuss privacy, screenshots, scams, sexual pressure, harassment, comparison, and sleep disruption. → *Expect:* the conversation covers concrete hazards.
3. **Agree on boundaries.** Set rules for private accounts, location sharing, sleep hours, unknown contacts, and reportable situations. → *Expect:* expectations are specific.
4. **Check settings together.** Review privacy, comments, direct messages, location, blocking, and reporting tools. → *Expect:* the teen knows how to reduce exposure.
5. **Plan help-seeking.** Identify when they should come to an adult, save evidence, block, report, or call for urgent help. → *Expect:* the teen has a response plan.
6. **Schedule follow-up.** Revisit the topic after a week or after a new app, conflict, or rule problem. → *Expect:* social media remains an ongoing conversation.

## Decision points

- If there is coercion, exploitation, threats, stalking, or self-harm content → preserve evidence and seek urgent professional, school, or legal help as appropriate.
- If social media is harming sleep, grades, or mood → tighten boundaries and consider pediatric or mental health support.
- If the teen has shown responsibility → use more collaborative monitoring and less intrusive checking.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Lecture shutdown:** detect one-word answers or defensiveness → recover by asking one open question and listening.
- **F2 Secret account:** detect hidden use after unrealistic bans → recover by resetting safety expectations and consequences calmly.
- **F3 Privacy breach:** detect adult reading everything without cause → recover by defining what is monitored and why.

## Verification

The teen can name the agreed boundaries, show key privacy settings, and identify when and how to seek adult help.

## Variations

- `younger-teen`: use more setup supervision and clearer app limits.
- `older-teen`: focus on consent, reputation, scams, and digital footprint.
- `after-incident`: prioritize safety, evidence, and emotional support before consequences.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk includes exploitation, harassment, scams, and mental health impact. Respect developmentally appropriate privacy while acting quickly for threats, sexual coercion, self-harm, or abuse concerns.
