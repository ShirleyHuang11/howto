## Goal

Introduce a baby and household pet gradually, with barriers, supervision, and routines that protect both.

## Preconditions

- The pet has basic management tools such as leash, crate, gate, carrier, or separate room.
- The baby has a safe place the pet cannot enter, such as a crib, bassinet, or gated room.
- The pet is healthy and not currently showing aggression, severe fear, or pain.

## Steps

1. **Prepare before the meeting.** Set up gates, pet beds, feeding areas, and baby gear so the pet has safe retreats. → *Expect:* the pet can move away without approaching the baby.
2. **Refresh pet routines.** Exercise the pet, feed on schedule, and practice cues such as settle, leave it, or go to mat. → *Expect:* the pet is not meeting the baby with pent-up energy.
3. **Introduce baby scent calmly.** Let the pet sniff a blanket or clothing item while receiving treats or calm praise. → *Expect:* the pet notices the scent without frantic fixation.
4. **Use distance and barriers first.** [BRANCH: dog | cat] keep a dog leashed behind a gate, or let a cat observe from a high escape route. → *Expect:* the pet can look, sniff the air, and disengage.
5. **Keep the first close look brief.** Hold the baby securely, have another adult manage the pet, and end after a calm moment. → *Expect:* the meeting ends before barking, jumping, swatting, or stress escalates.
6. **Reward calm behavior.** Treat or praise looking away, lying down, soft body posture, or leaving the room. → *Expect:* calm choices pay better than crowding the baby.
7. **Maintain supervision rules.** Never leave the baby and pet together unattended, even if early meetings go well. → *Expect:* barriers or adult eyes are always present.
8. **Protect pet resources.** Keep bowls, litter boxes, beds, and toys away from crawling paths as the baby grows. → *Expect:* the pet does not need to guard essentials from the baby.

## Decision points

- Pet growls, hisses, lunges, freezes, or stalks → increase distance and consult a qualified trainer or veterinarian.
- Baby starts crawling → redo management because mobility changes risk.
- Pet has prey drive or prior bite history → use professional guidance before direct access.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pet crowds the baby:** detect jumping, pawing, or nosing hard → increase distance, use leash or gate, and reward settling.
- **F2 Pet hides or stops eating:** detect withdrawal after baby arrives → create quiet pet-only spaces and consult a vet if it persists.
- **F3 Adults relax too soon:** detect baby and pet together without a barrier → restore supervision rules immediately.
- **F4 Resource guarding appears:** detect stiff posture near food, toys, or bed → separate resources and seek professional help.

## Verification

The pet can remain calm or disengage around the baby at a managed distance, and the household has barriers and a rule that baby and pet are never unsupervised together.

## Variations

- `dog`: use leash, gates, mat training, and walks to reduce arousal.
- `cat`: provide vertical escapes, covered litter access, and quiet rooms.
- `multiple-pets`: introduce one pet at a time with separate management plans.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because babies cannot protect themselves and pets can be stressed. Use barriers, supervise every interaction, protect pet resources, and get professional help for aggression, fear, or guarding.
