## Goal

Help a child build friendships by practicing social skills, creating opportunities, and respecting their pace.

## Preconditions

- The child is safe and not currently being bullied.
- The child can name at least one interest, classmate, neighbor, club, or activity they might enjoy.
- A caregiver can support logistics without controlling the relationship.

## Steps

1. **Ask what they want.** Find out whether the child wants one friend, a group, playdates, or less loneliness. → *Expect:* the goal belongs to the child.
2. **Identify likely matches.** Look for shared interests, kind classmates, clubs, teams, neighbors, or family friends. → *Expect:* there are one or two realistic connection options.
3. **Practice a small opener.** Role-play asking to join, giving a compliment, or inviting someone to an activity. → *Expect:* the child has a simple phrase ready.
4. **Arrange low-pressure time.** Set up a short playdate, activity, club meeting, or shared task. → *Expect:* interaction has structure and an endpoint.
5. **Coach during recovery.** Afterward, ask what felt good, awkward, or worth trying again. → *Expect:* the child reflects without feeling judged.
6. **Repeat consistently.** Offer regular chances with the same children when possible. → *Expect:* familiarity can turn into friendship.

## Decision points

- If the child prefers few friends → support quality over popularity.
- If attempts are repeatedly rejected or cruel → assess for bullying and involve school adults if needed.
- If social difficulty is persistent or developmentally concerning → consider pediatric, school, or therapist support.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Parent overmanages:** detect the adult scripting every exchange → recover by stepping back after setup.
- **F2 Bad match:** detect dread, meanness, or no shared interest → recover by choosing a different setting or child.
- **F3 Skill gap:** detect interrupting, grabbing, or not reading cues → recover by practicing one specific social skill at a time.

## Verification

The child has practiced one friendship skill and had or scheduled one low-pressure social opportunity aligned with their interests.

## Variations

- `shy-child`: start with parallel play or shared activities.
- `new-school`: ask teachers which welcoming peers or clubs fit.
- `neurodivergent-child`: use explicit scripts, predictable activities, and professional support when helpful.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with emotional safety and privacy central. Do not force friendship, expose social struggles publicly, or ignore bullying or exclusion patterns.
