## Goal

Help a child complete a science fair project that is safe, honest, and clearly their own work.

## Preconditions

- The assignment rules, deadline, display requirements, and allowed materials are known.
- The child has a question they can test with safe household or school-approved materials.
- An adult can supervise safety without taking over the thinking.

## Steps

1. **Read the rules together.** Identify required question, hypothesis, procedure, data, display, and citation parts. → *Expect:* the child can name what must be turned in.
2. **Narrow the question.** Help turn a broad interest into a testable question with one changed variable. → *Expect:* the project has a measurable comparison.
3. **Plan a safe procedure.** List materials, trials, timing, and adult-only tasks. → *Expect:* risks and steps are clear before starting.
4. **Run the experiment.** Let the child measure, observe, and record results while you supervise hazards. → *Expect:* the notebook contains real data from each trial.
5. **Discuss what happened.** Ask what the data shows, what surprised them, and what could be improved. → *Expect:* conclusions connect to evidence.
6. **Build the display.** Help with layout and proofreading while keeping the child's wording, drawings, and explanations central. → *Expect:* the final board is readable and honestly child-made.

## Decision points

- If the project involves heat, chemicals, electricity, animals, humans, or allergens → follow school rules and get adult or teacher approval first.
- If the result disproves the hypothesis → present the result honestly instead of changing data.
- If time is short → simplify the question rather than fabricating trials.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Parent project:** detect adult-level language or perfect construction the child cannot explain → recover by rewriting in the child's voice.
- **F2 Unsafe materials:** detect risky tools or substances without approval → recover by choosing a safer variable.
- **F3 Missing data:** detect a conclusion with no recorded measurements → recover by rerunning a smaller valid test.

## Verification

The child can explain the question, variable, method, data, and conclusion, and the display meets the teacher's required format.

## Variations

- `early-elementary`: use simple observations, pictures, and oral explanation.
- `middle-school`: add repeated trials, graphs, and clearer controls.
- `team-project`: define each child's role and keep shared records.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary projects, higher for hazardous materials or human subjects. Do not fake data, expose private information, or run experiments on people or animals without school approval.
