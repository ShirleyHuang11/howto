## Goal

Teach sharing as a practiced social skill using turn-taking, preparation, and clear limits instead of forced surrender.

## Preconditions

- The child is old enough to practice simple turns with adult support.
- Toys or materials are safe for the children's ages.
- Special comfort items can be put away before play starts.

## Steps

1. **Prepare the environment.** Put away fragile favorites, duplicate high-demand toys when possible, and choose a few shareable items. → *Expect:* the hardest conflicts are reduced before children start.
2. **Name the rule simply.** Say, "You can take turns. When the timer rings, it is the next person's turn." → *Expect:* children hear the rule before conflict begins.
3. **Use visible turns.** Give the first child a short turn with a timer, song, or count that matches the age. → *Expect:* everyone can see when a turn starts and ends.
4. **Coach the handoff.** Help the child give the toy to the next child and say, "Your turn is done; you can have another turn soon." → *Expect:* the toy changes hands without grabbing.
5. **Protect active play.** [BRANCH: child is still using toy | toy is abandoned] let a child finish a short active turn, or invite another child when the toy is free. → *Expect:* sharing does not feel like immediate confiscation.
6. **Praise the specific behavior.** Notice waiting, offering, trading, and returning, not just being "nice." → *Expect:* children know which action to repeat.
7. **Repair grabs or tears.** Block grabbing, return the toy to the turn holder, and restart with a shorter timer. → *Expect:* the rule remains predictable after conflict.

## Decision points

- The item is a comfort object or new birthday gift → allow it to be private and choose another sharing toy.
- Children are toddlers → focus on turn-taking with adult help, not abstract generosity.
- Conflict escalates to hitting or biting → separate briefly and return only when calm.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Forced sharing backfires:** detect crying or guarding after immediate removal → use turns and put away special items next time.
- **F2 Timer is too long:** detect waiting child melting down → shorten turns and offer a waiting activity.
- **F3 Adult inconsistency:** detect rule changes mid-conflict → restate the same turn rule and follow it.
- **F4 Grabbing continues:** detect repeated snatching → sit closer, reduce toys in dispute, or pause play.

## Verification

The child completes at least one supported turn-taking exchange, waits with help, and sees that toys return after a turn.

## Variations

- `siblings`: create household rules for private, shared, and ask-first items.
- `playdate`: ask the host child to put away off-limits toys before guests arrive.
- `classroom`: use picture schedules, labeled bins, and multiple stations.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, mostly social stress and age-inappropriate expectations. Do not force children to share comfort items, and intervene immediately if conflict becomes physical.
