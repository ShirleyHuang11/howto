## Goal

Help a toddler learn toileting with readiness, consistency, encouragement, and minimal shame.

## Preconditions

- The toddler shows readiness such as dry periods, interest, predictable bowel movements, or discomfort with dirty diapers.
- A potty chair or toilet insert, easy clothing, wipes, and cleanup supplies are ready.
- Caregivers can respond calmly to accidents.

## Steps

1. **Introduce the potty.** Let the toddler sit clothed, read a book, and learn simple words for pee and poop. → *Expect:* the potty feels familiar.
2. **Choose a start window.** Pick days with fewer outings and no major disruption. → *Expect:* caregivers can offer frequent practice.
3. **Use routine sits.** Invite sitting after waking, meals, before bath, and before leaving home. → *Expect:* the toddler gets repeated chances to notice body cues.
4. **Praise effort calmly.** Notice sitting, telling, trying, and successful toileting without big pressure. → *Expect:* the toddler connects practice with approval.
5. **Handle accidents neutrally.** Say pee or poop goes in the potty, clean up, and change clothes. → *Expect:* accidents become information, not shame.
6. **Coordinate caregivers.** Share words, timing, clothing, and reward choices with daycare or relatives. → *Expect:* the toddler gets consistent cues.

## Decision points

- If the toddler resists hard, withholds stool, or becomes distressed → pause and ask the pediatrician if constipation or pain is possible.
- If nighttime dryness is not present → keep nighttime protection and treat it as a separate developmental skill.
- If a regression follows illness, move, new sibling, or stress → return to routine and reassurance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Power struggle:** detect crying, hiding, or refusal every sit → recover by backing off and restarting later.
- **F2 Constipation cycle:** detect hard stool, pain, or withholding → recover by contacting the pediatrician and reducing pressure.
- **F3 Inconsistent words:** detect different caregivers using different rules → recover by writing a simple shared plan.

## Verification

The toddler willingly practices routine potty sits, accidents are handled calmly, and caregivers share the same toileting plan.

## Variations

- `child-led`: wait for strong interest and follow the toddler's pace.
- `daycare`: align clothing, reminders, and cleanup expectations with staff.
- `travel`: use pull-ups or portable potty without treating it as failure.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with dignity important. Do not punish, shame, or force sitting; seek pediatric help for pain, constipation, urinary symptoms, or major regression.
