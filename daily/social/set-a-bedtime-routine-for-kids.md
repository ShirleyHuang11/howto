## Goal

Create a predictable bedtime routine that helps children wind down, feel connected, and get enough sleep.

## Preconditions

- A target bedtime and wake time are known.
- The child has basic sleep needs met, including food, toileting or diaper, and safe sleep space.
- Caregivers can follow a similar order most nights.

## Steps

1. **Choose the bedtime window.** Work backward from wake time and age-appropriate sleep needs. → *Expect:* the routine has a realistic start time.
2. **Remove stimulating inputs.** Dim lights, reduce noise, and put away screens before the routine starts. → *Expect:* the environment signals winding down.
3. **Use the same sequence.** Do bathroom or diaper, pajamas, teeth, story, brief connection, and lights out in order. → *Expect:* the child can predict what comes next.
4. **Offer limited choices.** Let the child choose between two pajamas, books, songs, or stuffed animals. → *Expect:* the child has control without delaying bedtime.
5. **Hold the final boundary.** Return calmly after extra requests and repeat the same short phrase. → *Expect:* bedtime does not restart repeatedly.
6. **Review after several nights.** Adjust timing if the child is not sleepy, is overtired, or wakes hard in the morning. → *Expect:* the routine fits the child's actual sleep pattern.

## Decision points

- If bedtime takes more than an hour most nights → start earlier, shorten choices, or assess naps and screens.
- If snoring, breathing pauses, restless legs, night terrors, or chronic insomnia appear → ask a pediatrician.
- If fear is driving delays → add comfort and gradual independence rather than punishment.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Routine too long:** detect many books, snacks, or negotiations → recover by setting a short visual sequence.
- **F2 Inconsistent boundary:** detect different rules each night → recover by agreeing on one caregiver response.
- **F3 Medical sleep issue:** detect loud snoring, breathing pauses, or extreme daytime sleepiness → recover by seeking pediatric evaluation.

## Verification

The child completes the same bedtime sequence most nights and is in the sleep space at the target time with predictable caregiver responses.

## Variations

- `toddler`: use picture cards and very few words after lights out.
- `school-age`: include packing the bag and choosing clothes before the calming sequence.
- `shared-room`: stagger bedtimes or use quiet individual routines.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk unless sleep symptoms suggest a medical issue. Keep sleep spaces safe, avoid sedating products unless prescribed, and seek professional help for breathing concerns or severe sleep disruption.
