## Goal

Create a chore chart that gives children clear, age-appropriate household responsibilities without turning chores into constant conflict.

## Preconditions

- Household tasks that children can safely do are known.
- A visible paper chart, whiteboard, or shared app is available.
- Caregivers agree on expectations and follow-through.

## Steps

1. **List needed tasks.** Choose routine chores such as dishes, laundry sorting, pet water, trash, or toy cleanup. → *Expect:* the chart starts from real household needs.
2. **Match by age and ability.** Assign tasks the child can learn safely with reasonable effort. → *Expect:* chores are challenging but possible.
3. **Define done.** Show what finished looks like for each chore. → *Expect:* the child can tell when a task is complete.
4. **Set timing.** Put chores on specific days or daily windows. → *Expect:* the child knows when to act.
5. **Choose feedback.** Use checkmarks, rotation, allowance link, or family contribution language consistently. → *Expect:* progress is visible and predictable.
6. **Review weekly.** Adjust chores that are too hard, too easy, unsafe, or unfairly distributed. → *Expect:* the system improves without daily renegotiation.

## Decision points

- If a chore involves knives, heat, chemicals, heights, or heavy lifting → keep it adult-only or closely supervised.
- If allowance is connected to chores → separate basic family duties from optional paid extras if that fits your values.
- If the chart causes daily fights → reduce task count and reteach one chore at a time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague chore:** detect arguments about what counts as done → recover by adding a short done example.
- **F2 Adult rescues:** detect the adult redoing every task silently → recover by teaching and accepting age-appropriate results.
- **F3 Unfair load:** detect one child carrying too much → recover by rotating and considering age, schedule, and ability.

## Verification

The chart shows each child's chores, timing, and completion method, and each child can explain one assigned task.

## Variations

- `preschool`: use pictures and one-step chores.
- `school-age`: use weekly rotation and simple checkmarks.
- `teen`: negotiate standards, schedule constraints, and larger shared tasks.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk unless chores involve hazards. Keep dangerous tools and chemicals supervised, and avoid public shaming or posting chore failures online.
