## Goal

Support a shy child in social situations without shaming their temperament or forcing performance.

## Preconditions

- The child is safe and not being bullied or threatened.
- The social situation can be approached gradually.
- A caregiver can model calm introductions and respect pauses.

## Steps

1. **Accept the temperament.** Describe the child as warming up slowly rather than rude or broken. → *Expect:* the child is not shamed for needing time.
2. **Preview the situation.** Explain who will be there, what will happen, and how long it may last. → *Expect:* uncertainty decreases before arrival.
3. **Offer a small script.** Practice one greeting, question, or exit phrase the child can use. → *Expect:* the child has words ready if they choose to speak.
4. **Stay nearby first.** Let the child observe from beside you before expecting interaction. → *Expect:* the child can scan the room and settle.
5. **Praise brave behavior.** Name specific effort such as looking up, waving, or answering one question. → *Expect:* progress is recognized without demanding extroversion.
6. **Plan the next exposure.** Choose another small social step for later. → *Expect:* confidence builds through repetition.

## Decision points

- If the child wants help entering play → coach one sentence and walk over together.
- If the child freezes or cries often in ordinary settings → consider anxiety support from a pediatrician, school counselor, or therapist.
- If adults pressure hugs, kisses, or public speaking → protect the child's consent and offer alternatives.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Shaming label:** detect comments like rude, antisocial, or babyish → recover by restating the child needs warm-up time.
- **F2 Too big a leap:** detect shutdown after a demand to perform → recover by returning to observation and smaller steps.
- **F3 Hidden bullying:** detect avoidance of one person or place → recover by asking privately and investigating safety.

## Verification

The child completes one manageable social step while feeling respected, and the caregiver has a next gradual exposure planned.

## Variations

- `preschool`: use play beside other children before direct play.
- `school-age`: practice scripts for joining games and asking questions.
- `teen`: respect privacy and collaborate on goals rather than arranging everything.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with emotional privacy important. Do not shame, film, or force affection; seek professional help if fear prevents normal school, friendships, or family activities.
