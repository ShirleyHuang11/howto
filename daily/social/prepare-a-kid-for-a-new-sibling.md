## Goal

Prepare a child for a new sibling with honest expectations, emotional support, and safe ways to participate.

## Preconditions

- The family is ready to talk about the baby in age-appropriate terms.
- Basic plans for caregiving, hospital time, or placement transition are known.
- The child has a trusted adult who can listen to mixed feelings.

## Steps

1. **Tell the story simply.** Explain when the baby is expected and what will change in concrete child-sized terms. → *Expect:* the child knows a baby is joining the family.
2. **Name mixed feelings.** Say excitement, worry, jealousy, and annoyance can all happen. → *Expect:* the child does not feel required to be happy all the time.
3. **Practice baby safety.** Show gentle touch, hand washing, and adult-only carrying rules with a doll or stuffed toy. → *Expect:* the child knows what help is safe.
4. **Protect routines.** Keep bedtime, school, meals, and one-on-one time as predictable as possible. → *Expect:* the child has anchors during the transition.
5. **Give a real role.** Offer simple jobs such as choosing a song, bringing a diaper, or greeting visitors. → *Expect:* the child can contribute without being parentified.
6. **Plan the first meeting.** Arrange a calm introduction and let the child approach at their own pace. → *Expect:* the first contact feels supervised and unforced.

## Decision points

- If the child regresses in sleep, toileting, or clinginess → respond with reassurance and routine rather than punishment.
- If the birth, adoption, or medical situation is uncertain → give truthful limited information and update when facts change.
- If anxiety, aggression, or sadness persists → seek pediatrician, counselor, or family therapist guidance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Forced affection:** detect pressure to kiss, hold, or love the baby immediately → recover by allowing distance and supervised choice.
- **F2 Lost attention:** detect behavior spikes when adults focus on the baby → recover by scheduling brief reliable one-on-one time.
- **F3 Unsafe helping:** detect lifting, rough touch, or feeding attempts → recover by restating adult-only tasks and offering a safe job.

## Verification

The child can describe the new sibling in basic terms, knows at least two baby-safety rules, and has one predictable adult connection during the transition.

## Variations

- `toddler`: use short explanations, picture books, and repeated practice.
- `older-child`: invite questions about logistics and privacy.
- `adoption-or-foster`: use accurate family language and avoid promises that are not certain.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, centered on emotional security and infant safety. Keep medical and adoption details age-appropriate, supervise all sibling contact, and seek professional help for intense or persistent distress.
