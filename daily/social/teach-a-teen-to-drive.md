## Goal

Teach a teen driving skills through legal, supervised, gradual practice with safety as the first measure of progress.

## Preconditions

- The teen has the required permit or legal authorization for supervised practice.
- The supervising adult meets local legal requirements and can stay calm.
- The car is insured, roadworthy, and appropriate for practice.

## Steps

1. **Review legal limits.** Confirm permit rules, required supervisor, passenger limits, hours, and practice log needs. → *Expect:* the session is lawful before the car moves.
2. **Set the vehicle.** Adjust seat, mirrors, belt, phone storage, and controls while parked. → *Expect:* the teen can see, reach pedals, and avoid phone distraction.
3. **Start in an empty lot.** Practice starting, stopping, turning, parking, and scanning at very low speed. → *Expect:* basic control improves away from traffic.
4. **Add quiet streets.** Move to simple roads only after smooth braking and lane position are reliable. → *Expect:* the teen handles low-risk traffic calmly.
5. **Debrief immediately.** Discuss one strength and one specific next skill after each drive. → *Expect:* feedback is clear and limited.
6. **Increase complexity slowly.** Add rain, night, highways, or busy traffic only when earlier skills are consistent and legal. → *Expect:* difficulty rises by plan, not pressure.

## Decision points

- If either person is angry, tired, impaired, or panicked → end the session before driving continues.
- If local law requires certified instruction → schedule it and use home practice only as allowed.
- If attention, vision, anxiety, or motor concerns affect driving → seek medical or driver-training evaluation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unsafe escalation:** detect practice moving to traffic before basic control → recover by returning to parking-lot drills.
- **F2 Phone distraction:** detect a visible or buzzing phone → recover by silencing and storing it before moving.
- **F3 Adult yelling:** detect panic feedback or insults → recover by pulling over safely and ending or resetting the session.

## Verification

The teen practiced within legal limits, completed the planned skill safely, and logged or discussed one concrete next driving goal.

## Variations

- `manual-transmission`: add clutch practice in a flat empty lot before roads.
- `winter-driving`: use professional instruction or very controlled conditions.
- `anxious-teen`: shorten sessions and repeat familiar routes before adding complexity.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because driving errors can seriously injure people. Follow local law, avoid distraction, never practice while impaired, and use professional driving instruction when skills or emotions exceed home supervision.
