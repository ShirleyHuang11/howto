## Goal

Help a child prepare for exams with structure, rest, emotional support, and honest study habits.

## Preconditions

- Exam dates, subjects, formats, and allowed materials are known.
- The child has notes, books, study guides, or access to teacher resources.
- A quiet study space and basic schedule are available.

## Steps

1. **Map the exams.** Write each exam date, topic, and weight on a visible calendar. → *Expect:* the child sees what is coming.
2. **Break study blocks down.** Choose short sessions for one subject, skill, or chapter at a time. → *Expect:* studying has a doable next action.
3. **Use active practice.** Have the child retrieve facts, solve problems, explain concepts, or take practice questions. → *Expect:* the child tests memory instead of only rereading.
4. **Protect sleep and food.** Set bedtime, meals, water, and movement breaks during exam week. → *Expect:* the child's body is supported for learning.
5. **Plan test-day logistics.** Pack supplies, charge devices, check room or transport, and set alarms. → *Expect:* preventable stress is reduced.
6. **Debrief without shame.** After each exam, note what helped and what to change next time. → *Expect:* the child learns from the process without panic.

## Decision points

- If the child is overwhelmed → reduce the plan to the next 20 minutes and one subject.
- If anxiety causes sleep loss, panic, vomiting, or school refusal → seek school counselor, pediatrician, or mental health support.
- If accommodations may be needed → contact the school early with documentation if available.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Cramming collapse:** detect late-night panic and poor focus → recover by prioritizing sleep and the highest-yield review.
- **F2 Passive studying:** detect highlighting without recall → recover by switching to practice questions or teach-back.
- **F3 Grade fixation:** detect fear of worth depending on scores → recover by emphasizing effort, strategy, and support.

## Verification

The child has a dated study plan, completed at least one active practice block per exam, and has test-day materials ready.

## Variations

- `elementary`: use short review games and parent-read directions.
- `middle-school`: teach planner use and spaced study.
- `high-school`: include practice tests, office hours, and workload triage.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, with mental health important. Do not threaten or shame over grades; seek professional help if exam stress causes panic, self-harm talk, or persistent physical symptoms.
