## Goal

Help a child move through anger, fear, sadness, or overwhelm with safety, connection, and a simple regulation plan.

## Preconditions

- The child is physically safe or can be moved to a safer place.
- You can stay calm enough to guide without shouting or shaming.
- The situation is not a medical emergency or immediate threat.

## Steps

1. **Make the space safe.** Move sharp objects, separate kids who might hurt each other, and lower noise or crowding. → *Expect:* the child has room to be upset without causing harm.
2. **Get low and use few words.** Speak calmly at the child's level and name what you see: "You are really mad." → *Expect:* the child hears connection rather than a lecture.
3. **Validate the feeling, not unsafe behavior.** Say the feeling is allowed while holding the limit on hitting, throwing, or running away. → *Expect:* the child knows both the emotion and boundary are real.
4. **Offer a regulation choice.** [BRANCH: comfort | space | movement] offer a hug, quiet corner, breathing, wall pushes, water, or a short walk. → *Expect:* the child can choose one next action or reject fewer options.
5. **Wait out the peak.** Stay nearby, reduce demands, and repeat the limit only when needed. → *Expect:* crying, yelling, or shutdown begins to decrease.
6. **Reconnect before problem-solving.** After the child calms, briefly describe what happened and ask what would help next time. → *Expect:* the child can listen and answer in simple words.
7. **Repair harm.** Help the child apologize, clean up, check on someone, or try again without making shame the lesson. → *Expect:* the child practices responsibility after regulation returns.

## Decision points

- The child may injure themselves or others → get immediate help and prioritize safety over teaching.
- Hunger, sleep, sensory overload, or transition stress is likely → address that need before reasoning.
- Big emotions are frequent, extreme, or developmentally concerning → consult a pediatrician, school counselor, or child therapist.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adult escalates:** detect yelling, threats, or sarcasm → pause, lower your voice, and state one simple boundary.
- **F2 Too many choices:** detect the child getting more frantic → offer only two concrete options.
- **F3 Problem-solving too soon:** detect renewed crying or refusal → return to calming and try later.
- **F4 Unsafe behavior continues:** detect hitting, biting, bolting, or self-harm → block calmly, increase distance, and get support.

## Verification

The child is physically safe, intensity has decreased, a boundary was held, and one repair or next-time strategy was practiced after calm returned.

## Variations

- `toddler`: use fewer words, more sensory support, and shorter repair.
- `school-age`: invite the child to name triggers and choose a coping tool.
- `public-place`: move to a quieter edge, car, hallway, or bench before teaching.

## Safety & privacy

Low to medium risk depending on aggression or self-harm. Do not shame, film, or publicly expose the child during distress; seek urgent help if safety cannot be maintained.
