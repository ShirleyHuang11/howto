## Goal

Reduce mealtime conflict while helping a child gradually accept a wider range of foods.

## Preconditions

- The child has regular meals and snacks rather than constant grazing.
- At least one usually accepted food can be served with meals.
- Growth, swallowing, sensory, or medical concerns have been noted for a pediatrician if present.

## Steps

1. **Set the adult job.** Decide what food is offered, where meals happen, and when the meal ends. → *Expect:* the meal has clear structure.
2. **Set the child job.** Let the child decide whether and how much to eat from what is served. → *Expect:* pressure drops and appetite cues stay respected.
3. **Serve one safe food.** Put one familiar accepted item beside small portions of family food. → *Expect:* the child has something available without a separate rescue meal.
4. **Use tiny exposures.** Invite looking, smelling, touching, licking, or one small taste without requiring swallowing. → *Expect:* new food becomes less threatening over time.
5. **Keep language neutral.** Avoid bribing, shaming, or labeling the child as picky during the meal. → *Expect:* attention shifts away from power struggles.
6. **Review patterns weekly.** Track accepted foods, refused textures, constipation, fatigue, and growth concerns. → *Expect:* you can distinguish normal selectiveness from a bigger issue.

## Decision points

- If the child eats fewer than a small set of foods, loses weight, chokes, vomits, or fears eating → consult a pediatrician or feeding therapist.
- If snacks replace meals → set predictable snack times and avoid filling drinks before dinner.
- If the child tries a food once → keep offering it without making it a performance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pressure spiral:** detect bargaining, threats, or tears → recover by ending comments and returning to neutral meal structure.
- **F2 Short-order cooking:** detect a new meal made after refusal → recover by including one safe food in the planned meal next time.
- **F3 Hidden medical issue:** detect pain, gagging, constipation, or poor growth → recover by seeking pediatric assessment.

## Verification

Meals follow a predictable structure, the child has one accepted option, and at least one low-pressure exposure to a nonpreferred food happens without coercion.

## Variations

- `toddler`: use very small portions and expect appetite to vary day to day.
- `sensory-sensitive`: separate textures and allow smelling or touching before tasting.
- `teen`: involve the teen in planning one balanced meal or grocery choice.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk unless nutrition, growth, choking, or anxiety concerns appear. Do not shame body size or appetite, and seek professional help for persistent restriction or distress.
