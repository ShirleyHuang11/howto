## Goal

Teach children basic money concepts through honest, age-appropriate conversations and small real choices.

## Preconditions

- The child is old enough to understand simple wants, needs, saving, or tradeoffs.
- The adult can discuss money without exposing private account details or adult anxiety.
- A small example budget, allowance, or shopping choice is available.

## Steps

1. **Pick one concept.** Choose needs versus wants, saving, spending, giving, earning, or waiting. → *Expect:* the conversation has a clear focus.
2. **Use a real example.** Show a grocery choice, toy budget, coin jar, or simple allowance plan. → *Expect:* money is connected to something visible.
3. **Explain tradeoffs calmly.** Say what the money can do and what choosing it means giving up. → *Expect:* the child hears limits without fear.
4. **Let the child decide small.** Offer a safe choice within a fixed amount. → *Expect:* the child practices agency and consequence.
5. **Reflect afterward.** Ask what felt worth it, what they might save for, or what they learned. → *Expect:* the child links choice to outcome.
6. **Repeat regularly.** Use brief everyday moments rather than one large lecture. → *Expect:* money skills build over time.

## Decision points

- If the child asks about family hardship → answer truthfully but reassure them that adults are responsible for adult bills.
- If spending becomes secretive or compulsive → set clearer limits and consider professional support if anxiety or stealing appears.
- If online purchases are involved → require adult approval and explain saved payment risks.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adult overshares:** detect detailed debt, income, or panic given to the child → recover by returning to age-appropriate facts and reassurance.
- **F2 Shame language:** detect insults about being greedy or spoiled → recover by naming the skill being practiced.
- **F3 No real boundary:** detect repeated extra purchases after the budget is spent → recover by holding the limit next time.

## Verification

The child can explain the chosen money concept and has made or observed one small tradeoff within a clear boundary.

## Variations

- `young-child`: use coins, jars, and concrete categories.
- `preteen`: use allowance, saving goals, and price comparison.
- `teen`: include budgeting, debit cards, work income, scams, and credit basics.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, centered on family privacy and online spending. Do not burden children with adult financial stress, and protect bank, card, and account information.
