## Goal

Introduce first foods safely while preserving milk feeds and helping the baby explore taste, texture, and self-feeding.

## Preconditions

- The baby shows readiness, such as good head control, sitting with support, interest in food, and loss of tongue-thrust reflex.
- A stable high chair, clean hands, bib, small spoon, and soft age-appropriate food are ready.
- You know choking first aid and the difference between gagging and choking.

## Steps

1. **Choose a calm time.** Offer solids when the baby is awake, supported, and not frantic with hunger. → *Expect:* the baby can explore without distress.
2. **Seat the baby upright.** Buckle the high chair and keep the baby facing you. → *Expect:* the airway and posture are safer for swallowing.
3. **Start with a tiny portion.** Offer soft mashed food, puree, or safe finger food in a pea-sized amount. → *Expect:* the baby can taste without a large mouthful.
4. **Follow the baby's cues.** Pause for turning away, closed lips, coughing, gagging, or fussing. → *Expect:* eating stays responsive rather than forced.
5. **Introduce allergens thoughtfully.** Offer common allergens one at a time in safe forms after discussing special risks with the pediatrician. → *Expect:* you can notice and respond to any reaction.
6. **Keep milk central.** Continue breast milk or formula as the main nutrition unless the pediatrician gives different advice. → *Expect:* solids add practice without replacing needed calories too quickly.

## Decision points

- If the baby has eczema, known allergy, prematurity, feeding problems, or poor growth → ask the pediatrician before changing foods.
- If the baby gags but breathes and recovers → stay calm and use smaller or softer pieces next time.
- If choking, blue color, wheezing, facial swelling, or repeated vomiting occurs → treat as urgent and seek medical help.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Choking hazard:** detect round, hard, sticky, or large food pieces → recover by cutting, mashing, thinning, or choosing another food.
- **F2 Forced feeding:** detect crying, head turning, or clamped lips → recover by stopping and trying another day.
- **F3 Reaction signs:** detect hives, swelling, breathing changes, or repeated vomiting → recover by following emergency guidance and contacting a clinician.

## Verification

The baby sits upright, samples a safe texture, remains supervised the whole time, and milk feeds continue normally.

## Variations

- `puree-led`: use a spoon and gradually thicken textures as skills improve.
- `baby-led`: use soft graspable foods that squish easily and avoid small hard pieces.
- `allergy-risk`: make an individualized plan with the pediatrician or allergist.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from choking and allergic reactions. Supervise every bite, avoid honey before age one, and seek professional guidance for feeding delays, poor growth, or suspected allergy.
