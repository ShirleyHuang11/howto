## Goal

Teach a child to balance, steer, brake, and ride a bicycle with confidence and safe habits.

## Preconditions

- The bike fits, brakes work, tires are inflated, and the child has a correctly fitted helmet.
- A flat open area away from traffic, parked cars, and steep slopes is available.
- The child is willing to practice and can stop when tired or frightened.

## Steps

1. **Fit the gear.** Adjust the helmet level, tighten straps, and set the seat so feet can touch the ground for balance practice. → *Expect:* the child can sit and stop without tipping immediately.
2. **Practice braking.** Have the child walk the bike and squeeze or backpedal the brake repeatedly. → *Expect:* the child can stop on command.
3. **Glide first.** Remove pedals if practical or have the child push off and coast with feet lifted briefly. → *Expect:* the child feels balance before pedaling.
4. **Add pedaling.** Start with one pedal high, push off, and pedal while looking forward. → *Expect:* the bike rolls several yards under the child's power.
5. **Practice turns and stops.** Set wide markers and have the child ride, turn, brake, and put a foot down. → *Expect:* steering and stopping become deliberate.
6. **End before exhaustion.** Stop after a good attempt or when frustration rises. → *Expect:* the child wants to try again later.

## Decision points

- If the child panics when held from behind → switch back to gliding without grabbing the seat.
- If crashes repeat → check bike fit, speed, surface, and fatigue before continuing.
- If the child has balance, vision, motor, or anxiety concerns → consider pediatric, occupational therapy, or adaptive cycling guidance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Helmet misfit:** detect sliding, exposed forehead, or loose straps → recover by readjusting before riding.
- **F2 Adult overholds:** detect the child leaning into the adult instead of balancing → recover by using shorter light touches and more gliding.
- **F3 Unsafe practice area:** detect cars, dogs, crowds, or steep pavement → recover by moving to a calmer flat place.

## Verification

The child can start, ride straight for several yards, make a wide turn, brake, and stop with the helmet still fitted correctly.

## Variations

- `balance-bike`: skip pedal removal and progress from long glides to a pedal bike.
- `training-wheels`: use them for comfort, then raise or remove them when balance practice begins.
- `older-child`: preserve dignity by practicing in a private low-traffic space.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from falls and traffic. Use a helmet every ride, avoid roads until skills are reliable, and seek professional guidance for repeated injuries or motor concerns.
