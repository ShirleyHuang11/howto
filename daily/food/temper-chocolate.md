## Goal

Chocolate is melted and crystallized so it sets shiny, firm, and snappy at room temperature.

## Preconditions

- Real couverture or bar chocolate with cocoa butter, clean dry bowl, spatula, thermometer, parchment, and optional unmelted seed chocolate.
- No water near the chocolate; a few drops can seize it.

## Steps

1. **Chop chocolate.** Chop 500 g chocolate into small even pieces and reserve 125 g as seed. → *Expect:* pieces melt evenly and seed is dry and unmelted.
2. **Melt gently.** Warm 375 g chocolate over barely simmering water or in short microwave bursts. → *Expect:* chocolate is fluid with no steam or water contact.
3. **Reach the melt temperature.** Heat dark chocolate to 45-50 °C, milk to 40-45 °C, or white to 38-43 °C. → *Expect:* all visible pieces are melted and thermometer is in range.
4. **Cool with seed.** Remove from heat and stir in reserved seed gradually. → *Expect:* temperature drops and chocolate thickens slightly while remaining smooth.
5. **Stop at working temperature.** Use dark at 31-32 °C, milk at 29-30 °C, or white at 28-29 °C. → *Expect:* chocolate ribbons from the spatula and does not feel hot.
6. **Test the temper.** Spread a thin streak on parchment and wait 3-5 minutes. [BRANCH: sets shiny | stays dull or tacky] → *Expect:* properly tempered chocolate sets with a satin shine and clean edge.
7. **Use immediately.** Dip, mold, or spread while maintaining the working temperature. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* once decorations set in poor temper, they must be remelted to fix bloom. → *Expect:* coated pieces begin setting within minutes at cool room temperature.

## Decision points

- Test is dull or streaky → rewarm 1-2 °C if too thick, or add more seed and stir if too warm.
- Kitchen is hot above 24 °C → work in a cooler room or set pieces briefly in a wine fridge, not a humid refrigerator.
- Using compound coating → do not temper; melt according to package directions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Seized chocolate:** detect sudden grainy paste → use it for ganache with warm cream, or start over for tempering.
- **F2 Fat bloom:** detect gray streaks after setting → remelt completely and temper again with accurate temperatures.
- **F3 Overthick chocolate:** detect heavy drag while dipping → warm briefly to the top of the working range.
- **F4 Scorched chocolate:** detect burnt smell or gritty dark flecks → discard scorched portion and use lower heat.

## Verification

A parchment test sets within 5 minutes, releases cleanly, looks shiny, and snaps rather than bends at cool room temperature.

## Variations

- `tabling`: cool two-thirds of melted chocolate on marble, then return it to the bowl.
- `small-batch`: use the same temperatures with 100-200 g chocolate, stirring more often.
- `cocoa-butter-silk`: add 1 percent tempered cocoa butter silk at about 34 °C, then cool to working temperature.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, mostly burns and water contamination. Keep steam away from chocolate, use dry tools, and avoid overheating sugar and cocoa solids.
