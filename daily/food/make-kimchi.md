## Goal

Napa cabbage is salted, seasoned with chile paste, packed, fermented briefly, and refrigerated as kimchi.

## Preconditions

- Napa cabbage, coarse salt, gochugaru, garlic, ginger, scallions, optional fish sauce or salted shrimp, clean jar, gloves, and refrigerator space.
- A bowl large enough to salt and rinse cabbage.

## Steps

1. **Cut the cabbage.** Quarter 1 large napa cabbage lengthwise and cut into bite-size pieces or leave quarters attached at the core. → *Expect:* cabbage pieces are uniform enough to salt evenly.
2. **Salt the cabbage.** Toss with 50-70 g coarse salt, getting salt between thicker leaves. → *Expect:* cabbage is visibly salted and begins to wilt.
3. **Rest and turn.** Let stand 1.5-2 hours, turning every 30 minutes. → *Expect:* stems bend without snapping and liquid collects in the bowl.
4. **Rinse and drain.** Rinse cabbage 2-3 times, then drain 15-30 minutes. → *Expect:* cabbage tastes seasoned but not sharply salty.
5. **Make the paste.** Mix 3-5 tbsp gochugaru, 4 minced garlic cloves, 1 tsp grated ginger, 1-2 tbsp fish sauce, and optional sugar or rice paste. → *Expect:* paste is red, spreadable, and pungent.
6. **Coat cabbage.** Wear gloves and massage paste through cabbage and scallions. → *Expect:* every piece is lightly coated red with no dry white clumps.
7. **Pack the jar.** Press kimchi into a clean jar, leaving 3 cm headspace and pushing liquid above the vegetables. → *Expect:* few air pockets and vegetables sit under their own brine.
8. **Ferment briefly.** Leave at cool room temperature 12-48 hours, opening daily if sealed. [BRANCH: fresh kimchi | sour kimchi] ⚠️ *Irreversible:* gas pressure can crack jars; vent sealed jars. → *Expect:* bubbles rise and aroma turns tangy.
9. **Refrigerate.** Move kimchi to the refrigerator when it tastes right. → *Expect:* fermentation slows and kimchi remains crisp and bright red.

## Decision points

- Cabbage tastes too salty after rinsing → soak in cold water 10 minutes, drain, and taste again.
- Want vegan kimchi → replace fish sauce with soy sauce or miso, but keep salt level adequate.
- Jar fills to the top → remove some kimchi; fermentation needs headspace.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mold:** detect fuzzy or colored growth on the surface → discard the batch and sanitize the jar.
- **F2 Bitter raw garlic burn:** detect harsh paste after mixing → add a little sugar or grated pear and let it ferment longer.
- **F3 Mushy kimchi:** detect limp, soft cabbage → ferment cooler and shorten room-temperature time next batch.
- **F4 Explosive overflow:** detect brine leaking or bulging lid → open over a sink, clean the jar, and refrigerate sooner.

## Verification

Kimchi is refrigerated, vegetables are coated and mostly under brine, flavor is salty-sour and chile-forward, and there is no fuzzy mold, rotten smell, or slimy texture.

## Variations

- `kkakdugi`: use cubed daikon radish and salt until flexible.
- `fresh-geotjeori`: skip room-temperature fermentation and serve immediately.
- `seafood`: add raw oysters only for immediate, refrigerated consumption by people who accept raw-shellfish risk.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from fermentation and allergens. Keep jars vented, refrigerate after souring, disclose fish or shellfish ingredients, and discard kimchi with mold, putrid odor, pink growth, or slime.
