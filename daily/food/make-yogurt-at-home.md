## Goal

Milk is heated, inoculated with live yogurt culture, incubated until tangy and set, then chilled safely.

## Preconditions

- Pasteurized milk, 2 tbsp plain yogurt with live active cultures per 1 L milk, a pot, thermometer, clean jar, and warm incubation spot.
- Clean hands, washed utensils, and refrigerator space.

## Steps

1. **Clean the equipment.** Wash the pot, jar, spoon, and thermometer with hot soapy water and rinse well. → *Expect:* no visible food residue and equipment is air-drying or freshly dried.
2. **Heat the milk.** Warm 1 L milk to 82-85 °C, stirring to prevent scorching. → *Expect:* steam rises, small bubbles form at the edge, and the thermometer reads at least 82 °C.
3. **Hold the heat.** Keep milk at 82-85 °C for 5 minutes. → *Expect:* milk smells sweet, not scorched, and stays below a rolling boil.
4. **Cool to inoculation temperature.** Set the pot in cool water or leave it uncovered until milk reaches 43-46 °C. → *Expect:* milk feels warm but not hot, and the thermometer is under 46 °C.
5. **Add starter.** Whisk a ladle of warm milk into the yogurt, then stir that mixture back into the pot. → *Expect:* starter disappears smoothly with no large yogurt clumps.
6. **Incubate undisturbed.** Pour into the clean jar, cover, and hold at 40-45 °C for 6-10 hours. [BRANCH: mild yogurt | tangier yogurt] → *Expect:* surface sets like soft custard and smells cleanly sour.
7. **Chill promptly.** Move the jar to the refrigerator when set. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not taste yogurt that smells rotten, moldy, or alcoholic; discard it. → *Expect:* yogurt cools below 4 °C within a few hours and firms further.
8. **Save starter if desired.** Reserve a clean spoonful before serving from the jar. → *Expect:* starter is separated in a clean container without crumbs or saliva contact.

## Decision points

- Milk is ultrapasteurized → use it if needed, but expect a softer set than regular pasteurized milk.
- Incubation spot is below 40 °C → use a yogurt maker, warm oven with light on, insulated cooler, or heating pad.
- Yogurt is set before 10 hours → chill it; longer incubation makes it more sour.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Runny yogurt:** thermometer was inaccurate, starter was weak, or incubation was too cool → re-incubate up to 4 more hours if it smells clean, or use as cultured milk.
- **F2 Grainy texture:** milk overheated after starter was added → whisk smooth for sauce and keep the next batch below 46 °C before inoculating.
- **F3 Scorched flavor:** detect brown bits or burnt smell → strain immediately without scraping the pot and lower heat next batch.
- **F4 Unsafe fermentation:** detect mold, pink/orange discoloration, gas, or putrid smell → discard the entire batch and sanitize the jar.

## Verification

After chilling, the yogurt is below 4 °C, smells cleanly tangy, holds a spoon mark, and has no mold, discoloration, or off gas.

## Variations

- `greek-style`: strain chilled yogurt through cloth 2-8 hours until thick.
- `thermos`: preheat a thermos with hot water, empty it, add inoculated milk, and close tightly.
- `plant-milk`: use a culture compatible with the milk and add a tested thickener because many plant milks will not set alone.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from warm dairy fermentation. Start with pasteurized milk, clean equipment, correct temperatures, and refrigeration; discard any batch with mold, rotten odor, unexpected color, or swelling.
