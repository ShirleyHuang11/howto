## Goal

Egg whites and sugar are whipped into a stable foam with the right peak texture for topping, folding, or baking.

## Preconditions

- Fresh eggs or pasteurized egg whites, sugar, clean grease-free bowl, whisk or mixer, and optional cream of tartar.
- No yolk in the whites; fat prevents foam formation.

## Steps

1. **Clean the bowl.** Wipe bowl and whisk with vinegar or lemon juice if greasy. → *Expect:* surfaces feel clean and dry.
2. **Separate eggs.** Place whites in the bowl with no yolk. → *Expect:* whites are clear and free of yellow streaks.
3. **Foam the whites.** Beat on medium until bubbly, then add a pinch of cream of tartar if using. → *Expect:* foam covers the surface with small bubbles.
4. **Add sugar slowly.** Sprinkle in sugar 1 tbsp at a time while beating. → *Expect:* foam thickens and looks glossy instead of grainy.
5. **Whip to target peaks.** [BRANCH: soft peaks | stiff peaks] Continue until peaks curl softly or stand upright. → *Expect:* meringue holds the chosen peak shape.
6. **Check sugar dissolution.** Rub a little meringue between fingers. → *Expect:* texture feels mostly smooth, not sandy.
7. **Use promptly.** Spread, pipe, fold, or bake according to the dessert. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* overwhipped dry foam collapses when folded; stop as soon as target peaks appear. → *Expect:* meringue is glossy, cohesive, and holds volume.

## Decision points

- Serving meringue without baking → use Swiss, Italian, or pasteurized egg whites for safer eating.
- Folding into batter → stop at medium-stiff peaks so foam can incorporate without breaking.
- Humid weather → expect slower drying and possible weeping.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Whites will not whip:** detect thin bubbles after several minutes → check for yolk or grease and restart in a clean bowl.
- **F2 Grainy meringue:** detect sugar crystals → beat longer or use superfine sugar next time.
- **F3 Overwhipped foam:** detect dry clumps and liquid at bottom → fold in one fresh white briefly, or restart for delicate uses.
- **F4 Unsafe raw egg:** detect unbaked meringue made with shell eggs → bake fully or remake with pasteurized whites.

## Verification

Meringue is glossy, smooth, holds the required peak, has no liquid pooling, and is baked or made with pasteurized whites when served uncooked.

## Variations

- `french`: raw whites and sugar whipped together, then baked or folded.
- `swiss`: heat whites and sugar over a water bath to 71 °C, then whip.
- `italian`: pour hot sugar syrup into whipping whites for a stable cooked foam.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from raw eggs and hot syrup for cooked styles. Use pasteurized whites for uncooked service, avoid shell fragments, and handle hot syrup with care.
