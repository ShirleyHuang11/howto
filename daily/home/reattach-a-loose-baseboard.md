## Goal

A loose baseboard is refastened to the wall, gaps are closed, and the trim is ready for touch-up.

## Preconditions

- You have finish nails or brad nailer, hammer, nail set, stud finder, wood filler, painter's caulk, and touch-up paint.
- The wall behind the baseboard is dry and not crumbling.
- Call a pro if looseness is caused by water damage, pests, or a bowed wall that will not accept fasteners.

## Steps

1. **Inspect the gap.** Pull gently on the baseboard and look behind it with a flashlight. → *Expect:* the loose section and any damage are visible.
2. **Find studs.** Mark studs above the trim with painter's tape. → *Expect:* fastener locations align with framing.
3. **Clean behind trim.** Vacuum loose dust from the gap without prying the board farther out. → *Expect:* the baseboard can sit flat against the wall.
4. **Press the board in place.** Hold or clamp the baseboard tight to the wall and floor line. → *Expect:* the gap closes along the loose section.
5. **Fasten into studs.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* nail through the baseboard into studs after confirming no hidden cable or pipe risk. → *Expect:* nail heads seat close to the trim face.
6. **Set and fill nails.** Use a nail set, then fill nail holes with wood filler. → *Expect:* nail heads are below the surface and holes are filled.
7. **Caulk the top gap.** Apply a thin paintable caulk line where the baseboard meets the wall. → *Expect:* the wall-trim seam is continuous.
8. **Touch up finish.** After filler and caulk dry, sand lightly and paint as needed. → *Expect:* the repair blends with surrounding trim.

## Decision points

- Studs are not reachable at the loose spot → use construction adhesive plus nails into the nearest studs.
- Baseboard is split → glue and clamp the split or replace the board.
- Wall or trim is damp → fix the moisture source before reattaching.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Nail misses stud:** trim remains loose → pull the nail, find the stud, and refasten.
- **F2 Caulk cracks:** gap reopens after drying → refasten tighter before recaulking.
- **F3 Trim bows out:** board will not sit flat → check for debris, warped trim, or wall irregularity.

## Verification

The baseboard does not move when pressed, nail holes are filled, and the top seam has no open gap along the repaired section.

## Variations

- Masonry walls: use construction adhesive or masonry-compatible fasteners.
- Stained trim: use color-matched putty instead of paintable filler.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk: nails can hit hidden utilities. Use short finish nails, aim for studs, and stop if you suspect wiring or plumbing behind the wall.
