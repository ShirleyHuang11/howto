## Goal

Teach a puppy where to toilet by using supervision, frequent trips, rewards, and calm cleanup instead of punishment.

## Preconditions

- A legal toilet area, leash, treats, enzymatic cleaner, and confinement area or crate are available.
- The puppy is old enough to eliminate independently and has access to water and appropriate meals.
- Diarrhea, straining, blood, or excessive urination has veterinary attention.

## Steps

1. **Set the schedule.** Take the puppy out after waking, eating, drinking, playing, and at least every few hours. → *Expect:* the puppy reaches the toilet area before most accidents happen.
2. **Go to the same spot.** Walk calmly to the chosen area and wait without play until toileting happens or a few minutes pass. → *Expect:* the location becomes linked with elimination.
3. **Reward immediately.** Mark and treat within seconds after the puppy finishes outside. → *Expect:* the puppy learns that toileting there pays.
4. **Supervise indoors.** Use leash tethering, gates, or a crate sized for resting when you cannot watch. → *Expect:* sniffing, circling, or wandering can be interrupted early.
5. **Clean accidents calmly.** Interrupt only if caught in the act, take the puppy out, and clean with enzymatic cleaner. → *Expect:* odor is removed and the puppy is not frightened.
6. **Track progress.** Record accident times, successful trips, meals, and naps. → *Expect:* the schedule adjusts to the puppy's real bladder and bowel pattern.

## Decision points

- If accidents cluster at one time → add a planned trip before that time.
- If the puppy soils the crate repeatedly → check crate size, schedule, stress, and health.
- If urination is very frequent, painful, bloody, or paired with excessive thirst → call a vet.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Punishment setback:** puppy hides to toilet after scolding → stop punishment, supervise better, and reward outdoor success.
- **F2 Too much freedom:** accidents happen in distant rooms → reduce access until the schedule is reliable.
- **F3 Medical signs:** diarrhea, blood, straining, or sudden regression appears → schedule veterinary care.

## Verification

The puppy toilets in the chosen area most of the time, accidents are decreasing on the log, and indoor freedom is increased only after reliable supervised success.

## Variations

- `apartment`: use frequent elevator planning or a legal balcony/pad transition plan.
- `small-breed`: expect smaller bladder capacity and more frequent trips.
- `bad-weather`: keep the same reward rules and use safe footing.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if hygiene is maintained and punishment is avoided. See a vet for urinary pain, blood, diarrhea, vomiting, lethargy, excessive thirst, or sudden regression after progress.
