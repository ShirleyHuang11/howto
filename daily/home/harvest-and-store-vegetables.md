## Goal

Harvest vegetables at good eating quality and store each crop in conditions that preserve flavor, texture, and safety.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: clean basket, scissors or pruners, labels, towels, storage bags or containers, and refrigerator or cool storage space.
- You can identify ripe or mature crops and avoid harvesting from visibly contaminated soil or water.
- Produce will be handled with clean hands and clean containers.

## Steps

1. **Harvest during the cool part of day.** Pick in the morning or evening when crops are hydrated and heat stress is low. → *Expect:* produce is crisp and not sun-warmed.
2. **Use clean cuts.** Snip stems for greens, herbs, peppers, squash, and tomatoes rather than tearing plants. → *Expect:* plants and harvested vegetables have minimal damage.
3. **Sort as you pick.** Separate bruised, pest-damaged, or diseased produce from sound produce. → *Expect:* storage containers hold only good-quality vegetables.
4. **Brush off soil.** Remove loose dirt outdoors and avoid washing storage crops unless needed. → *Expect:* produce is cleaner without added surface moisture.
5. **Cool perishable crops.** Refrigerate leafy greens, peas, beans, broccoli, cucumbers, and herbs soon after harvest. → *Expect:* tender vegetables stop wilting.
6. **Keep dry-storage crops dry.** Cure onions, garlic, potatoes, winter squash, and sweet potatoes as appropriate before longer storage. → *Expect:* skins or necks are dry enough for storage.
7. **Match storage conditions.** Store tomatoes at room temperature until fully ripe, greens in humid refrigeration, and roots in cool humid conditions. → *Expect:* each crop is placed where it keeps best.
8. **Check stored produce.** Inspect every few days and remove soft, moldy, or leaking items. → *Expect:* one failing vegetable does not spoil the rest.

## Decision points

- Crop is leafy or tender → cool quickly and use within days.
- Crop needs curing → hold in a warm dry airy place before storage.
- Rain or irrigation just wet the crop → let surfaces dry before storing when food safety allows.
- Frost is forecast → harvest tender crops first and protect hardy crops if leaving them outside.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Greens wilt fast:** detect limp leaves soon after harvest → rehydrate briefly in cold water, dry, and store in a humid bag.
- **F2 Mold develops in storage:** detect fuzzy growth or soft spots → discard affected items and improve drying or airflow.
- **F3 Tomatoes lose flavor:** detect mealy texture after chilling → store uncut ripe tomatoes at room temperature when safe and use chilled ones in cooked dishes.
- **F4 Potatoes turn green:** detect green skins or bitter taste risk → keep potatoes in dark storage and discard green portions heavily.

## Verification

Harvested vegetables are cleanly cut, sorted, placed in crop-appropriate storage, labeled if needed, and checked so damaged produce is not mixed with sound produce.

## Variations

- `root-cellar`: use bins of slightly damp sand or perforated containers for roots where temperatures stay cool.
- `small-harvest`: use breathable refrigerator bags and harvest only what will be eaten soon.
- `seed-saving`: keep seed crops separate from eating harvests and label varieties.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wash produce before eating, discard produce contaminated by floodwater or animal waste, and use clean tools and containers to reduce foodborne illness risk.
