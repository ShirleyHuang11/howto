## Goal

Plant fall-planted spring bulbs at the correct depth and spacing so they root before winter and bloom reliably in spring.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: firm spring-blooming bulbs, trowel or bulb planter, compost if needed, labels, mulch, and water.
- The planting site drains well and receives sun before trees fully leaf out, if growing sun-loving bulbs.
- Bulbs are planted in fall when soil has cooled but before the ground is frozen.

## Steps

1. **Inspect the bulbs.** Keep firm bulbs and discard soft, moldy, or badly damaged ones. → *Expect:* only viable bulbs remain for planting.
2. **Choose a draining site.** Pick a bed where winter water will not stand around bulbs. → *Expect:* the planting area is not soggy.
3. **Dig to proper depth.** Make holes about two to three times as deep as the bulb is tall unless the label says otherwise. → *Expect:* holes match bulb size.
4. **Set bulbs point up.** Place pointed ends or shoots upward and root plates downward; set uncertain bulbs sideways. → *Expect:* each bulb is oriented for easy sprouting.
5. **Space for the display.** Plant in clusters or drifts with spacing from the package, avoiding straight single-file rows unless desired. → *Expect:* bulbs have room and a natural-looking layout.
6. **Backfill and firm.** Cover with soil and press gently to remove air pockets without compacting hard. → *Expect:* bulbs are covered at an even grade.
7. **Water once.** Water the planting area thoroughly after backfilling. → *Expect:* soil settles around the bulbs.
8. **Mulch and mark.** Add light mulch and labels so the area is not dug up before spring. → *Expect:* bulbs are protected and their location is known.

## Decision points

- Wildlife digs bulbs → use wire mesh over the planting area or choose less attractive bulbs such as daffodils.
- Soil is heavy clay → amend lightly and plant in raised areas rather than deep wet pockets.
- Bulbs arrive late → plant as long as the ground can be worked.
- Spring foliage yellows after bloom → leave it until it withers naturally before cutting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bulbs rot:** detect soft bulbs or no spring emergence in wet soil → improve drainage and replace with fresh bulbs.
- **F2 Bulbs are planted upside down:** detect delayed or weak emergence → plant uncertain bulbs sideways next time.
- **F3 Animals remove bulbs:** detect holes and missing bulbs → replant with protection or choose resistant species.
- **F4 Poor bloom:** detect leaves with few flowers → allow foliage to ripen and avoid cutting it early.

## Verification

Bulbs are firm, planted in fall at label or size-based depth, oriented correctly, watered in, mulched lightly, and marked for spring emergence.

## Variations

- `container`: use a deep draining pot and protect from freeze-thaw extremes.
- `naturalized-lawn`: plant small bulbs in drifts and delay mowing until foliage yellows.
- `warm-climate`: pre-chill tulips or hyacinths only if locally recommended.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Many bulbs are toxic if eaten, so keep them away from children and pets, and wear gloves if bulbs irritate skin.
