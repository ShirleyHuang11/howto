## Goal

Brush a long-haired cat to remove loose coat, prevent mats, and find skin problems without pulling painfully.

## Preconditions

- A comb, slicker or pin brush, treats, and a mat splitter only for trained use are available.
- The cat is dry, calm enough for brief handling, and free of obvious wounds.
- Severe tight mats, skin sores, or fecal matting can be referred to a groomer or vet.

## Steps

1. **Start with calm contact.** Pet the cat in favorite areas and offer treats before brushing. → *Expect:* the cat stays relaxed or signals a clear limit.
2. **Comb in sections.** Work from shoulders to tail, then chest, belly, armpits, and hind legs only as tolerated. → *Expect:* loose hair lifts out and the comb moves through small areas.
3. **Support the skin.** Hold fur near the base while easing through tangles from the ends inward. → *Expect:* tangles loosen without yanking the skin.
4. **Stop at tight mats.** Do not cut close mats with scissors; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* skin can tent into a mat and be cut accidentally. → *Expect:* dangerous mats are left for a groomer or vet.
5. **Reward breaks.** Pause often and let the cat change position. → *Expect:* the session continues without biting, swatting, or escape panic.
6. **Check skin and hygiene.** Look for fleas, dandruff, redness, lumps, fecal soiling, or painful areas. → *Expect:* grooming also becomes a health check.

## Decision points

- If mats are tight, widespread, wet, or painful → book a groomer or vet.
- If the cat suddenly stops grooming itself → schedule a vet check for pain or illness.
- If brushing causes aggression → shorten sessions and use desensitization.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mat pulls skin:** the cat flinches, cries, or bites → stop that area and seek professional removal.
- **F2 Static or breakage:** fur crackles or breaks → use gentler tools and shorter strokes.
- **F3 Skin problem found:** sores, parasites, swelling, or pain appears → stop brushing and call a vet.

## Verification

The coat has fewer loose hairs and surface tangles, the cat is not injured or distressed, and any tight mats or skin concerns are identified for professional care.

## Variations

- `daily-maintenance`: brush high-friction zones for five minutes instead of doing the whole cat.
- `shedding-season`: increase frequency and check armpits and belly.
- `senior-cat`: use shorter sessions because arthritis can make positions painful.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if pulling and scissors are avoided. See a vet for sudden matting, reduced self-grooming, skin lesions, parasites, pain, or fecal soiling the cat cannot manage.
