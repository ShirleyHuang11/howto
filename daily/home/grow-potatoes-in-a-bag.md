## Goal

Grow potatoes in a fabric grow bag or large container by planting seed potatoes, hilling gradually, watering evenly, and harvesting clean tubers.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: certified seed potatoes, 10 gallon or larger grow bag, loose potting mix blended with compost, balanced fertilizer, water, and mulch if desired.
- The site receives at least 6 hours of sun and the bag can drain freely.
- Frost risk is low or the bag can be protected during cold nights.

## Steps

1. **Chit or inspect seed potatoes.** Use firm disease-free seed pieces with at least one strong sprout or eye. → *Expect:* planting pieces are viable and not rotting.
2. **Prepare the bag.** Roll down the sides and add 4-6 inches of moist potting mix. → *Expect:* the bag is stable with a shallow planting layer.
3. **Plant seed pieces.** Place pieces sprout side up about 8-10 inches apart and cover with 3-4 inches of mix. → *Expect:* seed pieces are covered but not buried too deeply.
4. **Water gently.** Moisten the mix evenly without leaving the bag waterlogged. → *Expect:* water drains from the bottom.
5. **Hill as stems grow.** Add mix or compost around stems when they reach about 6-8 inches tall, leaving top leaves exposed. → *Expect:* buried stems have room to form more tubers.
6. **Feed and water consistently.** Fertilize lightly as directed and keep soil evenly moist during tuber formation. → *Expect:* foliage grows steadily without wilting.
7. **Watch for maturity.** Stop hilling when the bag is near full and reduce watering after foliage yellows naturally. → *Expect:* skins begin to set before harvest.
8. **Harvest carefully.** Dump the bag onto a tarp after plants die back, or gently take new potatoes earlier from the side. → *Expect:* potatoes separate from loose mix with minimal bruising.

## Decision points

- Growing early new potatoes → harvest a few small tubers after flowering while leaving the plant mostly intact.
- Foliage is hit by frost → harvest soon if plants collapse, or protect if tubers are still sizing.
- Leaves show blight symptoms → remove infected foliage and avoid composting it.
- Bag heats up on pavement → move to a cooler spot or shade the container sides.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Seed potatoes rot:** detect missing sprouts and mushy pieces → use better-draining mix and water less at planting.
- **F2 Tubers turn green:** detect potatoes exposed to light → hill higher and discard green portions.
- **F3 Bag dries out:** detect wilted foliage and dry mix below the surface → water deeply and mulch the top.
- **F4 Low yield:** detect few tubers at harvest → use certified seed, larger bags, more sun, and consistent moisture next season.

## Verification

The grow bag drains freely, seed potatoes sprout, stems are hilled while leaves remain exposed, soil stays evenly moist, and harvested tubers are firm and not green.

## Variations

- `small-patio`: grow one or two seed pieces in a smaller deep container with drainage.
- `hot-climate`: plant in the cool season and protect bags from hot pavement.
- `new-potatoes`: harvest small tender tubers before full plant dieback.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not eat green potato skin or sprouts, wash hands after handling soil, and lift full bags carefully because wet mix is heavy.
