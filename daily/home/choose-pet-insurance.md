## Goal

Choose a pet insurance policy or decide not to buy one after comparing coverage, exclusions, costs, and the pet's likely needs.

## Preconditions

- Pet species, breed or mix, age, sex, medical history, and current medications are known.
- You can read policy documents, not only marketing summaries.
- A monthly budget and emergency savings estimate are available.

## Steps

1. **List the pet's risk profile.** Write age, breed risks, chronic conditions, lifestyle, and prior diagnoses. → *Expect:* pre-existing conditions and likely exclusions are visible before comparing prices.
2. **Compare policy types.** Separate accident-only, accident-and-illness, wellness add-ons, reimbursement rates, deductibles, annual limits, and waiting periods. → *Expect:* plans are compared on the same terms.
3. **Read exclusions.** Check bilateral conditions, dental disease, hereditary conditions, exam fees, prescriptions, rehab, and alternative care. → *Expect:* you know what the policy will not pay for.
4. **Estimate real cost.** Calculate monthly premium, deductible, copay share, annual limit, and expected premium increases. → *Expect:* the plan's best and worst routine cost is understandable.
5. **Check claim logistics.** Confirm vet choice, reimbursement timing, records required, and cancellation rules. → *Expect:* you know how money returns after a vet bill.
6. **Choose or decline deliberately.** Pick the best fit or keep a dedicated emergency fund instead; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not enroll until policy documents match the quote and pet details. → *Expect:* the decision is documented with policy name, price, and reasons.

## Decision points

- If the pet already has chronic illness → focus on what future unrelated conditions may still be covered.
- If premiums would prevent routine care → prioritize vaccines, parasite prevention, and an emergency fund.
- If two policies look similar → compare exclusions and annual limit before premium.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pre-existing surprise:** a claim is denied for prior signs → request the medical-record basis and ask the insurer and vet to clarify.
- **F2 Underinsured plan:** annual limit is too low for major surgery or hospitalization → consider a higher limit before enrollment or strengthen savings.
- **F3 Wellness confusion:** routine-care add-on costs more than expected services → remove the add-on or self-fund routine care.

## Verification

A selected policy or no-insurance decision is supported by written comparisons of premium, deductible, reimbursement, annual limit, waiting periods, exclusions, and emergency budget.

## Variations

- `young-pet`: compare before diagnoses accumulate, but still read waiting periods.
- `senior-pet`: expect higher premiums and more exclusions.
- `exotic-pet`: confirm the species is covered and that appropriate vets are eligible.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk is financial and privacy-related. Insurance applications may expose veterinary records and personal billing data; do not rely on coverage for urgent care until waiting periods and exclusions are clear.
