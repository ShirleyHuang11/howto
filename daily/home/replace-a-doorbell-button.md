## Goal

A broken low-voltage doorbell button is replaced and rings the existing chime reliably.

## Preconditions

- You have a compatible doorbell button, screwdriver, tape, and voltage tester if available.
- The existing system is a low-voltage wired doorbell, not a line-voltage device.
- Call an electrician if wires appear to be line voltage, insulation is brittle, or the transformer or chime needs replacement.

## Steps

1. **Turn off doorbell power.** Switch off the doorbell transformer breaker if known, or turn off power before handling unknown wires. → *Expect:* the doorbell cannot ring while wires are separated.
2. **Remove the old button.** Unscrew the button from the wall or trim and pull it out gently. → *Expect:* two low-voltage wires are visible.
3. **Secure the wires.** Tape wires to the trim so they cannot slip into the wall. → *Expect:* both wire ends remain accessible.
4. **Disconnect the old button.** Loosen terminal screws and remove the wires. → *Expect:* the old button is free.
5. **Attach the new button.** Connect one wire to each terminal; polarity usually does not matter for a simple button. → *Expect:* both wires are clamped under terminal screws.
6. **Mount the button.** Tuck wires gently and screw the button to the trim or wall. → *Expect:* the button sits flat without pinched wires.
7. **Restore power and test.** Turn power back on and press the button. → *Expect:* the chime rings once per press.

## Decision points

- More than two wires are present → photograph and identify the circuit before changing connections.
- Button has a light or smart features → follow its transformer voltage requirements.
- Chime does not ring with new button → troubleshoot transformer, chime, or wire continuity.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wire slips inside wall:** conductor disappears → fish it out carefully or call a pro before enlarging the hole.
- **F2 Chime hums:** button or wires are shorted → turn power off and check that terminals are not touching.
- **F3 Button light fails:** chime rings but light is off → confirm the button supports illumination and transformer voltage.

## Verification

The mounted button is secure, does not hum or stick, and the chime sounds correctly on three separate presses.

## Variations

- Wireless buttons: pair the new button to the receiver instead of connecting wires.
- Video doorbells: transformer capacity, Wi-Fi setup, and privacy settings add extra steps.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk: most doorbells are low voltage, but unknown wiring should be treated cautiously. Shut off power for uncertain systems and call a pro for damaged or confusing wiring.
