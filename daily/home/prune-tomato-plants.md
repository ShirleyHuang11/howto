## Goal

Prune tomato plants to improve airflow, reduce soil-borne disease splash, and keep growth supported without removing too much fruiting canopy.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: clean pruners or scissors, plant ties, stake or cage, gloves, and a trash bag for diseased leaves.
- The tomato type is known or can be judged as determinate, indeterminate, or dwarf.
- Plants are dry enough that pruning will not spread disease on wet foliage.

## Steps

1. **Identify the plant type.** Check the tag or growth habit before cutting. → *Expect:* you know whether to prune lightly or manage ongoing vine growth.
2. **Clean the tool.** Wipe blades with alcohol or wash and dry them before starting. → *Expect:* blades are clean and sharp enough for neat cuts.
3. **Remove lower leaves.** Cut leaves touching soil and lower yellowing leaves below the first flower cluster. → *Expect:* the base has better airflow and less soil contact.
4. **Choose main stems.** For indeterminate tomatoes, keep one to three strong leaders and remove extra small suckers if desired. → *Expect:* growth is easier to support.
5. **Keep determinate plants fuller.** For determinate tomatoes, remove only diseased, crowded, or soil-touching foliage. → *Expect:* fruiting branches remain mostly intact.
6. **Cut small suckers early.** Pinch or snip suckers when they are small and avoid stripping large amounts at once. → *Expect:* cuts are small and the plant keeps enough leaves.
7. **Tie remaining stems.** Secure stems loosely to support below leaf nodes, leaving room for thickening. → *Expect:* stems are upright and not strangled.
8. **Dispose of diseased material.** Remove spotted leaves from the garden rather than composting active disease. → *Expect:* infected foliage is not left under plants.

## Decision points

- Plant is determinate or patio type → prune minimally to avoid reducing yield.
- Weather is hot and sunny → keep enough leaf cover to prevent sunscald on fruit.
- Disease pressure is high → prioritize lower leaf removal, spacing, and dry foliage.
- Suckers are thick and flowering → leave them unless they crowd the plant or exceed support.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overpruned plant:** detect exposed fruit and sparse leaves → stop pruning and provide shade cloth during heat.
- **F2 Disease spreads after pruning:** detect new spots after wet pruning → prune only dry plants and sanitize tools between plants.
- **F3 Stems snap:** detect unsupported heavy vines → add ties below fruit clusters and use a stronger cage or stake.
- **F4 Suckers regrow quickly:** detect new shoots at leaf joints → pinch weekly while small.

## Verification

The tomato has no leaves touching soil, diseased foliage is removed, main stems are supported, and enough healthy leaf canopy remains to shade fruit.

## Variations

- `greenhouse`: prune more consistently to one or two leaders for airflow.
- `humid-climate`: remove lower foliage earlier and space plants widely.
- `container`: prune to match the size of the cage and keep the pot from tipping.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use clean sharp tools, avoid pruning wet diseased foliage, and wash hands after handling tomato leaves, which can irritate skin.
