## Goal

A cooking-oil or grease stain is lifted from washable clothing before heat sets it.

## Preconditions

- Garment is washable and its care label is readable.
- Dish soap or liquid laundry detergent, absorbent powder, dull spoon, and clean cloth are available.
- The garment has not been machine dried since the stain appeared.

## Steps

1. **Blot fresh grease.** Press a clean cloth onto the stain without rubbing it wider. → *Expect:* surface oil transfers to the cloth.
2. **Lift solids.** Scrape food residue away with a dull spoon from the outside inward. → *Expect:* no chunks remain on the fabric.
3. **Apply absorbent powder.** Cover the stain with baking soda, cornstarch, or talc for 15 minutes. → *Expect:* powder clumps or darkens as it absorbs oil.
4. **Brush off powder.** Shake or brush powder into the trash. → *Expect:* the stain looks lighter but may still show a shadow.
5. **Pretreat with soap.** Work a drop of dish soap or liquid detergent into the greasy area with fingertips. → *Expect:* the stained fibers are evenly coated.
6. **Rest the pretreatment.** Let the soap sit for 10 minutes without drying completely. → *Expect:* detergent has time to break down the oil.
7. **Rinse from the back.** Flush warm water through the wrong side if the label allows warm water. → *Expect:* loosened grease moves out of the fabric.
8. **Wash normally.** Launder at the warmest label-safe temperature with regular detergent. → *Expect:* the garment finishes a full wash cycle.
9. **Air-dry and inspect.** Do not use dryer heat until the stain is gone. → *Expect:* the damp or dry fabric has no visible greasy outline.

## Decision points

- Fabric is dry-clean-only → blot and take it to a cleaner without wet pretreatment.
- Stain remains after washing → repeat pretreatment before any dryer cycle.
- Fabric is wool, silk, or rayon → use mild detergent and cool water, then test first.
- Grease is heavy motor oil → use a laundry degreaser safe for the fabric and wash separately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stain spreads:** rubbing creates a larger translucent area → blot outward edges and reapply powder.
- **F2 Soap ring appears:** concentrated soap dries on fabric → rinse from the back and wash again.
- **F3 Dryer set the stain:** oily mark remains after heat → treat with detergent overnight, but expect partial recovery only.
- **F4 Color lifts:** cloth shows dye during testing → stop wet treatment and use a cleaner.

## Verification

After air drying, the garment has no translucent grease shadow when viewed from the front and back in bright light.

## Variations

- Old stain: repeat powder and detergent treatment two or three times before washing.
- Synthetic activewear: use laundry detergent rather than dish soap if the label warns against degreasers.
- Tiny spot: treat with a cotton swab so soap does not spread.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep solvents away from flames, avoid chlorine bleach on grease stains, and never machine dry until inspection confirms the oil mark is gone.
