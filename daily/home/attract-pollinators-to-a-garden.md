## Goal

Create a garden area that offers nectar, pollen, shelter, and clean water for bees, butterflies, moths, flies, and other useful pollinators across the growing season.

## Preconditions

- You have a bed, border, patio containers, or lawn edge that receives at least partial sun.
- Supplies may include native flowering plants, compost, mulch, shallow water dish, stones, and labels.
- You can avoid broad-spectrum insecticides on or near blooming plants.

## Steps

1. **Map bloom gaps.** Note what flowers in spring, summer, and fall, including trees, herbs, vegetables, and ornamentals. → *Expect:* missing bloom seasons are visible on a short list.
2. **Choose region-suitable plants.** Select mostly native or well-adapted flowers with varied shapes, including early, midseason, and late blooms. → *Expect:* the plant list offers pollen or nectar for multiple months.
3. **Plant in clusters.** Group three or more of the same plant where space allows so pollinators can forage efficiently. → *Expect:* blooms form visible patches rather than scattered single plants.
4. **Include host plants.** Add plants that feed caterpillars or specialist insects, such as milkweed where appropriate or native grasses and herbs. → *Expect:* the garden supports life stages beyond adult nectar feeding.
5. **Provide shallow water.** Set a shallow dish with stones or keep a small muddy patch damp during dry weather. → *Expect:* insects can land and drink without drowning.
6. **Leave shelter.** Keep some hollow stems, leaf litter, bare soil patches, or brushy edges where they do not create hazards. → *Expect:* nesting and overwintering sites remain available.
7. **Avoid pesticide exposure.** Hand-pick pests, use barriers, and never spray flowers when pollinators are active. → *Expect:* blooming plants are not coated with insecticide.
8. **Observe and adjust.** Watch visits at different times of day and replace unused or failing plants with better-adapted choices. → *Expect:* pollinator visits increase over the season.

## Decision points

- Space is limited → use containers with herbs, compact native flowers, and a shallow water source.
- Deer or rabbits browse blooms → choose resistant plants or protect new plantings with cages.
- Local invasive lists flag a plant → choose a non-invasive alternative.
- Mosquitoes breed in water → empty and refresh dishes every few days.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No pollinators visit:** detect few visits during bloom → add more sun-loving clustered flowers and reduce pesticide use nearby.
- **F2 Plants fail in site:** detect wilting, rot, or poor flowering → match replacements to actual sun, soil moisture, and climate.
- **F3 Water dish breeds mosquitoes:** detect larvae or stagnant water → dump, scrub, refill, and use stones so water stays shallow.
- **F4 Aggressive plant spreads:** detect seedlings or runners beyond the bed → remove extras before seed set and replace if needed.

## Verification

The garden has overlapping bloom periods, clustered flowering plants, a safe shallow water source, some shelter habitat, and no insecticide applied to open flowers.

## Variations

- `vegetable-garden`: interplant herbs and flowers near cucumbers, squash, tomatoes, beans, and peppers.
- `rental-patio`: use movable containers and avoid planting species that self-sow into shared beds.
- `dry-climate`: choose drought-tolerant native flowers and water deeply during establishment.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid planting known toxic species where children or pets graze, keep paths clear, and follow local rules for balcony containers and shared landscapes.
