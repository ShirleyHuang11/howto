## Goal

Collect a representative soil sample and use a home kit or lab test to understand pH, nutrients, and amendment needs before planting or fertilizing.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: clean trowel or soil probe, clean bucket, sample bag, labels, and a soil test kit or lab submission form.
- Soil is workable, not frozen or waterlogged.
- You know which bed or lawn area the sample represents.

## Steps

1. **Choose one testing area.** Sample one bed, lawn, or planting zone at a time rather than mixing unrelated soils. → *Expect:* the test will represent a specific growing area.
2. **Remove surface debris.** Clear mulch, leaves, and compost from each sampling spot. → *Expect:* only mineral soil is sampled.
3. **Take multiple cores.** Collect 6-10 small samples from root depth, usually 6 inches for vegetables and flowers. → *Expect:* the bucket contains soil from across the area.
4. **Mix and remove debris.** Crumble the soil, remove stones and roots, and blend thoroughly in the clean bucket. → *Expect:* the composite sample is uniform.
5. **Dry if required.** Air-dry the sample on clean paper if the kit or lab requests dry soil. → *Expect:* soil is dry enough to package without molding.
6. **Package and label.** Fill the test container or bag with the requested amount and note crop, bed, and date. → *Expect:* the sample can be matched to the garden area.
7. **Run or submit the test.** Follow kit instructions exactly or mail/drop off the lab sample with payment if required. → *Expect:* results are produced or pending from the lab.
8. **Apply recommendations carefully.** Add lime, sulfur, compost, or fertilizer only at the recommended rate for the crop. → *Expect:* amendments match measured soil needs.

## Decision points

- Growing food near old paint, roads, or industrial soil → request heavy metal testing, especially lead.
- Soil was recently fertilized or limed → wait several weeks before testing for routine nutrients.
- Home kit results are unclear → use an extension or professional lab for more reliable recommendations.
- Beds differ in history or texture → test them separately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sample is unrepresentative:** detect one spot dominating the bucket → resample from multiple evenly spaced points.
- **F2 Results conflict with plant symptoms:** detect healthy test values but ongoing problems → check watering, drainage, pests, and compaction.
- **F3 Too much amendment added:** detect application above label or lab rate → stop, water in if appropriate, and retest before adding more.
- **F4 Sample contaminates indoors:** detect spilled soil or dust → clean surfaces and use sealed labeled bags next time.

## Verification

The sample combines multiple root-depth cores from one defined area, is labeled, tested by a kit or lab, and amendments are based on the written result rather than guessing.

## Variations

- `new-raised-bed`: test the imported mix after it settles, not the soil underneath unless roots will reach it.
- `lawn`: sample shallower if the testing service requests turf depth.
- `lead-risk`: use a lab that reports lead and follow local guidance before growing food.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wear gloves if contamination is possible, avoid breathing dry soil dust, and treat lab forms with address and payment details as personal information.
