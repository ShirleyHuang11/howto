## Goal

Run an immediate, organized search for a lost pet while notifying the right people and making it easy for the pet to return safely.

## Preconditions

- A recent photo, pet description, collar or microchip information, and last-known location are available.
- Someone can stay reachable by phone or messages.
- Searchers can avoid unsafe trespass, traffic risks, and confrontations.

## Steps

1. **Secure the escape point.** Leave a safe entry open only if it does not endanger other pets or people, and place familiar bedding or scent nearby. → *Expect:* the pet has a clear return route.
2. **Search the immediate area quietly.** Check closets, garages, sheds, under decks, bushes, and nearby hiding spots with a flashlight. → *Expect:* close hiding places are ruled out before the search radius expands.
3. **Alert nearby people.** Tell neighbors, building staff, postal workers, and local groups with a photo, name, and contact method. → *Expect:* more eyes are watching without chasing the pet.
4. **Contact shelters and vets.** File lost reports with animal control, shelters, microchip registry, and nearby veterinary clinics. → *Expect:* found-pet intake points can match the pet to you.
5. **Post clear notices.** Use large readable flyers and online posts with area, date, photo, and one contact number. → *Expect:* sightings can be reported quickly.
6. **Follow sightings carefully.** Go to sightings with food, carrier or leash, and calm voices; do not run after a scared pet. → *Expect:* the pet is approached slowly or the location is marked for trapping help.

## Decision points

- If the pet is seen but will not approach → use food, routine, and rescue trapping support rather than chasing.
- If the pet is injured, trapped, or near traffic → call animal control or emergency services appropriate to the location.
- If someone demands money before proof → request a current photo or identifying detail before meeting safely.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 False sighting:** description does not match → thank the caller, record location, and continue search patterns.
- **F2 Pet runs farther:** searchers chase or shout → stop pursuit, back away, and reset with quiet food stations or trapping help.
- **F3 Scam contact:** caller asks for codes, deposits, or shipping fees → do not pay or share account codes; ask for proof and report suspicious messages.

## Verification

The pet is physically recovered and checked for injury, or all local shelters, vets, microchip registry, neighbors, flyers, and online lost-pet channels have current reports and contact information.

## Variations

- `indoor-cat`: focus tightly near the home, especially hiding spots, dusk, and quiet calling.
- `dog`: notify animal control quickly because dogs may travel farther.
- `microchipped`: confirm the registry phone and address are current.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk includes traffic, unsafe searches, scams, and injured animals. Do not trespass or meet strangers alone; see a vet after recovery if the pet was missing, injured, overheated, cold, bitten, or exposed to toxins.
