## Goal

Keep potted plants alive through winter by matching each plant to dormancy, indoor protection, insulation, or sheltered outdoor storage.

## Preconditions

- Supplies may include pruners, insecticidal soap, saucers, plant labels, mulch, burlap, insulating wrap, and a bright indoor spot or protected outdoor area.
- You know whether each plant is tropical, hardy perennial, shrub, bulb, or dormant tuber.
- Pots can drain and will not sit in frozen standing water.

## Steps

1. **Sort plants by hardiness.** Compare each plant's cold tolerance to expected winter lows and container exposure. → *Expect:* plants are grouped by indoor, sheltered, or outdoor overwintering needs.
2. **Inspect before moving indoors.** Check leaf undersides, stems, and soil surface for insects, eggs, and disease. → *Expect:* pests are found before they spread inside.
3. **Clean and prune lightly.** Remove dead leaves, spent flowers, and diseased growth without forcing heavy new growth. → *Expect:* plants are tidier and less pest-prone.
4. **Transition tropical plants.** Move tender plants indoors before nights are consistently cold, placing them in bright light away from drafts. → *Expect:* tropical plants are protected before chilling injury.
5. **Protect hardy pots outdoors.** Cluster hardy containers, mulch the soil surface, and place them against a sheltered wall or inside an unheated garage if needed. → *Expect:* roots are buffered from freeze-thaw swings.
6. **Reduce watering.** Water dormant or slow-growing plants only when the mix is dry below the surface. → *Expect:* roots stay barely moist and not soggy.
7. **Label dormant plants.** Mark pots that die back so they are not mistaken for empty soil. → *Expect:* dormant plants remain identifiable.
8. **Resume growth gradually.** In late winter or spring, increase light and water slowly and harden outdoor plants off before full exposure. → *Expect:* new growth appears without sudden shock.

## Decision points

- Plant is tropical → bring indoors before cold nights and provide the brightest available light.
- Plant is hardy but in a small pot → insulate or sink the pot because roots are less protected than in ground.
- Plant is a bulb or tuber → follow its dry or barely moist dormancy requirement.
- Indoor pests appear → isolate the pot and treat before returning it near other plants.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Leaves drop indoors:** detect sudden yellowing or shedding → increase light, reduce watering, and allow acclimation.
- **F2 Roots rot:** detect sour soil and wilting in wet mix → stop watering, improve drainage, and repot if roots are mushy.
- **F3 Pot cracks from freezing:** detect split ceramic or trapped water → move to frost-safe containers and ensure drainage.
- **F4 Spider mites spread:** detect fine webbing and speckled leaves → isolate, rinse foliage, and repeat treatment.

## Verification

Each potted plant has a winter location matched to its hardiness, pots drain freely, watering is reduced for slow growth, and pests are not spreading among stored plants.

## Variations

- `unheated-garage`: use for dormant hardy plants that need cold but not deep root freezing.
- `apartment`: prioritize cuttings or compact plants when space and light are limited.
- `mild-winter`: group pots in a wind-sheltered area and cover during freezes.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Lift heavy pots with help, keep toxic plants away from children and pets indoors, and avoid blocking exits or heaters with overwintering plants.
