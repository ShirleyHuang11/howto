## Goal

A damaged plug end on a flexible cord is replaced with a matching rated plug so the appliance or tool can be used safely.

## Preconditions

- You have a replacement plug matching amperage, voltage, prong count, grounding, and cord type, plus wire stripper, screwdriver, and utility knife.
- The cord jacket is otherwise intact and the device itself is not damaged.
- Call an electrician or replace the entire cord if insulation is brittle, copper is blackened, the device draws high current, or the plug type is uncertain.

## Steps

1. **Unplug the cord.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* disconnect the cord from power and confirm the plug cannot be energized while working. → *Expect:* the cord is fully separated from any outlet.
2. **Inspect the cord.** Check the full visible length for cuts, heat damage, or crushed jacket. → *Expect:* damage is limited to the plug end.
3. **Cut off the old end.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* cut the cord behind the damaged strain relief or burned insulation. → *Expect:* only clean, flexible cord remains.
4. **Open the new plug.** Disassemble the replacement plug and note terminal colors or markings. → *Expect:* hot, neutral, and ground terminals are identifiable.
5. **Strip the jacket.** Remove only enough outer jacket and conductor insulation to fit the plug terminals. → *Expect:* copper is exposed at the correct length without nicked strands.
6. **Connect conductors.** Attach green to ground, white or ribbed to silver neutral, and black or smooth to brass hot. → *Expect:* each conductor is under the correct terminal with no stray strands.
7. **Secure strain relief.** Clamp the plug body onto the outer jacket, not individual wires. → *Expect:* pulling lightly on the cord does not stress terminals.
8. **Close and test.** Reassemble the plug and test in a GFCI-protected outlet if possible. → *Expect:* the device powers normally without heat, sparks, or flicker.

## Decision points

- Cord has only two conductors → use a two-prong polarized plug only if the device was designed that way.
- Ground prong was removed or missing → replace with a grounded plug and correct ground conductor, or replace the cord.
- Device trips a breaker or GFCI → stop using it and have it serviced.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong polarity:** conductors are reversed → unplug immediately and reconnect to the proper brass and silver terminals.
- **F2 Loose strands:** copper protrudes from terminal → trim and reconnect cleanly before use.
- **F3 Plug warms:** plug heats during use → unplug and replace with a correctly rated plug or retire the cord.

## Verification

The plug strain relief grips the jacket, conductors are on correct terminals, and the device runs for one minute without sparks, odor, flicker, or heat at the plug.

## Variations

- Outdoor cords: use a plug rated for outdoor and flexible-cord use.
- Heavy tools: match the original amperage and grounding exactly.

## Safety & privacy

High risk: wiring errors can shock or start a fire. Work only unplugged, preserve grounding and polarity, and call a pro when ratings or conductor identity are unclear.
