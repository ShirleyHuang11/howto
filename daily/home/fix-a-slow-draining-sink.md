## Goal

A bathroom or kitchen sink drains normally after the stopper, trap, and near-drain buildup are cleared without damaging plumbing.

## Preconditions

- You have gloves, bucket, towels, flashlight, pliers, a drain snake or zip tool, and mild dish soap.
- You can reach the trap under the sink and know where the fixture shutoff valves are.
- Do not use chemical drain cleaner before opening the trap; call a plumber for repeated backups, sewage odor, or water backing up into other fixtures.

## Steps

1. **Clear the basin.** Remove standing water with a cup and bucket, then wipe the drain area. → *Expect:* the basin is empty enough to work without spilling.
2. **Remove the stopper.** Lift out the stopper or loosen the pivot nut under the sink to release a pop-up stopper. → *Expect:* hair and debris around the stopper are visible.
3. **Clean the stopper.** Pull off hair and sludge, wash the stopper with dish soap, and set it aside. → *Expect:* the stopper moves freely and no longer carries buildup.
4. **Snake the drain opening.** Feed a plastic zip tool or small snake into the drain and pull out debris slowly. → *Expect:* hair or soft buildup comes out, or the tool passes freely.
5. **Flush with hot tap water.** Run hot tap water for one minute if the pipe is not fully blocked. → *Expect:* flow improves or the blockage location is confirmed.
6. **Open the trap.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* place a bucket under the P-trap, confirm no chemical cleaner is present, then loosen the slip nuts by hand or with pliers. → *Expect:* water drains into the bucket and the trap comes free.
7. **Clean the trap.** Empty the trap, scrub visible sludge, and check for hard objects. → *Expect:* the trap interior is clear and the washers are intact.
8. **Reassemble and test.** Reinstall washers in the same orientation, tighten slip nuts snugly, then run water while watching joints. → *Expect:* water drains quickly and no trap joints drip.

## Decision points

- Drain improves after stopper cleaning → skip trap removal and verify with a full-basin drain test.
- Trap nut is cracked, corroded, or will not loosen → stop and call a plumber.
- Multiple fixtures drain slowly → the clog is likely downstream and needs professional clearing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Trap leak:** joint drips during testing → reseat the washer, hand-tighten, then add a small plier snug.
- **F2 Tool stuck:** snake or zip tool catches → rotate gently while withdrawing; do not yank hard enough to crack plastic.
- **F3 Slow drain returns:** basin still drains slowly → snake farther from the wall bend or call a plumber for a branch-line clog.

## Verification

A full sink basin drains completely within two minutes, and the P-trap and slip nuts stay dry during and after the test.

## Variations

- Bathroom sinks: hair is usually at the stopper and pivot rod.
- Kitchen sinks: grease buildup may require repeated hot-water flushing after mechanical cleaning.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk: dirty water can contain bacteria, and opened traps can leak. Wear gloves, avoid chemical drain cleaners, keep the bucket in place, and call a pro for hidden or recurring clogs.
