## Goal

Install a simple drip irrigation system that delivers water slowly to plant roots without overspray, runoff, or frequent hand watering.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: outdoor faucet or hose bib, backflow preventer, pressure regulator, filter, tubing, emitters or dripline, stakes, end caps, hole punch, and optional timer.
- The garden layout and plant water needs are known.
- Local water rules allow drip irrigation and the faucet works without leaking.

## Steps

1. **Sketch the zones.** Mark beds, containers, plant spacing, water source, and high-water or low-water groups. → *Expect:* tubing routes and zones are planned before cutting.
2. **Attach head assembly.** Install the backflow preventer, filter, pressure regulator, and timer if using one at the faucet. → *Expect:* water supply is protected and pressure is suitable for drip parts.
3. **Lay main tubing.** Run half-inch tubing along bed edges or rows, warming it in sun if it is stiff. → *Expect:* tubing follows the planned route without kinks.
4. **Add emitters or dripline.** Punch holes and insert emitters near root zones, or connect dripline with emitters facing up or sideways as directed. → *Expect:* every plant has a water outlet nearby.
5. **Cap and flush lines.** Open line ends briefly to flush debris, then install end caps or figure-eight closures. → *Expect:* water runs clear before lines are closed.
6. **Test at low flow.** Turn on water and inspect every connector, emitter, and end cap. → *Expect:* emitters drip steadily and fittings do not spray.
7. **Stake and cover.** Pin tubing in place and cover with mulch while keeping emitters findable. → *Expect:* lines stay put and evaporation is reduced.
8. **Set a watering schedule.** Run long enough to wet the root zone, then adjust by digging a small check hole after watering. → *Expect:* soil is moist at root depth without runoff.

## Decision points

- Containers are included → use adjustable emitters and check them more often than in-ground beds.
- Beds have different water needs → create separate zones or shutoff valves.
- Water pressure is high → use the correct pressure regulator before tubing.
- Winter freezing occurs → drain lines and store timers before hard freezes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Emitters clog:** detect dry spots or stopped drips → flush lines, clean filter, and replace clogged emitters.
- **F2 Fittings blow apart:** detect tubing popping off → reduce pressure and push fittings fully into warmed tubing.
- **F3 Plants wilt despite watering:** detect dry soil below emitters → lengthen run time or add emitters.
- **F4 Runoff forms:** detect water leaving the bed → shorten cycles, use multiple starts, or improve soil infiltration.

## Verification

With water running, each plant receives steady drips near its roots, fittings stay dry except at emitters, and soil is moist at root depth after the scheduled run.

## Variations

- `raised-bed`: run parallel drip lines 8-12 inches apart under mulch.
- `container-garden`: use quarter-inch tubing with one or more emitters per pot.
- `slope`: use pressure-compensating emitters and shorter zones.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use a backflow preventer to protect drinking water, keep hoses from creating trip hazards, and follow watering restrictions.
