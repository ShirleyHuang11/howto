## Goal

Trim only the sharp transparent tips of a cat's claws while keeping the cat calm and avoiding the quick.

## Preconditions

- Cat nail clippers, styptic powder or cornstarch, a towel, and small treats are within reach.
- The cat is awake, breathing normally, and not injured, limping, or in obvious pain.
- You can identify the pink quick in light nails or will trim only tiny tips on dark nails.

## Steps

1. **Choose a quiet moment.** Sit in a calm room after play or a meal, with doors closed and supplies beside you. → *Expect:* the cat stays near you without immediate hiding or struggling.
2. **Handle one paw gently.** Touch a shoulder, then a leg, then one paw, rewarding calm pauses. → *Expect:* the cat allows brief paw contact or recovers quickly after pulling away.
3. **Expose one claw.** Press the toe pad lightly until the claw extends, then locate the narrow sharp hook beyond the quick. → *Expect:* the cutting zone is visible and is only the translucent tip.
4. **Clip a tiny tip.** Hold the clipper perpendicular to the claw hook and remove 1 to 2 mm at a time; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* never cut into the pink quick or opaque base. → *Expect:* the sharp point is blunted and the cat does not yelp, bleed, or jerk in pain.
5. **Pause between claws.** Give a treat and release the paw after each claw or each two claws. → *Expect:* the session remains calm enough to continue or stop without a chase.
6. **Stop before panic.** End the session if the cat growls, pants, thrashes, or hides, even if only one paw is finished. → *Expect:* the cat leaves with no injury and can try again later.

## Decision points

- If the cat has thick, curled, split, or ingrown nails → book a vet or groomer instead of forcing a trim.
- If the cat cannot tolerate paw handling → use a gradual desensitization plan over several days.
- If a nail bleeds or the cat seems painful after trimming → treat as a failure mode and call a vet if bleeding or limping persists.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Quick nicked:** blood appears or the cat cries sharply → press styptic powder or cornstarch to the nail for several minutes and call a vet if bleeding does not stop.
- **F2 Cat panics:** twisting, biting, panting, or escape attempts begin → release the cat, lower the next session to one claw, and ask a vet or groomer for help if needed.
- **F3 Nail unclear:** dark nail or furry toe hides the quick → clip only the needle tip or skip that claw.

## Verification

Each trimmed claw has a blunt tip, no nail is bleeding, the cat is walking normally, and the cat can leave the area without being chased.

## Variations

- `kitten`: trim fewer claws per session and pair every touch with food.
- `senior-cat`: inspect for overgrown or thickened claws more often and use a vet for painful feet.
- `two-person`: one person feeds treats while the other clips only visible tips.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from cutting the quick, restraining too hard, or being bitten. Do not punish the cat; seek veterinary care for persistent bleeding, limping, swelling, or infected-looking nails.
