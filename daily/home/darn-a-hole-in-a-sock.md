## Goal

A sock hole is reinforced with woven thread so the patch is smooth enough to wear and strong enough for laundering.

## Preconditions

- Clean sock, darning needle, matching yarn or embroidery floss, scissors, and a darning egg, ball, or lightbulb-shaped form.
- Hole is small enough that surrounding fabric is still strong.
- Good light is available.

## Steps

1. **Inspect the hole.** Trim loose lint but leave sound loops around the edge. → *Expect:* the weak area and solid fabric boundary are visible.
2. **Place the form inside.** Stretch the sock lightly over a darning egg or rounded object. → *Expect:* the hole lies flat without being stretched wide.
3. **Thread the needle.** Use yarn close to the sock thickness and knot only if the inside lump will not bother you. → *Expect:* needle passes through fabric without forcing.
4. **Anchor beside the hole.** Make small running stitches in solid fabric 1/4 inch from the edge. → *Expect:* thread is secured outside the weak area.
5. **Lay warp threads.** Stitch parallel strands across the hole, catching solid fabric on both sides. → *Expect:* the opening is bridged by evenly spaced threads.
6. **Weave cross threads.** Turn the sock and weave over-under through the first strands. → *Expect:* a small woven patch fills the hole.
7. **Secure the end.** Stitch several tiny backstitches in solid fabric and trim excess thread. → *Expect:* no loose thread tails snag inside the sock.
8. **Check the inside.** Remove the form and feel the patch with fingertips. → *Expect:* the repair is flat enough to wear comfortably.

## Decision points

- Hole is larger than a coin → consider a sewn patch or replacing the sock.
- Fabric around the hole tears while sewing → widen the repair into stronger material.
- Sock is thin dress fabric → use fine thread and smaller stitches to avoid a lump.
- Repair is on the toe seam → keep knots outside pressure points.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Patch puckers:** stitches were pulled too tight → loosen new stitches or resew over the form with less tension.
- **F2 Thread breaks:** yarn is weak or too thin → replace with stronger matching yarn.
- **F3 Hole grows:** stitches caught rotten fabric → extend anchoring into sound fabric.
- **F4 Patch rubs:** knots or bulky thread press the foot → trim, flatten, or redo with finer thread.

## Verification

The hole is fully covered by woven thread, the surrounding fabric is not puckered, and the inside patch feels flat under light finger pressure.

## Variations

- Contrast repair: use visible colored yarn for an intentional mended look.
- Wool sock: use wool yarn so the patch flexes similarly.
- Heel wear: darn a larger oval around the thin area before an open hole forms.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use scissors and needles carefully, and avoid darning over dirty socks because grit weakens both fabric and thread.
