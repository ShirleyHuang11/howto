## Goal

Remove dead, damaged, crossing, or overgrown shrub branches while preserving the plant's natural shape and seasonal bloom.

## Preconditions

- You have clean bypass pruners, loppers for thicker stems, gloves, and eye protection.
- You know whether the shrub blooms on old wood or new wood, or you will prune only dead and damaged growth.
- The shrub is not holding an active bird nest or stinging insects.

## Steps

1. **Identify the shrub's timing.** [BRANCH: spring-blooming | summer-blooming | unknown] prune spring bloomers after flowering, summer bloomers in late winter or early spring, and unknown shrubs lightly. → *Expect:* you avoid removing the season's flower buds.
2. **Clean the tools.** Wipe blades with alcohol or disinfectant before cutting, especially between diseased plants. → *Expect:* blades are sharp-looking and free of sap or soil.
3. **Remove dead or diseased wood first.** Cut back to healthy tissue or the branch collar without leaving long stubs. → *Expect:* dead, brittle, or spotted material is gone.
4. **Thin crossing stems.** Choose one rubbing or inward-growing branch and cut it at its base with bypass pruners or loppers. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* each cut changes growth for the season, so step back and confirm the branch before cutting. → *Expect:* the center has more air and no obvious rubbing pair remains.
5. **Shape with selective cuts.** Shorten long shoots back to a side branch or outward-facing bud, not by shearing the whole face unless it is a hedge. → *Expect:* the shrub looks natural rather than flat-topped.
6. **Limit the session.** Stop after removing about one-quarter of live growth unless rejuvenation pruning is appropriate for that species. → *Expect:* the plant still has enough leaves and structure to recover.
7. **Clean up debris.** Bag diseased material for trash and compost healthy clippings if allowed. → *Expect:* no thorny or diseased cuttings remain underfoot.

## Decision points

- The shrub has a nest, bees, or wasps → stop and return when wildlife has moved or use professional help.
- Branches are thicker than your thumb or near utility lines → use an arborist or qualified professional.
- The plant is severely overgrown → plan staged pruning over several seasons.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No blooms after pruning:** detect missing flowers in season → adjust timing next year based on old-wood versus new-wood bloom habit.
- **F2 Ragged cuts:** detect crushed stems → sharpen or replace tools and recut cleanly just outside a bud or collar.
- **F3 Overpruned shrub:** detect sparse structure or sunscald risk → stop, water during drought, and let regrowth fill in.
- **F4 Disease spreads:** detect suspicious spots on multiple plants → disinfect tools and dispose of infected cuttings in trash.

## Verification

Dead, damaged, crossing, and overlong branches are removed with clean cuts; the shrub keeps a balanced shape; and no wildlife, diseased debris, or sharp cuttings remain.

## Variations

- `flowering-shrub`: time pruning around bloom cycle before removing live wood.
- `hedge`: shear lightly after thinning interior deadwood.
- `thorny-shrub`: use gauntlet gloves, long sleeves, and eye protection.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from sharp tools, thorns, eye injuries, insects, and accidental severe pruning. Keep hands behind blades, confirm each cut, and avoid utility lines or unstable footing.
