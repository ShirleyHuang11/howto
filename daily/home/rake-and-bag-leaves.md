## Goal

Collect autumn leaves into bags or yard-waste containers without damaging grass, storm drains, or your back.

## Preconditions

- Leaves are mostly dry or only lightly damp.
- You have a rake, gloves, yard-waste bags or bins, and sturdy shoes.
- Local rules allow bagged leaf pickup or you have a compost area.

## Steps

1. **Choose the collection route.** Start upwind and uphill, working toward the curb, compost pile, or bagging spot. → *Expect:* leaves move with you instead of being dragged across the whole yard.
2. **Clear drains and beds first.** Pull leaves away from storm drains, window wells, young shrubs, and pet paths. → *Expect:* water can flow and delicate plants are visible.
3. **Rake in small rows.** Use short strokes, switch hands often, and keep the rake close to your body. → *Expect:* leaves form manageable windrows without shoulder strain.
4. **Move piles onto a tarp or into bags.** [BRANCH: bag pickup | compost] scoop dry leaves into paper bags, or drag a tarp to the compost area. → *Expect:* each load can be lifted without tearing or spilling.
5. **Keep bags light.** Fill bags about two-thirds full if leaves are damp, then fold or tie according to local pickup rules. → *Expect:* bags stand upright and can be moved by one person.
6. **Leave useful mulch where appropriate.** Spread a thin leaf layer under shrubs or mow dry leaves into the lawn if local practice allows. → *Expect:* beds are protected without burying crowns or turf.
7. **Stage bags safely.** Place bags at the pickup point away from fire hydrants, sidewalks, bike lanes, and street drains. → *Expect:* the yard is clear and public access remains open.

## Decision points

- Leaves are soaked → wait for a dry day or use smaller loads.
- Local pickup requires paper bags or loose curb piles → follow that rule instead of plastic bags.
- Acorns, pine cones, or branches are mixed in → separate them so bags do not rip.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bag tears:** detect splitting or wet bottom → double-bag with an approved paper bag or reduce the load.
- **F2 Back strain:** detect pulling pain or fatigue → stop, stretch, use a tarp, and make smaller piles.
- **F3 Leaves blow back:** detect piles scattering → rake with the wind, wet piles lightly, or bag in smaller batches.
- **F4 Grass smothering:** detect thick leaves left on turf → remove or mulch them before they mat down.

## Verification

Leaves are removed from lawn, drains, walkways, and plant crowns; bags or compost piles are stable; and pickup bags comply with local rules.

## Variations

- `wildlife-friendly`: leave some dry leaf litter in garden beds away from foundations for overwintering insects.
- `large-yard`: use a tarp or leaf blower to move leaves, then rake final edges.
- `snow-forecast`: clear leaves before snow so they do not freeze into slippery mats.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, mainly repetitive strain, slippery wet leaves, hidden sharp debris, and blocked drainage. Wear gloves, lift small loads, and keep leaf piles out of streets and storm drains.
