## Goal

Clean a dog's outer ear canal with a vet-approved ear cleaner without pushing debris deeper or hurting the ear.

## Preconditions

- A dog ear-cleaning solution, cotton pads or gauze, towel, and treats are ready.
- Cotton swabs are not inserted into the ear canal.
- The ear is not severely painful, swollen, bleeding, full of pus, or suspected to have a ruptured eardrum.

## Steps

1. **Inspect both ears.** Look and smell before adding cleaner. → *Expect:* you can tell whether wax is mild or whether pain, odor, redness, or discharge suggests a vet visit.
2. **Position calmly.** Hold the ear flap upright and steady the head without pinning the dog hard. → *Expect:* the ear canal opening is visible and the dog can still breathe and stand normally.
3. **Fill with cleaner.** Place the bottle tip near the opening without touching skin and squeeze in the label amount. → *Expect:* cleaner pools in the canal entrance.
4. **Massage the base.** Rub the base of the ear for 20 to 30 seconds. → *Expect:* a soft squishing sound indicates cleaner is loosening wax.
5. **Let the dog shake.** Step back with the towel ready. → *Expect:* loosened fluid and debris move outward.
6. **Wipe only what you can see.** Use cotton pads on the ear flap and canal entrance; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not push tools down the canal where they can damage the eardrum. → *Expect:* visible wax is removed without scraping or pain.

## Decision points

- If the ear is painful, smells bad, has dark coffee-ground debris, pus, swelling, or head tilt → stop and see a vet.
- If the dog swims often or has allergies → ask the vet how often cleaning is appropriate.
- If cleaner causes strong pain → stop immediately and contact a vet.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Infection signs:** redness, odor, discharge, head shaking, or pain is present → do not keep cleaning; arrange veterinary diagnosis and medication.
- **F2 Cleaner contamination:** bottle tip touches the ear or debris → wipe the tip clean before storage and avoid contact next time.
- **F3 Dog resists:** the dog yelps, snaps, or pulls hard → stop, avoid restraint escalation, and seek vet or groomer help.

## Verification

Visible outer-ear wax is reduced, no tool entered deep into the canal, and the dog has no increased pain, head tilt, discharge, or persistent shaking afterward.

## Variations

- `floppy-ear`: lift the ear flap long enough for air and visibility before wiping.
- `post-swim`: use only products and frequency recommended by a vet.
- `chronic-ear-dog`: follow the veterinary treatment plan rather than routine cleaning advice.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk comes from ear injury and missed infection. Dogs with painful, smelly, swollen, or draining ears need a veterinarian, not repeated home cleaning.
