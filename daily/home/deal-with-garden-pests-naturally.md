## Goal

Reduce garden pest damage using identification, monitoring, barriers, hand removal, plant health, and least-toxic controls before stronger interventions.

## Preconditions

- Supplies may include gloves, bucket of soapy water, hose, row cover, pruning snips, sticky cards, and a hand lens.
- You can inspect plants closely, including leaf undersides and new growth.
- You are willing to tolerate minor cosmetic damage that does not threaten harvest or plant survival.

## Steps

1. **Identify the pest and damage.** Look for insects, eggs, frass, chewed edges, stippling, slime trails, or webbing before acting. → *Expect:* the likely pest and affected plants are named.
2. **Decide whether action is needed.** Compare damage to plant vigor, crop stage, and predator presence. → *Expect:* minor damage is monitored and serious damage is targeted.
3. **Remove pests by hand.** Pick large caterpillars, beetles, and eggs into soapy water, or prune heavily infested shoots. → *Expect:* visible pest numbers drop immediately.
4. **Use water and barriers.** Blast aphids with water, install row cover, collars, netting, or copper barriers where suited. → *Expect:* new damage slows without spraying.
5. **Improve growing conditions.** Water consistently, mulch, thin crowded plants, and remove diseased debris. → *Expect:* plants recover with better airflow and less stress.
6. **Invite beneficial insects.** Keep flowering herbs and small blooms nearby and avoid broad-spectrum insecticides. → *Expect:* lady beetles, lacewings, hoverflies, wasps, or spiders are not harmed.
7. **Apply least-toxic products only if needed.** Use insecticidal soap, horticultural oil, Bt, or iron phosphate according to the label and pest type. → *Expect:* the treatment matches the pest and avoids open flowers when possible.
8. **Recheck after several days.** Inspect the same leaves and new growth to confirm damage is slowing. → *Expect:* pest activity is reduced or the plan is adjusted.

## Decision points

- Pest is not identified → delay treatment and use photos, extension guides, or local nursery advice.
- Pollinators are active on flowers → do not spray; use hand removal or evening non-bloom treatments only if the label allows.
- Damage is from mammals or birds → use fencing, netting, or exclusion rather than insect controls.
- Plant is near harvest → check product pre-harvest intervals and use physical controls when unsure.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong pest treated:** detect continued damage with no matching dead or reduced pest → re-identify from fresh damage and change method.
- **F2 Beneficial insects harmed:** detect predators disappearing after spraying → stop broad sprays and rebuild habitat with flowers and shelter.
- **F3 Row cover overheats plants:** detect wilting under cover in hot sun → vent, remove during heat, or switch to insect netting.
- **F4 Pest returns quickly:** detect new eggs or colonies weekly → repeat manual removal and break life cycles with sanitation or rotation.

## Verification

The pest is identified, plant damage is no longer increasing rapidly, chosen controls match the pest, and pollinators or beneficial insects are not being exposed to broad sprays.

## Variations

- `slugs-snails`: hand-pick at dusk, remove hiding boards, and use iron phosphate bait if needed.
- `aphids`: rinse off colonies and protect natural predators from sprays.
- `cabbage-worms`: inspect brassicas, remove eggs, and use Bt only on caterpillar feeding plants.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wear gloves, follow all product labels, keep sprays off edible harvest portions when possible, and store pest products away from children and pets.
