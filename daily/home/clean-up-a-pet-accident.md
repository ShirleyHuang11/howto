## Goal

Remove pet urine, feces, or vomit from a household surface with odor control and hygiene while watching for signs of illness.

## Preconditions

- Gloves, paper towels or cloths, bags, enzymatic pet cleaner, and a washable barrier are available.
- The pet is safely away from the cleaning area.
- The surface can tolerate moisture and the cleaner's label instructions.

## Steps

1. **Check the pet first.** Look for repeated vomiting, blood, straining, weakness, or toxin exposure before focusing on the mess. → *Expect:* urgent health concerns are either absent or routed to a vet.
2. **Remove solids.** Wearing gloves, pick up feces or vomit without rubbing it into the surface. → *Expect:* bulk material is bagged and the stain is not spread.
3. **Blot liquids.** Press towels into urine or liquid vomit until no more transfers easily. → *Expect:* moisture is reduced before cleaner is added.
4. **Apply enzymatic cleaner.** Saturate the affected area according to the label and allow the required contact time. → *Expect:* odor-causing residue is being broken down rather than perfumed.
5. **Dry and block access.** Let the area air-dry fully and keep pets away until dry. → *Expect:* the pet cannot lick cleaner or reuse the damp spot.
6. **Wash hands and tools.** Launder washable items and dispose of waste securely. → *Expect:* hygiene is restored and supplies are not cross-contaminated.

## Decision points

- If urine accidents are new, frequent, bloody, or paired with straining → contact a vet promptly.
- If vomiting or diarrhea repeats, contains blood, or the pet is weak → seek veterinary advice.
- If the surface is delicate wool, leather, or untreated wood → test cleaner in a hidden area or use a professional cleaner.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Odor remains:** the pet returns to sniff or mark the spot → repeat enzymatic saturation and increase drying time.
- **F2 Stain spreads:** scrubbing pushes material outward → blot from the outside inward and avoid rubbing.
- **F3 Medical accident:** accidents repeat after cleaning → schedule a veterinary check rather than assuming behavior.

## Verification

The visible mess is removed, the area smells neutral after drying, the pet is blocked from cleaner exposure, and any repeated or abnormal symptoms have a veterinary plan.

## Variations

- `carpet`: use enough enzymatic cleaner to reach the pad if urine soaked through.
- `hard-floor`: wipe seams and baseboards where liquid can travel.
- `bedding`: rinse solids, pre-treat, and launder on the hottest safe setting.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk is mostly hygiene and cleaner exposure. Avoid ammonia-based cleaners for urine odor, keep pets away from wet chemicals, and call a vet for blood, straining, repeated vomiting or diarrhea, or sudden accidents.
