## Goal

Build and install a sturdy garden trellis that supports climbing crops or vines without collapsing, shading the wrong plants, or damaging roots.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: stakes, posts, bamboo, lumber, cattle panel, twine, netting, screws, clips, mallet, drill or driver, gloves, and eye protection as needed.
- The crop's mature height and weight are known.
- Underground utilities, irrigation lines, and nearby roots are avoided before driving posts.

## Steps

1. **Match design to crop.** Choose a teepee, A-frame, panel, or vertical trellis based on crop weight and height. → *Expect:* the structure is sized for mature vines.
2. **Place the trellis before roots spread.** Set it at planting time or while plants are still small. → *Expect:* supports do not crush established roots.
3. **Anchor the main supports.** Drive stakes or posts deep enough to resist wind and wet soil movement. → *Expect:* uprights stand firm when pushed.
4. **Add crosspieces or mesh.** Attach horizontal supports, netting, twine, or panel securely with plant-safe spacing. → *Expect:* vines have reachable places to climb.
5. **Check sun and access.** Position tall trellises on the north or shaded side of lower crops where appropriate. → *Expect:* the trellis will not unnecessarily shade sun-loving plants.
6. **Guide young vines.** Tie stems loosely or weave tendrils onto the support without tightening around growth. → *Expect:* plants begin climbing in the intended direction.
7. **Reinforce under load.** Add guy lines, extra stakes, or braces before heavy fruit develops. → *Expect:* the trellis remains stable as weight increases.
8. **Inspect through the season.** Tighten loose fasteners and cut off ties that begin to constrict stems. → *Expect:* support remains secure without girdling plants.

## Decision points

- Crop is heavy, such as melons or winter squash → use rigid panels and slings for fruit.
- Crop has tendrils, such as peas or cucumbers → provide thin mesh or twine.
- Site is windy → use deeper posts and diagonal bracing.
- Trellis is near a wall → leave airflow space to reduce disease.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Trellis leans:** detect posts shifting after wind or watering → drive additional stakes and add diagonal bracing.
- **F2 Vines do not climb:** detect stems sprawling on soil → guide with loose ties and add finer netting.
- **F3 Fruit pulls vines down:** detect sagging stems or torn tendrils → add slings and stronger cross supports.
- **F4 Ties girdle stems:** detect tight loops cutting into growth → remove and retie loosely with soft material.

## Verification

The trellis is anchored, sized for the crop, provides climbable surfaces, does not block necessary access, and supports vines without tight ties or leaning.

## Variations

- `beans`: use tall poles, teepees, or netting at least 6 feet high.
- `peas`: use shorter twiggy branches, string, or mesh before tendrils form.
- `tomatoes`: use rigid cages, stakes, or Florida weave rather than delicate netting.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from drills, mallets, sharp wire, and falling supports. Wear gloves and eye protection, confirm post locations before driving, and keep trellises clear of walkways.
