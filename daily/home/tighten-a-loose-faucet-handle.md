## Goal

A loose faucet handle is tightened so it turns the valve cleanly without wobbling or leaking.

## Preconditions

- You have hex keys, screwdriver, towel, flashlight, and plumber's grease if the cap must be removed.
- You know the under-sink shutoff locations before starting.
- Call a plumber if water leaks from the handle body, the shutoff valve leaks, or the cartridge stem is damaged.

## Steps

1. **Close the drain.** Cover the drain with a stopper or towel to catch small screws. → *Expect:* dropped hardware cannot enter the drain.
2. **Find the set screw.** Look under the handle, behind a cap, or beneath a decorative button. → *Expect:* the fastener location is visible.
3. **Expose the screw.** Remove the cap gently with a fingernail or plastic tool. → *Expect:* the screw head is accessible without scratched finish.
4. **Tighten the handle.** Turn the set screw or handle screw clockwise until snug. → *Expect:* the handle no longer rocks on the stem.
5. **Test movement.** Turn the water on and off through the handle's full range. → *Expect:* the handle moves smoothly and stops firmly.
6. **Reinstall the cap.** Press the decorative cap back into place. → *Expect:* the cap sits flush and covers the screw.
7. **Check for leaks.** Run water and inspect around the handle and under the sink. → *Expect:* no water appears around the handle base or supply connections.

## Decision points

- Handle screw spins without tightening → the screw or stem threads may be stripped.
- Handle is tight but dripping continues → replace the cartridge or call a plumber.
- Cap will not come off safely → check the faucet manual to avoid damaging trim.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stripped screw:** tool slips in the set screw → use the exact-size hex key or replace the screw.
- **F2 Loose stem:** handle tightens but valve stem wobbles → shut off water and inspect the cartridge retaining nut.
- **F3 Finish scratches:** metal tool marks trim → stop prying and switch to a plastic tool.

## Verification

The faucet handle has no side-to-side wobble, turns water fully on and off, and shows no leak during one minute of running water.

## Variations

- Single-handle faucets: set screws are often under the front or rear of the lever.
- Two-handle faucets: decorative caps may hide Phillips screws on top.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk: small leaks can damage cabinets. Know where the shutoffs are, avoid overtightening, and call a plumber for active leaks.
