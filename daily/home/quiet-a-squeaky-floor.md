## Goal

A localized squeaky floor is quieted by fastening loose subfloor or floorboards without damaging visible flooring.

## Preconditions

- You have a stud finder or joist finder, masking tape, drill, trim screws or squeak-repair kit, wood shims if working from below, and helper.
- The squeak is localized and the floor is structurally sound.
- Call a flooring pro if the floor sags, feels soft, has water damage, or contains radiant heat tubing.

## Steps

1. **Locate the squeak.** Walk slowly while a helper marks the loudest spot with tape. → *Expect:* the squeak point is marked.
2. **Find joist direction.** Use a stud finder, floor registers, or basement view to identify joist locations. → *Expect:* the nearest joist line is marked.
3. **Choose access.** [BRANCH: from above | from below] use surface screws from above or shims and adhesive from an exposed basement ceiling. → *Expect:* the least visible repair path is selected.
4. **Drill a pilot from above.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm no radiant heat, wiring, or plumbing is below before drilling into the floor. → *Expect:* the pilot enters solid joist material.
5. **Drive the repair screw.** Use a breakaway squeak screw or trim screw into the joist. → *Expect:* the board pulls tight without dimpling badly.
6. **Shim from below if accessible.** Apply wood glue to a thin shim and tap it lightly between subfloor and joist. → *Expect:* the gap closes without lifting the floor.
7. **Conceal the repair.** Break off special screw heads or fill trim-screw holes with matching filler. → *Expect:* the visible repair is small and flush.
8. **Retest the area.** Walk over the marked spot and nearby boards. → *Expect:* the squeak is gone or significantly reduced.

## Decision points

- Floor has tile or stone → do not drive surface screws; diagnose from below or call a pro.
- Squeak spans a wide area → subfloor fastening or structural work may be needed.
- You cannot identify joists → stop before drilling visible flooring.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Screw misses joist:** squeak remains and screw feels loose → remove it, fill the hole, and re-mark joist center.
- **F2 Floor lifts:** shim is too thick → remove it before glue sets and use a thinner shim.
- **F3 Finish damage:** screw hole is obvious → use color-matched filler or a professional floor repair kit.

## Verification

The marked floor area no longer squeaks during normal walking, and any fastener hole is flush and stable.

## Variations

- Carpeted floors: use a squeak kit with depth guards and breakaway screws.
- Hardwood floors: place fasteners carefully and fill with color-matched putty.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk: drilling into floors can hit hidden utilities or radiant heat. Confirm what is below before drilling and avoid structural repairs beyond localized squeaks.
