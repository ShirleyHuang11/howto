## Goal

Reduce fleas on a pet and in the home using species-safe treatment, cleaning, and veterinary guidance when infestation or illness is present.

## Preconditions

- The pet's species, age, weight, pregnancy status, and current medications are known.
- A vet-approved flea product for that exact species is available or can be obtained.
- Household pets can all be checked because untreated animals can restart the infestation.

## Steps

1. **Confirm fleas.** Part the coat near the neck, tail base, and belly, looking for moving fleas or black flea dirt that reddens when wet. → *Expect:* evidence of fleas is found or another skin problem is suspected.
2. **Choose a species-safe product.** Read the label for species, weight, age, dose, and repeat interval; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* never put dog-only permethrin products on cats. → *Expect:* the treatment matches the pet exactly.
3. **Apply or give the treatment as labeled.** Follow the product directions and prevent licking or contact between pets until dry if topical. → *Expect:* the pet receives the full correct dose without cross-exposure.
4. **Clean the environment.** Wash bedding on hot if fabric allows and vacuum carpets, cracks, furniture, and sleeping areas. → *Expect:* eggs, larvae, and flea dirt are physically reduced.
5. **Repeat cleaning and prevention.** Vacuum frequently and maintain the product schedule for all household pets. → *Expect:* new flea sightings decline over several weeks.
6. **Watch health signs.** Check for pale gums, weakness, skin infection, hair loss, tapeworm segments, or severe itching. → *Expect:* illness triggers veterinary care instead of more home treatment.

## Decision points

- If the pet is very young, old, pregnant, sick, or underweight → ask a vet before treatment.
- If using home sprays or foggers → follow label safety exactly and remove pets and people as directed.
- If fleas persist despite correct treatment → treat all pets, intensify cleaning, and call a vet or pest professional.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong species product:** a cat contacts dog flea medication or any pet gets the wrong dose → wash if directed by a vet and call emergency veterinary help or poison control.
- **F2 Flea anemia:** pale gums, weakness, or heavy flea load appears → seek urgent veterinary care.
- **F3 Reinfestation:** fleas return after a week → continue prevention, vacuum life stages, and treat every eligible pet on schedule.

## Verification

The pet has received a correct species-safe flea treatment, bedding and floors have been cleaned, all household pets are accounted for, and flea sightings decrease without signs of illness.

## Variations

- `cat-household`: confirm every product is labeled for cats before opening it.
- `multi-pet`: separate pets after topical treatment until dry.
- `rental`: coordinate pest treatment with lease rules and pet safety instructions.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk includes toxic products, anemia, skin infection, and chemical exposure. See a vet for kittens, puppies, sick pets, severe itching, pale gums, or any suspected product poisoning.
