## Goal

Prepare and manage dog-baby introductions so the dog stays under control and the baby is never exposed to preventable risk.

## Preconditions

- The dog has no unmanaged aggression, severe fear, or bite history around children without professional help.
- Baby gates, leash, mat or crate, treats, and a separate dog rest area are ready.
- Adults agree that dog and baby are never left together unsupervised.

## Steps

1. **Practice before arrival.** Reward the dog for settling on a mat, walking beside baby gear, and hearing baby sounds at low volume. → *Expect:* baby-related cues begin to predict calm rewards.
2. **Refresh routines.** Arrange walking, feeding, toileting, and rest so the dog is not ignored or frantic. → *Expect:* basic needs are met before introductions.
3. **Bring home scent first.** Let the dog sniff a baby blanket while calm and then redirect to a treat or mat. → *Expect:* the dog investigates briefly without grabbing or guarding.
4. **Use barriers for first viewing.** Keep the dog leashed or behind a gate while an adult holds the baby at a distance. → *Expect:* the dog can look, sniff the air, and reorient to the handler.
5. **Reward calm proximity.** Feed treats for relaxed body language, soft eyes, and settling away from the baby. → *Expect:* calm distance becomes the default behavior.
6. **Maintain separation rules.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* a bite can permanently injure a baby, so never allow unsupervised contact or face-level access. → *Expect:* every dog-baby moment has an awake adult controlling distance.

## Decision points

- If the dog stiffens, growls, guards, lunges, or fixates → increase distance and contact a qualified professional.
- If adults are exhausted or distracted → use gates, crates, or separate rooms instead of trusting supervision.
- If the dog has bitten or threatened children before → get professional help before the baby comes home.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Fixation:** the dog stares, stalks, or cannot respond to cues → block view, increase distance, and seek behavior help.
- **F2 Resource guarding:** dog guards baby items, food, or resting spots → remove conflict access and consult a professional.
- **F3 Supervision lapse:** dog and baby are found together unattended → separate immediately and change barriers so it cannot recur.

## Verification

The baby and dog are separated by management unless an awake adult is actively supervising, and the dog can remain calm, respond to cues, and disengage near baby-related sights, sounds, and smells.

## Variations

- `small-home`: use gates, closed doors, and tethered adult handling to create distance.
- `multiple-dogs`: introduce one dog at a time and maintain separate rest areas.
- `reactive-dog`: start with a vet behaviorist or qualified trainer before direct introductions.

## Safety & privacy

High risk involves infant injury and dog stress. Never leave a baby alone with any dog, and seek professional veterinary behavior or trainer support for growling, bites, guarding, or fixation.
