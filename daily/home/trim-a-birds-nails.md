## Goal

Shorten a bird's overgrown nail tips only when it can be done calmly and safely, with bleeding control ready and a vet option preferred for uncertain cases.

## Preconditions

- Appropriate small nail trimmers or an emery board, styptic powder, towel, good light, and a warm quiet room are ready.
- You know how to hold the bird without compressing the chest.
- The bird is not weak, breathing hard, injured, or excessively stressed.

## Steps

1. **Decide if home trimming is appropriate.** Inspect nail length, foot posture, and your confidence. → *Expect:* simple sharp tips can be handled, while curled, cracked, or embedded nails are referred to an avian vet.
2. **Prepare safe restraint.** Wrap the body lightly in a towel while keeping the chest free to expand. → *Expect:* the bird is contained but breathing normally.
3. **Identify the quick.** Use strong light to find the blood supply when visible and plan a tiny tip trim only. → *Expect:* the cut line is well away from living tissue.
4. **Trim one tiny tip.** Clip or file only the point; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* a deep cut can bleed seriously in a small bird. → *Expect:* the nail is less sharp and no bleeding starts.
5. **Check breathing and stress.** Pause after each nail and release the bird if panting, weakness, or frantic struggling appears. → *Expect:* the bird remains alert, upright, and breathing comfortably.
6. **Return and monitor.** Put the bird back in the cage with stable perches and observe feet and behavior. → *Expect:* the bird perches normally and no nail is bleeding.

## Decision points

- If you cannot see the quick or the bird is small, fragile, or panicked → use an avian vet or experienced groomer.
- If nails overgrow repeatedly → review perch sizes and health with a vet.
- If bleeding starts → treat immediately and seek veterinary help if it does not stop quickly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Nail bleeding:** red blood appears → apply styptic powder with gentle pressure and call an avian vet if bleeding continues.
- **F2 Breathing impaired:** open-mouth breathing, weakness, or collapse appears → release restraint and seek emergency avian care.
- **F3 Foot injury found:** swelling, sores, or abnormal grip is noticed → stop trimming and schedule an avian vet visit.

## Verification

Only nail tips are shortened, every nail is dry with no bleeding, and the bird is alert, breathing normally, and perching after the session.

## Variations

- `small-bird`: prefer filing or an avian professional because blood loss tolerance is low.
- `large-parrot`: use trained cooperative handling or a vet to reduce bite and restraint risk.
- `dark-nails`: remove only the smallest hook or file the tip.

## Safety & privacy

High risk comes from bleeding, restraint injury, and respiratory stress. Birds hide illness; use an avian vet for uncertain trims, abnormal feet, weakness, breathing changes, or uncontrolled bleeding.
