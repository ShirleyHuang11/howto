## Goal

Set up a reptile enclosure with species-appropriate heat, light, humidity, substrate, hides, and safety before the animal moves in.

## Preconditions

- The exact reptile species, age, adult size, diet, temperature range, humidity range, and UVB need are known from reputable care guidance or a reptile vet.
- Enclosure, thermostat, thermometers, hygrometer, hides, water dish, substrate, lighting, and heat source are available.
- The enclosure can run and stabilize before the reptile is added.

## Steps

1. **Place the enclosure.** Set it away from direct sun, drafts, fumes, and unstable furniture. → *Expect:* the habitat is level, secure, and not overheated by windows.
2. **Install controlled heat.** Connect heat sources through a thermostat and place probes where the animal will experience heat. → *Expect:* the warm side and cool side can be measured separately.
3. **Add light and UVB if needed.** Mount bulbs at the species-appropriate distance and replace old UVB bulbs by schedule. → *Expect:* basking and UV access match the species plan.
4. **Build the interior.** Add safe substrate, at least two hides, water, climbing or basking structures, and humidity support. → *Expect:* the animal will have a warm hide, cool hide, and secure movement.
5. **Run and measure.** Operate the setup for a full day and record temperature and humidity at several times. → *Expect:* readings stay inside the target range before the reptile arrives.
6. **Move the reptile in calmly.** Transfer the reptile after parameters are stable; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not house incompatible reptiles together or use unregulated heat rocks. → *Expect:* the reptile can hide, thermoregulate, and access water.

## Decision points

- If temperatures or humidity cannot stabilize → fix equipment before adding the animal.
- If the species requires UVB, brumation, aquatic filtration, or high humidity → follow species-specific veterinary guidance.
- If the reptile stops eating, loses weight, wheezes, or has stuck shed → consult a reptile veterinarian.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overheating:** basking area exceeds the species range → shut off heat, adjust thermostat or bulb distance, and remeasure.
- **F2 Low humidity:** shed sticks or hygrometer reads low → add moisture sources, change substrate strategy, and verify ventilation.
- **F3 Unsafe substrate:** loose substrate is eaten or causes dust → switch to a safer substrate and call a vet for suspected impaction.

## Verification

The enclosure holds species-appropriate warm-side, cool-side, basking, nighttime, humidity, hide, water, substrate, and lighting conditions for at least 24 hours before or after move-in.

## Variations

- `desert-species`: prioritize strong basking gradient, dry zones, and correct UVB.
- `tropical-species`: prioritize humidity, ventilation balance, and mold control.
- `aquatic-turtle`: add filtration, basking dock, water heater if needed, and UVB.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk includes burns, dehydration, respiratory disease, salmonella exposure, and escape. Wash hands after handling, keep reptiles away from food-prep areas, and see a reptile vet for illness or husbandry uncertainty.
