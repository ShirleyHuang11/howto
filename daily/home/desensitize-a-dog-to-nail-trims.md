## Goal

Teach a dog to tolerate nail-trim handling gradually, so future trims can happen with less fear and less restraint.

## Preconditions

- High-value treats, nail clippers or grinder, and a non-slip surface are available.
- Current nails are not painfully overgrown, cracked, bleeding, or embedded.
- Sessions can stay under a few minutes and stop before panic.

## Steps

1. **Pick a calm marker.** Use a clicker or short word followed by a treat. → *Expect:* the dog looks for food after the marker.
2. **Reward paw-adjacent touch.** Touch shoulder, leg, then paw for one second, staying below the dog's fear threshold. → *Expect:* the dog keeps eating and does not pull away hard.
3. **Introduce the tool at a distance.** Show the clipper or grinder, mark calm looking, and put it away. → *Expect:* the tool predicts food instead of restraint.
4. **Pair tool sound with food.** Click clippers in the air or run the grinder far away, then feed. → *Expect:* the dog hears the sound and remains able to eat.
5. **Touch nail without cutting.** Tap the tool to one nail, mark, feed, and stop. → *Expect:* brief nail contact is tolerated.
6. **Trim one tiny tip only when ready.** Clip or grind a minimal amount; ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not cut deeper to make up for missed sessions. → *Expect:* one nail changes slightly and the dog recovers immediately.

## Decision points

- If the dog cannot eat during a step → make the step easier.
- If nails are already causing pain or posture problems → use a vet or groomer while training continues separately.
- If the dog bites or has severe panic → work with a qualified trainer, behavior professional, or veterinarian.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Threshold exceeded:** panting, freezing, growling, or food refusal appears → stop and restart later at an easier step.
- **F2 Quick cut memory:** dog becomes worse after a painful trim → return to tool-at-distance games and consider professional trimming.
- **F3 Slippery setup:** paws slide or the dog feels trapped → add traction and allow an exit cue.

## Verification

The dog can calmly accept the current training step, such as paw touch, tool sound, nail tap, or one tiny trim, while still taking treats and recovering quickly.

## Variations

- `grinder`: introduce vibration and sound separately before touching nails.
- `scratch-board`: teach the dog to file front nails on sandpaper as a cooperative alternative.
- `puppy`: keep sessions playful and stop after seconds.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if thresholds are respected. Avoid punishment and forced restraint; see a vet for painful nails, bleeding, limping, infection, or sedation questions.
