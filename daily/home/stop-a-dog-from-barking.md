## Goal

Reduce problem barking by identifying the trigger, meeting the dog's needs, and rewarding quiet alternatives instead of punishment.

## Preconditions

- The dog has daily exercise, toileting, food, water, and safe rest.
- You can observe when barking happens and what starts it.
- Pain, sudden behavior change, or senior confusion has been discussed with a vet.

## Steps

1. **Log the pattern.** Note time, trigger, location, body language, and duration for several barking episodes. → *Expect:* one or two main causes become visible.
2. **Remove easy triggers.** Close curtains, add white noise, move the dog from fence lines, or block window access during peak times. → *Expect:* barking opportunities decrease immediately.
3. **Teach a quiet alternative.** Cue a mat, recall, hand target, or toy search before the dog explodes. → *Expect:* the dog has a specific behavior to do instead of barking.
4. **Reward quiet seconds.** Mark and treat pauses, looking back at you, or settling after one alert bark. → *Expect:* quiet behavior begins to pay better than continued barking.
5. **Practice below threshold.** Use distant, mild triggers and increase difficulty only when the dog can recover. → *Expect:* the dog notices the trigger and then reorients to you.
6. **Avoid fear tools.** Do not yell, shock, or punish anxious barking; use management, training, and professional help when needed. → *Expect:* barking is not replaced by fear, hiding, or aggression.

## Decision points

- If barking is from separation distress → use a separation plan and consult a vet or qualified behavior professional.
- If barking includes lunging, biting, or panic → prioritize safety and professional help.
- If barking is new or paired with pain, disorientation, or hearing loss → schedule a vet exam.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Trigger too close:** the dog cannot eat, listen, or stop barking → increase distance, block the view, and restart easier.
- **F2 Accidental reward:** barking makes people open doors, throw toys, or shout attention → change the consequence and reward quiet first.
- **F3 Medical driver:** sudden barking or night barking appears → have a veterinarian check pain, senses, and cognitive changes.

## Verification

Barking episodes are shorter, less frequent, or easier to interrupt for the logged trigger, and the dog can perform a rewarded quiet alternative in that situation.

## Variations

- `apartment`: focus on white noise, hallway distance, and mat training near the door.
- `yard`: supervise outdoor time and bring the dog inside before fence barking escalates.
- `visitor-alert`: teach one alert bark followed by a station cue.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when force is avoided. Do not use punishment that increases fear or aggression; seek veterinary or behavior help for panic, bites, self-injury, or sudden behavior change.
