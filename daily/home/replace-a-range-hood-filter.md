## Goal

The range hood grease or charcoal filter is replaced or refreshed so airflow and odor capture improve.

## Preconditions

- You have the correct replacement filter or dishwasher-safe metal filter, gloves, towel, and the hood model number if needed.
- The cooktop and hood are cool before starting.
- Call appliance service if the hood fan sparks, vibrates heavily, or the filter frame is melted.

## Steps

1. **Turn off the hood.** Switch off fan and light, and let surfaces cool. → *Expect:* the hood is quiet and safe to touch.
2. **Remove the filter.** Slide, unlatch, or push the filter toward the spring side and lower it out. → *Expect:* the filter comes free without bending.
3. **Identify filter type.** Check whether it is washable metal mesh, disposable grease, or charcoal. → *Expect:* you know whether to wash or replace it.
4. **Clean the opening.** Wipe grease from the filter frame and surrounding hood lip. → *Expect:* new filter edges will sit on a clean surface.
5. **Install or wash.** Insert a new disposable or charcoal filter, or wash and dry a metal filter fully before reinstalling. → *Expect:* the filter is clean, dry, and correctly oriented.
6. **Lock it in place.** Seat the filter tabs or latch until the frame is flush. → *Expect:* the filter does not sag or rattle.
7. **Test airflow.** Run the fan on each speed and listen for vibration. → *Expect:* airflow is steady and the filter stays seated.

## Decision points

- Filter is charcoal → replace it; do not wash unless the manufacturer says it is washable.
- Filter size is unavailable → use the hood model number to order the exact part.
- Heavy grease is inside the hood body → clean carefully or schedule service before using high heat.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Filter falls out:** tabs do not engage → remove it and reinstall in the correct orientation.
- **F2 Fan rattles:** filter vibrates → reseat the frame or replace a bent filter.
- **F3 Odor remains:** recirculating hood still smells → replace the charcoal filter and clean grease surfaces.

## Verification

The filter sits flush, stays locked while the fan runs on high, and visible grease buildup is removed from the filter area.

## Variations

- Ducted hoods: usually use metal grease filters only.
- Recirculating hoods: usually need both grease and charcoal filtration.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk: grease is flammable and sharp filter edges can cut. Work on cool surfaces, wear gloves, and never run the hood without its grease filter.
