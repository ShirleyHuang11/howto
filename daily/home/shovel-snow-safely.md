## Goal

Clear snow from essential walkways and exits while reducing fall, back, and cardiac strain.

## Preconditions

- You have a shovel, warm layers, waterproof boots with traction, gloves, and a phone nearby.
- You know where steps, edges, drains, and driveway seams are under the snow.
- You are physically able to do moderate exertion, or you have arranged help.

## Steps

1. **Dress and warm up.** Put on traction footwear, layers, and gloves, then do a few minutes of gentle movement indoors. → *Expect:* you are warm, visible, and not starting cold.
2. **Prioritize exits.** Clear the door swing, steps, mailbox path, sidewalk, and a narrow driveway lane before cosmetic areas. → *Expect:* people can leave safely in an emergency.
3. **Push light snow first.** Use the shovel like a plow when snow is fluffy and under a few inches deep. → *Expect:* snow moves to the side without repeated lifting.
4. **Lift small loads correctly.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* snow shoveling can trigger serious injury or heart strain, so keep loads light, bend knees, and stop for chest pain, dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath. → *Expect:* each scoop moves without twisting your torso.
5. **Place snow where it will not refreeze into hazards.** Pile snow downhill from paths, away from drains, vents, fire hydrants, and the street. → *Expect:* meltwater has a route away from walking surfaces.
6. **Take timed breaks.** [BRANCH: light snow | heavy wet snow] pause every 15 minutes for light snow or every 5-10 minutes for wet snow. → *Expect:* breathing and grip recover before the next section.
7. **Treat remaining ice.** Scrape compacted areas and add sand, grit, or ice melt suitable for the surface and pets. → *Expect:* cleared surfaces have traction, not a slick glaze.

## Decision points

- Snow is wet, deep, or plow-packed → clear in layers or hire help.
- You feel chest pressure, radiating pain, faintness, or nausea → stop immediately and seek emergency help.
- A snowblower is used → follow its manual and clear jams only with the engine off and tool provided.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Back pain starts:** detect sharp or worsening pain → stop, switch to pushing, and resume only with smaller loads or help.
- **F2 Ice remains:** detect shiny patches after shoveling → apply grit or ice melt and recheck after it works.
- **F3 Door blocked again:** detect drifting or plow return → reclear exit paths first, then widen later.
- **F4 Meltwater refreezes:** detect water flowing across cleared path → redirect snow piles and treat the path before evening.

## Verification

Primary exits, steps, sidewalk, and needed driveway access are clear to the surface or treated for traction, with no snow blocking vents, hydrants, drains, or door swings.

## Variations

- `apartment`: clear only assigned steps or stoop and notify maintenance for shared sidewalks.
- `pet-household`: use pet-safer ice melt sparingly and wipe paws after walks.
- `heavy-storm`: shovel in repeated passes during the storm instead of waiting for the full accumulation.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from exertion, slips, cold exposure, and hidden obstacles. Stop at symptoms of cardiac distress, keep a phone accessible, avoid twisting lifts, and ask for help when snow is heavy or health risk is elevated.
