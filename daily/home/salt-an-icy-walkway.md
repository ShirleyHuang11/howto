## Goal

Improve traction on an icy walkway using the right amount of ice melt, sand, or grit for the surface and temperature.

## Preconditions

- You have ice melt, sand, or grit; gloves; and a shovel or scraper.
- The walkway, steps, and pet routes are visible enough to treat.
- You know whether nearby concrete is new, sealed, brick, stone, or metal.

## Steps

1. **Remove loose snow first.** Shovel or sweep powder off steps and the path before applying any product. → *Expect:* ice patches are visible and product will reach the slippery surface.
2. **Choose the treatment.** [BRANCH: above product rating | colder than rating] use ice melt within its temperature range, or use sand/grit for traction when melting will not work. → *Expect:* the chosen material matches the weather and walkway.
3. **Apply sparingly and evenly.** Scatter granules by hand with gloves or a cup, focusing on steps, slope changes, and the main walking line. → *Expect:* granules are spaced like light seasoning, not piled.
4. **Wait and scrape.** Give the product time to loosen the bond, then scrape slush away from the walking surface. → *Expect:* ice breaks up and the path exposes pavement or rough traction.
5. **Protect pets and plants.** Keep product away from lawn edges, garden beds, and dog potty spots where possible. → *Expect:* treated areas are limited to necessary walking zones.
6. **Recheck at dusk.** Look for meltwater refreezing before temperatures drop again. → *Expect:* new slick spots are treated or gritted before nighttime use.

## Decision points

- Concrete is less than one winter old → prefer sand or calcium-magnesium products labeled for concrete.
- Pets use the walkway → choose pet-safer product, use less, and wipe paws.
- Ice is thick and bonded → repeat scrape-and-treat cycles rather than dumping more salt.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Product does not melt:** detect unchanged ice after the label's wait time → scrape what you can and switch to sand or grit.
- **F2 White residue appears:** detect crust on concrete or entry floors → sweep excess and rinse only when water will not refreeze.
- **F3 Plants are burned:** detect brown grass at walkway edges → reduce application and shovel slush away from beds.
- **F4 Slush refreezes:** detect wet shine after sunset → scrape runoff and add traction grit.

## Verification

The main walking line has visible traction or exposed surface, steps are treated, excess product is swept from edges, and no meltwater is flowing across the path.

## Variations

- `wooden-steps`: use sand or grit; many salts damage wood and fasteners.
- `steep-walkway`: prioritize traction grit and handrail access over melting alone.
- `shared-sidewalk`: treat the full pedestrian width required by local rules.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from falls, chemical irritation, pet paw burns, plant damage, and concrete scaling. Wear gloves, use the smallest effective amount, and store ice melt away from children and animals.
