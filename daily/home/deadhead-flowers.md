## Goal

Remove spent flowers at the correct point so plants look tidy, direct energy away from seed production, and may bloom again.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: clean snips or pruners, gloves, and a bucket for spent blooms.
- The plant is identified well enough to know whether it reblooms, sets useful seed, or should be left for wildlife.
- Foliage is dry enough to reduce disease spread while cutting.

## Steps

1. **Identify spent blooms.** Look for faded petals, browning heads, or seed pods forming after flowers finish. → *Expect:* only finished flowers are targeted.
2. **Find the next cut point.** Trace the flower stem to the first healthy leaf, bud, branching point, or basal foliage. → *Expect:* cuts will not leave long bare stems.
3. **Use clean sharp snips.** Cut just above the chosen leaf or bud without crushing the stem. → *Expect:* the cut is neat and angled slightly away from buds.
4. **Pinch soft annuals.** For tender stems such as petunias or marigolds, pinch spent blooms with fingertips if easier. → *Expect:* the old flower and seed base are removed.
5. **Leave useful seed heads.** Skip flowers you want for seed, bird food, winter interest, or self-sowing. → *Expect:* selected heads remain on the plant.
6. **Remove diseased material.** Put mildewed or spotted blooms in trash rather than compost. → *Expect:* disease pressure is not spread through compost.
7. **Water or feed if needed.** After heavy deadheading, water dry plants and fertilize repeat bloomers only as appropriate. → *Expect:* plants have resources for new buds.
8. **Repeat during bloom season.** Check weekly for new spent flowers. → *Expect:* the display stays tidy and rebloomers keep producing buds.

## Decision points

- Plant blooms once and has attractive seed heads → leave some or all heads standing.
- Plant is a repeat-blooming annual → deadhead regularly to reduce seed set.
- Plant spreads aggressively by seed → remove heads before seeds mature.
- Weather is wet and disease-prone → clean tools between plants and remove debris promptly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Buds are removed accidentally:** detect missing unopened buds → cut less aggressively and identify buds before snipping.
- **F2 Long stubs remain:** detect bare stems above leaves → recut to a leaf node or branching point.
- **F3 Plant stops blooming:** detect no new buds despite deadheading → check season, light, water, fertility, and plant type.
- **F4 Disease spreads:** detect spots or mildew after cutting → dispose of infected debris and sanitize tools.

## Verification

Spent flowers and seed bases are removed to appropriate leaf or bud points, desired seed heads are intentionally left, and the plant has no crushed stems or diseased debris left beneath it.

## Variations

- `roses`: cut above an outward-facing leaflet with clean pruners.
- `daisies-coneflowers`: leave some late-season seed heads for birds if desired.
- `annual-containers`: combine deadheading with light trimming and watering.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wear gloves for thorns or irritating sap, use sharp snips carefully, and discard diseased flowers away from active beds.
