## Goal

Support a leaning garden or house plant so stems stand upright while roots and trunks can still move and grow normally.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: bamboo stake, metal stake, wooden post, soft ties, mallet, scissors, and gloves.
- The plant is worth supporting and is not collapsing from severe rot or breakage.
- The stake can be inserted without damaging buried utilities, irrigation, or major roots.

## Steps

1. **Find why the plant leans.** Check for wind, heavy blooms, weak light, loose roots, dry soil, or broken stems. → *Expect:* the cause is understood before staking.
2. **Choose the support.** Use a stake shorter than the mature top for small stems or a stronger post for shrubs and top-heavy plants. → *Expect:* support strength matches plant size.
3. **Insert the stake off-center.** Place it on the windward side or behind the lean, a few inches from the stem. → *Expect:* the stake is firm without piercing the main crown.
4. **Straighten gradually.** Move the plant upright only as far as it bends without cracking. → *Expect:* stems improve position without snapping.
5. **Tie with soft loops.** Use figure-eight ties or flexible plant tape around the stake and stem. → *Expect:* the stem is supported but not tightly bound.
6. **Stabilize the root zone.** Firm loose soil, water deeply if dry, and add mulch if roots were disturbed. → *Expect:* the base no longer rocks easily.
7. **Remove excess weight.** Deadhead heavy flowers or prune broken growth that pulls the plant over. → *Expect:* the plant has less top-heavy stress.
8. **Recheck ties regularly.** Loosen or remove ties as the stem thickens and the plant can stand. → *Expect:* support helps temporarily without girdling.

## Decision points

- Lean is caused by low light → move containers or prune nearby shade rather than relying only on a stake.
- Stem is cracked → splint gently or prune below the break if recovery is unlikely.
- Root ball rocks after planting → stake low and correct planting depth.
- Plant is a tree → use two or three flexible guy ties and remove them after establishment.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Tie cuts into stem:** detect constriction or bark damage → remove the tie and retie loosely with softer material.
- **F2 Stake falls over:** detect wobbling support → drive it deeper or use a larger stake and second anchor.
- **F3 Stem snaps during straightening:** detect cracking or collapse → prune damaged tissue and support remaining growth.
- **F4 Lean returns:** detect plant tilting after watering or wind → correct root instability, light imbalance, or top weight.

## Verification

The plant stands closer to upright, the stake is firm, ties are soft and loose enough for growth, and the root zone no longer rocks excessively.

## Variations

- `houseplant`: rotate toward light and use a moss pole or bamboo stake in the pot.
- `peony`: use a grow-through ring before buds become heavy.
- `young-tree`: stake only if unstable and allow slight trunk movement.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wear gloves, keep stakes from becoming eye-level hazards, and avoid driving supports where irrigation or utility lines may be present.
