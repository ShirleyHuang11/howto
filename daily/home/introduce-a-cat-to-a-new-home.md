## Goal

Help a cat settle into a new home gradually, with food, litter, hiding places, and territory introduced before full-house access.

## Preconditions

- One quiet starter room is prepared with litter, water, food, bedding, scratching surface, and hiding options.
- Windows, vents, fireplaces, toxic plants, cords, and small escape gaps are secured.
- Other pets and children can be kept out at first.

## Steps

1. **Start in one room.** Place the carrier in the prepared room and close the door before opening it. → *Expect:* the cat can exit into a small safe territory or stay hidden by choice.
2. **Let the cat choose distance.** Sit quietly and avoid pulling the cat from the carrier or hiding spot. → *Expect:* the cat sniffs, hides, or explores without being chased.
3. **Keep essentials predictable.** Put food, water, and litter in stable locations and scoop the box daily. → *Expect:* the cat can find resources without crossing scary open space.
4. **Add gentle interaction.** Offer play, treats, or soft talk only when the cat approaches or relaxes. → *Expect:* the cat shows curiosity, blinking, eating, grooming, or resting.
5. **Expand territory slowly.** Open one additional room after the cat is eating, using the litter box, and moving confidently. → *Expect:* the cat investigates and can retreat to the starter room.
6. **Introduce residents gradually.** Exchange scents before visual meetings with other pets and supervise children calmly. → *Expect:* meetings are brief and end before chasing, swatting, or cornering.

## Decision points

- If the cat has not eaten for about 24 hours, especially a kitten or overweight cat → call a vet.
- If hiding continues but the cat eats, drinks, and uses the litter box → give more time and reduce noise.
- If resident pets fixate or chase → return to scent-only introductions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Escape attempt:** the cat bolts toward a door or window → close access points, return to the starter room, and delay expansion.
- **F2 Litter miss:** urine or stool appears outside the box → add a second box, check access and litter type, and see a vet for repeated misses.
- **F3 Not eating:** food remains untouched for a day or the cat seems weak → contact a veterinarian promptly.

## Verification

The cat eats, drinks, uses the litter box, rests in the home, and can retreat to a safe room while new spaces and residents are introduced without forced contact.

## Variations

- `kitten`: use a smaller starter area and block tiny gaps behind appliances.
- `shy-cat`: keep the starter room for several weeks if needed.
- `multi-cat`: use scent swapping and short separated feeding before shared space.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if the cat controls distance. See a vet for not eating, repeated vomiting or diarrhea, breathing trouble, injury, or litter-box changes that could signal illness.
