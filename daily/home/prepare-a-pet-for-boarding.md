## Goal

Prepare a pet for boarding with health records, supplies, emergency instructions, and a lower-stress drop-off.

## Preconditions

- Boarding dates, facility requirements, vaccination rules, medication needs, and emergency contacts are known.
- The pet's ID tag and microchip information are current.
- The facility is licensed or reputable for the species and can handle the pet's medical or behavior needs.

## Steps

1. **Confirm requirements early.** Ask the facility for vaccines, parasite prevention, exam records, food rules, medication forms, and drop-off windows. → *Expect:* missing requirements are known before the travel week.
2. **Book needed vet care.** Schedule vaccines, refills, or health certificates with enough time for waiting periods. → *Expect:* records will be valid on boarding day.
3. **Prepare written instructions.** List feeding amounts, medication dose and timing, behavior notes, vet contact, and emergency spending authorization. → *Expect:* staff can follow care without guessing.
4. **Pack labeled supplies.** Portion food, medications in original containers, comfort item if allowed, leash or carrier, and records. → *Expect:* every item is labeled with pet name and instructions.
5. **Do a calm drop-off.** Exercise or toilet the pet first, keep goodbye brief, and hand instructions directly to staff. → *Expect:* the pet transfers safely and staff confirm intake.
6. **Stay reachable.** Keep phone access and respond quickly if the facility reports illness or stress. → *Expect:* urgent decisions do not stall.

## Decision points

- If the pet needs injections, seizure care, exotic care, or behavior precautions → confirm the facility has trained staff before booking.
- If the pet becomes sick before boarding → call the vet and facility before drop-off.
- If vaccines are overdue → allow enough time for protection and facility acceptance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing records:** facility refuses intake → contact the vet for records or arrange alternate care.
- **F2 Medication error risk:** instructions are unclear or pills are unlabeled → rewrite directions and use original containers.
- **F3 Boarding stress:** pet stops eating, panics, or becomes ill → authorize vet care and discuss future sitter or medical boarding options.

## Verification

The facility has accepted the pet with current records, labeled supplies, written care instructions, emergency contacts, and a reachable decision-maker.

## Variations

- `cat`: ask about quiet housing away from barking and bring familiar bedding if allowed.
- `dog`: confirm exercise, group-play rules, and temperament screening.
- `exotic-pet`: use species-specialized boarding or veterinary boarding.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk includes infectious disease, medication errors, escape, and emergency spending decisions. Share only necessary contact and veterinary data, and see a vet before boarding a sick or medically fragile pet.
