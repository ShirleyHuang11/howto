## Goal

A household emergency kit is assembled, stored accessibly, and scheduled for regular checks.

## Preconditions

- The household knows its likely hazards and how many people and pets the kit must support.
- Emergency supplies do not replace evacuation orders or emergency services.
- Store medicines, batteries, fuels, and tools safely away from children.
- Include accessibility, dietary, infant, pet, and medical needs.

## Steps

1. **Choose kit locations.** Pick accessible spots for home, vehicle, and go-bag if needed. → *Expect:* supplies can be reached in darkness or evacuation.
2. **Store water.** Add enough sealed water for drinking and basic hygiene for each person and pet. → *Expect:* water is present and dated.
3. **Add shelf-stable food.** Pack ready-to-eat food, manual can opener, and food for dietary needs. → *Expect:* meals can be eaten without power.
4. **Pack light and power.** Add flashlights, headlamps, batteries, power banks, and charging cables. → *Expect:* the kit works during an outage.
5. **Add first aid and hygiene.** Include first aid supplies, masks, sanitizer, soap, wipes, menstrual products, diapers, and trash bags. → *Expect:* basic care and sanitation are covered.
6. **Add medications and medical items.** Include copies of prescriptions, spare glasses, hearing aid batteries, and essential supplies where safe and legal. → *Expect:* critical health needs are represented.
7. **Add communication documents.** Include emergency contacts, paper maps, copies of key documents, and cash in small bills. → *Expect:* information and payment still work offline.
8. **Add warmth and tools.** Pack blankets, clothing, gloves, whistle, wrench or pliers, duct tape, and multipurpose tool. → *Expect:* basic shelter and utility tasks are possible.
9. **Label and date items.** Mark expiration dates and create a replacement calendar. → *Expect:* stale supplies are easy to find.
10. **Review every six months.** Replace expired food, water, batteries, medications, and outgrown clothing. → *Expect:* the kit stays usable.

## Decision points

- Household has refrigerated medicine → plan cooler packs, backup power, and pharmacy guidance.
- Local hazards include evacuation → build lighter go-bags in addition to home supplies.
- Pets or infants are present → add species- or age-specific food, supplies, and records.
- Budget is tight → build over time, prioritizing water, light, first aid, communication, and essential medicines.
- Item is hazardous or regulated → store according to label and local rules.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Expired supplies:** detect old food, water, batteries, or medicine → recover by replacing and dating items.
- **F2 Kit inaccessible:** detect supplies buried in storage → recover by moving to an obvious exit-adjacent location.
- **F3 Missing special needs:** detect no glasses, chargers, formula, or pet supplies → recover by adding person-specific items.
- **F4 Too heavy to carry:** detect go-bag cannot be lifted by intended person → recover by splitting into smaller bags.

## Verification

The kit contains water, food, light, power, first aid, hygiene, medications or medical supports, documents, cash, warmth, and tools, and each expiring item has a review date.

## Variations

- Apartment: include compact water storage, stairwell lighting, and building contact information.
- Vehicle: add seasonal clothing, jumper cables, reflective triangles, and water protected from extreme heat or freezing.
- Disaster-prone region: add hazard-specific items recommended by local emergency management.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if stored safely, but documents, medications, blades, batteries, and tools need secure handling. Keep sensitive copies protected and replace expired medical items through proper channels.
