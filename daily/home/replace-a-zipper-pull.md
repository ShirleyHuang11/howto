## Goal

A broken zipper pull tab is replaced with a functional pull without replacing the zipper slider.

## Preconditions

- Zipper slider still moves and closes the teeth correctly.
- Replacement pull tab, split ring, cord pull, or small pliers are available.
- Good light is available to see the slider loop.

## Steps

1. **Check the slider.** Move the slider up and down using a temporary paper clip if needed. → *Expect:* the zipper teeth open and close normally.
2. **Remove broken pieces.** Take off any sharp or dangling fragments from the slider loop. → *Expect:* the loop is clear and not cracked through.
3. **Choose the replacement.** Match a metal tab, split ring, or cord pull to the size of the slider loop. → *Expect:* the replacement can pass through the loop without forcing.
4. **Open the connector.** Use fingernails or pliers to open the split ring or jump ring just enough. → *Expect:* the connector gap accepts the slider loop.
5. **Attach the pull.** Thread the connector through the slider loop and rotate or close it fully. → *Expect:* the pull hangs from the slider without falling out.
6. **Close sharp gaps.** Squeeze any jump ring ends flush with pliers. → *Expect:* no edge catches on fabric or skin.
7. **Test under load.** Zip and unzip several times while holding the new pull. → *Expect:* the pull moves the slider smoothly.

## Decision points

- Slider loop is broken off → replace the slider or zipper, not just the pull.
- Teeth separate after closing → the slider is worn and needs replacement.
- Pull is for a suitcase or boot → choose a stronger metal pull or cord loop.
- Fabric catches near the slider → clear the fabric before testing forcefully.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pull falls off:** ring was not fully closed → crimp it shut or use a split ring.
- **F2 Slider breaks:** loop was weak or overbent → replace the slider with the same size and type.
- **F3 Pull is uncomfortable:** replacement is too small or sharp → swap for a larger smooth tab or cord pull.
- **F4 Zipper still jams:** problem is teeth, fabric, or slider wear → clean teeth and consider slider replacement.

## Verification

The new pull is securely attached, has no sharp open gap, and opens and closes the zipper through its full travel three times.

## Variations

- Jacket: use a cord pull with a knot for gloved hands.
- Bag: use a split ring rated for key use if the zipper sees heavy force.
- Invisible zipper: use a small low-profile tab so it does not distort the seam.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Pliers can pinch, and broken metal can scratch fabric or skin; remove sharp fragments before use.
