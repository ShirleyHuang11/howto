## Goal

Everyone who can evacuate leaves a house fire quickly, calls emergency services from outside, and stays out.

## Preconditions

- Smoke alarm, visible fire, smoke, heat, or smell of burning indicates a possible house fire.
- Call emergency services once outside or from a safe location.
- Do not re-enter a burning building for people, pets, possessions, or documents.
- If trapped, shelter in place at a window or safer room and call emergency services.
- Practice escape routes before an emergency.

## Steps

1. **React immediately.** Treat alarms, smoke, or fire as real and start leaving without collecting belongings. → *Expect:* evacuation begins without delay.
2. **Alert others while moving.** Shout "fire" and wake household members on your route out. → *Expect:* others are warned without stopping evacuation.
3. **Stay low under smoke.** Crawl or crouch where smoke is present. → *Expect:* breathing air is clearer near the floor.
4. **Check doors before opening.** Feel the door and knob with the back of your hand and look for smoke around edges. → *Expect:* you know whether that route may be dangerous.
5. **Use the safest exit.** [BRANCH: door cool and clear | door hot or smoky] open slowly and leave if clear; use another exit or shelter if heat or smoke is present. → *Expect:* you avoid moving into intense fire or smoke.
6. **Close doors behind you.** Close each door you pass through if safe. → *Expect:* fire and smoke spread may slow.
7. **Go to the meeting place.** Move to the planned outdoor location away from the building. → *Expect:* people can be accounted for safely.
8. **Call emergency services from outside.** Give the address, fire location, trapped people, and hazards such as gas cylinders. → *Expect:* firefighters are dispatched with useful information.
9. **Stay out.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* re-entering can be fatal; confirm firefighters have been told about anyone missing instead of going back inside. → *Expect:* no one re-enters before firefighters say it is safe.
10. **Signal if trapped.** If you cannot escape, close doors, block smoke gaps with cloth, call emergency services, and signal from a window. → *Expect:* rescuers know where to find you.

## Decision points

- Smoke blocks the hall → use a second exit or shelter in a room with a window.
- Clothes catch fire → stop, drop, cover face, and roll until flames are out.
- Someone is missing → tell firefighters last known location; do not re-enter.
- Door is hot → keep it closed and use another exit or window plan.
- Mobility needs slow escape → use the preplanned helper, refuge area, or call emergency services with exact location.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Belongings delay escape:** detect searching for items → recover by leaving immediately.
- **F2 Re-entry attempt:** detect someone going back inside → recover by stopping them if safe and telling firefighters.
- **F3 Wrong meeting place:** detect people gathering near the building or driveway → recover by moving to the planned safe spot.
- **F4 Open doors feed fire:** detect doors left open behind evacuees → recover by closing only if safe and already on the way out.

## Verification

People who escaped are outside at the meeting place, emergency services have the address and missing-person information, and no one re-enters until firefighters declare it safe.

## Variations

- Apartment: use stairs, not elevators, and follow building fire instructions unless smoke or heat blocks them.
- Second floor: use a practiced window escape ladder only if primary routes are blocked.
- Hearing or mobility impairment: use accessible alarms and assigned helpers in the family plan.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because smoke inhalation and re-entry can be fatal. Call emergency services from outside and treat possessions, documents, and pets as secondary to human life.
