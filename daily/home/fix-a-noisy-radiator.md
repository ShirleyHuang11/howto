## Goal

A noisy hot-water or steam radiator is checked and corrected for common air, pitch, or valve issues.

## Preconditions

- You have a radiator key for hot-water radiators, towel, small cup, level, and gloves.
- The radiator type is known: hot-water radiators have bleed valves; one-pipe steam radiators have air vents.
- Call a heating pro for leaking valves, banging pipes throughout the home, pressure problems, or any boiler fault.

## Steps

1. **Identify the system.** Look for a small bleed screw on hot-water radiators or a side air vent on steam radiators. → *Expect:* the radiator type is clear.
2. **Let it cool enough to touch.** Wait until surfaces are warm rather than hot before working near valves. → *Expect:* you can touch controls safely with gloves.
3. **Open the supply valve fully.** Turn the radiator supply valve fully open, not halfway. → *Expect:* valve chatter from partial opening is eliminated.
4. **Bleed hot-water air.** For hot-water radiators, hold a cup under the bleed valve and open it slowly until water appears. → *Expect:* air hisses first, then a steady water trickle appears.
5. **Close the bleed valve.** Tighten the bleed valve gently when water flows. → *Expect:* no water drips from the bleeder.
6. **Check steam radiator pitch.** For one-pipe steam, slope the radiator slightly toward the supply valve using a level. → *Expect:* condensate can drain back toward the pipe.
7. **Inspect the steam vent.** Replace a spitting, stuck, or paint-clogged steam air vent with the same style. → *Expect:* the vent admits air quietly and closes when hot.
8. **Run a heat cycle.** Observe the radiator during the next call for heat. → *Expect:* noise is reduced and no valve or vent leaks.

## Decision points

- Hot-water radiator has no water at bleeder → system pressure may be low; call a pro.
- Steam radiator bangs hard → check pitch first, then call a pro if banging persists.
- Any valve leaks after adjustment → stop and call a heating pro.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bleeder leaks:** water beads at the screw → gently snug it; replace the bleeder if it still leaks.
- **F2 Vent spits steam water:** steam vent sprays → replace the vent and verify radiator pitch.
- **F3 Whole system noisy:** many radiators bang or gurgle → boiler pressure, near-boiler piping, or controls need professional diagnosis.

## Verification

During a normal heating cycle, the radiator heats without persistent banging, hissing, or water leakage from the valve, vent, or bleeder.

## Variations

- Hot-water systems: bleeding air is common after service or seasonal startup.
- Steam systems: never bleed with a hot-water key; focus on vent condition and radiator pitch.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk: radiators, steam, and hot water can burn. Work only when cool enough to touch, open valves slowly, and call a pro for pressure or boiler issues.
