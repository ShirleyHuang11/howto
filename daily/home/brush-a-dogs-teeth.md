## Goal

Clean a dog's outer tooth surfaces with pet-safe toothpaste while making the routine tolerable enough to repeat.

## Preconditions

- Dog toothpaste and a dog toothbrush, finger brush, or soft gauze are available.
- Human toothpaste, baking soda, and forceful mouth opening are avoided.
- The dog's mouth is not bleeding, swollen, fractured, or too painful to touch.

## Steps

1. **Let the dog taste the paste.** Offer a pea-sized amount from your finger or brush. → *Expect:* the dog sniffs, licks, or accepts the flavor without gagging.
2. **Lift the lip, not the jaw.** Touch the muzzle and lift one side lip briefly while praising calm behavior. → *Expect:* the outer teeth and gumline are visible for a second or two.
3. **Brush the front teeth.** Use small circles along the gumline on the outside of the incisors and canines. → *Expect:* paste spreads over the teeth and the dog stays relaxed enough for a short pass.
4. **Move to cheek teeth.** Slide the brush under the cheek and brush the outside surfaces on each side; do not force the mouth open. → *Expect:* the back outer teeth get contact from the brush.
5. **End with a reward.** Stop after 30 to 60 seconds at first, then praise and give water. → *Expect:* the dog disengages calmly and the brush can be cleaned.

## Decision points

- If gums bleed easily, breath is foul, teeth are loose, or chewing hurts → schedule a veterinary dental exam.
- If the dog mouths or bites the brush → return to paste tasting and lip handling for several days.
- If daily brushing is unrealistic → brush as often as possible and ask the vet about safe dental chews or diets.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Dog resists mouth touch:** head turning, pawing, or backing away appears → shorten to one lip lift and reward before trying again later.
- **F2 Wrong toothpaste:** human toothpaste was used or swallowed → stop, rinse the mouth with water if possible, and call a vet or poison hotline for xylitol or fluoride concerns.
- **F3 Pain found:** yelping, bleeding, swelling, or a broken tooth is noticed → stop brushing and arrange veterinary care.

## Verification

Pet toothpaste has contacted the outer gumline surfaces without distress, no painful mouth problem was ignored, and the dog accepts the routine better or at least no worse than before.

## Variations

- `puppy`: use a finger and paste first, then introduce a brush after several calm sessions.
- `small-dog`: use a small angled brush to reach crowded cheek teeth.
- `senior-dog`: start only after a vet checks painful dental disease.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if pet toothpaste is used and the dog is not forced. See a vet for oral pain, swelling, loose teeth, repeated bleeding, or sudden bad breath.
