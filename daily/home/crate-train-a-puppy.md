## Goal

Teach a puppy to rest calmly in a crate that is safe, comfortable, and associated with predictable good things.

## Preconditions

- You have a crate large enough for the puppy to stand, turn, and lie down, with divider if needed.
- The puppy has had a potty break, water access appropriate for age, and some play or training.
- You have treats, chew-safe toys, bedding, and a quiet location near family activity.

## Steps

1. **Set up the crate safely.** Remove collars or dangling tags, add washable bedding, and place the crate away from heat vents, cords, and direct sun. → *Expect:* the puppy can enter without snagging or overheating.
2. **Build curiosity with the door open.** Toss treats inside and let the puppy walk in and out freely. → *Expect:* the puppy enters voluntarily at least once.
3. **Feed near or inside the crate.** Move meals gradually from the doorway to the back of the crate. → *Expect:* the puppy eats without bracing or backing away.
4. **Close the door briefly.** Close it while the puppy chews or eats, then open it before distress escalates. → *Expect:* the puppy notices the door but can stay engaged.
5. **Add short absences.** Step away for 30 seconds, return calmly, and release during a quiet moment. → *Expect:* the puppy learns quiet behavior opens the door.
6. **Extend time after needs are met.** [BRANCH: sleepy puppy | restless puppy] crate after potty and play for naps, or shorten the session if the puppy is not ready. → *Expect:* the puppy settles or at least remains recoverable.
7. **Use a consistent cue.** Say the same cue, reward entry, and keep departures low-key. → *Expect:* the puppy starts entering on cue.
8. **Keep nights practical.** Place the crate close enough to hear potty needs and take young puppies out calmly when needed. → *Expect:* nighttime crying is interpreted as either adjustment or potty need.

## Decision points

- Puppy screams, panics, or tries to escape → shorten sessions and consult a trainer if distress persists.
- Puppy soils the crate → review crate size, potty schedule, and medical issues.
- Household includes children → teach them the crate is the puppy's rest space, not a play cage.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Crate becomes punishment:** detect puppy avoiding it after scolding → restart with meals, treats, and open-door sessions.
- **F2 Sessions are too long:** detect frantic biting, drooling, or soiling → reduce duration and meet potty/exercise needs first.
- **F3 Bedding is chewed:** detect shredded fabric → remove unsafe bedding and use a chew-safe mat or bare crate temporarily.
- **F4 Night whining is misread:** detect repeated crying after sleep → take a quiet potty trip, then return to crate without play.

## Verification

The puppy enters the crate willingly, can stay with the door closed for an age-appropriate short period after potty and exercise, and leaves calmly when released.

## Variations

- `apartment`: add white noise and practice daytime crate naps before leaving.
- `rescue-puppy`: move more slowly if confinement history is unknown.
- `multi-pet-home`: keep other pets from crowding the crate door.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when the crate is used for rest, not punishment. Remove collars, avoid excessive confinement, provide age-appropriate potty breaks, and do not ignore signs of panic or illness.
