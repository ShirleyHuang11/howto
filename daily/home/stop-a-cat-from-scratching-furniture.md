## Goal

Redirect a cat's scratching from furniture to acceptable surfaces without punishment or declawing.

## Preconditions

- At least one stable scratching post or board is available near the target furniture.
- The cat has normal mobility and no painful paws or nails.
- Furniture can be temporarily protected with covers or tape.

## Steps

1. **Identify the preferred texture.** Notice whether the cat scratches vertical fabric, carpet, wood, cardboard, or horizontal surfaces. → *Expect:* the replacement scratcher can match the cat's preference.
2. **Place a better scratcher nearby.** Put a tall stable post or board directly beside the scratched furniture. → *Expect:* the acceptable surface is easier to choose than the furniture.
3. **Make furniture less rewarding.** Cover the target with a washable throw, smooth barrier, or cat-safe double-sided tape. → *Expect:* claws no longer catch satisfyingly on the furniture.
4. **Reward scratcher use.** Add catnip if appropriate, play near the post, and praise or treat when claws touch it. → *Expect:* the cat repeats scratching on the approved surface.
5. **Trim sharp tips if tolerated.** Maintain nail tips separately and gently. → *Expect:* accidental furniture damage is reduced.
6. **Move the scratcher gradually.** After consistent use, shift it a little at a time if you need a different final location. → *Expect:* the cat follows the scratcher instead of returning to furniture.

## Decision points

- If scratching is sudden, frantic, or paired with paw pain → see a vet.
- If the cat scratches after conflict with other pets → add territory, vertical space, and separate resources.
- If furniture damage is severe → use more scratchers before considering deterrents.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Scratcher ignored:** cat keeps using the couch → change texture, height, stability, or location.
- **F2 Punishment backfires:** spraying or yelling makes the cat hide or scratch when alone → stop punishment and improve surfaces.
- **F3 Nail problem:** claws catch, split, or grow into pads → arrange veterinary or groomer care.

## Verification

The cat uses an approved scratching surface daily, furniture access is protected or no longer attractive, and no paw pain or nail injury is present.

## Variations

- `cardboard-cat`: use horizontal cardboard scratchers and replace them when shredded.
- `multi-cat`: provide multiple scratching stations in different territories.
- `rental`: use removable barriers that do not damage fabric or finishes.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when using enrichment and barriers. Declawing is not a routine furniture solution; discuss humane options with a vet for injury, pain, or severe behavior concerns.
