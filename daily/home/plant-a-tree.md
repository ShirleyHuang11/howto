## Goal

Plant a young tree at the correct depth and location so roots can establish without trunk rot, girdling, or utility conflicts.

## Preconditions

- Underground utilities have been marked where required before digging.
- You have a site with enough mature canopy space away from buildings, wires, sidewalks, and septic areas.
- You have a shovel, water source, mulch, and a tree suited to the local climate and soil.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the planting site.** Check mature height and spread, sun exposure, drainage, and distance from utilities or foundations. → *Expect:* the tree has room to grow without future conflict.
2. **Find the root flare.** Pull soil or nursery mulch away from the trunk until the first major roots begin to widen. → *Expect:* the flare is visible above the root ball.
3. **Dig a wide shallow hole.** Make the hole two to three times wider than the root ball and no deeper than the root flare height. → *Expect:* the tree will sit with the flare at or slightly above grade.
4. **Remove container problems.** Slide off the pot, cut circling roots, and remove burlap, twine, and wire from the top and sides where practical. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* root and wrapping cuts affect establishment, so confirm the flare and root direction before cutting. → *Expect:* roots can grow outward into native soil.
5. **Set and backfill.** Place the tree straight, refill with native soil, and firm gently without stomping hard. → *Expect:* the trunk is vertical and the root flare remains visible.
6. **Water deeply.** Soak the root ball and surrounding soil until water reaches the planting depth. → *Expect:* soil settles without leaving the tree too low.
7. **Mulch correctly.** Spread 2-3 inches of mulch in a wide ring, keeping it several inches away from the trunk. → *Expect:* the mulch looks like a doughnut, not a volcano.
8. **Stake only if needed.** [BRANCH: stable tree | unstable site] skip stakes for a firm tree, or use loose flexible ties and remove them within a year. → *Expect:* the trunk can move slightly but will not fall.

## Decision points

- Utilities are unmarked or the site may contain pipes or wires → stop and arrange marking before digging.
- Soil drains poorly → choose a water-tolerant species or plant slightly high in a wider mound.
- The tree is pot-bound → correct circling roots before planting or exchange a severely girdled tree.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Tree planted too deep:** detect buried root flare → lift and reset before roots establish.
- **F2 Mulch against trunk:** detect mulch touching bark → pull mulch back to expose the flare.
- **F3 Root ball dries out:** detect dry soil several inches down → water slowly and add mulch.
- **F4 Tree leans:** detect shifting after watering → straighten while soil is loose and stake only if necessary.

## Verification

The root flare is visible at grade, the tree is upright, mulch is not touching bark, and the root zone has been watered deeply.

## Variations

- `bare-root`: soak roots briefly, spread them over a soil cone, and plant while dormant.
- `hot-climate`: plant in the cooler season and water more often during establishment.
- `street-tree`: follow municipal species, spacing, and permit rules.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from digging into utilities, heavy lifting, sharp tools, and long-term property damage from poor placement. Call utility marking services where required and get help moving large root balls.
