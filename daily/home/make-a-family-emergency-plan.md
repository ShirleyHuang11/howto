## Goal

A household has a written emergency plan for communication, evacuation, sheltering, reunification, and essential needs.

## Preconditions

- Household members can discuss likely local hazards such as fire, severe weather, flood, earthquake, outage, or medical emergency.
- Phone numbers, addresses, medication needs, pet needs, and school or workplace contacts are available.
- Emergency plans do not replace calling emergency services during immediate danger.
- Include children, older adults, disabled people, visitors, and pets in the plan.

## Steps

1. **List household members and needs.** Write names, phone numbers, medications, mobility needs, languages, pets, and key medical information. → *Expect:* the plan reflects who actually needs help.
2. **Choose emergency contacts.** Pick one local contact and one out-of-area contact everyone can reach. → *Expect:* everyone knows who to call or text.
3. **Set meeting places.** Choose one near-home spot and one outside the neighborhood. → *Expect:* the household has reunification locations for different scenarios.
4. **Map exits and routes.** Identify two ways out of each room and two routes out of the neighborhood when possible. → *Expect:* evacuation does not depend on one path.
5. **Assign roles.** Decide who helps children, pets, medication, documents, utilities, and emergency kit when time allows. → *Expect:* responsibilities are clear.
6. **Write communication rules.** Prefer texts when networks are overloaded, leave short status messages, and call emergency services for immediate danger. → *Expect:* contact attempts are coordinated.
7. **Plan sheltering actions.** Identify interior rooms, basement, upper floor, or safe rooms for likely hazards. → *Expect:* shelter locations match local risks.
8. **Record utility shutoffs.** Note gas, water, and electrical shutoff locations and who is allowed or trained to use them. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* shutting off gas may require the utility to restore service; confirm emergency need first. → *Expect:* utility controls are known without unsafe practice shutoffs.
9. **Share and store copies.** Put copies on phones, paper near exits, and with trusted contacts as appropriate. → *Expect:* the plan is accessible during an outage.
10. **Practice and update.** Run a drill and update the plan after moves, new phone numbers, new medications, or household changes. → *Expect:* gaps are found before an emergency.

## Decision points

- Household includes someone who needs evacuation assistance → prearrange helpers, transport, and backup power.
- Pets are present → include carriers, leashes, vaccination records, and pet-friendly destinations.
- Local hazard requires special action → add the relevant local authority guidance.
- Someone lacks a phone → assign a buddy and paper contact card.
- Plan conflicts with emergency instructions → follow emergency services or local officials.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Plan only in one phone:** detect no paper copy → recover by printing or writing copies for exits and kits.
- **F2 Meeting place unsafe:** detect flood, fire, or traffic risk at the spot → recover by choosing an alternate.
- **F3 Roles vague:** detect "someone gets the kit" → recover by naming primary and backup people.
- **F4 Needs omitted:** detect missing medication, mobility, or pet plan → recover by adding supplies and helpers.

## Verification

Every household member can name the emergency contacts, two meeting places, primary roles, and where the written plan is stored.

## Variations

- Apartment: include stairwells, refuge areas, building alarms, and management contacts.
- Rural home: include well pump power, long driveway access, livestock, and backup transport.
- Multilingual household: keep copies in the languages people actually use.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because plans include sensitive addresses, medical needs, and access details. Share copies only with trusted people and call emergency services for immediate threats.
