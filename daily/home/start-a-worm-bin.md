## Goal

Set up a contained worm bin that converts appropriate kitchen scraps into castings without odors, pests, overheating, or drowning worms.

## Preconditions

- Supplies: ventilated bin with drainage or moisture control, bedding such as shredded plain paper or coir, red wiggler composting worms, small amount of food scraps, and a dark location.
- The bin location stays roughly 55-80 F and is protected from direct sun and freezing.
- You can avoid meat, dairy, grease, pet waste, salty foods, and large amounts of citrus or onion.

## Steps

1. **Prepare bedding.** Moisten shredded paper, cardboard, or coir until it feels like a wrung-out sponge. → *Expect:* bedding is damp and fluffy, not dripping.
2. **Set up the bin.** Add bedding 6-8 inches deep and confirm air holes and drainage are open. → *Expect:* worms will have air and moisture control.
3. **Add worms gently.** Place red wigglers on the bedding and leave the lid open briefly under light so they move down. → *Expect:* worms burrow into the bedding.
4. **Feed a small starter amount.** Bury chopped vegetable scraps in one corner and cover completely with bedding. → *Expect:* no food is exposed on the surface.
5. **Place the bin in stable conditions.** Keep it dark, shaded, and away from temperature extremes. → *Expect:* the bin stays within a worm-safe range.
6. **Check moisture weekly.** Add dry bedding if wet or mist lightly if dry. → *Expect:* bedding remains damp but airy.
7. **Feed only what disappears.** Add more scraps when most previous food is partly processed. → *Expect:* food does not accumulate faster than worms can eat it.
8. **Harvest castings when mature.** Move fresh food to one side and collect dark crumbly castings from the other side after worms migrate. → *Expect:* finished castings separate with few worms.

## Decision points

- Bin smells sour → pause feeding and add dry bedding.
- Fruit flies appear → bury food deeper, freeze scraps first, and cover with dry bedding.
- Bin will be outdoors → protect from heat, rain flooding, and freezing.
- Worms try to escape → check heat, acidity, moisture, and recent disturbance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bin smells rotten:** detect sour or garbage odor → remove excess food and mix in dry bedding.
- **F2 Worms die or disappear:** detect few living worms → check temperature and moisture, then restart with stable conditions.
- **F3 Fruit flies multiply:** detect adults around the lid → stop exposed feeding, bury scraps, and use a vinegar trap nearby.
- **F4 Bin becomes too wet:** detect pooling liquid or compact bedding → drain, add dry bedding, and feed less watery food.

## Verification

The bin contains damp airy bedding, composting worms are below the surface, food scraps are buried, temperature is moderate, and there is no rotten odor or standing liquid.

## Variations

- `apartment`: use a tight-fitting ventilated bin and freeze scraps before feeding.
- `classroom`: feed predictable small amounts and assign moisture checks.
- `outdoor-shade`: insulate against heat and cold, and raise the bin above floodwater.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Wash hands after handling bedding or castings, avoid mold inhalation, and do not add pet waste or animal products to a home worm bin.
