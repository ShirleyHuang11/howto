## Goal

You recognize and stop common marketplace seller scams before releasing goods, account access, money, or personal information.

## Preconditions

- An active listing or buyer inquiry.
- Access to your marketplace account and payment app directly.
- A safe default rule: no item release until verified payment.

## Steps

1. **Keep the conversation in the marketplace.** Reply through the platform unless the platform itself provides a protected checkout or pickup workflow. → *Expect:* there is a traceable record.
2. **Check for scam signals.** Watch for overpayment, courier pickup, urgent scripts, bad grammar patterns, verification-code requests, external links, and payment screenshots. → *Expect:* suspicious buyers are identified before commitment.
3. **Verify payment only in your own account.** Open the payment app or marketplace order directly; do not trust emails, screenshots, or buyer confirmations. → *Expect:* payment status is independently confirmed or not present.
4. **Refuse security-code requests.** Never share one-time passcodes, login links, or account recovery codes. → *Expect:* your marketplace, email, and payment accounts remain protected.
5. **Use safe address rules.** Ship only to the platform-provided address and meet locally only in a public safe place when possible. → *Expect:* the buyer cannot redirect goods outside protection.
6. **Apply a release checklist.** Confirm buyer identity only as needed, final price, cleared payment, correct item, and safe handoff or tracked shipping. → *Expect:* all release conditions are satisfied.
7. **Release the item only after confirmation.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* once handed over or shipped, recovery is uncertain; confirm payment and terms first. → *Expect:* the item leaves your control only under protected conditions.
8. **Report and block suspicious buyers.** Use platform reporting tools and save evidence. → *Expect:* the scam attempt is documented and contact stops.

## Decision points

- Buyer asks you to refund an overpayment → stop; do not send money or goods.
- Buyer sends a link to receive payment → navigate independently to your account instead.
- Buyer wants a shipping address change → cancel and require purchase with the correct platform address.
- Buyer becomes threatening or manipulative → disengage and report.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Fake payment email:** detect sender domain, wording, or account mismatch → ignore the email and verify in the actual app.
- **F2 Account takeover attempt:** detect request for a verification code → change password if shared, enable two-factor authentication, and contact platform support.
- **F3 Chargeback risk:** detect payment method lacks seller protection → use platform checkout, cash verified in person, or decline.
- **F4 Courier scam:** detect buyer arranging third-party pickup before payment clears → refuse and use your shipping method or public meet.
- **F5 Personal information leak:** detect you sent address or phone too early → stop contact, tighten account privacy, and choose public handoff.

## Verification

No goods, money, account codes, or private details have been released to a suspicious buyer, and any completed sale has independently verified payment plus protected handoff or tracked shipment.

## Variations

- Local sale: use cash or instant payment verified in your own app before handoff.
- Shipping sale: use marketplace checkout and tracking to the order address.
- High-value electronics: document serials and require stronger payment and shipping protection.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from fraud, identity theft, and personal safety exposure. Never share verification codes, never refund overpayments, avoid off-platform deals, and verify payment directly.
