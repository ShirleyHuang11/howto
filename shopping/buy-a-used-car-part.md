## Goal

Buy a used car part that matches the vehicle, has acceptable condition and return terms, and avoids unsafe or incompatible components.

## Preconditions

- You know the vehicle year, make, model, trim, engine, drivetrain, VIN, and production date if relevant.
- You know the failed part name, part number, or diagnostic result.
- You can compare seller reputation, return policy, shipping cost, and core charges.

## Steps

1. **Identify the exact part.** Use VIN, OEM part number, casting number, trim, engine, and side or position. → *Expect:* compatibility requirements are specific.
2. **Decide used is appropriate.** Avoid used safety-critical or wear-heavy parts when new or remanufactured is safer. → *Expect:* the part category is reasonable for used purchase.
3. **Search reputable sources.** [BRANCH: local salvage yard | recycler database | marketplace | forum] compare sellers with ratings, warranty, and return terms. → *Expect:* candidates include price, condition, and seller identity.
4. **Verify compatibility with seller.** Send VIN or exact specs and ask for confirmation in writing. → *Expect:* seller confirms fitment or explains uncertainty.
5. **Inspect condition evidence.** Review photos, mileage, damage, rust, connector pins, broken tabs, fluid leaks, and included accessories. → *Expect:* condition risks are visible before payment.
6. **Compare total cost.** Add part price, shipping, tax, core charge, return shipping, restocking fee, programming, gaskets, fluids, and labor. → *Expect:* used part savings survive full-cost comparison.
7. **Confirm warranty and returns.** Check duration, start date, labor coverage, electrical exclusions, installation requirements, and return authorization process. → *Expect:* you know what happens if the part fails or does not fit.
8. **Pay through a protected method.** Use a marketplace checkout, card, or invoiced payment with part number and condition listed. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* cash, wire, or friends-and-family payments may remove dispute rights, so confirm part identity and seller terms first. → *Expect:* receipt includes part details and seller contact.
9. **Inspect on arrival.** Compare markings, connectors, damage, and included pieces before installation. → *Expect:* mismatch or shipping damage is documented before labor begins.

## Decision points

- The part controls braking, steering, airbags, or restraint systems → prefer new, OEM, or professional guidance.
- The part requires programming → confirm dealer or mechanic capability before buying.
- Seller will not confirm VIN fitment → buy from another source or accept no-fit risk knowingly.
- Return shipping is expensive → prefer local pickup or a seller with prepaid returns.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong fitment:** detect mismatched connector, mounting, or part number, recover by using written compatibility proof for return.
- **F2 Hidden damage:** detect cracks, rust, or broken tabs on arrival, recover with photos and prompt claim.
- **F3 Core charge surprise:** detect deposit added for old part return, recover by returning core under instructions.
- **F4 No programming support:** detect module cannot be coded, recover by confirming specialist options or returning before installation.
- **F5 Unsafe used part:** detect safety-critical category, recover by choosing new or certified replacement.

## Verification

The received part matches the vehicle specs and receipt, passes visual inspection, and has saved warranty and return documentation before installation.

## Variations

- Local salvage yard: inspect in person and ask whether removal damage is covered.
- Online recycler: confirm freight delivery inspection rules for engines, transmissions, and body panels.
- Mechanic-installed: have the mechanic approve the part before ordering.

## Safety & privacy

Vehicle parts affect safety. Do not install questionable safety components, protect VIN and address details, and use payment methods with dispute protection.
