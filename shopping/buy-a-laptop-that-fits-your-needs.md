## Goal

Buy a laptop that matches your workload, budget, portability needs, support expectations, and upgrade horizon.

## Preconditions

- You know your main uses, required apps, operating system needs, and budget.
- You can compare specifications, return policies, warranties, and student or employer requirements.
- You know whether you need new, refurbished, or used.

## Steps

1. **List required work.** Note apps, browser tabs, video calls, coding, gaming, design, school testing software, or accessibility needs. → *Expect:* minimum performance needs are explicit.
2. **Choose platform.** [BRANCH: Windows | macOS | ChromeOS | Linux] match required software, peripherals, IT support, and budget. → *Expect:* incompatible platforms are removed.
3. **Set core specs.** Choose processor class, memory, storage, screen size, resolution, battery target, ports, webcam, and weight. → *Expect:* listings can be filtered consistently.
4. **Compare real prices.** Add tax, shipping, warranty, adapters, dock, software, cloud storage, and financing cost. → *Expect:* total ownership cost is clear.
5. **Check reviews and reliability.** Look for thermal throttling, keyboard issues, battery life tests, repairability, noise, and common defects. → *Expect:* weak models are flagged.
6. **Review return and warranty terms.** Check return window, restocking fee, open-box policy, accidental coverage, repair turnaround, and support location. → *Expect:* recovery options are known before purchase.
7. **Verify seller and condition.** For refurbished or used laptops, confirm grade, battery health, charger, serial status, warranty, and reset state. → *Expect:* condition and support risk are understood.
8. **Buy the selected configuration.** Confirm exact model, CPU, RAM, storage, screen, keyboard language, and warranty before paying. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* custom builds, opened software, and financing agreements may be hard to unwind, so check configuration before checkout. → *Expect:* receipt lists the exact configuration.
9. **Test during return window.** Run updates, battery test, webcam, keyboard, ports, Wi-Fi, screen, speakers, and required apps. → *Expect:* defects are found while return is still possible.

## Decision points

- You keep many apps open → prioritize memory over small CPU upgrades.
- You travel daily → prioritize weight, battery, charger size, and durable build.
- You game or render video → check GPU, cooling, and power adapter size.
- Budget is tight → compare previous-generation new or manufacturer refurbished before unknown used listings.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Underpowered laptop:** detect slow required apps, recover by returning within window and raising RAM or CPU class.
- **F2 Storage too small:** detect low free space after setup, recover by choosing larger storage or external/cloud plan.
- **F3 Bad battery:** detect short runtime or poor battery health, recover through return or warranty claim.
- **F4 Seller misrepresented condition:** detect scratches, locks, missing charger, or wrong specs, recover with photos and platform dispute.
- **F5 Compatibility miss:** detect required app unsupported, recover by returning or using a different platform.

## Verification

The laptop receipt matches the chosen specs, required apps run acceptably, hardware tests pass, and the return deadline is saved.

## Variations

- Student: school testing software and warranty support may dictate platform.
- Business: security, docking, manageability, and on-site service may matter more than raw specs.
- Creative work: color-accurate display, RAM, GPU, and storage speed are priority checks.

## Safety & privacy

Laptop purchases expose payment and address data. For used devices, verify they are not locked, stolen, or enrolled in another organization's management before storing personal data.
