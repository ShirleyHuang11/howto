## Goal

Create clear digital copies of printed photos, name and organize them, and back them up so the originals can be handled less often.

## Preconditions

- You have printed photos, a clean flatbed scanner or phone scanning app, and good lighting.
- You have a computer or cloud storage location for the final files.
- You can handle fragile originals safely.

## Steps

1. **Sort photos into batches.** Group by album, year, event, person, or envelope before scanning. → *Expect:* each batch has a simple label you can use for filenames.
2. **Clean the scanning surface.** Wipe scanner glass or phone camera lens with a microfiber cloth and remove dust from photos gently. → *Expect:* the glass or lens is clear and photos are flat.
3. **Choose scan settings.** Use 300 dpi for normal sharing, 600 dpi for archiving small prints, and JPEG or TIFF depending on quality needs. → *Expect:* the first test scan is sharp at full-screen size.
4. **Scan a small test batch.** Capture 3-5 photos and check crop, glare, color, and orientation before continuing. → *Expect:* the files look straight, evenly lit, and complete.
5. **Scan the full batch.** Work in labeled batches and keep originals in order until each batch is verified. → *Expect:* every physical photo has a matching digital file.
6. **Rename and organize files.** Use folder names like `1978-family-trip` and filenames with dates or sequence numbers when exact dates are unknown. → *Expect:* files sort in a useful order without relying on memory.
7. **Back up the scans.** Copy the finished folders to cloud storage or an external drive in addition to the computer. → *Expect:* the same folders exist in at least two locations.
8. **Return originals safely.** Put photos back into archival sleeves or their original album after digital copies are verified. → *Expect:* originals are protected and the digital files are easy to find.

## Decision points

- Photos are curled, torn, or stuck in albums → use a phone scanning app and avoid forcing them flat.
- You need restoration → scan first, then edit copies rather than altering the only original.
- Many boxes of photos → scan representative batches yourself or use a reputable mail-in/local service.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Glare or reflections:** detect shiny bright patches → change angle, lighting, or use a flatbed scanner.
- **F2 Cropped edges:** detect missing borders or heads → rescan with manual crop or extra margin.
- **F3 Wrong order:** detect filenames out of sequence → keep each physical batch together and renumber before mixing batches.
- **F4 Cloud upload incomplete:** detect missing files in cloud view → wait for sync to finish and compare file counts.

## Verification

Each batch has matching digital files, sample images open clearly at full size, filenames or folders identify the batch, and at least one backup copy exists.

## Variations

- Flatbed scanner: best for fragile, curled, or high-value photos.
- Phone scanning app: faster for casual albums and can auto-crop multiple photos.
- Archival project: use 600 dpi or higher and keep unedited master files separate from edited copies.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Old photos may reveal addresses, children, medical events, or family history; choose private storage and ask before sharing sensitive images publicly.
