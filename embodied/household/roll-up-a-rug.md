## Goal

Roll a rug into a compact cylinder for moving, storage, or cleaning access without trapping furniture or creating a trip hazard.

## Preconditions

- Furniture and loose objects are removed from the rug.
- Rug is dry.
- Floor path around the rug is clear.
- Strap, cord, or tape is available if the rug must stay rolled.

## Steps

1. **Clear the rug surface.** Remove objects, cords, and small furniture from the rug. → *Expect:* the rug surface is empty from edge to edge.
2. **Straighten one short edge.** Lift and pull the edge until it lies square across the width. → *Expect:* the starting edge forms a straight line.
3. **Make the first fold-over.** Curl the starting edge over itself by 5 to 10 cm and press it evenly across the width. → *Expect:* a straight roll core forms.
4. **Roll forward with both hands.** Push the roll away while guiding both ends so they stay aligned. → *Expect:* the cylinder grows without diagonal drift.
5. **Tuck loose fringe or corners.** Pause when fringe appears and fold it inward before continuing. → *Expect:* no fabric strings trail outside the roll.
6. **Secure the roll.** Wrap a strap or loose tie around the middle and near each end if needed. → *Expect:* the rug remains rolled when released.

## Decision points

- Rug is large or heavy → use two people or roll from both sides toward the center.
- Rug pad is underneath → remove or roll it separately to avoid shifting layers.
- Rug is damp → dry it flat before rolling for storage.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Diagonal roll:** detect one end advancing faster → unroll partway and restart with both ends aligned.
- **F2 Object trapped:** detect a lump in the roll → unroll to the lump and remove the object.
- **F3 Rug unrolls:** detect cylinder springing open → add ties near both ends before lifting.
- **F4 Furniture snag:** detect edge caught under furniture → stop pulling and lift or move the furniture first.

## Verification

The rug is a secure, aligned cylinder with no trapped objects, no loose fringe dragging, and no raised edge left as a trip hazard.

## Variations

- `thin-runner`: roll from the short end and secure only the middle.
- `area-rug`: roll around a cardboard tube if long-term storage must prevent creases.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Large rugs can strain backs or block walkways; keep the roll low, coordinate with nearby people, and do not leave a partial roll across a walking path.
