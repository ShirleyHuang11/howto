## Goal

Reduce a firm food item into shreds using a box grater while keeping fingers clear of the cutting surface.

## Preconditions

- Box grater is clean and undamaged.
- Food item is firm enough to grate and large enough to hold safely.
- Cutting board, bowl, or plate is stable under the grater.
- A discard plan exists for the final small nub.

## Steps

1. **Set the grater upright.** Place the wide base flat on a board or inside a shallow bowl with the chosen grating face outward. → *Expect:* the grater stands vertical and does not rock.
2. **Grip the handle and food.** Hold the grater handle from above and hold the food with fingertips curled away from the grating face. → *Expect:* both the grater and food are controlled before contact.
3. **Start with light downward strokes.** Press the food against the upper part of the face and slide down in one straight motion. → *Expect:* shreds appear on the inside or below the grater.
4. **Repeat with consistent pressure.** Lift the food off the teeth before moving it back upward, then grate downward again. → *Expect:* the food shortens gradually and the grater remains planted.
5. **Stop before the unsafe nub.** When less than about 2 cm of food remains between fingers and teeth, stop grating. → *Expect:* fingers never cross the plane of the grater teeth.
6. **Collect the shreds.** Lift the grater vertically, tap lightly over the bowl, and scrape loose shreds from the safe outside edges. → *Expect:* grated food is gathered and the sharp face is clear of hands.

## Decision points

- Food is slippery → dry it with a towel or use a cut-resistant glove.
- Shreds are too large or small → rotate the grater to a different face before continuing.
- Food is very soft → chill it briefly or use a knife instead of forcing it through the teeth.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Grater skids:** detect base moving during a stroke → stop, place a damp towel under the board or use a heavier bowl.
- **F2 Food crumbles:** detect chunks breaking off instead of shreds → reduce pressure and use shorter strokes.
- **F3 Finger approaches teeth:** detect grip within 2 cm of the grating surface → stop and discard or hand-cut the remaining nub.
- **F4 Clogged teeth:** detect shreds stuck in holes → tap the grater and clear from the non-sharp side with a brush.

## Verification

The requested amount of food is shredded into the collection area, the grater stayed stable, and no finger or gripper contacted the cutting teeth.

## Variations

- `cheese`: grate cold cheese for cleaner shreds.
- `citrus-zest`: use only the colored outer peel and avoid the bitter white pith.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from sharp teeth. Keep human hands outside the stroke path, stop before the final nub, and wash the grater by brushing away from the cutting edges.
