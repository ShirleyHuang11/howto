## Goal

A mild allergic reaction is managed with an appropriate antihistamine while anaphylaxis signs are recognized and escalated immediately.

## Preconditions

- Symptoms are mild and limited, such as itchy rash, hives, sneezing, or mild itching, with normal breathing and alertness.
- Call emergency services and use prescribed epinephrine for trouble breathing, throat/tongue/lip swelling, wheezing, faintness, repeated vomiting, confusion, or symptoms affecting more than one body system.
- Antihistamines do not treat life-threatening anaphylaxis.
- Use only medication appropriate for the person's age, allergies, pregnancy status, medical conditions, and label directions.
- This is first aid guidance, not a substitute for medical care.

## Steps

1. **Check for anaphylaxis first.** Look for breathing trouble, swelling of throat or tongue, faintness, severe vomiting, confusion, or rapid worsening. → *Expect:* emergency signs are ruled in or out.
2. **Use epinephrine for emergency signs.** [BRANCH: anaphylaxis signs | mild symptoms only] help use prescribed epinephrine and call emergency services for anaphylaxis; continue antihistamine care only for mild symptoms. → *Expect:* life-threatening allergy is not treated with antihistamine alone.
3. **Remove the trigger if safe.** Stop eating the food, move away from the allergen, remove the stinger by scraping, or wash skin exposure. → *Expect:* exposure is reduced.
4. **Read the antihistamine label.** Confirm active ingredient, dose, age limits, warnings, and whether it causes drowsiness. → *Expect:* the medication is appropriate before use.
5. **Check contraindications.** Ask about allergy to the medicine, interacting sedatives, alcohol, glaucoma, urinary retention, pregnancy, or clinician warnings. → *Expect:* obvious medication risks are screened.
6. **Give the labeled dose.** Use the measuring device for liquids and do not combine multiple antihistamines unless directed by a clinician. → *Expect:* one appropriate dose is taken.
7. **Record timing and dose.** Write down medication name, amount, and time. → *Expect:* caregivers or clinicians can avoid duplicate dosing.
8. **Monitor closely.** Watch breathing, swelling, hives spread, vomiting, dizziness, and alertness for worsening. → *Expect:* escalation signs are detected.
9. **Avoid driving if sedating.** Keep the person from driving or risky tasks if the medicine can cause drowsiness. → *Expect:* medication side-effect risk is reduced.
10. **Seek medical advice if persistent.** Contact a clinician if symptoms last, recur, worsen, or the trigger was high risk. → *Expect:* unresolved reactions get follow-up.

## Decision points

- Any airway, breathing, circulation, or multi-system symptoms → epinephrine if prescribed and call emergency services.
- Person has an epinephrine auto-injector and known severe allergy → use their allergy action plan.
- Reaction is mild but trigger was food, sting, or medication → monitor longer and seek advice sooner.
- Child needs liquid medicine → use weight-based label or clinician instructions, not guesswork.
- Symptoms return after improving → seek urgent medical advice because biphasic reactions can occur.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Antihistamine used instead of epinephrine:** detect breathing trouble or swelling with only pills given → recover by using epinephrine if available and calling emergency services.
- **F2 Double dosing:** detect multiple products with the same active ingredient → recover by stopping and calling poison control or a clinician.
- **F3 Sedation ignored:** detect drowsy medicine before driving → recover by arranging another driver or staying put.
- **F4 Trigger remains present:** detect ongoing exposure → recover by moving away, washing skin, or stopping the food.

## Verification

Anaphylaxis signs were checked, emergency services and epinephrine were used for red flags, any antihistamine dose followed the label, and symptoms are improving without breathing, swelling, faintness, or multi-system involvement.

## Variations

- Non-drowsy antihistamine: may be preferable for daytime mild symptoms if appropriate for the person.
- Insect sting: remove stinger by scraping and monitor for systemic symptoms.
- Medication reaction: stop the suspected medicine only as medically appropriate and contact a clinician promptly.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because anaphylaxis can progress quickly and antihistamines cannot reverse airway or circulation collapse. Call emergency services for red flags and share medication timing with responders.
