## Goal

A hot liquid burn is cooled promptly, protected cleanly, and escalated for medical care when severity or location requires it.

## Preconditions

- A person has been burned by hot water, steam, soup, grease, tea, or another hot liquid.
- Call emergency services for burns causing breathing trouble, large or deep burns, burns to face, hands, genitals, major joints, circumferential burns, electrical or chemical burns, shock signs, or burns in infants and vulnerable adults.
- Stop the burning process before dressing the injury.
- Do not apply ice, butter, oils, toothpaste, or adhesive dressings.
- This is first aid guidance, not definitive burn care.

## Steps

1. **Move away from heat.** Get the person away from the hot liquid, steam, stove, kettle, or spill. → *Expect:* burning exposure has stopped.
2. **Remove wet hot items.** Carefully remove hot wet clothing, jewelry, diapers, or watches unless stuck to skin. → *Expect:* retained heat and swelling risk are reduced.
3. **Cool with running water.** Hold the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible. → *Expect:* pain and tissue heating decrease.
4. **Protect from chilling.** Keep the rest of the person warm while cooling only the burn. → *Expect:* hypothermia risk is reduced.
5. **Assess severity.** Look for size, location, blistering, white or charred skin, numbness, and breathing symptoms. → *Expect:* red flags are identified.
6. **Call for help when indicated.** [BRANCH: red flags present | minor burn] call emergency services for severe burns; arrange medical advice for uncertain burns. → *Expect:* serious burns are escalated.
7. **Cover loosely.** After cooling, cover with clean non-stick dressing or plastic wrap laid loosely, not wrapped tightly. → *Expect:* the burn is protected from contamination.
8. **Do not burst blisters.** Leave blisters intact and avoid peeling skin. → *Expect:* infection protection remains intact.
9. **Use simple pain relief if safe.** Offer age-appropriate over-the-counter pain medicine only if the person can take it safely. → *Expect:* pain is addressed without unsafe dosing.
10. **Monitor for shock.** Watch for pale clammy skin, faintness, confusion, or worsening pain while awaiting care. → *Expect:* deterioration is caught early.

## Decision points

- Burn is larger than the person's palm, deep, or on high-risk areas → seek urgent medical care.
- Clothing is stuck to skin → do not pull it off; cool around it and get emergency care.
- Burn is from hot grease → cool the burn the same way and seek care sooner if deep or extensive.
- Infant, older adult, or person with diabetes/immunosuppression → seek medical advice for even smaller burns.
- Signs of inhalation injury after steam or fire → call emergency services immediately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Ice applied:** detect ice or freezing pack on skin → recover by removing it and using cool running water.
- **F2 Cooling too short:** detect only a brief rinse → recover by completing 20 minutes of cooling if within a reasonable time after injury.
- **F3 Tight dressing:** detect wrap constricting swelling → recover by loosening or replacing with a loose clean cover.
- **F4 Home remedy applied:** detect butter, oil, toothpaste, or powder → recover by gently rinsing away if possible and seeking medical advice.

## Verification

The burn source is removed, the burn was cooled with running water for 20 minutes, severe-burn criteria were checked, medical help was called when indicated, and the burn is loosely covered.

## Variations

- No running water: use repeated cool clean water pours or wet clean cloths while arranging care.
- Child scald: remove diapers or soaked clothing quickly because they hold heat.
- Work kitchen: report the injury and use workplace first aid procedures after immediate cooling.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because serious burns can cause shock, infection, scarring, and airway injury. Call emergency services for red flags and avoid ice, oils, and adhesive materials on the burn.
