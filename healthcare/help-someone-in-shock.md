## Goal

A person with possible shock is kept still, warm, and monitored while emergency services are called.

## Preconditions

- Suspect shock after major injury, severe bleeding, allergic reaction, infection, burns, dehydration, heart problem, or trauma with pale clammy skin, weakness, confusion, rapid breathing, or faintness.
- Call emergency services immediately for suspected shock.
- Control severe bleeding first if present.
- Do not give food or drink.
- This is emergency first aid, not definitive treatment.

## Steps

1. **Check scene safety.** Look for traffic, violence, electricity, fire, or other hazards before approaching. → *Expect:* you can help without adding another casualty.
2. **Call emergency services.** Say the person may be in shock and report the suspected cause. → *Expect:* dispatch is connected and can guide you.
3. **Stop major bleeding.** Apply firm direct pressure with clean cloth or dressing if bleeding is present. → *Expect:* visible bleeding slows or stops.
4. **Lay the person flat if tolerated.** Help them lie on their back unless breathing, injury, or comfort prevents it. → *Expect:* they are resting and not exerting themselves.
5. **Avoid unsafe leg elevation.** [BRANCH: no injury and comfortable | trauma, pain, breathing trouble, pregnancy] raise legs slightly only if it does not cause pain or breathing trouble; otherwise keep flat or position of comfort. → *Expect:* positioning does not worsen injuries.
6. **Keep warm.** Cover with a coat or blanket while avoiding overheating. → *Expect:* chills and heat loss are reduced.
7. **Loosen tight clothing.** Loosen belts, collars, or restrictive clothing if easy and respectful. → *Expect:* breathing and circulation are not restricted.
8. **Monitor breathing and alertness.** Watch for worsening confusion, blue color, vomiting, or loss of consciousness. → *Expect:* deterioration is detected quickly.
9. **Use recovery position if needed.** If unconscious but breathing normally and no suspected spine injury prevents movement, roll them onto their side. → *Expect:* vomit or fluids can drain.
10. **Prepare handoff.** Note symptoms, injuries, allergies, medications, and what changed over time. → *Expect:* responders get a clear summary.

## Decision points

- Person is not breathing normally → start CPR if trained or instructed by dispatch.
- Severe bleeding is present → control bleeding while emergency services are called.
- Suspected spine, hip, leg, or pelvic injury → do not raise legs and avoid unnecessary movement.
- Breathing is easier sitting up → allow position of comfort while waiting for help.
- Allergic reaction with prescribed epinephrine available → help use it and still call emergency services.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Food or drink offered:** detect attempts to give water, alcohol, or pills → recover by stopping because surgery, vomiting, or airway risk may follow.
- **F2 Person made to stand:** detect faintness or collapse risk → recover by keeping them still and low.
- **F3 Leg elevation worsens pain:** detect pain, shortness of breath, or trauma signs → recover by lowering legs and using a comfortable position.
- **F4 Bleeding missed:** detect soaked clothing or pooling blood → recover by exposing enough to find the bleed and applying pressure.

## Verification

Emergency services have been called, life-threatening bleeding is controlled, the person is kept still and warm, and breathing plus alertness are monitored until responders arrive.

## Variations

- Anaphylaxis: use prescribed epinephrine promptly and call emergency services.
- Heat illness: cool the person while still treating shock symptoms and calling for help.
- Trauma scene: minimize movement and protect the neck and spine if mechanism suggests injury.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because shock can rapidly become fatal. Call emergency services, avoid food and drink, and share relevant medical information only with dispatchers and responders.
