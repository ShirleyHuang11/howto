## Goal

A person with suspected low blood sugar receives fast sugar if awake and safe to swallow, or emergency help if severe.

## Preconditions

- The person has diabetes or suspected hypoglycemia with sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion, irritability, weakness, or drowsiness.
- Call emergency services if they are unconscious, having a seizure, unable to swallow safely, severely confused, injured, or not improving after treatment.
- Do not give food or drink to someone who is unconscious, seizing, or unable to swallow.
- Use the person's glucose meter, continuous glucose monitor, glucagon, or diabetes plan if available.
- This is first aid guidance, not a substitute for medical care.

## Steps

1. **Check responsiveness and swallowing.** Ask simple questions and confirm they can sit up and swallow safely. → *Expect:* you know whether oral sugar is safe.
2. **Call emergency services for severe signs.** If unconscious, seizing, or unable to swallow, call immediately and use glucagon if available and trained. → *Expect:* severe hypoglycemia is escalated.
3. **Check glucose if possible.** Use their meter or CGM if it does not delay treatment. → *Expect:* low or suspected low blood sugar is identified.
4. **Give fast sugar if awake.** Provide glucose tablets, gel, juice, regular soda, candy, or sugar equivalent according to their plan. → *Expect:* quick carbohydrate is swallowed.
5. **Wait 15 minutes.** Keep them seated and observe alertness, sweating, shaking, and behavior. → *Expect:* symptoms begin to improve if sugar is working.
6. **Recheck or reassess.** Test glucose again if possible, or reassess symptoms. → *Expect:* improvement or persistent low signs are clear.
7. **Repeat fast sugar if still low.** [BRANCH: improving | still low] repeat fast sugar if glucose remains low or symptoms persist and they can swallow. → *Expect:* a second correction is given safely.
8. **Give longer-acting food when better.** Once alert and improving, provide a snack or meal with carbohydrate and protein if the next meal is not soon. → *Expect:* blood sugar is less likely to drop again.
9. **Avoid insulin correction.** Do not give insulin for the hypo unless a clinician or the person's plan explicitly directs it. → *Expect:* low blood sugar is not worsened.
10. **Arrange follow-up or supervision.** Stay with them until normal, and contact a caregiver or clinician if cause is unclear. → *Expect:* recurrence risk is addressed.

## Decision points

- Person cannot swallow safely → call emergency services and use glucagon if available.
- Symptoms improve after sugar → give follow-up snack or meal and monitor.
- No improvement after repeated treatment → call emergency services.
- Glucose is high but symptoms are concerning → seek medical advice because another condition may be present.
- Person uses an insulin pump → do not adjust it unless they can do so or their plan instructs it.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Food given while unsafe:** detect choking risk, unconsciousness, or seizure → recover by stopping oral intake and calling emergency services.
- **F2 Slow food only:** detect chocolate or fatty food used first → recover by using fast sugar such as glucose or juice.
- **F3 Person left alone:** detect no supervision after improvement → recover by staying or arranging a responsible adult.
- **F4 Insulin given during low:** detect planned insulin correction → recover by stopping and contacting medical help.

## Verification

The person is awake, able to swallow, symptoms and glucose are improving after fast sugar, a longer-acting snack or meal is provided when appropriate, and emergency services were called for severe or non-improving signs.

## Variations

- Glucagon kit: follow the device instructions and call emergency services after use.
- Child at school: follow the diabetes care plan and notify guardians.
- No glucose meter: treat suspected hypo with fast sugar if safe to swallow; sugar is usually safer than waiting when low is likely.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because severe hypoglycemia can cause seizure, coma, or death. Do not give oral sugar to someone who cannot swallow, and share diabetes details only with caregivers, dispatchers, or clinicians who need them.
