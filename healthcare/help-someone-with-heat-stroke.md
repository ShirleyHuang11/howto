## Goal

A person with suspected heat stroke is cooled aggressively while emergency services are called.

## Preconditions

- Suspect heat stroke with high heat exposure plus confusion, collapse, seizure, fainting, severe weakness, very hot skin, or altered behavior.
- Call emergency services immediately for suspected heat stroke.
- Start cooling before responders arrive.
- Do not give fluids to a confused, unconscious, vomiting, or seizing person.
- This is emergency first aid, not a substitute for medical treatment.

## Steps

1. **Call emergency services.** Say suspected heat stroke and report mental status, exertion, and location. → *Expect:* urgent medical response is activated.
2. **Move to a cooler place.** Get the person into shade, air conditioning, or a cool room if safe. → *Expect:* heat exposure decreases.
3. **Remove excess clothing.** Loosen or remove outer layers, helmet, pads, or gear while preserving privacy. → *Expect:* skin is available for cooling.
4. **Start rapid cooling.** Use cold water immersion if available, or soak with cold water and fan continuously. → *Expect:* body cooling begins immediately.
5. **Cool key areas.** Place cold packs or wet cloths on neck, armpits, and groin while continuing whole-body cooling. → *Expect:* major blood-flow areas are cooled.
6. **Monitor airway and breathing.** Watch for vomiting, seizure, or loss of consciousness. → *Expect:* airway danger is recognized.
7. **Do not force drinks.** [BRANCH: fully alert | confused or vomiting] offer small sips only if fully alert and able to swallow; otherwise give nothing by mouth. → *Expect:* choking risk is avoided.
8. **Continue cooling until help arrives.** Keep cooling unless shivering becomes severe or dispatch instructs otherwise. → *Expect:* cooling does not stop prematurely.
9. **Prepare facts for responders.** Note exertion, heat exposure, medications, medical conditions, and cooling start time. → *Expect:* responders can judge severity.
10. **Transfer care.** Tell responders what cooling was used and how the person changed. → *Expect:* emergency care continues without information loss.

## Decision points

- Any confusion, seizure, collapse, or altered behavior in heat → treat as heat stroke and call emergency services.
- Person is alert with cramps or heavy sweating only → cool, hydrate, and monitor; escalate if worsening.
- Cold-water tub, kiddie pool, or shower is available → use it for rapid cooling if safe and airway can be monitored.
- No water source → move to shade, remove excess clothing, fan, and use any cool packs while waiting.
- Person stops breathing normally → start CPR if trained or instructed by dispatch.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Waiting for temperature measurement:** detect delay while searching for thermometer → recover by calling and cooling based on symptoms.
- **F2 Cooling too gentle:** detect only shade with no active cooling → recover by adding cold water, fanning, or immersion.
- **F3 Forced drinking:** detect fluids offered to confused person → recover by stopping oral intake and monitoring airway.
- **F4 Privacy blocks cooling:** detect reluctance to remove gear → recover by using bystanders or towels to preserve modesty while cooling effectively.

## Verification

Emergency services have been called, active cooling is underway, the person is out of heat exposure, no unsafe fluids are given, and breathing plus mental status are monitored until responders arrive.

## Variations

- Athlete in gear: remove helmet and pads quickly if trained or with help, then cool.
- Outdoor worksite: use hose water, shade, fans, and crew assignment while emergency services are en route.
- Older adult indoors: check for broken air conditioning and call emergency services for confusion or collapse.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because heat stroke can cause organ failure or death. Cooling is urgent, but protect the airway, avoid forced fluids, and preserve privacy while removing heat-trapping clothing.
