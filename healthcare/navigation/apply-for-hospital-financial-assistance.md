## Goal

You request charity care or financial assistance from a hospital and get a written decision before paying more than you can afford.

## Preconditions

- Hospital bill or account number, dates of service, and patient information.
- Household income documents, tax return or pay stubs, bank statements if required, and insurance details.
- Access to the hospital billing office, patient financial services, or online financial assistance policy.

## Steps

1. **Find the hospital's financial assistance policy.** Search the hospital website for "financial assistance," "charity care," or "patient billing." → *Expect:* you have the policy, application, and income guidelines.
2. **Pause non-urgent payment pressure.** Call billing and say you are applying for financial assistance; ask them to place the account on hold from collections while reviewed. → *Expect:* billing notes the account.
3. **Collect required documents.** Gather proof of income, household size, address, insurance denial or explanation of benefits, and any hardship letter requested. → *Expect:* the application packet is complete.
4. **Complete the application accurately.** Use the same name and account number as the bill and answer every required field. → *Expect:* no blank required sections remain.
5. **Submit through a trackable method.** Upload, portal-message, fax with confirmation, mail with tracking, or deliver in person and request a receipt. → *Expect:* you have proof of submission date.
6. **Follow up on missing items.** Call after the stated processing time or within 1-2 weeks if no timeframe is given. → *Expect:* the office confirms complete, pending, approved, denied, or missing documents.
7. **Get the decision in writing.** Save approval percentage, covered dates, covered providers, remaining balance, and appeal rights if denied. → *Expect:* you have a written determination.
8. **Settle only the corrected balance.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* payment plans and collections agreements can be hard to unwind; confirm assistance is applied before agreeing to long-term payment. → *Expect:* the bill reflects the assistance adjustment.

## Decision points

- Denied but income or hardship qualifies under the policy → appeal and provide additional documents.
- Bill includes non-hospital physicians → ask whether separate physician groups have their own assistance programs.
- You may qualify for Medicaid or public coverage → apply, because hospitals may require or help with that step.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Application lost:** detect no record of submission → resend with fax receipt, upload confirmation, or tracking proof.
- **F2 Collections continue:** detect collection calls during review → tell the agency the account is under assistance review and call hospital billing.
- **F3 Partial approval confusing:** detect multiple balances after approval → request an itemized statement showing adjustments by date of service.
- **F4 Deadline missed:** detect old bill or denial for late application → ask for exception, retroactive review, or state hospital charity care rights if applicable.

## Verification

The hospital issues a written approval, denial, or appeal decision, and the billing account shows the adjusted balance before you pay or sign a payment plan.

## Variations

- `us`: nonprofit hospitals are required to have financial assistance policies; state charity care rules may add protections.
- Insured patient: assistance may still apply to deductibles, coinsurance, or noncovered emergency care depending on policy.
- Uninsured patient: ask for both financial assistance and uninsured/self-pay discounts.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because bills, credit, and identity documents are involved. Submit only through official hospital channels, keep copies, and confirm written adjustments before payment.
