## Goal

You arrive at the appointment with a prioritized question list so you understand the diagnosis, choices, risks, and next steps.

## Preconditions

- The diagnosis name, visit note, lab result, imaging report, or discharge paperwork.
- Your medication list, allergies, major medical history, and current symptoms.
- A way to take notes or bring a trusted person, if allowed.

## Steps

1. **Write the diagnosis exactly as given.** Copy the medical name, stage/severity if present, and who gave it. → *Expect:* your question sheet starts with the exact diagnosis and source.
2. **Separate facts from worries.** List what you know from records, then list fears or assumptions you want clarified. → *Expect:* questions are grounded in the actual report, not only internet searches.
3. **Ask what it means.** Prepare questions about cause, certainty, severity, expected course, and what symptoms require urgent care. → *Expect:* you have plain-language questions about the condition itself.
4. **Ask what comes next.** Include tests, referrals, follow-up timing, treatment options, watchful waiting, lifestyle changes, and what happens if you do nothing. → *Expect:* your list covers the decision pathway.
5. **Ask about treatment tradeoffs.** For each recommended treatment, ask benefits, common side effects, serious risks, alternatives, cost, and how success will be measured. → *Expect:* you can compare options rather than just accept a label.
6. **Prioritize the top five.** Put the most time-sensitive or life-impacting questions first in case the visit is short. → *Expect:* the first page has a short ranked list.
7. **Bring your constraints.** Note pregnancy possibility, caregiving duties, work limits, transportation, insurance, cost concerns, religious or personal preferences, and communication needs. → *Expect:* the clinician can tailor the plan to your real situation.
8. **Confirm the plan out loud.** At the visit, repeat back the diagnosis, next test or treatment, warning signs, and follow-up date. → *Expect:* the clinician corrects or confirms your understanding.

## Decision points

- Diagnosis is serious, rare, or treatment has major risks → ask whether a second opinion or specialist referral is appropriate.
- You do not understand the explanation → ask for plain-language wording, diagrams, or written instructions before leaving.
- Cost or insurance may block the plan → ask for generic options, prior authorization support, financial assistance, or social work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Visit runs out of time:** detect unanswered questions → ask which questions are urgent and send the rest through the portal.
- **F2 Internet information conflicts:** detect alarming or contradictory claims → ask the clinician which sources apply to your case.
- **F3 Plan is unclear after leaving:** detect uncertainty about medication, tests, or follow-up → call the office or send a portal message summarizing what you think was agreed.
- **F4 Specialist referral stalls:** detect no appointment or authorization after several business days → call both offices and your insurer to confirm the referral status.

## Verification

You leave with the diagnosis explained in plain language, next steps, warning signs, responsible offices, medication or treatment instructions, and a follow-up plan documented in notes or the portal.

## Variations

- `us`: ask whether tests or referrals require insurance prior authorization and which billing codes may be used.
- Pediatric diagnosis: include school, childcare, vaccination, growth, and caregiver instructions.
- Chronic diagnosis: ask how often monitoring is needed and what numbers or symptoms should trigger contact.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because diagnosis decisions affect treatment, money, and legal privacy. Do not stop prescribed medication or delay urgent care based only on outside reading; confirm major decisions with a licensed clinician.
