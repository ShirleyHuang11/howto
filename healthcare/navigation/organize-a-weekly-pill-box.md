## Goal

You fill a weekly pill box accurately so each dose is available at the right day and time without mixing up medicines.

## Preconditions

- Current medication list verified against prescription labels.
- Clean weekly pill organizer with compartments matching the dosing schedule.
- Good lighting, clean dry hands, and no distractions.

## Steps

1. **Set up a clean workspace.** Clear the table, wash hands, and keep food, pets, and children away. → *Expect:* medicines can be handled without contamination or interruption.
2. **Line up medicines with the current list.** Match each bottle to name, strength, and instructions before opening. → *Expect:* every medicine is verified before placement.
3. **Remove discontinued or old bottles.** Keep them away from the filling area until disposal or clinician review. → *Expect:* only active medicines are available to fill.
4. **Fill one medication at a time.** Place doses into each correct day and time compartment, then close the bottle before opening the next one. → *Expect:* the pill box matches the written schedule.
5. **Handle special medicines separately.** Leave liquids, inhalers, refrigerated medicines, patches, and as-needed medicines outside the box with clear reminders. → *Expect:* medicines unsuitable for the pill box are not forced into it.
6. **Double-check every compartment.** Compare the filled organizer against the medication list and labels. ⚠️ *Confirm first:* wrong doses can cause harm; verify before taking or giving any dose. → *Expect:* each compartment contains the expected pills and counts.
7. **Store safely and record completion.** Keep the organizer in a dry, secure place and note the fill date. → *Expect:* the correct weekly organizer is ready and protected.

## Decision points

- Pill appearance changed after refill → ask the pharmacist to confirm before filling.
- Instructions say "as needed" or variable dose → do not preload unless the clinician or pharmacist says how.
- Caregiver fills for someone else → use a second-person check for high-risk medicines when possible.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Dropped or mixed pills:** detect pills on the table or floor → identify each with the bottle or pharmacist; discard contaminated pills if needed.
- **F2 Missed compartment:** detect an empty or extra-filled slot during check → correct it before the week starts.
- **F3 Duplicate medicine:** detect two bottles with same drug or purpose → pause and ask pharmacist or prescriber before filling both.
- **F4 Moisture damage:** detect soft, crumbling, or discolored pills → do not use them; ask the pharmacy for replacement guidance.

## Verification

Every compartment in the weekly pill box matches the current medication list by day and dosing time, special medicines are handled separately, and the organizer is stored safely.

## Variations

- Morning/evening organizer: use a box with enough daily compartments for the actual schedule.
- High-risk regimen: pharmacy blister packs or medication synchronization may be safer than home filling.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because medication mistakes can cause serious harm. Work without distractions, verify against labels, keep medicines away from children, and ask a pharmacist when pill identity or instructions are unclear.
