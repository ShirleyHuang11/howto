## Goal

You identify a licensed therapist who fits your needs, cost constraints, schedule, and preferred care format.

## Preconditions

- Your insurance card or budget for private pay.
- A short description of what you want help with and any preferences about language, identity, modality, or telehealth.
- Emergency contact options if you are in immediate danger or crisis.

## Steps

1. **Handle urgent safety first.** If you may harm yourself or someone else, use emergency services or a crisis line before searching for routine therapy. → *Expect:* immediate danger is routed to urgent support.
2. **Define your requirements.** Note issue areas, preferred session type, language, location, telehealth needs, budget, and availability. → *Expect:* you have a short filter list.
3. **Check coverage or payment options.** [BRANCH: insured, search your insurer directory and verify benefits | uninsured/private pay, search community clinics, training clinics, sliding-scale directories, or employee assistance programs] → *Expect:* you know likely cost per session.
4. **Search licensed providers.** Use insurer directories, professional associations, community clinics, primary-care referrals, or reputable therapist directories. → *Expect:* you have 5-10 candidate names.
5. **Verify license and scope.** Check the state/provincial licensing board or clinic website for active credentials and discipline history where available. → *Expect:* candidates appear licensed for psychotherapy in your location.
6. **Contact several at once.** Send a brief inquiry with your concern, insurance/payment method, availability, telehealth preference, and callback information. → *Expect:* messages are sent to multiple candidates.
7. **Screen responses.** Ask about experience with your concern, fees, cancellation policy, availability, confidentiality, crisis coverage, and whether they are accepting new clients. → *Expect:* at least one candidate meets practical and clinical fit criteria.
8. **Book an initial session.** Confirm date, format, forms, payment, and what to do if you need to cancel. → *Expect:* you receive an appointment confirmation and intake paperwork.
9. **Evaluate fit after 1-3 sessions.** Notice whether you feel respected, understand the plan, and can discuss goals. → *Expect:* you decide to continue, adjust goals, or seek another therapist.

## Decision points

- No one has availability → ask about waitlists, group therapy, short-term clinics, or your insurer's care navigation service.
- You need medication evaluation → look for a psychiatrist, psychiatric nurse practitioner, or primary-care referral in addition to therapy.
- You need culturally or language-specific care → prioritize directories and clinics serving that community and request an interpreter if needed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Directory is inaccurate:** detect disconnected numbers or providers not accepting your insurance → call the insurer and ask for verified available providers.
- **F2 Surprise bill:** detect a higher-than-expected invoice → confirm in-network status, deductible, copay, and billing codes before the next session.
- **F3 Poor fit:** detect feeling dismissed or unsafe in routine care → state concerns, request a different approach, or switch providers.
- **F4 Crisis needs exceed outpatient therapy:** detect active suicidal intent, psychosis, violence risk, or inability to stay safe → use emergency services, crisis care, or the therapist's crisis instructions.

## Verification

You have a confirmed first therapy appointment with a licensed provider, known cost or coverage, intake instructions, and a plan for urgent support if needed.

## Variations

- `us`: insurers must provide mental health benefits under many plans, but networks and deductibles vary; verify benefits directly.
- Campus or employer: counseling centers and employee assistance programs may offer short-term sessions or referrals.
- Rural area: telehealth can expand options, but the provider usually must be licensed where you are located during the session.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because mental health care involves sensitive information and safety planning. Use secure contact methods, verify licensure, understand confidentiality limits, and seek immediate help for danger instead of waiting for a routine appointment.
