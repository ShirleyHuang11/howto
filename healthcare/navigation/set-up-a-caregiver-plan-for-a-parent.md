## Goal

You create a practical caregiving plan for a parent that covers medical contacts, medications, appointments, daily support, emergency response, and decision-making permissions.

## Preconditions

- Parent's consent and participation unless there is a legal guardianship or emergency exception.
- Medication list, diagnoses, clinician contacts, insurance details, pharmacy, emergency contacts, and current support needs.
- Copies or status of healthcare proxy, advance directive, HIPAA releases, and financial or legal powers if relevant.

## Steps

1. **Start with a consent-based conversation.** Ask what help your parent wants, what they want to keep private, and who may be involved. → *Expect:* boundaries and priorities are explicit.
2. **Map current needs.** Review medical care, medications, mobility, meals, transportation, finances, home safety, memory, and social support. → *Expect:* the plan is based on actual needs.
3. **Organize key information.** Create a shared document or binder with contacts, medications, allergies, insurance, pharmacy, diagnoses, and appointment schedule. → *Expect:* authorized helpers can find essential information quickly.
4. **Set communication permissions.** Ask clinicians about HIPAA release forms, portal proxy access, and who can receive information. → *Expect:* care teams can legally speak with designated caregivers.
5. **Assign roles and backup coverage.** Name who handles appointments, medications, transportation, meals, bills, and emergencies. → *Expect:* every recurring need has an owner and backup.
6. **Plan emergency response.** Decide when to call emergency services, which hospital is preferred, where documents are stored, and who is notified first. → *Expect:* crisis steps are written and accessible.
7. **Review and update regularly.** Schedule monthly or quarterly check-ins and after hospitalizations or major changes. → *Expect:* the plan stays current as needs change.

## Decision points

- Parent has decision-making capacity → they decide what help and access to grant.
- Safety risks exceed family capacity → ask about home health, social work, adult protective services, or facility care options.
- Siblings or relatives disagree → document roles and consider a family meeting with a neutral clinician or social worker.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No legal access:** detect clinicians refuse to speak with caregiver → complete HIPAA releases or proxy access forms with parent consent.
- **F2 Medication confusion:** detect missed doses or duplicate bottles → ask a pharmacist for reconciliation and consider pill packaging.
- **F3 Caregiver burnout:** detect missed tasks, resentment, or exhaustion → add respite care, paid help, or redistribute roles.
- **F4 Emergency document missing:** detect proxy or directive cannot be found → gather signed copies and place them in the binder and portal.

## Verification

The plan names authorized contacts, roles, backup caregivers, medication process, appointment process, emergency steps, and document locations, and the parent has approved it where they have capacity.

## Variations

- Dementia or cognitive decline: legal planning should happen early while the parent can still consent.
- Long-distance caregiving: emphasize local backup contacts, pharmacy delivery, telehealth access, and home-safety checks.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because caregiving involves medical privacy, autonomy, safety, and finances. Respect the parent's consent, use legal authorization for access, and escalate immediate danger through emergency or protective services.
