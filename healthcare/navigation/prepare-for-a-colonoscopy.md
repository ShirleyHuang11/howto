## Goal

You complete the clinic's colonoscopy preparation safely so the exam can be performed with clear visualization and minimal avoidable delay.

## Preconditions

- Colonoscopy date, arrival time, facility address, and written prep instructions from the endoscopy center.
- Prescribed bowel-prep medication, clear liquids, and approved medication plan.
- A responsible adult escort if sedation will be used.

## Steps

1. **Read the facility instructions early.** Note diet changes, prep timing, medication rules, and when to stop liquids. → *Expect:* you have a dated preparation schedule.
2. **Confirm medication instructions.** Ask the prescribing clinician or endoscopy team about blood thinners, diabetes medicines, GLP-1 medicines, iron, supplements, and other high-risk medications. → *Expect:* you have written or clearly documented medication guidance.
3. **Arrange escort and transportation.** Sedation usually means you cannot drive, work, or make major decisions afterward. → *Expect:* an adult can take you home and stay as required.
4. **Buy prep supplies.** Pick up bowel prep, clear liquids, petroleum jelly or barrier cream, soft toilet paper, and approved flavorings if allowed. → *Expect:* all supplies are ready before prep day.
5. **Follow diet restrictions exactly.** Switch to low-fiber or clear-liquid diet only as instructed by your facility. → *Expect:* you are eating and drinking only allowed items.
6. **Take bowel prep on schedule.** Use split-dose timing if prescribed and drink allowed fluids to reduce dehydration. → *Expect:* bowel movements become watery and eventually pale yellow or clear.
7. **Stop intake at the instructed time and arrive prepared.** Bring ID, insurance, medication list, escort contact, and forms. → *Expect:* the facility can check you in and proceed safely.

## Decision points

- You vomit repeatedly or cannot finish prep → call the endoscopy center's after-hours number for instructions.
- Stool is still brown or solid near departure time → call the facility; they may adjust prep or reschedule.
- You have diabetes, kidney disease, heart failure, pregnancy, or blood thinners → get individualized instructions before prep day.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Inadequate bowel prep:** detect stool not clear enough → call the facility before leaving; do not invent extra dosing without instructions.
- **F2 No escort:** detect ride cancels → reschedule unless the facility confirms a permitted alternative.
- **F3 Medication conflict:** detect you took a medicine you were told to hold → call the endoscopy team before the procedure.
- **F4 Dehydration symptoms:** detect dizziness, fainting, confusion, or inability to keep fluids down → contact the facility urgently or seek medical care.

## Verification

You followed the facility's diet, medication, and prep timing instructions; stool output matches the prep target; an escort is confirmed; and the facility checks you in for the procedure.

## Variations

- `us`: insurers may classify screening versus diagnostic colonoscopy differently if polyps are found or symptoms prompted the exam.
- Prep products differ: follow the exact instructions for your prescribed product, not a generic internet schedule.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because bowel prep, sedation, and medication holds can affect health. Follow the facility's instructions, confirm medication changes with clinicians, and do not drive after sedation.
