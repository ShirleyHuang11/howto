## Goal

You reach a trained crisis responder quickly, explain what is happening, and agree on the next step to stay safe.

## Preconditions

- A phone, text-capable device, or internet connection.
- Your current location if emergency help may be needed.
- If possible, a safer place away from weapons, medications, traffic, or other immediate hazards.

## Steps

1. **Move toward immediate safety.** Put distance between yourself and anything you could use to harm yourself or someone else; ask a nearby person to stay if possible. → *Expect:* the immediate environment is safer than it was.
2. **Contact the crisis line.** In the US and Canada, call or text 988; elsewhere use your local suicide or mental health crisis line, emergency number, or nearest emergency department. → *Expect:* the call, text, or chat connects to a crisis service.
3. **Say the risk plainly.** Use direct words: "I might hurt myself," "I have a plan," "I cannot stay safe," or "I am worried about someone else." → *Expect:* the responder understands the urgency.
4. **Answer safety questions honestly.** Share whether there is a plan, means, substances, injuries, medical danger, psychosis, violence risk, or someone else at risk. → *Expect:* the responder can judge whether phone support is enough.
5. **Give location if help may be needed.** If emergency response, mobile crisis, or welfare check might be necessary, provide where you are and any access instructions. → *Expect:* responders can send local help if needed.
6. **Make a short safety plan.** Agree on one next action: stay on the line, contact a trusted person, remove means, go to urgent care, or accept mobile crisis/emergency help. → *Expect:* there is a concrete next step for the next hour.
7. **Follow through before ending.** Do not disconnect until you know who you will be with, where you are going, or what you will do next. → *Expect:* support continues after the call ends.
8. **Use emergency services for imminent danger.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* if someone is injured, unconscious, violent, overdosing, or cannot be kept safe, call the local emergency number now. → *Expect:* emergency medical or safety help is being dispatched.

## Decision points

- You can stay safe with support → continue with the crisis responder and contact a trusted person or clinician.
- You cannot stay safe or have acted on a plan → call emergency services or go to an emergency department.
- You are calling for someone else → stay nearby if safe, reduce access to lethal means, and follow the responder's instructions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Long hold or dropped call:** detect no responder or a disconnected line → call again, text if available, contact emergency services, or go to the nearest emergency department.
- **F2 Fear of saying too much:** detect minimizing the risk → state the concrete facts anyway; crisis responders need accurate information.
- **F3 Person refuses help:** detect someone at imminent risk who will not engage → call emergency services or local mobile crisis if available.
- **F4 Privacy concern blocks action:** detect hesitation because others may find out → prioritize life safety; ask the responder what information is confidential and what triggers emergency intervention.

## Verification

You have connected with a crisis responder or emergency service, disclosed the immediate risk, and have an active safety step in place before ending contact.

## Variations

- `us`: call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; call 911 for immediate medical danger, weapons, violence, or an overdose.
- `canada`: 988 is available for suicide crisis support; emergency danger still goes to 911.
- Traveler abroad: use the local emergency number, hotel/front desk, embassy emergency line, or local hospital if you cannot find a crisis line.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because delay can lead to serious injury or death. Crisis lines are for immediate support, not routine scheduling; disclose plans, means, location, and injuries when safety is uncertain.
