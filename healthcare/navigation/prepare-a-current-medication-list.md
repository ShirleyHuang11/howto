## Goal

You create an accurate medication list that a clinician, pharmacist, caregiver, or emergency team can use safely.

## Preconditions

- All prescription bottles, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, supplements, injections, inhalers, patches, drops, and topical medicines.
- Pharmacy app or printout if available.
- Current allergy and adverse-reaction information.

## Steps

1. **Gather every product in one place.** Include medicines taken daily, occasionally, or only as needed. → *Expect:* nothing is hidden in a purse, cabinet, refrigerator, or travel bag.
2. **Record exact medication details.** Write generic or brand name, strength, form, dose, route, frequency, and reason if known. → *Expect:* each entry could be matched to a real label.
3. **Mark how you actually take it.** Note differences from the label, missed doses, stopped medicines, and as-needed frequency. → *Expect:* the list reflects reality, not only prescriptions.
4. **Add prescriber and pharmacy information.** Include clinician name, pharmacy, and refill status if useful. → *Expect:* staff can clarify questions quickly.
5. **List allergies and serious side effects separately.** Include the reaction, not just the drug name. → *Expect:* a clinician can distinguish allergy from intolerance.
6. **Remove outdated entries from the active list.** Keep discontinued medicines in a separate "stopped" section with dates if relevant. → *Expect:* active and inactive medicines are not confused.
7. **Share and update the list.** Bring it to visits, upload to the portal if requested, and update after medication changes. → *Expect:* the same current list is available in print or on your phone.

## Decision points

- You cannot identify a pill or bottle → ask a pharmacist before guessing.
- Hospital discharge changed medicines → reconcile the list against discharge instructions immediately.
- Caregiver manages medicines → give them an editable copy and define who owns updates.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Duplicate therapy:** detect two names for the same medicine or class → ask a pharmacist or clinician to review before taking both if unsure.
- **F2 Dose uncertainty:** detect missing strength or unclear instructions → call the pharmacy or prescriber to verify.
- **F3 Outdated portal list:** detect the portal differs from bottles at home → send corrections before the next visit or review during rooming.
- **F4 Supplement omitted:** detect vitamins or herbal products missing → add them because they can interact with prescriptions.

## Verification

The list includes every current medicine and supplement with name, strength, dose, route, frequency, reason if known, prescriber or pharmacy, and allergies with reactions.

## Variations

- Paper wallet card: use small print but keep the full list at home or in a phone file.
- Complex regimen: ask the pharmacy for a medication synchronization review or blister packaging options.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because wrong medication information can cause harmful prescribing decisions. Do not guess doses, do not discard labels until reconciled, and keep the list private except with care teams or trusted caregivers.
