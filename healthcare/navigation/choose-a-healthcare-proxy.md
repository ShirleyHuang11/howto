## Goal

You choose a healthcare proxy or agent who can make medical decisions for you if you cannot communicate, and you document that choice properly.

## Preconditions

- A basic understanding of your medical values and treatment preferences.
- Names and contact details for a primary agent and at least one alternate.
- The healthcare proxy or medical power-of-attorney form used in your jurisdiction.

## Steps

1. **Understand the role.** A healthcare proxy makes medical decisions only when the law and clinicians determine you cannot decide or communicate. → *Expect:* you know the role is decision-making authority, not just emergency contact status.
2. **Identify candidates who can serve.** Consider trust, availability, emotional steadiness, ability to understand medical information, and willingness to follow your wishes. → *Expect:* you have one primary candidate and possible alternates.
3. **Check legal eligibility and conflicts.** Some jurisdictions restrict treating clinicians, facility employees, witnesses, or minors from serving. → *Expect:* your candidate is likely eligible.
4. **Ask the person directly.** Explain what the role may require and ask whether they are willing. → *Expect:* the candidate clearly accepts or declines.
5. **Discuss concrete preferences.** Talk through life support, comfort-focused care, severe brain injury, long-term dependence, religious preferences, and who they should consult. → *Expect:* the proxy can describe your wishes without guessing.
6. **Complete the appointment form.** ⚠️ *Irreversible/legal significance:* signing gives the named person future medical decision authority if you cannot decide; confirm the person, alternates, and limits before signing. → *Expect:* the form is correctly signed, witnessed, or notarized as required.
7. **Distribute and record the proxy.** Give copies to the proxy, alternates, primary care clinician, hospital portal, and relevant family. → *Expect:* the appointment can be found during a medical crisis.

## Decision points

- Best emotional support person is not the best decision-maker → name the decision-maker as proxy and list others for support.
- Candidate wants to substitute their own values → choose someone else.
- You have complex family dynamics → consider naming alternates clearly and adding written instructions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Proxy refuses later:** detect hesitation or changed circumstances → appoint a new proxy and redistribute the updated form.
- **F2 Family disputes authority:** detect relatives challenge the proxy → provide the signed form to clinicians and consider legal advice.
- **F3 Proxy cannot be reached:** detect outdated phone or travel barriers → update contact information and name alternates.
- **F4 Form not legally valid:** detect missing witnesses, notary, or required language → redo the form under current local rules.

## Verification

Your chosen proxy has accepted, a valid signed form names them and alternates, and copies are available to your proxy, clinicians, and medical record.

## Variations

- `us`: terms vary by state, including healthcare proxy, healthcare agent, durable power of attorney for health care, or medical power of attorney.
- Married or partnered people: default next-of-kin rules may not match your wishes, especially for unmarried partners or blended families.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because the proxy may make major medical decisions when you cannot. Do not choose under pressure, do not leave the proxy uninformed, and confirm legal signing requirements before relying on the form.
