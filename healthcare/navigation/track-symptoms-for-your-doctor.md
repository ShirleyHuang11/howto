## Goal

You create a clear symptom record your clinician can quickly scan and use to decide next steps.

## Preconditions

- A notebook, notes app, spreadsheet, or patient portal message draft.
- The symptom you want to track and the approximate date it started.
- Any relevant medication list, home readings, photos, or test results.

## Steps

1. **Choose one tracking place.** Use a format you can update daily, not scattered texts or memory. → *Expect:* one notebook page, note, or file is dedicated to this symptom.
2. **Record the baseline facts.** Write when it started, where it happens, how often it occurs, and whether it is improving, worsening, or unchanged. → *Expect:* the first entry answers "when, where, how often, and trend."
3. **Use consistent measures.** Rate pain or severity from 0-10, note temperature, blood pressure, glucose, peak flow, or other readings only if you normally measure them or were told to. → *Expect:* each entry uses the same scale or units.
4. **Log triggers and relief.** Note food, activity, sleep, stress, menstrual cycle, medication timing, injuries, or exposures that seem connected. → *Expect:* each entry includes what made symptoms better or worse, if known.
5. **Add medication and treatment notes.** Write the dose, time taken, missed doses, side effects, and whether it helped. → *Expect:* your clinician can see what you tried and the response.
6. **Attach objective evidence.** Photograph visible rashes, swelling, wounds, or device readings with dates; keep copies of urgent-care or lab paperwork. → *Expect:* supporting evidence is dated and easy to find.
7. **Summarize before the visit.** Convert the log into a short timeline, top concerns, and the 2-3 most important patterns. → *Expect:* you have a one-page summary or concise portal message.
8. **Escalate urgent warning signs.** If symptoms include chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke symptoms, severe allergic reaction, suicidal intent, severe dehydration, or sudden severe pain, seek emergency help instead of tracking. → *Expect:* urgent symptoms are handled through emergency or same-day care.

## Decision points

- Symptoms are mild and improving → keep tracking until the scheduled visit unless new warning signs appear.
- Symptoms are worsening, recurrent, or interfering with daily life → request a sooner appointment or nurse advice line review.
- You are tracking for a child, older adult, pregnancy, or complex condition → include caregiver observations and call sooner for changes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Entries are too vague:** detect notes like "felt bad" without timing or severity → rewrite future entries with time, severity, location, and duration.
- **F2 Too much raw detail:** detect several pages the doctor cannot scan → bring the raw log but lead with a one-page summary.
- **F3 Missing medication context:** detect symptoms without dose timing → add a medication timeline for the same dates.
- **F4 Concerning symptoms are delayed:** detect red flags being logged instead of escalated → call emergency services, urgent care, or the clinician's after-hours line.

## Verification

Before the visit, you have a dated symptom timeline, severity scale, relevant measurements or photos, medication notes, and a short summary ready to share.

## Variations

- `us`: many patient portals allow photos or PDFs as message attachments; use them for non-urgent updates only.
- Chronic condition: ask the clinician which measurements matter and how often to record them.
- Limited literacy or language access: use voice memos or photos and request an interpreter for the visit.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because symptom records can affect health decisions and contain sensitive information. Store them in a private place, avoid sharing them on public forums, and seek urgent care for warning signs rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
