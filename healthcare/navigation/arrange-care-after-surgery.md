## Goal

You arrange safe transportation, supervision, medications, wound care, meals, and follow-up support for the first days after surgery.

## Preconditions

- Surgery date, expected anesthesia type, discharge instructions if already available, and recovery restrictions.
- Names and contact information for caregivers, pharmacy, surgeon, and after-hours line.
- Home access, food, hygiene supplies, and any prescribed equipment.

## Steps

1. **Ask the surgical team what help is required.** Confirm driving restrictions, supervision duration, lifting limits, wound care, and expected recovery timeline. → *Expect:* the care needs are specific rather than guessed.
2. **Name a primary caregiver and backup.** Confirm availability for pickup, first-night monitoring, medication reminders, and emergency contact. → *Expect:* at least one reliable adult is committed.
3. **Set up transportation.** Arrange a permitted ride home and follow-up rides; rideshare alone may not satisfy discharge rules after sedation. → *Expect:* transportation meets the facility's policy.
4. **Prepare medications and supplies.** Pick up prescriptions if allowed, buy dressings or stool softeners if instructed, and organize the medication schedule. → *Expect:* essential supplies are ready before discharge.
5. **Prepare the home.** Clear tripping hazards, set up a sleeping area, place water and phone within reach, and arrange meals. → *Expect:* the patient can move safely with expected restrictions.
6. **Create a warning-sign plan.** Write down who to call for fever, uncontrolled pain, bleeding, breathing problems, confusion, or wound concerns. → *Expect:* caregiver and patient know when and how to escalate.
7. **Confirm follow-up appointments.** Schedule post-op visits, therapy, labs, or dressing checks. → *Expect:* follow-up care appears on the calendar.

## Decision points

- Patient lives alone → arrange overnight supervision if anesthesia or surgery instructions require it.
- Mobility will be limited → ask about home health, physical therapy, walker, crutches, or temporary bathroom aids.
- Pain medicine has safety risks → ask the clinician or pharmacist about dosing, sedation, constipation, and interactions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Caregiver cancels:** detect loss of pickup or supervision → call the facility before discharge and activate backup or reschedule if needed.
- **F2 Prescription unavailable:** detect pharmacy out of stock or closed → ask the surgical team to redirect the prescription.
- **F3 Warning signs ignored:** detect fever, heavy bleeding, chest pain, shortness of breath, or severe confusion → call the surgical team, urgent line, or emergency services as appropriate.
- **F4 Home unsafe:** detect stairs, clutter, or no bathroom access with restrictions → arrange temporary stay or extra support before surgery.

## Verification

Transportation, caregiver coverage, prescriptions, home supplies, warning-sign instructions, and follow-up appointments are confirmed before the patient leaves the facility.

## Variations

- `us`: home health, durable medical equipment, and physical therapy may require insurance authorization or physician orders.
- Minor procedure: support may be limited to ride home and same-day observation, but follow written discharge rules.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because poor post-op planning can cause falls, medication errors, or delayed response to complications. Confirm discharge restrictions and do not leave a sedated patient alone unless the care team explicitly permits it.
