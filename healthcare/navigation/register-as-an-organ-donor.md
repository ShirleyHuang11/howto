## Goal

You record your organ, eye, and tissue donation decision in the official registry for your jurisdiction and make sure your family knows your choice.

## Preconditions

- Legal name, date of birth, address, and identification information required by your registry.
- Access to your DMV/driver licensing portal, national registry, or health authority donor registry.
- Enough time to read what donation consent covers in your jurisdiction.

## Steps

1. **Find the official registry.** Use your state/province DMV, national health service, or recognized organ donor registry; avoid unofficial lead-generation sites. → *Expect:* the page is operated by a government or authorized donation organization.
2. **Read what registration means.** Check whether consent includes organs, eyes, tissue, research, transplant only, or optional limits. → *Expect:* you understand the scope before registering.
3. **Enter identity details.** Provide the exact legal name, birth date, address, and ID requested. → *Expect:* the form matches your official records.
4. **Choose donation preferences.** Select full donation or any allowed limits according to your decision. → *Expect:* the registry summary reflects your preference.
5. **Submit the registration.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* registration can guide decisions after death when you cannot speak; confirm identity and preferences before submitting. → *Expect:* the registry confirms you are registered or updated.
6. **Save proof.** Download, print, screenshot, or note the confirmation number if available. → *Expect:* you have evidence of the registration.
7. **Update your driver's license if relevant.** At renewal or through the DMV portal, confirm the donor designation appears if your jurisdiction uses one. → *Expect:* your license or DMV record reflects donor status where applicable.
8. **Tell key people.** Inform family, healthcare proxy, executor, or emergency contact so they are not surprised. → *Expect:* decision-makers know your documented choice.

## Decision points

- You have religious, cultural, or medical questions → consult a trusted advisor or transplant organization before registering.
- You want to change your mind → use the official registry update/removal process and tell family.
- You are completing advance care planning → align donor registration with healthcare proxy and advance directive documents.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unofficial site:** detect ads, fees, or vague ownership → leave and use the DMV, Donate Life, NHS, or official government/health registry.
- **F2 Identity mismatch:** detect registration cannot find your record → retry with legal name, former name, or driver license number.
- **F3 Family unaware:** detect loved ones object or are confused → share the confirmation and explain your decision while you can.
- **F4 Preferences changed:** detect old donor status on license or registry → update through the official site and keep the new confirmation.

## Verification

The official registry or DMV record shows your donor status and preferences, and you have saved confirmation plus told at least one trusted decision-maker.

## Variations

- `us`: many states register donors through DMV transactions and state Donate Life registries; rules and ability to limit donation vary by state.
- `uk`: the NHS Organ Donor Register records opt-in and opt-out decisions; family discussion remains important.
- Opt-out jurisdictions: the default may differ, but recording and communicating your preference still matters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because this is a legal health decision involving identity data and end-of-life choices. Use official registries, confirm preferences before submitting, and communicate your decision to people likely to be contacted.
