## Goal

A knocked-out permanent tooth is handled by the crown, kept moist, and taken to urgent dental care quickly.

## Preconditions

- A tooth has been completely knocked out.
- Call emergency services for head injury, loss of consciousness, uncontrolled bleeding, trouble breathing, severe facial trauma, or suspected abuse.
- Do not reinsert a baby tooth.
- Handle the tooth only by the crown, not the root.
- Urgent dental care is needed, ideally within 30-60 minutes for a permanent tooth.

## Steps

1. **Check for serious injury.** Look for airway trouble, heavy bleeding, confusion, vomiting, neck pain, or loss of consciousness. → *Expect:* life-threatening trauma is addressed first.
2. **Find the tooth.** Pick it up by the white crown and avoid touching the root. → *Expect:* delicate root cells are protected.
3. **Rinse only if dirty.** Briefly rinse with milk or clean water without scrubbing, soap, alcohol, or scraping. → *Expect:* visible dirt is removed while root tissue remains intact.
4. **Decide if it is permanent.** [BRANCH: permanent tooth | baby tooth or unsure] reinsert only a permanent tooth if the person is alert and cooperative; store baby teeth or uncertain teeth moist. → *Expect:* a baby tooth is not forced into the socket.
5. **Reinsert if appropriate.** Place the permanent tooth gently back into the socket facing the correct way. → *Expect:* the tooth sits near its normal position.
6. **Hold in place.** Ask the person to bite gently on clean gauze or cloth. → *Expect:* the tooth stays seated without hard chewing.
7. **Store moist if not reinserted.** Put the tooth in milk, tooth preservation solution, or inside the person's cheek if safe and they are old enough not to swallow it. → *Expect:* the tooth root stays wet.
8. **Control mouth bleeding.** Apply gentle pressure with clean gauze or cloth. → *Expect:* bleeding slows without disturbing the tooth.
9. **Call a dentist or emergency dental clinic.** Say a permanent tooth was avulsed and ask for immediate care. → *Expect:* urgent dental treatment is arranged.
10. **Go promptly.** Bring the tooth, timing, and injury details to dental or emergency care. → *Expect:* reimplantation or stabilization can happen as soon as possible.

## Decision points

- Tooth is a baby tooth → do not reinsert; contact a dentist urgently.
- Person is unconscious, confused, or might swallow the tooth → do not place it in the mouth; store it in milk or preservation solution.
- Heavy bleeding or facial trauma is present → call emergency services.
- Tooth is cracked but not fully out → avoid wiggling it and seek urgent dental care.
- Tetanus risk or dirty injury exists → ask clinicians about tetanus status.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Root touched or scrubbed:** detect handling by the root → recover by stopping, keeping it moist, and telling the dentist.
- **F2 Tooth allowed to dry:** detect dry storage → recover by placing it immediately in milk or preservation solution.
- **F3 Baby tooth reinserted:** detect young child or primary tooth → recover by stopping and calling a dentist for instructions.
- **F4 Dental care delayed:** detect waiting for routine appointment → recover by calling emergency dental services now.

## Verification

Serious injuries have been screened, the tooth is reinserted or stored moist, bleeding is controlled, and urgent dental care is underway with the time of injury known.

## Variations

- Sports event: use a tooth rescue kit if available and notify guardians for a child.
- Braces: do not force the tooth through wires; store it moist and seek emergency dental care.
- Rural setting: call the nearest emergency department or dentist for transport advice.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because mishandling can permanently reduce the chance of saving the tooth, and associated head or face injuries may be serious. Escalate immediately for trauma red flags.
