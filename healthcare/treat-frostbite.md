## Goal

Suspected frostbite is protected from refreezing, rewarmed safely when appropriate, and escalated for urgent medical care.

## Preconditions

- Skin is very cold, numb, waxy, white, gray, yellow, hard, blistered, or painful after cold exposure.
- Call emergency services for hypothermia signs, severe frostbite, deep numbness, blistering, confusion, shivering that stops, or inability to reach warmth.
- Do not rewarm if the part may refreeze before care.
- Do not rub, massage, use direct heat, or walk on frostbitten feet unless necessary for survival.
- This is first aid guidance, not a substitute for medical care.

## Steps

1. **Move to warmth.** Get the person indoors, into a vehicle, tent, or sheltered place out of wind and cold. → *Expect:* ongoing freezing exposure stops.
2. **Check for hypothermia.** Look for confusion, slurred speech, severe shivering, drowsiness, or clumsiness. → *Expect:* life-threatening cold illness is recognized.
3. **Call emergency services for red flags.** Contact help if hypothermia, severe frostbite, blistering, or evacuation difficulty is present. → *Expect:* professional help is being arranged.
4. **Remove constricting items.** Take off wet gloves, boots, socks, rings, watches, and tight clothing gently. → *Expect:* swelling will not be trapped by tight items.
5. **Protect the area.** Cover loosely with clean dry cloth and separate fingers or toes with gauze if available. → *Expect:* skin is protected from pressure and rubbing.
6. **Decide whether rewarming is safe.** [BRANCH: no refreezing risk | possible refreezing] rewarm only if the area can stay warm afterward; otherwise keep it protected. → *Expect:* tissue is not thawed and frozen again.
7. **Rewarm in warm water.** Immerse the part in warm, not hot, water for 15-30 minutes if safe to rewarm. → *Expect:* skin softens and color may return while pain increases.
8. **Avoid direct heat.** Keep the part away from fires, heaters, heating pads, stoves, and hot water. → *Expect:* numb skin is not burned.
9. **Do not break blisters.** Leave blisters intact and cover loosely. → *Expect:* infection risk is reduced.
10. **Keep warm during transport.** Elevate if practical, avoid walking on affected feet, and continue medical evaluation. → *Expect:* injury is protected until care takes over.

## Decision points

- Refreezing is possible during evacuation → do not actively rewarm; protect and evacuate.
- Hypothermia signs are present → treat as an emergency and warm the whole person gently.
- Frostbite involves face, hands, feet, genitals, deep numbness, hard skin, or blisters → seek urgent medical care.
- Skin is only mildly cold and sensation returns quickly with gentle warming → monitor for delayed pain, numbness, or color change.
- Pain becomes severe during rewarming → continue gentle rewarming and seek urgent care.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rubbing snow or skin:** detect friction on frozen tissue → recover by stopping and covering loosely.
- **F2 Direct heat burn:** detect use of fire, heater, or heating pad → recover by removing heat source and using warm water only when appropriate.
- **F3 Refreezing after thaw:** detect thawing before outdoor evacuation → recover by stopping rewarming and protecting from further injury.
- **F4 Tight jewelry left on:** detect rings or watches near swelling → recover by removing early if it slides off easily.

## Verification

The person is out of cold exposure, the affected area is protected from rubbing and refreezing, emergency services or urgent care is involved for red flags, and rewarming was done only when the area can stay warm.

## Variations

- Backcountry: prioritize shelter, hypothermia prevention, and evacuation; avoid rewarming if travel will refreeze the part.
- Child or older adult: call for medical advice sooner because cold injury can progress quickly.
- Frostnip: mild numbness that resolves with gentle warming still needs monitoring for worsening symptoms.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because frostbite can cause permanent tissue loss and hypothermia can be fatal. Avoid direct heat, friction, refreezing, and unnecessary walking on affected feet.
