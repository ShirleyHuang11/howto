## Goal

A suspected fracture is kept still, circulation is monitored, and emergency care is obtained when needed.

## Preconditions

- Suspect a broken bone after trauma with deformity, swelling, severe pain, inability to bear weight, grinding sensation, numbness, or visible bone.
- Call emergency services for severe bleeding, open fracture, deformity, numb or blue limb, major trauma, suspected spine/pelvis/femur fracture, or inability to move safely.
- Do not try to straighten a deformed limb.
- Control life-threatening bleeding first.
- This is first aid guidance, not definitive fracture treatment.

## Steps

1. **Check scene and person.** Make sure the area is safe and check breathing, bleeding, and alertness first. → *Expect:* immediate life threats are recognized.
2. **Call for emergency help if indicated.** Report suspected fracture, location, bleeding, deformity, and circulation changes. → *Expect:* urgent care is activated for serious injuries.
3. **Tell them not to move.** Support the injured part in the position found. → *Expect:* pain and further tissue injury are reduced.
4. **Check circulation beyond injury.** Look for color, warmth, sensation, movement, and pulse if you know how. → *Expect:* baseline circulation and nerve status are known.
5. **Cover open wounds.** Place clean dressing over any wound without pushing exposed bone back in. → *Expect:* contamination is reduced.
6. **Apply a splint if transport requires it.** [BRANCH: awaiting ambulance | must move] avoid unnecessary splinting if help is coming; splint only to prevent movement during necessary transport. → *Expect:* movement is minimized without extra manipulation.
7. **Pad and secure above and below.** Use boards, rolled magazines, pillows, or clothing, tying above and below the injury without covering fingers or toes. → *Expect:* joints around the injury move less.
8. **Recheck circulation after splinting.** Compare color, warmth, numbness, and movement beyond the splint. → *Expect:* the splint is not cutting off blood flow.
9. **Use cold pack safely.** Apply wrapped cold pack for swelling if available and tolerated. → *Expect:* swelling and pain may decrease without skin freezing.
10. **Monitor until care.** Watch pain, swelling, numbness, color, and shock signs while waiting or transporting. → *Expect:* worsening is caught and reported.

## Decision points

- Limb is blue, numb, cold, or pulseless → call emergency services and loosen tight wraps.
- Bone is visible or wound is deep → cover, do not push bone in, and call emergency services.
- Neck, back, pelvis, hip, or thigh injury suspected → avoid movement and call emergency services.
- Person must be moved from danger → support the injury and move only as needed for safety.
- Pain increases after splinting → loosen and recheck position and circulation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Limb straightened:** detect pulling or alignment attempts → recover by stopping and supporting the position found.
- **F2 Splint too tight:** detect numbness, blue color, swelling, or increased pain → recover by loosening ties and rechecking.
- **F3 Wound contaminated:** detect exposed wound left uncovered → recover by placing a clean dressing lightly over it.
- **F4 Hidden shock:** detect pale clammy skin or confusion → recover by calling emergency services and treating for shock.

## Verification

The injured area is supported with minimal movement, emergency services are called for red flags, circulation beyond the injury is checked before and after splinting, and worsening symptoms are monitored.

## Variations

- Finger injury: buddy tape to the neighboring finger only if circulation and skin are intact.
- Ankle injury: immobilize with a pillow or folded blanket and avoid weight bearing.
- Remote setting: splint for evacuation and recheck circulation frequently.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because fractures can involve bleeding, nerve injury, shock, and permanent damage. Do not straighten deformities or push exposed bone back in, and call emergency services for serious signs.
