## Goal

Open a college savings plan, name the beneficiary, choose investments, and set contribution records for education funding.

## Preconditions

- You know the account owner, beneficiary, relationship, addresses, dates of birth, and tax IDs if required.
- You can compare state tax benefits, fees, investment options, contribution limits, and qualified expense rules.
- You have bank routing/account information for contributions.
- You understand market risk and financial-aid implications.

## Steps

1. **Choose the plan type.** Compare 529 savings plans, prepaid tuition plans, custodial accounts, Coverdell accounts, and ordinary brokerage savings. → *Expect:* you know why the chosen plan fits the education goal.
2. **Compare state and fee details.** Check in-state tax deductions/credits, annual fees, investment expenses, age-based portfolios, direct-sold vs advisor-sold options, and rollover rules. → *Expect:* one plan is selected with known costs and state benefits.
3. **Gather owner and beneficiary details.** Prepare legal names, addresses, dates of birth, tax IDs, citizenship/residency questions, and successor owner information. → *Expect:* the application can be completed accurately.
4. **Open the account.** Complete the official plan application and beneficiary designation. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* account ownership and beneficiary setup can affect taxes, aid, and control, so confirm details before submitting. → *Expect:* the plan issues an account number or pending application confirmation.
5. **Choose investments.** Select age-based, static, bank, or custom portfolios based on time horizon and risk tolerance. → *Expect:* contributions have an investment allocation, not unassigned cash unless intentional.
6. **Link funding source.** Add bank information or payroll/gift contribution instructions and verify microdeposits if required. → *Expect:* the funding source is confirmed or pending verification.
7. **Make the first contribution.** Enter amount, contribution year if asked, and timing. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* contributions may have tax and gift-tax reporting consequences, so confirm amount and source before transfer. → *Expect:* the plan shows a scheduled or posted contribution.
8. **Save account documents.** Download the program disclosure, confirmation, investment selection, beneficiary details, and contribution receipt. → *Expect:* you have records for taxes and future account management.
9. **Set review reminders.** Revisit contributions, investment age band, beneficiary, and qualified expense rules at least yearly. → *Expect:* the plan remains aligned with school timeline and family changes.

## Decision points

- Your state offers a tax deduction or credit → compare whether the benefit outweighs another state's lower fees.
- Beneficiary may not attend college → review options for changing beneficiary, apprenticeships, student loans, rollovers, or penalties on nonqualified withdrawals.
- Grandparent owns the account → financial-aid treatment and control may differ.
- You want guaranteed tuition → prepaid plans have different residency, school, and refund limits.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bank link fails:** detect failed microdeposits or rejected ACH → recover by re-entering routing/account numbers from a bank statement.
- **F2 Wrong beneficiary:** detect name, date of birth, or tax ID error → recover by filing the plan's correction form immediately.
- **F3 Contribution misclassified:** detect wrong tax year or gift source → recover by contacting the plan before tax filing.
- **F4 Investment not selected:** detect cash/core holding only → recover by choosing an allocation or confirming cash is intentional.
- **F5 Nonqualified withdrawal risk:** detect expense not eligible → recover by paying from other funds or documenting qualified expenses before withdrawal.

## Verification

The plan account is open, beneficiary and successor owner are correct, investment allocation is selected, and the first contribution is scheduled or posted.

## Variations

- `us-529`: state tax benefits, fees, creditor protection, and qualified expense rules vary by state.
- `able-rollover`: some families may coordinate with ABLE accounts for eligible disabled beneficiaries.
- `custodial-account`: UTMA/UGMA assets belong to the child and may affect aid differently.
- `multiple-children`: separate beneficiaries or future beneficiary changes need tracking.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from investment loss, tax mistakes, and child identity data. Use official plan sites, save disclosures, and do not overfund money that may be needed for non-education expenses.
