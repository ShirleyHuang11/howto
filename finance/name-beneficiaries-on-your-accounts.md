## Goal

Add or update beneficiaries on financial accounts so assets pass to the intended people or organizations outside ordinary account access.

## Preconditions

- You have account logins or paper forms for retirement accounts, life insurance, bank accounts, brokerage accounts, and employer benefits.
- You know each beneficiary's legal name, relationship, date of birth, address, and tax ID if required.
- You have chosen primary and contingent beneficiaries and percentage shares that total 100%.
- You understand beneficiary designations can override a will.

## Steps

1. **Inventory accounts.** List retirement plans, IRAs, life insurance, annuities, bank payable-on-death accounts, brokerage transfer-on-death accounts, HSAs, and employer benefits. → *Expect:* every account that can name a beneficiary is on one checklist.
2. **Review current beneficiaries.** Log in or request beneficiary forms and note primary, contingent, percentages, and last update date. → *Expect:* you know what would happen today if you died.
3. **Choose updated beneficiaries.** Decide primary and backup recipients, percentage splits, and whether any minors, trusts, charities, or estates are involved. → *Expect:* designations total 100% and include backups.
4. **Check legal and tax complications.** Review spousal consent, divorce orders, community property, minor beneficiaries, special-needs benefits, and trust naming rules. → *Expect:* you know whether professional advice or notarized consent is needed.
5. **Complete the institution's form.** Enter beneficiary details exactly, including full legal names, relationship, dates of birth, addresses, tax IDs if required, and percentages. → *Expect:* the form has no blanks or percentage errors.
6. **Submit the beneficiary change.** [BRANCH: online | upload | mail | employer portal] Follow signature, notarization, medallion, or spousal-consent requirements. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* beneficiary designations can control assets at death and may override your will, so confirm names and shares before submission. → *Expect:* the institution accepts the change or shows pending review.
7. **Save confirmation.** Download the confirmation page, updated beneficiary summary, or stamped form. → *Expect:* you have proof of current beneficiaries for each account.
8. **Tell fiduciaries where records are.** Inform executor, trustee, or trusted person where the account inventory and confirmations are stored without sharing passwords. → *Expect:* someone responsible can find the records later.
9. **Set a review schedule.** Recheck after marriage, divorce, birth, death, account rollover, job change, or every two years. → *Expect:* stale designations are less likely to survive unnoticed.

## Decision points

- Spouse is not primary beneficiary on a retirement plan → spousal consent may be required.
- Beneficiary is a minor → consider trust or custodial arrangement instead of outright designation.
- You name a trust or charity → use exact legal name and tax ID from the governing document or organization.
- Divorce occurred → update accounts directly; divorce decrees do not always change beneficiary records automatically.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Percentages rejected:** detect total not equal to 100% → recover by recalculating primary and contingent shares separately.
- **F2 Name mismatch:** detect institution cannot verify beneficiary → recover with legal names, dates of birth, and relationship details.
- **F3 Spousal consent missing:** detect pending or rejected retirement-plan change → recover with the required notarized consent form.
- **F4 Old beneficiary still listed:** detect no confirmation after submission → recover by calling the institution and resubmitting if needed.
- **F5 Will conflict discovered:** detect estate plan says something different → recover by coordinating will, trust, and beneficiary forms with advice if needed.

## Verification

Each account shows accepted primary and contingent beneficiaries with correct names and percentage shares, and confirmations are saved.

## Variations

- `us-retirement`: ERISA plans and some community-property states have spousal-rights rules.
- `bank-pod`: payable-on-death rules and forms vary by bank and state.
- `brokerage-tod`: transfer-on-death registration may need a separate account agreement.
- `employer-benefits`: beneficiary elections may reset or need review after job changes.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because designations can transfer large assets outside the will. Protect beneficiary tax IDs and dates of birth, avoid naming minors outright without advice, and keep confirmations private but findable.
