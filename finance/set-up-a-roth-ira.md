## Goal

Open a Roth IRA, fund it within current eligibility rules, select investments, and save the records needed for taxes and beneficiary tracking.

## Preconditions

- You have earned income or other compensation that counts for Roth IRA contribution eligibility.
- You can check current income limits, contribution limits, and deadlines from official tax guidance.
- You have legal name, tax ID, date of birth, address, employment details, bank information, and beneficiary information.
- You understand that investments can lose value and that early withdrawals may have tax or penalty consequences.

## Steps

1. **Confirm Roth eligibility.** Check current income limits, contribution limits, earned-income requirements, and filing-status rules. → *Expect:* you know whether you can contribute directly and how much.
2. **Choose a provider.** Compare brokerages or fund companies on account fees, fund expenses, investment choices, automatic investing, and service. → *Expect:* one Roth IRA provider is selected.
3. **Open the account.** Enter identity, tax, employment, address, communication, and regulatory information through the provider's official site. → *Expect:* the Roth IRA is open or pending identity review.
4. **Name beneficiaries.** Add primary and contingent beneficiaries with percentages totaling 100%. → *Expect:* beneficiary designations show accepted.
5. **Link funding source.** Add a bank account or transfer source and verify any microdeposits or login connection. → *Expect:* the provider shows an approved funding source.
6. **Contribute for the correct year.** Enter amount, tax year, and frequency, staying within the current allowed limit. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* contributions and investment purchases can create tax corrections if ineligible, so confirm amount, tax year, and eligibility first. → *Expect:* the contribution is scheduled, pending, or posted for the intended year.
7. **Select investments.** Choose a target-date fund, index funds, or another diversified option that fits your risk tolerance and timeline. → *Expect:* cash contributions have an investment allocation or order.
8. **Save confirmations.** Download account opening, beneficiary, contribution, investment, and tax-form notices. → *Expect:* records are stored for the contribution year.

## Decision points

- Income is above the direct Roth range → pause and review alternatives with current tax guidance before contributing.
- Unsure about investments → use a simple diversified option available in the account until you can review.
- Funding near the deadline → confirm whether the provider records contribution year by initiation date, settlement date, or tax deadline rules.
- Spousal contribution → verify spouse, filing, and compensation rules for the current year.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Excess contribution:** detect provider or tax software warning that contribution exceeds eligibility → recover by contacting the provider about corrective distribution or recharacterization.
- **F2 Money uninvested:** detect a cash or settlement fund balance after contribution posts → recover by placing an investment order or setting automatic investment.
- **F3 Wrong tax year:** detect contribution coded to the wrong year → recover by asking the provider to recode it before tax forms are finalized.
- **F4 Beneficiary missing:** detect blank beneficiary page → recover by submitting beneficiary designations immediately.

## Verification

The Roth IRA is open, beneficiary designations are accepted, the intended contribution is posted or scheduled for the correct year, and the balance is invested or has a documented investment order.

## Variations

- `us`: Roth IRA limits, income phaseouts, deadlines, and correction methods are set by IRS rules and change over time.
- `backdoor-roth`: existing pre-tax IRA balances can affect taxes, so review conversion and pro-rata rules before acting.
- `minor-roth`: a custodial Roth IRA requires a minor with eligible earned income and a custodian.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from tax eligibility errors, market loss, and exposure of identity, bank, and beneficiary data. Use the provider's official site and keep contribution records secure.
