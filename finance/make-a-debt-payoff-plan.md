## Goal

Create a realistic debt payoff plan that covers minimum payments, chooses a strategy, and prevents new missed-payment fees.

## Preconditions

- You have current statements for credit cards, loans, medical bills, and collections.
- You know your monthly take-home income and essential expenses.
- You can log in to each account or contact each creditor.

## Steps

1. **List every debt.** Record creditor, balance, interest rate, minimum payment, due date, account status, and any promo expiration. → *Expect:* no debt is hidden from the plan.
2. **Protect minimum payments.** Add all minimums to a calendar or autopay from a funded account. → *Expect:* late fees and credit damage are less likely.
3. **Calculate payoff cash.** Subtract rent, food, utilities, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments, and a small cash buffer from income. → *Expect:* you know the extra monthly amount available.
4. **Pick a strategy.** [BRANCH: avalanche | snowball | urgent-default first] target highest interest, smallest balance, or accounts at risk of lawsuit/repossession first. → *Expect:* each extra dollar has a clear destination.
5. **Check fee traps.** Note balance-transfer fees, origination fees, prepayment penalties, deferred-interest promotions, and hardship-plan credit reporting. → *Expect:* the plan does not accidentally increase cost.
6. **Contact creditors if needed.** Ask about hardship plans, lower rates, due-date changes, fee waivers, or medical financial assistance. → *Expect:* any changed terms are confirmed in writing.
7. **Build the monthly schedule.** Assign payment dates, minimum amounts, target extra payment, and payoff order for the next three months. → *Expect:* the plan fits actual paydays.
8. **Make only confirmed payments.** Pay through official creditor portals or bill pay, starting with minimums and then the target extra payment. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* payments usually cannot be pulled back after processing, so confirm account, amount, and date before submitting. → *Expect:* receipts match the schedule.
9. **Review monthly.** Update balances, interest rates, income changes, and emergency expenses. → *Expect:* the payoff date and next target stay current.

## Decision points

- Minimums exceed available cash → contact creditors, consider nonprofit credit counseling, and avoid taking new high-cost debt.
- Interest rates are very high but credit is good → compare consolidation or balance transfer offers after fees.
- A debt is in collections or legal status → prioritize validation, deadlines, and legal advice before voluntary payment.
- You lack a small emergency buffer → keep a minimal cash cushion so one surprise expense does not restart borrowing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed due date:** detect late notice or fee, recover by paying current amount and requesting a one-time waiver.
- **F2 Autopay overdraft:** detect payment larger than cash available, recover by adjusting autopay dates and maintaining a buffer.
- **F3 Promo interest expires:** detect deferred or promotional APR deadline, recover by moving that debt earlier in the payoff order.
- **F4 Settlement tax surprise:** detect forgiven debt offer, recover by asking about tax forms and getting advice before accepting.
- **F5 Scam debt help:** detect upfront-fee promises or pressure, recover by using nonprofit counseling or official creditor programs.

## Verification

Your plan lists every debt, covers every minimum payment, names one target debt, and shows receipts or scheduled payments for the current month.

## Variations

- `us`: nonprofit credit counseling may offer debt management plans; debt settlement and bankruptcy have legal and tax consequences.
- Student loans: federal repayment, deferment, forbearance, and forgiveness rules differ from private loans.
- Medical bills: request itemized bills and financial assistance before using credit cards.

## Safety & privacy

Debt work exposes account numbers, income, and identity data. Avoid sharing credentials, beware upfront-fee debt relief, and confirm payment terms in writing.
