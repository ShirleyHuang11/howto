## Goal

Dispute or validate a collections notice before paying, while preserving deadlines, evidence, and consumer rights.

## Preconditions

- You have the collection letter, text, email, voicemail, or credit-report entry.
- You can identify the collector, creditor, account number, amount, and notice date if provided.
- You have records of the original account, payments, insurance, returns, or disputes if available.

## Steps

1. **Do not admit or pay immediately.** Save the notice and avoid discussing payment until you identify the debt. → *Expect:* you have not restarted avoidable risk through rushed statements or payments.
2. **Verify the collector.** Use the collector's official website, licensing lookup, or original creditor contact information instead of caller-provided pressure. → *Expect:* you know whether the contact appears legitimate.
3. **Record notice details.** Write the date received, amount, original creditor, collector name, account number, and response deadline. → *Expect:* the timeline is clear.
4. **Request validation in writing.** Send a debt validation or dispute letter through a trackable method, asking for creditor, amount, itemization, authority to collect, and judgment status. → *Expect:* you have proof of your dispute or validation request.
5. **Compare records.** Check bills, EOBs, receipts, cancellation confirmations, return tracking, and bank statements against the claimed amount. → *Expect:* valid, invalid, paid, duplicate, or identity-theft issues are identified.
6. **Check credit reports.** Look for the collection account and dispute inaccurate reporting with bureaus if needed. → *Expect:* credit-report impact is documented.
7. **Choose a response.** [BRANCH: not yours | already paid | amount wrong | valid debt] dispute, send proof, request correction, negotiate, or plan payment. → *Expect:* your action matches the evidence.
8. **Get any agreement in writing.** If settling or paying, require the collector's written terms, amount, due date, account release, and reporting treatment before sending money. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* payments or settlement admissions can affect legal and credit rights, so confirm the debt and terms first. → *Expect:* no money leaves until terms are documented.
9. **Save final proof.** Keep validation, dispute letters, receipts, settlement letter, deletion or correction notices, and credit-report updates. → *Expect:* you can defend against duplicate collection.

## Decision points

- The notice threatens a lawsuit or wage garnishment → check court records and consider legal aid quickly.
- The debt is medical → confirm insurance processing, financial assistance, and provider recall options.
- The debt is old → check statute-of-limitations issues before acknowledging or paying.
- The account is identity theft → file identity-theft reports and dispute with collector and credit bureaus.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Scam collector:** detect threats, refusal to provide address, or demand for gift cards, recover by ending contact and reporting to regulators.
- **F2 Deadline missed:** detect validation window passed, recover by still disputing inaccuracies and requesting documentation.
- **F3 Duplicate collection:** detect same debt collected twice, recover by sending prior settlement or payment proof.
- **F4 Credit report inaccurate:** detect wrong amount, date, or ownership, recover by disputing with bureaus and furnisher.
- **F5 Verbal settlement:** detect no written terms, recover by refusing payment until written confirmation arrives.

## Verification

You have written validation, a written dispute record, correction confirmation, or a signed payment or settlement agreement that identifies the exact debt and outcome.

## Variations

- `us`: federal and state debt-collection rights, validation windows, and statutes of limitation vary.
- Medical collections: reporting rules and provider financial assistance may change the strategy.
- Court judgment: contesting a judgment requires court-specific procedures, not only collector letters.

## Safety & privacy

Collections can affect credit, lawsuits, and bank accounts. Do not share full bank credentials, do not pay by gift card or wire, and get legal help for court papers.
