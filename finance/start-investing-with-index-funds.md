## Goal

Start investing through low-cost index funds using an appropriate account, target allocation, funding plan, and saved confirmations.

## Preconditions

- High-interest bills and near-term cash needs have been considered before investing.
- You know the investing goal, time horizon, and account type you intend to use.
- You can open or access a brokerage, retirement, or employer investment account.
- You understand that index funds can lose value and are not guaranteed.

## Steps

1. **Define the goal and timeline.** Choose retirement, long-term wealth, education, or another goal and estimate when the money may be needed. → *Expect:* time horizon and purpose are written down.
2. **Choose account type.** Select employer plan, IRA, taxable brokerage, education account, or another account based on goal and tax rules. → *Expect:* the account type matches the goal.
3. **Set an allocation.** Choose a simple mix such as target-date fund, total stock index, total bond index, or global stock and bond indexes. → *Expect:* target percentages add to 100%.
4. **Compare fund costs.** Check expense ratio, minimum investment, tracking index, diversification, and transaction fees. → *Expect:* chosen funds are broad and low cost relative to alternatives.
5. **Open or access the account.** Complete identity, tax, funding, and beneficiary steps if the account is new. → *Expect:* the account is open and ready for funding.
6. **Fund the account.** Link a bank or payroll source and transfer an amount that fits the budget and contribution limits. → *Expect:* cash is scheduled, pending, or posted.
7. **Buy the fund.** Enter the ticker, dollar amount or shares, account, and order type, then submit after checking details. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* investment orders may execute and change value quickly, so confirm ticker, account, and amount first. → *Expect:* order is accepted, filled, or pending.
8. **Set automation and review.** Schedule recurring investments and a periodic allocation review. → *Expect:* future contributions and review dates are set.

## Decision points

- Money needed within a few years → consider keeping it in cash or low-risk savings instead of stock index funds.
- Employer match exists → compare investing there before a taxable account.
- Unsure about allocation → a target-date index fund can provide a simple diversified default.
- Taxable account used → consider tax efficiency, dividends, and capital gains.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bought wrong fund:** detect ticker, share class, or account mismatch → recover by canceling if open or correcting after reviewing tax impact.
- **F2 Cash not invested:** detect settlement balance after transfer → recover by placing the fund order or enabling automatic investment.
- **F3 Contribution exceeds limit:** detect warning or tax-form issue → recover by contacting provider about correction.
- **F4 Panic sale:** detect urge to sell after market drop → recover by rereading the written goal and rebalance rule before trading.

## Verification

The account is open, funded, invested in the selected index fund or funds, target allocation is recorded, and recurring contribution or review reminder is active.

## Variations

- `us`: IRA, 401(k), taxable, and education accounts have different contribution, tax, and withdrawal rules.
- `employer-plan`: fund choices may be limited to the plan menu.
- `taxable-brokerage`: realized gains and dividends may create tax forms.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from market loss, tax mistakes, and exposure of identity and bank details. Use official provider sites, avoid concentrated bets when seeking broad index exposure, and keep account credentials secure.
