## Goal

You negotiate a written settlement for a debt you can afford, pay only under confirmed terms, and receive proof that the account is resolved.

## Preconditions

- The creditor or collector name, account number, claimed balance, and current contact information.
- A realistic lump-sum amount or short payment plan you can complete without missing essentials.
- Copies of prior bills, collection letters, dispute letters, and payment history.

## Steps

1. **Verify who owns the debt.** Ask whether the caller is the original creditor, a collector, or a debt buyer, and request written validation if you do not already have it. → *Expect:* a creditor name, account number, balance, and mailing or online portal path that match your records.
2. **Check the age and legal status before negotiating.** Confirm the date of last payment and whether the debt is outside the limitations period in your jurisdiction; avoid making any payment or promise until you understand the effect. → *Expect:* you know whether payment could revive an old debt or affect credit reporting.
3. **Set your settlement ceiling privately.** Choose a maximum total payment and payment date you can actually meet. → *Expect:* a written walk-away number that is below available cash after essentials.
4. **Call the settlement department with a specific offer.** State that you are trying to resolve the account and can pay a defined amount if they agree in writing to accept it as settlement in full. → *Expect:* the agent either rejects, counters, or gives a conditional settlement amount.
5. **Negotiate credit-reporting language.** Ask how the account will be reported, such as "settled," "paid in full for less than full balance," or deletion where legally and contractually allowed. → *Expect:* the representative states the exact post-payment reporting treatment.
6. **Get the agreement in writing before paying.** Require a letter or portal document showing creditor, account, settlement amount, due date, payment method, and that the remaining balance will not be pursued. → *Expect:* a written settlement offer that matches the verbal terms.
7. **Pay through a traceable method only after review.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before sending money, confirm the written settlement amount, due date, account number, and payee; do not pay by gift card, wire to a person, or cryptocurrency. → *Expect:* a confirmation number or receipt from the creditor's official payment channel.
8. **Save proof and request closure confirmation.** Store the settlement letter, receipt, and final zero-balance or settled letter. → *Expect:* your records show the account resolved under the agreed terms.
9. **Check credit reports after the reporting cycle.** Review reports 30-60 days later and dispute inaccurate balances or statuses with your evidence. → *Expect:* the account reports according to the agreement or you have filed a documented dispute.

## Decision points

- The collector cannot validate the debt → pause negotiation and request validation in writing.
- The debt may be time-barred → get local legal advice before acknowledging or paying.
- The creditor offers a payment plan you cannot finish → counter with a smaller lump sum or walk away.
- The written letter differs from the call → do not pay until corrected.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Invalid collector:** detect mismatched account details or pressure for unusual payment → call the creditor using a known number and verify placement.
- **F2 Revived old debt:** detect that a payment or promise could restart limitations → stop, document the call, and consult a qualified local debt adviser.
- **F3 Verbal-only deal:** detect refusal to send written terms → decline payment and ask for a supervisor or official settlement portal.
- **F4 Wrong credit reporting:** detect a balance still due after settlement → dispute with the bureaus and send the settlement letter and receipt to the creditor.
- **F5 Unaffordable plan default:** detect a missed installment risk → call before the due date to renegotiate rather than silently defaulting.

## Verification

The creditor or collector has issued a written settlement agreement, you paid no more than your preset maximum through an official traceable channel, and you have a receipt plus written confirmation that the account is settled or closed.

## Variations

- `us`: debt-validation, credit-reporting, and limitation rules vary by state; keep all notices and dates.
- Original creditor: settlements may be less discounted but easier to verify.
- Debt buyer: require stronger validation because ownership and balance records are more error-prone.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because payment and credit status are at stake. Confirm identity before sharing personal data, never rely on verbal promises, and do not authorize bank drafts unless you understand the amount and date.
