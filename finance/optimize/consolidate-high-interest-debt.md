## Goal

You combine high-interest debts into a lower-cost repayment structure while avoiding new debt accumulation and hidden fees.

## Preconditions

- Current balances, APRs, minimum payments, due dates, and payoff restrictions for each debt.
- Candidate consolidation loan, balance transfer, hardship plan, or debt-management plan terms.
- Monthly budget showing an affordable payment.
- Commitment to stop adding balances to paid-off accounts.

## Steps

1. **List every debt.** Record creditor, balance, APR, minimum payment, due date, fees, and promotional expiration. → *Expect:* a complete debt inventory.
2. **Calculate current payoff cost.** Estimate total interest and payoff time using current minimums and any extra payment amount. → *Expect:* baseline cost and timeline.
3. **Compare consolidation options.** Evaluate personal loan APR, origination fee, balance transfer fee, promotional APR, nonprofit debt-management fees, and hardship plans. → *Expect:* options ranked by total cost and payment fit.
4. **Check eligibility without unnecessary hard pulls.** Use prequalification where possible and verify final application impact. → *Expect:* likely approval terms are known before committing.
5. **Choose a payoff plan.** Select the option that lowers total cost or stabilizes payment without extending debt beyond your target. → *Expect:* chosen plan has a payment you can sustain.
6. **Execute the consolidation.** Apply, accept terms, and direct funds to creditors or deposit for immediate payoff. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm APR, fees, term, payment, and creditor payoff amounts before accepting. → *Expect:* consolidation account or transfer confirmations exist.
7. **Verify old balances are paid.** Check each creditor after settlement and pay any residual interest. → *Expect:* old accounts show zero or the expected remaining balance.
8. **Prevent re-accumulation.** Remove cards from wallets, lower limits if appropriate, set autopay, and budget the consolidation payment. → *Expect:* old credit lines cannot easily rebuild balances.

## Decision points

- Consolidation payment is lower only because term is much longer → compare total interest before accepting.
- Credit score is too low for lower APR → consider hardship plans or nonprofit counseling instead of high-fee loans.
- Spending behavior is not controlled → consolidation can worsen debt; address budget first.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Origination fee erases savings:** detect loan proceeds are reduced by fees → recalculate total cost before accepting.
- **F2 Residual interest remains:** detect old account still shows a small balance → pay it before it becomes late.
- **F3 Paid-off card reused:** detect new charges on old card → freeze or remove the card and revise budget.
- **F4 Promotional rate expires:** detect balance transfer not paid before promo end → accelerate payments or refinance before standard APR applies.

## Verification

The selected consolidation option is active, targeted high-interest debts show paid or transferred, the new payment schedule is set, and projected total cost is below the baseline or meets the stated cash-flow constraint.

## Variations

- Balance transfer: works best for borrowers who can repay before the promotional APR ends.
- Debt-management plan: may close cards but can reduce rates through a nonprofit counselor.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because credit applications and debt contracts are involved. Avoid upfront-fee scams, verify nonprofit credentials where relevant, and confirm old balances after payoff rather than assuming transfers completed.
