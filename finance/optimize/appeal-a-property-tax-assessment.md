## Goal

You file a timely property-tax assessment appeal supported by comparable evidence and obtain a corrected assessed value if the assessment is too high.

## Preconditions

- The assessment notice, parcel number, filing deadline, and appeal instructions.
- Recent sales data, property record card, photos, repair estimates, or appraisal evidence.
- Understanding that tax rate and assessed value are different issues.

## Steps

1. **Read the assessment notice completely.** Identify assessed value, taxable value, exemptions, appeal deadline, filing method, and required forms. → *Expect:* a deadline and submission checklist.
2. **Verify the property record.** Check square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size, year built, condition, and exemptions. → *Expect:* any factual errors are listed with proof.
3. **Build comparable-sales evidence.** Find similar properties sold near the valuation date, adjusting for size, condition, location, and features. → *Expect:* three to six relevant comparables with sale dates and prices.
4. **Estimate a defensible value.** Use the best comparables and corrections rather than simply asking for a lower tax bill. → *Expect:* a requested assessed value supported by evidence.
5. **Prepare the appeal package.** Complete the official form and attach assessment notice, comparables, photos, appraisals, repair estimates, and exemption proof. → *Expect:* a complete package that addresses the assessor's valuation basis.
6. **File before the deadline.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm parcel number, tax year, requested value, and all attachments before submitting because late or incomplete appeals may be rejected. → *Expect:* a submission receipt, case number, or stamped copy.
7. **Respond to assessor contact or settlement offers.** Review any proposed reduction against your evidence and tax impact. → *Expect:* either a signed agreement or a hearing date.
8. **Attend the hearing if required.** Present facts concisely: record errors, best comparable sales, and requested value. → *Expect:* the board accepts evidence and tells you when a decision will issue.
9. **Confirm the corrected tax record.** After a decision or settlement, check the parcel account and bill. → *Expect:* the assessed value and estimated tax reflect the decision.

## Decision points

- The only issue is a missing exemption → file the exemption correction path instead of a valuation appeal if available.
- Comparable sales support the assessment → do not appeal solely because taxes feel high.
- A settlement offer is close to your evidence value → accept if hearing risk and time outweigh the difference.
- Deadline has passed → ask whether late correction, clerical-error, or next-year appeal options exist.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed deadline:** detect online portal closed or mailed appeal late → ask the assessor about late-abatement or next-cycle remedies.
- **F2 Weak comparables:** detect rejected evidence because homes are dissimilar → replace with closer sales and explain adjustments.
- **F3 Tax-rate confusion:** detect appeal argues tax burden rather than assessed value → reframe around market value or record errors.
- **F4 Exemption error:** detect owner-occupant or senior exemption missing → file the specific exemption correction with required proof.

## Verification

The appeal is accepted with a receipt or case number, and success is a written decision or updated parcel record reducing the assessed value or correcting the exemption for the target tax year.

## Variations

- `us`: appeal windows, valuation dates, homestead exemptions, and hearing procedures are county or municipality specific.
- Recently purchased property: sale price may be strong evidence if it was an arm's-length transaction near the valuation date.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because property records and tax liability are involved. Submit only accurate evidence, redact unnecessary personal information, and calendar every deadline.
