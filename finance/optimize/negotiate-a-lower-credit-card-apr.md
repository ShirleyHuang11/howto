## Goal

You ask your credit card issuer to reduce the purchase APR on an existing card and leave with either a confirmed lower rate or a documented refusal and next option.

## Preconditions

- Access to the card account, current APR, balance, payment history, and credit score range.
- Competing offers or lower-rate cards you qualify for.
- Ability to call the issuer and verify your identity.

## Steps

1. **Review your current terms.** Find purchase APR, cash-advance APR, penalty APR, balance, fees, and whether any promo rate is active. → *Expect:* the exact APR you want lowered is known.
2. **Check your leverage.** Note on-time payment history, account age, credit score improvement, income stability, and competing offers. → *Expect:* a short case for why the issuer should reduce the rate.
3. **Set a target and fallback.** Choose a target APR, maximum acceptable APR, or fallback such as balance transfer, product change, or payoff plan. → *Expect:* a private walk-away standard.
4. **Call the number on the card or statement.** Navigate to account services or retention and verify identity. → *Expect:* you are speaking with an authorized issuer representative.
5. **Make a specific request.** Ask whether they can lower the purchase APR permanently or for a defined period based on your history and competing offers. → *Expect:* the representative checks available rate offers.
6. **Ask for escalation if needed.** If the first agent says no, politely ask whether an account specialist or supervisor has additional options. → *Expect:* either a second review occurs or the refusal is confirmed.
7. **Confirm the offer details.** [BRANCH: lower APR offered, confirm new APR, start date, duration, affected balances, and any fees | no APR offer, ask for hardship, product-change, or balance-transfer options] → *Expect:* you have exact terms or exact next alternatives.
8. **Accept only understood terms.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* account changes can affect billing and benefits, so confirm APR, duration, fees, and credit-report impact before accepting. → *Expect:* the issuer confirms the change or documents that no change was made.
9. **Save proof and monitor the statement.** Record date, representative, confirmation number, and check the next statement. → *Expect:* the APR shown online or on the statement matches the agreed terms.

## Decision points

- You carry a balance → prioritize APR reduction, hardship plan, or balance transfer over rewards.
- You pay in full every month → APR matters less; ask for fee waiver or retention offer instead.
- Issuer says APR cannot change → ask when you can request again and what account conditions matter.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Promotional APR misunderstood:** detect only new purchases or only a short window are covered → ask for written terms and avoid new debt if it does not solve the problem.
- **F2 Product change removes benefits:** detect downgrade tied to rate reduction → compare benefits and fees before accepting.
- **F3 Hard credit pull risk:** detect issuer asks permission for credit review → decide explicitly whether the pull is worth it.
- **F4 No confirmation appears:** detect next statement still has old APR → call back with date, time, and confirmation notes.

## Verification

The issuer confirms a new lower APR with start date and duration, or the account notes show no offer was available and the next best option is documented.

## Variations

- `us`: credit card APRs and hardship programs vary by issuer; use the number on the card and avoid third-party debt-settlement pitches.
- Credit union cards: local underwriting may allow more manual review.
- Business cards: issuer may consider business revenue, personal guarantee, and account history.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because account identity and debt terms are involved. Call only official numbers, never share full card details with outbound callers, and confirm fees or credit-report impact before accepting changes.
