## Goal

Report a covered home loss, document damage, prevent further damage, and track the claim through payment or written denial.

## Preconditions

- People and pets are safe, and emergency services have been contacted if needed.
- You have the current policy, insurer claim contact, photos, receipts, and date of loss.
- You can make temporary repairs or hire emergency mitigation when safe.
- You understand that filing a claim can affect premiums or renewal.

## Steps

1. **Make the property safe.** Stop active damage if safe, shut off water or power if appropriate, and avoid unsafe areas. → *Expect:* immediate hazards are controlled or emergency help is on site.
2. **Document before cleanup.** Take photos and video of damage, affected rooms, damaged belongings, source of loss, and temporary repairs. → *Expect:* evidence exists before items are moved or discarded.
3. **Review coverage and deductible.** Check policy limits, exclusions, claim duties, and deductible for the loss type. → *Expect:* you know whether filing is likely worth the deductible and record impact.
4. **Prevent further damage.** Arrange tarping, drying, boarding, or emergency repairs and keep receipts. → *Expect:* mitigation actions and costs are documented.
5. **Open the claim.** Use the insurer's official claim phone, app, or portal and provide date, cause, address, damage summary, and contact details. → *Expect:* claim number and adjuster or next-step timeline are recorded.
6. **Submit evidence.** Upload photos, inventory, receipts, repair estimates, police or fire reports, and mitigation invoices. → *Expect:* claim file shows documents received.
7. **Meet the adjuster.** Walk through damage, ask what is covered, and request the estimate in writing. → *Expect:* adjuster inspection or virtual review is completed.
8. **Review settlement.** Compare insurer estimate with contractor estimates, deductible, depreciation, and recoverable amounts. → *Expect:* payment, supplemental request, or denial reason is understood.
9. **Accept or dispute outcome.** Deposit payment only when you understand the settlement, or request reconsideration with evidence. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* signing a release or final settlement can limit later recovery, so read it before agreeing. → *Expect:* final payment, supplement, or appeal path is documented.

## Decision points

- Damage is below or near deductible → consider paying out of pocket after documenting the event.
- Cause may be excluded → ask for a written coverage explanation before expensive repairs.
- Contractor asks for assignment of benefits or full upfront payment → read carefully and verify licensing.
- Claim is denied → request the policy language and appeal process in writing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Evidence lost:** detect cleanup occurred before photos → recover with contractor reports, dated receipts, witness statements, and remaining damage photos.
- **F2 Late notice:** detect insurer says claim was delayed → recover by explaining discovery date and providing mitigation proof.
- **F3 Underpaid estimate:** detect contractor quote exceeds insurer estimate → recover by requesting a supplement with itemized differences.
- **F4 Fraudulent contractor:** detect pressure, no license, or large cash demand → recover by stopping work and checking insurer or regulator resources.
- **F5 Uncovered damage:** detect written denial cites exclusion → recover by reviewing policy, asking for reconsideration, or using regulator complaint options.

## Verification

The claim has a claim number, submitted evidence, mitigation receipts, adjuster estimate, written coverage decision, and either payment record or documented appeal next step.

## Variations

- `us`: state claim-handling timelines, catastrophe rules, public adjuster rules, and regulator complaint paths vary.
- `renter-or-condo`: landlord, condo association, and personal property insurers may each handle different parts.
- `catastrophe`: insurer response and contractor availability may be slower after regional disasters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from unsafe structures, claim fraud, contractor scams, and disclosure of address and property details. Prioritize life safety and use official insurer contact channels.
