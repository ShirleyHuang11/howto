## Goal

Create a clear, documented system for sharing expenses with a partner while preserving bill reliability, consent, and personal privacy.

## Preconditions

- Both partners agree to discuss income, bills, debts, shared goals, and boundaries.
- You have recent bills, rent or mortgage amount, subscriptions, groceries, utilities, insurance, and debt obligations.
- You can use a spreadsheet, budgeting app, shared account, or reimbursement app.
- You understand legal ownership and debt responsibility may differ by marriage status and jurisdiction.

## Steps

1. **List shared expenses.** Identify rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, household supplies, pets, subscriptions, travel, and shared debt if any. → *Expect:* shared and individual expenses are separated.
2. **Choose a split rule.** Pick equal split, income-proportional split, category ownership, allowance method, or another explicit rule. → *Expect:* both partners can state the rule.
3. **Set payment workflow.** Decide who pays each bill, when reimbursements happen, and which account or app tracks them. → *Expect:* every shared bill has an owner and due date.
4. **Create a shared record.** Build a spreadsheet, budget app category, or shared note with amounts, due dates, and settlement status. → *Expect:* both partners can view the current state.
5. **Decide account structure.** Choose separate accounts, one joint bill account, or partial pooling based on trust, legal status, and privacy needs. → *Expect:* account roles and access are documented.
6. **Set boundaries.** Agree on spending thresholds, personal money, debt privacy, emergency support, and what requires prior discussion. → *Expect:* consent rules are written down.
7. **Automate predictable transfers.** Schedule recurring transfers or reminders for fixed shared bills. → *Expect:* routine bills are funded before due dates.
8. **Review monthly.** Compare actual expenses, settle differences, and update the rule when income or bills change. → *Expect:* balances are current and disagreements are recorded for discussion.

## Decision points

- Income differs substantially → proportional split may feel more workable than equal split.
- One partner has debt or unstable income → keep debt responsibility explicit and avoid surprise obligations.
- Opening a joint account → understand that both users may have access and responsibility for activity.
- Relationship or safety concerns exist → keep independent funds and seek trusted support before combining money.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bill owner unclear:** detect late fee or duplicate payment → recover by assigning one owner and backup reminder for each bill.
- **F2 Unequal cash strain:** detect one partner repeatedly short after shared payments → recover by revisiting the split rule and timing.
- **F3 Hidden expense conflict:** detect purchases disputed after the fact → recover by setting a dollar threshold for prior agreement.
- **F4 Joint account misuse:** detect unauthorized withdrawals or overdraft → recover by pausing joint funding and separating accounts.

## Verification

Both partners can view a current shared-expense list showing split rule, bill owners, due dates, paid status, reimbursement status, and next review date.

## Variations

- `us`: marriage, domestic partnership, community property, tenancy, and debt rules vary by state.
- `roommates`: use the same workflow but avoid shared debt and joint credit products.
- `one-income-household`: document personal spending access and emergency funds for both partners.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because shared finances can expose income, debt, location, and spending patterns. Preserve individual account access, avoid coercive control, and do not open joint credit without clear consent.
