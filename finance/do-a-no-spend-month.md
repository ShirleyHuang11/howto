## Goal

Run a one-month spending reset that pauses non-essential purchases, protects required expenses, and redirects saved money to a chosen goal.

## Preconditions

- Essential bills, food, medicine, transport, caregiving, and work requirements are known.
- You have a savings, debt payoff, emergency fund, or awareness goal for the month.
- You can track purchases with a bank app, spreadsheet, notes app, or paper log.
- Household members affected by the plan know the rules.

## Steps

1. **Define the purpose.** Choose one reason such as emergency fund, debt payoff, overspending reset, or saving for a known expense. → *Expect:* the month has a measurable goal.
2. **Write allowed spending.** List rent, utilities, groceries, medicine, fuel, transit, childcare, pet needs, minimum debt payments, and other true essentials. → *Expect:* necessary spending is protected.
3. **Write paused spending.** List restaurants, clothing, decor, apps, entertainment, impulse shopping, delivery, and upgrades that will wait. → *Expect:* non-essential categories are clear.
4. **Plan substitutes.** Use pantry meals, library resources, free activities, wish lists, repair, borrowing, or delayed purchases. → *Expect:* common temptations have alternatives.
5. **Remove frictionless spending.** Pause shopping apps, unsubscribe from sale emails, remove saved cards where practical, and set alerts. → *Expect:* impulse purchases require extra effort.
6. **Track every purchase.** Record amount, category, and whether it was allowed or paused. → *Expect:* spending is visible during the month.
7. **Move saved money.** Transfer the avoided amount or a weekly target to the chosen savings or debt goal. → *Expect:* progress is captured instead of absorbed by other spending.
8. **Close the month.** Review what worked, what was unrealistic, and which habits to keep. → *Expect:* final savings and lessons are documented.

## Decision points

- A genuine need appears → buy the needed item and mark why it was allowed.
- Household member disagrees → adjust shared rules rather than policing personal spending.
- No-spend rules cause missed medical, safety, or work needs → change the rules immediately.
- Savings goal is met early → continue tracking or end the challenge deliberately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rules too strict:** detect repeated exceptions or stress over essentials → recover by adding realistic allowed categories.
- **F2 Savings vanish:** detect less spending but no balance increase → recover by moving saved money weekly.
- **F3 Hidden subscription renews:** detect automatic charge in paused category → recover by canceling or moving renewal date.
- **F4 Rebound spending:** detect large purchases immediately after the month → recover by using a wish-list waiting period.

## Verification

At month end, the spending log shows allowed and paused categories, the target account or debt received the planned saved amount, and any rule changes are documented.

## Variations

- `family`: set shared rules for groceries, school, childcare, and events before the month starts.
- `low-income`: do not pause necessities; focus on tracking, fees, subscriptions, and planned purchases.
- `digital`: app and email controls may be the main friction point.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but do not skip medicine, safe transport, adequate food, housing, or required bills. Spending logs can reveal sensitive habits, so store them privately.
