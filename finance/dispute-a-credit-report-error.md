## Goal

File a documented dispute for inaccurate credit report information and track the bureau or furnisher response to correction, deletion, or verified status.

## Preconditions

- You have a current credit report from the relevant bureau or official report source.
- You can identify the exact inaccurate account, inquiry, personal information, balance, date, or status.
- You have supporting documents such as statements, letters, identity documents, payment proof, police report, or fraud report if applicable.
- You can receive mail, email, or portal messages about the dispute.

## Steps

1. **Get the current report.** Download or save the report showing bureau name, report date, and disputed item. → *Expect:* the error is visible in a saved report.
2. **Define the error.** Write the exact field that is wrong and the correction requested. → *Expect:* the dispute issue fits in one clear sentence.
3. **Gather evidence.** Attach statements, payment records, identity documents, court records, fraud reports, or creditor letters that prove the correction. → *Expect:* each claim has supporting evidence.
4. **Submit to the bureau.** Use the bureau's official dispute portal or mail address and include identifying information, report item, explanation, and evidence. → *Expect:* a dispute confirmation number or mailing proof is saved.
5. **Notify the furnisher if useful.** Send the same dispute to the creditor, collector, or lender reporting the error. → *Expect:* the furnisher has written notice and evidence.
6. **Track investigation timeline.** Calendar the bureau's expected response date and monitor mail, email, and portal messages. → *Expect:* follow-up requests are not missed.
7. **Review the result.** Read whether the item was corrected, deleted, updated, or verified as accurate. → *Expect:* the bureau's decision and updated report are saved.
8. **Escalate if unresolved.** If evidence was ignored, resubmit with clearer documents, add a consumer statement, or complain to the regulator for your jurisdiction. → *Expect:* a second dispute or complaint record exists.

## Decision points

- Error suggests identity theft → file fraud reports and consider credit freezes before ordinary dispute escalation.
- The item belongs to someone else → dispute personal information and mixed-file evidence.
- The item is accurate but negative → dispute will not remove it just because it is unfavorable.
- Applying for credit soon → ask the lender how open disputes affect underwriting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague dispute:** detect response says verified without addressing evidence → recover by resubmitting with exact field, correction, and proof.
- **F2 Wrong bureau:** detect error remains on another bureau's report → recover by disputing with each bureau that shows it.
- **F3 Furnisher repeats error:** detect corrected item reappears → recover by disputing with reinsertion notice and furnisher evidence.
- **F4 Scam repair service:** detect demands for upfront payment or guaranteed deletion → recover by stopping and using official bureau or regulator resources.

## Verification

The bureau response and updated report show the inaccurate item corrected or deleted, or you have a written verified decision with next escalation documented.

## Variations

- `us`: disputes can be filed with Equifax, Experian, TransUnion, and the furnisher; identity-theft disputes may use FTC identity-theft reports.
- `mail`: certified or tracked mail creates proof of delivery for evidence-heavy disputes.
- `fraud`: credit freezes and fraud alerts may be needed in parallel.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because reports contain Social Security or tax ID fragments, addresses, accounts, and identity documents. Use official bureau sites and redact unrelated data when allowed.
