## Goal

Create a simple will that names beneficiaries, an executor, guardians if relevant, and is signed according to local formalities.

## Preconditions

- Your situation is simple enough for a basic will, without complex trusts, contested family issues, large tax planning, or cross-border assets.
- You know your legal name, address, family members, major assets, debts, desired beneficiaries, backup beneficiaries, executor, and backup executor.
- You know whether you need guardians for minor children or pet-care instructions.
- You can follow your jurisdiction's witness and notarization requirements.

## Steps

1. **Confirm a simple will is appropriate.** Review whether blended families, disabled beneficiaries, business ownership, estate tax, foreign property, or likely disputes require a lawyer. → *Expect:* you know whether to proceed or get legal help.
2. **List assets and transfers outside the will.** Note real estate, bank accounts, retirement accounts, insurance, vehicles, digital assets, and accounts with beneficiaries or joint owners. → *Expect:* you know what the will controls and what passes separately.
3. **Choose beneficiaries and backups.** Decide who receives specific items, residue, and alternate gifts if someone dies first. → *Expect:* every major gift has a primary and backup plan.
4. **Choose executor and guardian roles.** Pick a responsible executor, backup executor, and guardians for minor children if needed; ask them before naming them. → *Expect:* named people are willing and reachable.
5. **Draft the will.** Use a jurisdiction-appropriate form, attorney, or reputable estate-planning service and include revocation of prior wills, gifts, executor powers, guardians, and residuary clause. → *Expect:* the draft reflects your current wishes in plain language.
6. **Review for conflicts.** Compare the will against beneficiary designations, joint ownership, divorce orders, prenups, and payable-on-death accounts. → *Expect:* obvious contradictions are fixed before signing.
7. **Sign with required formalities.** Arrange disinterested witnesses and notary/self-proving affidavit if applicable. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* an incorrectly signed will may fail after death when it cannot be corrected, so follow local signing instructions exactly. → *Expect:* the will is signed, witnessed, dated, and notarized/self-proved if available.
8. **Store the original safely.** Put the original where the executor can access it, and keep copies labeled as copies. → *Expect:* the executor knows where the original is and how to retrieve it.
9. **Calendar review triggers.** Review after marriage, divorce, birth/adoption, death, major move, major asset change, or law change. → *Expect:* the will has a future review reminder.

## Decision points

- Minor children are involved → guardianship language is important, but court approval may still be required.
- Beneficiary has disability or receives benefits → get advice before leaving assets outright.
- You want to disinherit a close family member → local law may limit that or require precise drafting.
- You move jurisdictions → review the will under the new location's law.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Invalid witnesses:** detect beneficiary witnesses, too few witnesses, or missing signatures → recover by re-executing the will correctly.
- **F2 Missing residuary clause:** detect assets not covered by specific gifts → recover by adding a residuary beneficiary clause before signing.
- **F3 Beneficiary conflict:** detect retirement or insurance beneficiaries that contradict the will → recover by updating the account beneficiary or revising the will intentionally.
- **F4 Original lost:** detect only copies exist → recover by executing a new will and revoking prior wills.
- **F5 Complex estate hidden:** detect business, tax, special-needs, or conflict issues → recover by using an estate attorney instead of a simple form.

## Verification

The original will is signed, dated, witnessed according to local law, stored safely, and the executor knows where it is.

## Variations

- `us`: will execution, witness rules, self-proving affidavits, elective shares, and community property vary by state.
- `holographic-will`: handwritten wills are accepted only in some places and have strict requirements.
- `living-trust`: useful for probate avoidance or management during incapacity, but it is not the same as a will.
- `digital-assets`: some jurisdictions allow specific digital fiduciary access language.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because errors may surface only after death. Do not rely on a simple will for complex family, tax, trust, or cross-border issues; store the original securely and avoid naming beneficiaries as witnesses where prohibited.
