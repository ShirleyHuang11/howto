## Goal

Understand a home insurance policy's coverages, deductibles, exclusions, limits, and claim steps before a loss happens.

## Preconditions

- You have the current declarations page, policy booklet, endorsements, and billing notice.
- You know the property address, mortgage requirements if any, and household risk concerns.
- You can contact the insurer or agent for unclear terms.
- You have a way to store policy documents securely.

## Steps

1. **Confirm policy identity.** Check insurer, named insureds, property address, policy number, term dates, and premium. → *Expect:* the policy matches the home and coverage period.
2. **Read dwelling coverage.** Find the limit for the structure and whether replacement cost, extended replacement cost, or actual cash value applies. → *Expect:* the main home coverage basis is known.
3. **Review other coverages.** Check personal property, loss of use, other structures, personal liability, and medical payments limits. → *Expect:* each major coverage limit is listed.
4. **Identify deductibles.** Note standard, wind, hail, hurricane, earthquake, flood, or percentage deductibles. → *Expect:* you know what you pay before insurance responds.
5. **Find exclusions.** Read exclusions for flood, earth movement, wear and tear, vacancy, business use, mold, sewer backup, or high-value items. → *Expect:* uncovered risks are marked.
6. **Check endorsements.** Review scheduled property, water backup, service line, equipment breakdown, identity theft, or ordinance coverage. → *Expect:* add-ons and missing add-ons are visible.
7. **Compare to needs.** Match limits against rebuild estimate, possessions, mortgage requirements, and special risks. → *Expect:* gaps or questions for the agent are documented.
8. **Save claim instructions.** Record emergency number, claim portal, mitigation duties, documentation rules, and contractor guidance. → *Expect:* claim steps are available without searching during a loss.

## Decision points

- Flood or earthquake is excluded → consider separate coverage if the risk matters.
- Personal property is actual cash value → decide whether replacement-cost coverage is needed.
- Valuable items exceed sublimits → schedule them or buy separate coverage.
- Deductible is unaffordable → request quotes for different deductible levels.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong policy version:** detect expired term or renewal mismatch → recover by downloading current declarations and endorsements.
- **F2 Deductible missed:** detect percentage or separate peril deductible after a loss → recover by updating your emergency fund and asking the insurer to explain examples.
- **F3 Exclusion overlooked:** detect policy excludes the risk you expected → recover by requesting endorsement, separate policy, or risk mitigation.
- **F4 Coverage limit stale:** detect remodel or price changes not reflected → recover by updating replacement-cost estimate with insurer or agent.

## Verification

You have a summary of current term dates, coverage limits, deductibles, exclusions, endorsements, claim contact method, and unresolved questions for the insurer or agent.

## Variations

- `us`: homeowners, condo, landlord, and mobile-home policies use different forms and state rules.
- `mortgage`: lender-required coverage and escrow billing may affect changes.
- `high-risk-area`: wind, wildfire, flood, or earthquake coverage may be separate or limited.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because coverage gaps can create large losses and policy documents expose address, lender, and property details. Store documents securely and verify insurer contacts.
