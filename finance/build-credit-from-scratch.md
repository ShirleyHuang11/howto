## Goal

Start a credit history with low-cost accounts, on-time payments, and low balances while avoiding unnecessary fees and debt.

## Preconditions

- You have legal identification and a mailing address.
- You have steady enough cash flow to pay any account in full on time.
- You can access official bank, credit union, or card issuer sites.

## Steps

1. **Check existing reports.** Use official credit-report access or a trusted monitoring tool to see whether a file already exists. → *Expect:* you know whether you are new, thin-file, or have errors to fix first.
2. **Choose a starter product.** [BRANCH: secured card | student card | credit-builder loan | authorized user] pick the lowest-fee option you can manage. → *Expect:* the product reports to major credit bureaus and has clear costs.
3. **Compare fees and terms.** Check annual fee, security deposit, APR, late fee, credit limit, graduation policy, and whether the deposit is refundable. → *Expect:* you avoid products with excessive fees or unclear reporting.
4. **Apply carefully.** Enter name, address, income, Social Security number or local tax ID if required, and bank funding details only on the official site. → *Expect:* you receive approval, denial, or a request for identity verification.
5. **Set autopay.** Turn on autopay for the statement balance or at least the minimum from a funded checking account. → *Expect:* late payments are less likely.
6. **Use lightly.** Put one small recurring purchase on the account and keep reported balance well below the limit. → *Expect:* utilization stays low and affordable.
7. **Pay in full monthly.** Review the statement, then pay the full statement balance by the due date. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* missed payments can stay on credit reports for years, so confirm autopay and manual payments before the due date. → *Expect:* the account reports on-time payment.
8. **Monitor progress.** Check score trends, account reporting, and credit reports after several statement cycles. → *Expect:* the new account appears with positive payment history.
9. **Expand slowly.** After six to twelve months of clean history, consider a no-fee unsecured card or credit-limit increase. → *Expect:* credit improves without taking on unnecessary debt.

## Decision points

- You cannot pay balances in full → delay credit use or choose a credit-builder product with fixed payments.
- A secured card has high fees or no graduation path → compare credit unions or major issuers.
- You can be an authorized user on a trusted person's old low-balance account → confirm it reports authorized users and does not expose either person to misuse.
- Your report has fraud or errors → fix those before applying widely.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Denied application:** detect denial notice, recover by reading adverse-action reasons and applying for a better-fit product.
- **F2 High utilization:** detect balance above roughly one-third of limit, recover by paying before statement close or reducing spending.
- **F3 Late payment:** detect missed due date, recover by paying immediately and requesting a goodwill waiver if appropriate.
- **F4 Fee-heavy product:** detect monthly maintenance or processing fees, recover by closing only after finding a better option and understanding credit impact.
- **F5 Credit report error:** detect wrong identity or account, recover by disputing with bureau and furnisher.

## Verification

At least one account appears on your credit report with correct identity information, low balance, and on-time payment history after a reporting cycle.

## Variations

- `us`: secured cards, credit-builder loans, rent reporting, and authorized-user reporting vary by issuer and bureau.
- New immigrant: some issuers use alternative underwriting, international records, or bank relationships.
- Student: student cards may have lower fees but still require income and responsible use.

## Safety & privacy

Credit applications require sensitive identity and income data. Avoid upfront-fee credit repair, do not carry balances to build credit, and use official issuer sites.
