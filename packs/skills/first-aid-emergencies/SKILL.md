# First-Aid & Emergencies — howto skill

When seconds count — the recipes you hope you never need.

When the user needs any task below, follow its verified steps in order. Each step's **Expect** is the observation that confirms it worked; steps marked ⚠ are irreversible — confirm before doing them. For the full recipe (decision points, failure recovery, variations) use the howto MCP `get_howto(<id>)` or read the linked source.

## call emergency services  
`healthcare/call-emergency-services`

**Goal:** Emergency services are contacted quickly, given accurate location and problem details, and kept connected until help is arranged.

1. **Get to relative safety.** Move away from traffic, fire, weapons, electrical hazards, or toxic fumes if you can do so without delaying urgent care.  → *Expect:* you can speak and observe without becoming injured.
2. **Call the local emergency number.** Dial the emergency number for the location, or use the phone's emergency-call function.  → *Expect:* the call rings or connects to a dispatcher.
3. **State the need first.** Say "medical emergency," "fire," "police," or "not sure" and describe the main problem in one sentence.  → *Expect:* the dispatcher starts routing the right responders.
4. **Give the exact location.** Provide address, business name, floor, room, entrance, cross streets, landmarks, or GPS/map information.  → *Expect:* responders have a place they can find.
5. **Give the callback number.** Say the number of the phone you are using, even if you think it appears automatically.  → *Expect:* dispatch can reconnect if the call drops.
6. **Answer focused questions.** Report age if known, breathing status, consciousness, severe bleeding, hazards, number of people hurt, and what first aid is happening.  → *Expect:* dispatch has enough detail to prioritize and guide care.
7. **Follow dispatcher instructions.** Put the phone on speaker if safe and do exactly what the dispatcher tells you.  → *Expect:* care continues while help is being sent.
8. **Assign bystanders.** Ask specific people to meet responders, control pets, bring an AED or naloxone if relevant, and keep the area clear.  → *Expect:* useful tasks happen without crowding the person.
9. **Stay on the line.** Do not hang up until the dispatcher says to, unless staying connected creates immediate danger.  → *Expect:* dispatch can update instructions as the situation changes.
10. **Flag responders when they arrive.** Send someone visible to the entrance or wave responders in from a safe place.  → *Expect:* emergency crews reach the person faster.

**Done when:** Emergency dispatch has the exact location, callback number, emergency type, current condition, known hazards, and the caller remains available until dispatch releases the call or responders arrive.

## do hands only cpr  
`healthcare/do-hands-only-cpr`

**Goal:** A bystander starts hands-only CPR for an unresponsive person who is not breathing normally while emergency services are being called.

1. **Check scene safety.** Look for traffic, electricity, fire, violence, or other immediate danger before approaching.  → *Expect:* you can reach the person without becoming another casualty.
2. **Check responsiveness.** Tap shoulders and shout to ask if they are okay.  → *Expect:* no purposeful response means emergency action is needed.
3. **Call emergency services immediately.** Tell a specific person to call, or call yourself on speaker.  → *Expect:* dispatch is contacted and can guide you.
4. **Send for an AED.** Point to someone and ask them to bring an AED if available.  → *Expect:* a search starts without delaying compressions.
5. **Check breathing quickly.** Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds; gasping is not normal breathing.  → *Expect:* absent or abnormal breathing triggers CPR.
6. **Position the person.** Lay them flat on their back on a firm surface if possible.  → *Expect:* chest is accessible and not on a soft mattress.
7. **Place hands correctly.** Put the heel of one hand in the center of the chest on the lower half of the breastbone, other hand on top.  → *Expect:* shoulders stack over locked elbows.
8. **Push hard and fast.** Compress at least 2 inches deep in adults at 100-120 compressions per minute, letting the chest recoil fully.  → *Expect:* chest visibly moves down and springs back each compression.
9. **Do not stop unnecessarily.** Continue until an AED is ready, trained help takes over, the person breathes normally, or you are physically unable.  → *Expect:* interruptions stay very short.
10. ⚠ **Use the AED when it arrives.** Turn it on and follow voice prompts. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* shock delivery can harm someone touching the person; loudly clear everyone before shock.  → *Expect:* AED prompts guide pads, analysis, and any shock.

**Done when:** Emergency services have been called, chest compressions are ongoing at 100-120 per minute with full recoil, and interruptions occur only for AED prompts, responder takeover, normal breathing, or rescuer inability.

## help someone who is choking  
`healthcare/help-someone-who-is-choking`

**Goal:** A responsive choking adult or child is helped while emergency services are called when the airway is blocked.

1. **Check for severe choking.** Ask if they are choking and look for silent coughing, inability to speak, blue or pale color, panic, or hands at the throat.  → *Expect:* you can distinguish mild choking from a blocked airway.
2. **Encourage coughing if air is moving.** [BRANCH: effective cough | weak or silent cough] tell them to keep coughing if they can breathe or speak; act immediately if they cannot.  → *Expect:* mild choking is not interrupted with forceful maneuvers.
3. **Call emergency services for severe choking.** Point to a specific person to call, or call on speaker if alone.  → *Expect:* dispatch is reached or being contacted while care begins.
4. **Position for back blows.** Stand to the side and slightly behind, support the chest with one arm, and lean the person forward.  → *Expect:* the airway points downward so the object can come out.
5. **Give five back blows.** Strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand, checking after each blow.  → *Expect:* the object may dislodge or the person may begin coughing strongly.
6. **Position for abdominal thrusts.** Stand behind, place a fist just above the navel and below the breastbone, and grasp it with the other hand.  → *Expect:* your hands are on the abdomen, not the ribs or breastbone.
7. **Give five abdominal thrusts.** Pull sharply inward and upward, checking after each thrust.  → *Expect:* the pressure may force air out and expel the object.
8. **Repeat cycles.** Alternate five back blows and five abdominal thrusts until the object clears, help takes over, or the person becomes unresponsive.  → *Expect:* care continues without long pauses.
9. **If they become unresponsive, lower them safely.** Place them on a firm, flat surface and start CPR if trained or instructed by dispatch.  → *Expect:* compressions begin and emergency services are already involved.
10. **After breathing returns, arrange medical evaluation.** Keep watching breathing and encourage evaluation, especially after thrusts, persistent coughing, or injury.  → *Expect:* delayed airway or internal injury is not ignored.

**Done when:** The person can breathe, speak, or cough effectively, emergency services have been called if severe choking occurred, and they are monitored until safe care takes over.

## help a choking infant  
`healthcare/help-a-choking-infant`

**Goal:** An infant under 1 year with a blocked airway receives age-appropriate choking first aid while emergency services are contacted.

1. **Check for severe choking.** Look for weak or silent cough, inability to cry, noisy or absent breathing, blue or pale color, or panic.  → *Expect:* severe airway obstruction is recognized.
2. **Call or assign a caller.** Point to a specific person to call emergency services, or call on speaker if alone.  → *Expect:* emergency help is being contacted.
3. **Position face down.** Support the infant's head and jaw, place them face down along your forearm, and keep the head lower than the chest.  → *Expect:* the airway points downward and the head is supported.
4. **Give five back blows.** Use the heel of your hand to strike firmly between the shoulder blades, checking after each blow.  → *Expect:* the object may come out or the infant may cry or cough strongly.
5. **Turn face up safely.** Sandwich the infant between your forearms, support the head, and turn them face up with the head still lower than the chest.  → *Expect:* the infant is controlled and supported.
6. **Give five chest thrusts.** Place two fingers on the center of the chest just below the nipple line and press about one-third chest depth.  → *Expect:* pressure may force air out and dislodge the object.
7. **Repeat the cycle.** Continue five back blows and five chest thrusts until the object clears, help takes over, or the infant becomes unresponsive.  → *Expect:* care continues without long pauses.
8. **Remove only a visible object.** If you can clearly see and grasp the object, remove it gently.  → *Expect:* the object is removed without pushing it deeper.
9. **Start CPR if unresponsive.** Put the infant on a firm surface and begin infant CPR if trained or instructed by dispatch.  → *Expect:* compressions and rescue guidance begin immediately.
10. **Seek medical evaluation after clearing.** Keep watching breathing and have the infant evaluated after severe choking or chest thrusts.  → *Expect:* delayed airway swelling or injury is not missed.

**Done when:** The infant can cry, breathe, or cough effectively, emergency services were called for severe choking, and the infant is monitored until medical help or evaluation occurs.

## stop severe bleeding  
`healthcare/stop-severe-bleeding`

**Goal:** Life-threatening external bleeding is controlled with direct pressure while emergency services are called.

1. **Check scene safety.** Look for traffic, weapons, electricity, glass, or other hazards before approaching.  → *Expect:* you can help without becoming injured.
2. **Call emergency services.** Assign a caller or call on speaker for severe bleeding.  → *Expect:* EMS is activated early.
3. **Expose the wound.** Move or cut clothing enough to see the bleeding source, leaving embedded objects in place.  → *Expect:* pressure can be applied to the correct spot.
4. **Apply direct pressure.** Press firmly on the wound with gloved hands, gauze, a clean cloth, or clothing.  → *Expect:* bleeding slows under steady pressure.
5. **Maintain pressure.** Keep constant firm pressure without peeking repeatedly.  → *Expect:* clotting is not disrupted.
6. **Add dressing if soaked.** Place more material on top if blood soaks through, but do not remove the original dressing.  → *Expect:* pressure continues over the wound.
7. **Elevate if practical.** Raise the injured limb above heart level only if it does not worsen pain or disturb pressure.  → *Expect:* bleeding may slow while direct pressure remains primary.
8. ⚠ **Use tourniquet for uncontrolled limb bleeding.** Place a commercial tourniquet high and tight above the wound if direct pressure fails or bleeding is immediately life-threatening. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* prolonged tourniquet use can damage tissue; note the time and do not remove it once tightened.  → *Expect:* limb bleeding stops or greatly decreases.
9. **Treat for shock.** Have the person lie still, keep them warm, and avoid food or drink.  → *Expect:* faintness and chilling are reduced while waiting.
10. **Monitor breathing and responsiveness.** Continue pressure until EMS takes over or you are unable to continue.  → *Expect:* changes are noticed and reported.

**Done when:** Emergency services have been called, firm direct pressure is maintained, soaked dressings are left in place, and any tourniquet time is recorded.

## recognize a heart attack  
`healthcare/recognize-a-heart-attack`

**Goal:** Possible heart attack symptoms are recognized early, emergency services are called, and the person rests while waiting for medical care.

1. **Look for chest discomfort.** Ask about pressure, squeezing, fullness, burning, or pain in the center or left chest.  → *Expect:* chest symptoms are described in the person's own words.
2. **Check related symptoms.** Ask about shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, lightheadedness, unusual fatigue, or a fast or irregular heartbeat.  → *Expect:* non-chest warning signs are not missed.
3. **Check spreading discomfort.** Ask about pain or pressure in one or both arms, back, neck, jaw, shoulder, or upper abdomen.  → *Expect:* referred symptoms are recognized.
4. **Consider atypical patterns.** [BRANCH: chest pain present | no chest pain but concerning symptoms] treat either pattern as possible heart attack, especially in women, older adults, and people with diabetes.  → *Expect:* absence of classic chest pain does not delay action.
5. **Call emergency services.** Call on speaker or assign one named bystander to call.  → *Expect:* dispatch is connected and can guide care.
6. **Keep the person resting.** Have them sit or lie in a comfortable position and loosen tight clothing.  → *Expect:* exertion stops and breathing is easier.
7. **Avoid self-transport.** Tell the person not to drive and arrange EMS transport unless dispatch says otherwise.  → *Expect:* rapid medical transport is handled by professionals.
8. **Ask about aspirin only if appropriate.** Follow dispatcher or clinician advice about chewing aspirin, and skip it for allergy, active bleeding, blood thinner concern, or prior instruction to avoid aspirin.  → *Expect:* aspirin is not given blindly.
9. **Watch for cardiac arrest.** Monitor responsiveness and normal breathing until help arrives.  → *Expect:* collapse or abnormal breathing is detected immediately.
10. **Start CPR if needed.** If unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin hands-only CPR and use an AED if available.  → *Expect:* circulation support starts while EMS is on the way.

**Done when:** Emergency services have been called, the person is resting, self-transport is avoided, and CPR or AED readiness is in place if they collapse.

## recognize stroke signs  
`healthcare/recognize-stroke-signs`

**Goal:** Possible stroke signs are recognized quickly, emergency services are called immediately, and the time symptoms began is recorded.

1. **Check scene safety.** Make sure you can approach and speak with the person without danger.  → *Expect:* you can focus on symptoms without creating another emergency.
2. **Test face.** Ask the person to smile and look for one side drooping or not moving normally.  → *Expect:* facial asymmetry is noticed quickly.
3. **Test arms.** Ask them to raise both arms and watch for one arm drifting down or failing to lift.  → *Expect:* one-sided weakness is identified if present.
4. **Test speech.** Ask them to repeat a simple sentence and listen for slurring, wrong words, or inability to speak.  → *Expect:* speech change is recognized without arguing about cause.
5. **Act on time.** [BRANCH: any FAST sign | no FAST sign but sudden neurologic symptom] call emergency services now if any sign is present or symptoms are sudden and concerning.  → *Expect:* EMS activation is not delayed.
6. **Note symptom timing.** Ask when the person was last known well and when symptoms were first noticed.  → *Expect:* a specific clock time or best estimate is available for responders.
7. **Keep them resting.** Have the person sit or lie safely with the head and shoulders slightly raised if comfortable.  → *Expect:* they are protected from falls while waiting.
8. **Give nothing by mouth.** Do not offer food, drink, or pills because swallowing may be unsafe.  → *Expect:* choking risk is reduced.
9. **Monitor breathing and alertness.** Recheck responsiveness and breathing until help arrives.  → *Expect:* any deterioration is noticed and reported.
10. **Prepare information.** Gather medication list, allergies, known medical problems, and witness contact information if available.  → *Expect:* responders receive useful context quickly.

**Done when:** Emergency services have been called, the last-known-well time is recorded or estimated, and the person receives no food or drink while being monitored.

## use an epipen  
`healthcare/use-an-epipen`

**Goal:** An epinephrine auto-injector is used promptly for suspected anaphylaxis while emergency services are called.

1. **Recognize anaphylaxis.** Look for hives, swelling, breathing trouble, wheeze, throat tightness, hoarse voice, vomiting, dizziness, or collapse after a possible allergen.  → *Expect:* a severe allergic reaction is treated as time-critical.
2. **Call emergency services.** Assign a caller or call on speaker before or immediately after giving epinephrine.  → *Expect:* EMS is on the way while care continues.
3. **Position the person.** Have them lie flat with legs raised if tolerated; sit upright if breathing is difficult; do not let them stand or walk.  → *Expect:* circulation and breathing are supported.
4. **Prepare the EpiPen.** Hold it in your fist with blue safety cap up and orange tip down: blue to sky, orange to thigh.  → *Expect:* fingers are away from both ends.
5. **Remove the blue cap.** Pull the safety cap straight off only when ready to inject.  → *Expect:* the device is armed and pointed at the outer thigh.
6. **Inject into the thigh.** Press the orange tip firmly into the outer mid-thigh through clothing if needed until it clicks.  → *Expect:* the dose starts delivering into the thigh muscle.
7. **Hold in place.** Keep firm pressure for the time listed on the device label, commonly 3 seconds for current EpiPen devices.  → *Expect:* the full dose has time to deliver.
8. **Remove and protect.** Lift the device away and keep it for EMS, needle end covered by the device guard.  → *Expect:* responders can identify what was given and when.
9. **Monitor for return of symptoms.** If symptoms persist or return and a second prescribed auto-injector is available, give it after about 5 to 15 minutes or as instructed by dispatch.  → *Expect:* worsening anaphylaxis is treated promptly.
10. **Prepare for CPR.** If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, begin CPR if trained and follow dispatch instructions.  → *Expect:* life support starts if anaphylaxis causes collapse.

**Done when:** Epinephrine has been given into the outer thigh, emergency services have been called, the injection time is noted, and the person is monitored for recurrent symptoms.

## put someone in the recovery position  
`healthcare/put-someone-in-the-recovery-position`

**Goal:** An unresponsive person who is breathing normally is placed on their side with an open airway while emergency services are called when appropriate.

1. **Check safety and response.** Make sure the scene is safe, then tap shoulders and speak loudly.  → *Expect:* you know the person is unresponsive or not fully responsive.
2. **Call for help.** Ask a bystander to call emergency services and get an AED, or call on speaker if alone.  → *Expect:* help is on the way or being contacted.
3. **Check breathing.** Look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.  → *Expect:* normal breathing is confirmed before rolling.
4. **Decide whether to move.** [BRANCH: no spinal concern | spinal concern] roll if airway protection is needed and no spinal concern; avoid movement if spinal injury is suspected and breathing is clear.  → *Expect:* movement risk is considered.
5. **Position the near arm.** Place the arm nearest you at a right angle to the body with palm up.  → *Expect:* the arm will help stop over-rolling.
6. **Place the far hand.** Bring the far hand across the chest and hold the back of that hand against the cheek nearest you.  → *Expect:* the hand supports the head during the roll.
7. **Bend the far knee.** Lift the far knee so the foot stays flat on the ground.  → *Expect:* the leg becomes a lever for rolling.
8. **Roll toward you.** Pull the bent knee gently so the person rolls onto their side, keeping the hand against the cheek.  → *Expect:* the person rests stably on their side.
9. **Open the airway.** Tilt the head slightly back and lift the chin, checking that the mouth points downward for drainage.  → *Expect:* the airway is open and fluids can drain.
10. **Monitor continuously.** Recheck breathing and responsiveness until help arrives, and roll onto the other side after about 30 minutes if waiting long and no injury prevents it.  → *Expect:* breathing changes are detected early.

**Done when:** The person is on their side, breathing normally, airway open with the mouth draining downward, emergency services are called if indicated, and breathing is monitored.

## treat a burn  
`healthcare/treat-a-burn`

**Goal:** A minor burn is cooled, protected, and escalated for medical care when the burn may be serious.

1. **Stop the burning.** Move the person away from the heat source, turn off power if electrical, or remove them from smoke if safe.  → *Expect:* no ongoing heat or exposure is injuring the skin.
2. **Call for serious burns.** [BRANCH: serious burn signs | small superficial burn] call emergency services now for serious signs; continue first aid while waiting.  → *Expect:* urgent care is activated when needed.
3. **Cool the burn.** Hold the burned area under cool running water for 20 minutes, starting as soon as possible.  → *Expect:* pain and heat begin to reduce.
4. **Protect from chilling.** Cool the burn area, not the whole body, and keep the rest of the person warm.  → *Expect:* the person is not shivering or becoming cold.
5. **Remove tight items.** Remove rings, watches, belts, or tight clothing near the burn unless stuck to skin.  → *Expect:* swelling will not trap jewelry or fabric.
6. **Leave stuck material alone.** Do not pull off clothing or objects attached to burned skin.  → *Expect:* deeper tissue is not torn.
7. **Cover loosely.** Use a sterile nonstick dressing, clean cloth, or clean plastic wrap laid loosely over the burn.  → *Expect:* the burn is protected without pressure or sticking.
8. **Avoid home substances.** Do not apply ice, butter, oils, toothpaste, or creams unless a clinician instructs you.  → *Expect:* heat is not trapped and skin is not damaged further.
9. **Reassess severity.** Look for blistering, white or charred skin, numbness, spreading redness, severe pain, or burns crossing joints.  → *Expect:* the need for medical care is reconsidered.
10. **Arrange follow-up.** Seek urgent care if pain is severe, the burn is worsening, tetanus status is uncertain, or infection signs appear.  → *Expect:* the burn is not left unmanaged.

**Done when:** The burn has been cooled for 20 minutes, covered loosely with a clean nonstick barrier, and serious burn criteria have been checked for escalation.
