# Move Into a New Place — howto skill

Everything for the first week in a new home — utilities to Wi-Fi to the breaker box.

When the user needs any task below, follow its verified steps in order. Each step's **Expect** is the observation that confirms it worked; steps marked ⚠ are irreversible — confirm before doing them. For the full recipe (decision points, failure recovery, variations) use the howto MCP `get_howto(<id>)` or read the linked source.

## set up utilities  
`housing/set-up-utilities`

**Goal:** A new home has working electricity, gas (if applicable), water, and internet — each in your name, on sane tariffs, with first meter readings recorded and billing running to you.

1. **Inventory the property's connections before moving day.** Electricity always; gas maybe; water (often municipal and landlord-mediated in rentals); internet options at *this address* (checker tools on providers' sites — availability is address-specific). Ask the outgoing party which suppliers currently serve it.  → *Expect:* a per-utility list: supplier-now, options, and who's responsible (you vs. landlord) per your lease.
2. **Sequence by lead time, internet first.** Internet installation appointments run days-to-weeks out — book it first against your move date. Electricity/gas/water are usually switchable in days.  → *Expect:* an internet install date near move-in; the rest scheduled around it.
3. **Open each account (or transfer service) effective move-in date.** Supplier's website/phone: address, your details, start date, tariff choice. [BRANCH: regulated single-supplier region → registration is the whole task | competitive market → compare tariffs on a comparison site first: unit rate, standing charge, contract length, exit fees]  → *Expect:* per utility: an account number, a start date, and a chosen tariff you can name the shape of.
4. **On move-in day: read every meter and photograph it.** Electricity, gas, water meters — numbers + photo with the move-in date. This is the boundary between the previous occupant's consumption and yours.  → *Expect:* dated photos of all meters; readings submitted to each supplier as opening readings.
5. **Verify the physical services actually work.** Lights and sockets; gas appliances light (smell gas → stop, ventilate, no switches/flames, call the gas emergency line — this is the recipe's hard-stop); water runs clear after a few minutes; heating/hot water fire up.  → *Expect:* every service functioning, or a fault reported to the right party (supplier vs. landlord per the lease).
6. ⚠ **Set billing to autopay + e-billing, and file the details.** ⚠️ *Irreversible-ish:* missed utility bills damage credit records and can end in disconnection — autopay converts the whole class of failure into a solved problem.  → *Expect:* each account showing an active payment method and your contact details; account numbers stored where you store such things.
7. **Confirm the internet install and test it.** Be present for the appointment; test speed against the contracted tier before the technician leaves.  → *Expect:* live connection at roughly the promised speed; router details recorded.
8. **Close the loop on the old place (if you moved from one).** Final readings + photos there, accounts closed or transferred, forwarding address given for final bills.  → *Expect:* no zombie account billing you for a stranger's showers.

**Done when:** Every utility serving the property has an account in your name with the right start date, opening meter readings are submitted and photo-archived, all services physically work, autopay + e-billing are live on each, internet performs to contract — and the old address (if any) is fully closed out.

## do a move in inspection  
`housing/do-a-move-in-inspection`

**Goal:** On move-in day you document the property's exact condition with dated photos, agree a signed inventory, record meter readings, and report every defect in writing so nothing is later deducted from your deposit.

1. **Do a first walkthrough before you unload anything.** Empty rooms reveal scuffs, stains, and damage that furniture will hide for months.  → *Expect:* a mental map of every room and problem area.
2. **Photograph everything, systematically.** One wide shot per room, then close-ups of every mark, chip, appliance, and existing damage. Enable the timestamp or shoot a phone with the date visible.  → *Expect:* a dated photo set covering all rooms, floors, walls, and appliances.
3. **Test appliances and fixtures and film them working (or not).** Oven, hob, extractor, boiler, taps, toilet flush, window locks, smoke/CO alarms.  → *Expect:* a short clip or note per item confirming function or fault.
4. **Read every meter and photograph the dial.** Electricity, gas, and water, with the serial number visible in the shot.  → *Expect:* meter type, serial, and reading captured for each utility.
5. **Complete the inventory line by line.** Go through the landlord's list; mark each item's true condition and add anything missing or damaged that they omitted.  → *Expect:* an inventory where your notes match your photos.
6. **Report defects in writing the same day.** Email the landlord/agent the full list plus photos, keep a copy, and ask them to acknowledge.  → *Expect:* a sent, timestamped message with the defect list attached.
7. **Sign the inventory only when it is accurate.** [BRANCH: inventory matches reality → sign and keep your copy | discrepancies remain → annotate "signed subject to the attached corrections" and send both]  → *Expect:* a signed document you and the landlord both hold. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* an unqualified signature on an inaccurate inventory becomes the agreed baseline against your deposit; correct it before signing.
8. **Store the whole record safely.** Back up photos, meter shots, the signed inventory, and the sent email to cloud storage.  → *Expect:* one dated, backed-up folder you can retrieve at move-out.

**Done when:** You hold a dated photo/video set of every room and defect, meter readings with serials, and a signed inventory that matches those photos, and you have sent (and kept) a same-day defect report to the landlord with proof of delivery.

## move house  
`housing/move-house`

**Goal:** You relocate your household to a new home on schedule, with belongings packed and labelled, transport arranged, utilities and address switched, and a first-night box that means you can eat and sleep the moment you arrive.

1. **Build a backward timeline from move day.** Six weeks out: book transport. Three weeks: notify utilities, mail redirect, and change of address. One week: pack non-essentials. Move day: essentials only.  → *Expect:* a dated checklist, not a vague intention.
2. **Decide movers versus friends honestly.** [BRANCH: professional movers (heavy/large home, tight schedule, insurance matters) | friends and a rented van (small home, budget, flexible timing)]  → *Expect:* a booked mover with written quote, or a confirmed van and a headcount of helpers.
3. **Declutter before you pack, not after.** Sell/donate/bin anything you would not pay to transport.  → *Expect:* fewer boxes than you first estimated.
4. **Pack room by room and label every box on the top and one side.** Write the destination room and contents, and mark fragile boxes clearly. Number boxes and keep a master list.  → *Expect:* no unlabelled box; the count matches your list.
5. **Pack a first-night box and keep it with you, not on the truck.** Phone chargers, medications, toiletries, a change of clothes, bedding, basic tools, snacks, kettle/mug, toilet paper, and important documents.  → *Expect:* one clearly marked box that never leaves your side.
6. **Protect fragiles and heavy items properly.** Plates on edge not flat, glasses wrapped individually, heavy items in small boxes so they stay liftable.  → *Expect:* boxes you can carry without the bottom failing.
7. ⚠ **Take final meter readings and photos at the old home; do the same at the new one.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* leaving without readings can leave you billed for the next occupant's usage.  → *Expect:* dated meter photos for both properties.
8. **Do the load-out and a final sweep.** Load heavy first and low, check every cupboard and the loft, then hand back keys.  → *Expect:* an empty old home and a full, secured vehicle.
9. **At the new home, direct boxes to their labelled rooms and set up beds first.**  → *Expect:* boxes in the right rooms and somewhere to sleep before nightfall.

**Done when:** Your belongings are in the new home in their labelled rooms, the vehicle and helpers are released, meter readings for both properties are recorded, utilities and address are switched, and the first-night box gives you food, bedding, and documents without unpacking anything else.

## forward your mail  
`housing/forward-your-mail`

**Goal:** Set up temporary mail forwarding and directly update important senders so letters stop going to the old address.

1. **List everyone covered.** Include each adult, common name variants, and dependents if the postal service permits.  → *Expect:* the forwarding order will catch the names that actually receive mail.
2. **Choose the postal redirect service.** Use the official postal service for the old address, not an ad or reseller.  → *Expect:* you are on the legitimate forwarding or change-of-address page.
3. **Set the start date.** Pick the move date or the first day mail should leave the old address. [BRANCH: already moved | moving in future] start immediately or schedule ahead.  → *Expect:* forwarding starts when the old mailbox stops being safe to use.
4. **Choose the duration.** Select the shortest duration that covers the transition, commonly months rather than weeks.  → *Expect:* you have time to update senders directly before forwarding expires.
5. **Submit and save confirmation.** Enter addresses exactly, pay if required, and save the confirmation number.  → *Expect:* the redirect is active or scheduled.
6. **Notify high-risk senders directly.** Update banks, tax agencies, employer, insurers, medical providers, schools, licenses, subscriptions, and legal contacts.  → *Expect:* critical mail is addressed to the new home without relying on forwarding.
7. **Notify ordinary senders next.** Update shopping accounts, memberships, charities, magazines, loyalty cards, and friends.  → *Expect:* low-risk mail gradually shifts to the new address.
8. **Monitor the first forwarded letters.** Check labels and dates to see which senders still need direct updates.  → *Expect:* each forwarded item becomes an action item.
9. **Close out the old mailbox.** Ask the landlord, buyer, or building manager not to discard stray mail during the first weeks.  → *Expect:* accidental deliveries have a recovery path.

**Done when:** The postal forwarding confirmation is saved, critical senders show the new address in their own systems, and no important mail is expected at the old address without a recovery plan.

## set up broadband in a new home  
`housing/set-up-broadband-in-a-new-home`

**Goal:** Order, install, and verify home broadband so the new home has usable internet with documented account and router details.

1. **Check availability by exact address.** Use provider and independent coverage checkers, including unit number.  → *Expect:* a short list of actual service types and speeds available at the home.
2. **Compare plan terms.** Check speed, upload rate, data caps, contract length, install fees, equipment fees, and cancellation rules.  → *Expect:* the chosen plan matches your usage and stay length.
3. **Order ahead.** Book as soon as you have a move-in date because install appointments can have lead time.  → *Expect:* an install or activation date near move-in.
4. **Prepare the install location.** Find the existing coax, fiber, phone, or network point and clear access around it.  → *Expect:* the technician or self-install kit can reach the entry point.
5. **Install or receive the router.** [BRANCH: technician install | self-install kit] let the technician finish line activation or connect modem and router per labels.  → *Expect:* router lights show service, internet, and Wi-Fi readiness.
6. **Place the router sensibly.** Put it high, central, ventilated, and away from thick walls, metal cabinets, and electrical clutter.  → *Expect:* main living and work areas get stronger signal.
7. **Secure Wi-Fi.** Change the admin password if allowed, use WPA2 or WPA3, set a strong network password, and disable unsafe defaults.  → *Expect:* unknown users cannot join or manage the router.
8. **Test speed and latency.** Run tests near the router and in normal work areas, preferably wired and wireless.  → *Expect:* speeds are close to the plan near the router and acceptable where you use devices.
9. **Save account and equipment details.** Record account number, support contact, install date, router login method, Wi-Fi name, and return obligations.  → *Expect:* support calls and future moves are easier.

**Done when:** The broadband account is active, the router is secured, and a speed test near the router plus one normal-use room meets the practical needs for the home.

## connect to wifi  
`digital/connect-to-wifi`

**Goal:** Your device is connected to the intended Wi-Fi network — home, work, or public — with working internet and sane security posture for the network's trust level.

1. **Open Wi-Fi settings and identify the *exact* network name.** Venues post theirs; homes have it on the router sticker. Twin-named networks ("CafeFreeWiFi" vs "Cafe_Free_WiFi") are how evil-twin attacks work — the posted name, character for character, is the one.  → *Expect:* the intended SSID visible in the list with plausible signal strength.
2. **Select it and authenticate.** [BRANCH: password network → enter the key exactly (case matters) | open venue network → connect, then expect a captive portal | enterprise (work/university) → username + password, accept the org's certificate only if it matches expectations]  → *Expect:* status moves to "connected"; wrong-password errors mean the key, not the world, is at fault.
3. **Clear the captive portal if one appears.** Open a browser if it doesn't auto-pop (try any http site); accept terms / enter the room number or voucher. Give marketing fields the minimum.  → *Expect:* the portal releases you; general sites load.
4. **Verify actual internet.** Load a known site or run the OS's connectivity check. Connected-but-no-internet → F2.  → *Expect:* pages load at usable speed.
5. **Set the trust posture to match the network.** Public/venue: mark it "public" when the OS asks (disables file sharing), avoid logging into sensitive accounts without HTTPS (the padlock), and prefer your phone's hotspot for banking-class tasks. Home: fine.  → *Expect:* sharing off on public networks; sensitive tasks deferred or tunneled.
6. **Manage the remembering.** Home/work: let it auto-join. One-time public networks: forget them after use — auto-rejoining "Airport_Free" forever is a tracking and evil-twin surface.  → *Expect:* the saved-networks list holds only networks you actually trust.

**Done when:** The device shows the intended SSID connected, real pages load, sharing is off if the network is public, and the saved list contains no one-time networks set to auto-join.

## update your address across accounts  
`digital/update-your-address-across-accounts`

**Goal:** Your mailing and service address is updated across important accounts in a planned order, with each change confirmed and tracked.

1. **Make the master list.** List banks, credit cards, payroll, tax or government agencies, insurance, healthcare, utilities, subscriptions, deliveries, schools, and employers.  → *Expect:* every account that sends mail or verifies address is visible in one list.
2. **Mark priorities.** Put bank, government, insurance, healthcare, payroll, and utilities at the top.  → *Expect:* identity, money, and essential service accounts are first.
3. **Start mail forwarding.** File forwarding with the postal service or local equivalent for the move date.  → *Expect:* confirmation or receipt shows forwarding is scheduled.
4. **Update financial accounts.** Sign in through official sites or apps and change mailing, residential, and billing addresses as needed.  → *Expect:* each account shows the new address or a pending confirmation.
5. **Update government and healthcare.** Use official portals or phone numbers for ID, tax, benefits, voter, insurer, doctor, and pharmacy records.  → *Expect:* each record has a confirmation page, letter, or case number.
6. **Update deliveries and subscriptions.** Change retailers, meal delivery, memberships, and recurring shipments before the next order date.  → *Expect:* saved shipping addresses and active orders point to the new address.
7. **Confirm each change.** Record the date, account, confirmation number, and whether proof is still needed.  → *Expect:* the master list shows done, pending, or blocked for every entry.
8. **Watch old-address mail.** For the first month, check forwarded mail for missed accounts.  → *Expect:* any missed sender is added back to the list.

**Done when:** The master list has every account marked done or intentionally pending, with confirmation details for each completed address change.

## register a change of address  
`government/register-a-change-of-address`

**Goal:** After a move, your new address is registered where the law requires it, your mail follows you, and the institutions that matter (bank, employer, insurance, subscriptions) all point at the new address, in the right order.

1. **Establish your country's rule and deadline first.** Search the official municipal or government site for "change of address" or the local term (Anmeldung/Ummeldung, 転居届, verhuizing doorgeven). Note the deadline; several countries fine late registration.  → *Expect:* you know whether you owe a formal registration, to whom, and by when.
2. **Do the formal registration where required.** Book the municipal appointment (slots run out; book the moment the move date is known), bring the document set, and leave with the confirmation certificate. That certificate unlocks banks, contracts, and utilities in registration countries, so store it carefully.  → *Expect:* registration completed within the deadline, certificate in hand or in the mail.
3. **Set up postal forwarding before the move if possible.** The postal service's mail-forwarding product (USPS change of address, Royal Mail redirection, Nachsendeauftrag) buys you months of catching whatever you forgot. Pay for the longest sensible window.  → *Expect:* mail addressed to the old place arrives at the new one, each piece a reminder of an institution still to update.
4. **Update the money layer next.** Bank and cards, payroll or pension, tax authority, insurance policies. Copy the address exactly from the registration or lease; inconsistent formats across institutions cause verification failures later (`finance/open-a-bank-account` step 2's lesson in reverse).  → *Expect:* the institutions that send legally important mail all show the new address.
5. **Update the identity layer.** Driving licence and vehicle registration where required (often with its own deadline), voter registration, national ID where it carries an address.  → *Expect:* every government-issued document that shows an address either updated or its update in progress.
6. **Sweep the long tail with the forwarded mail as your checklist.** Doctor and dentist (`healthcare/book-a-doctors-appointment` records), phone and internet (`housing/set-up-utilities` step 8 closes the old, this step opens the new), online shopping default addresses (`shopping/buy-a-product-online` will otherwise ship to your past), subscriptions, loyalty programs, school or university. Each forwarded envelope names a laggard; update it and the stream dries up.  → *Expect:* forwarded mail volume visibly declining month over month.
7. **Tell the humans.** A short new-address message to family and friends, and neighbors at both ends where relevant (`daily/social/greet-a-neighbor` begins at the new place).  → *Expect:* people who send you things know where you are.

**Done when:** Formal registration (where required) is done within deadline with the certificate stored, mail forwarding is active, the money and identity layers show the new address, forwarded mail has slowed to junk, and nothing that mails you codes, cards, or legal notices still believes in the old address.

## reset a tripped breaker  
`daily/reset-a-tripped-breaker`

**Goal:** Power is restored to the dead circuit by resetting the tripped breaker, and the overload or fault that tripped it has been identified so it does not immediately trip again.

1. **Unplug or switch off the suspects first.** On the dead circuit, unplug whatever was running when the power dropped, especially high-draw items (space heater, kettle, microwave, hair dryer, vacuum) and anything that smelled hot or sparked. Resetting into a live fault just trips it again, or worse.  → *Expect:* the likely culprits are physically unplugged, not just switched off.
2. **Go to the panel and open the door.**  → *Expect:* one or two columns of breaker handles, ideally with a circuit label sheet inside the door.
3. **Identify the tripped breaker.** Scan for the handle that is out of line with its neighbors: most breakers trip to a middle position, some show an orange or red flag window, a few trip fully to OFF. Run a finger lightly across the handles if the light is bad; the tripped one feels loose or springy.  → *Expect:* exactly one handle in the middle or flagged position that matches the dead area's label.
4. **Reset with off-then-on.** Push the handle firmly all the way to OFF first (you will feel a click as the mechanism resets), then firmly to ON. A tripped breaker pushed straight toward ON will not latch.  → *Expect:* the handle latches solidly at ON and stays there.
5. **Confirm power is back.** Check a light or outlet on the circuit.  → *Expect:* the dead area is live again.
6. **Reintroduce loads one at a time.** Plug the suspects back in individually, waiting a minute between each, starting with the least suspicious.  → *Expect:* each device runs without the breaker tripping; if one device trips it, you have found the fault.
7. **Fix the root cause.** [BRANCH: overload (too many high-draw devices on one circuit) → redistribute them to outlets on other circuits | one faulty device → retire or repair it, do not plug it in elsewhere | no cause found and no re-trip → note the event and move on]  → *Expect:* circuit holds under its normal load.

**Done when:** The breaker handle sits fully at ON, all normal loads on the circuit are running, and the breaker has held for at least 15 minutes under the circuit's usual load. If it would not hold, the circuit is OFF and an electrician is scheduled: that is also a valid safe end state.

## find a water leak  
`daily/find-a-water-leak`

**Goal:** Locate likely water leak sources and decide whether to shut off water or call a professional.

1. **Look for urgent hazards.** Check for water near outlets, breaker panels, ceilings, or sagging drywall.  → *Expect:* hazards are identified before touching plumbing.
2. **Stop using water.** Turn off faucets, appliances, irrigation, humidifiers, and ice makers.  → *Expect:* no intentional water use is happening.
3. **Check the water meter.** Watch the low-flow indicator or record the meter reading for 10 to 15 minutes.  → *Expect:* movement with all water off suggests an active supply-side leak.
4. **Inspect visible fixtures.** Wipe supply valves and trap joints under sinks, then look for new droplets.  → *Expect:* wet joints show the likely leak location.
5. **Check toilets with dye.** Put dye in the tank, wait 10 minutes without flushing, then inspect the bowl.  → *Expect:* colored bowl water indicates a leaking flapper or flush valve.
6. **Check appliances.** Inspect water heater, washing machine hoses, dishwasher base, refrigerator line, and HVAC condensate drain.  → *Expect:* pooling, corrosion, or damp flooring points to a source.
7. **Trace stains and sounds.** Follow ceiling stains, wall dampness, musty smells, or hissing toward the nearest plumbing line.  → *Expect:* the likely source area narrows.
8. **Shut off water if the leak is active.** Close the fixture valve or the water main if water is escaping continuously.  → *Expect:* flow slows or stops.
9. **Call a professional when the source is hidden or damage is spreading.** Contact a plumber, building maintenance, or water mitigation company.  → *Expect:* the issue is handed to someone equipped to open walls or dry structures.

**Done when:** The suspected leak source is identified or isolated, water flow has stopped or been shut off, and a professional has been called for hidden, electrical, structural, or continuing leaks.
