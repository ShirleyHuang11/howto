## Goal

You find a language exchange partner, set fair expectations, and practice safely in a way that benefits both people.

## Preconditions

- A target language and a language you can offer in exchange.
- A basic self-introduction and schedule availability.
- A platform, school group, community center, or local meetup option.

## Steps

1. **Choose a reputable place to search.** Use language exchange apps, university boards, libraries, cultural centers, or moderated communities. → *Expect:* you are looking where language exchange is the stated purpose.
2. **Write a clear profile or message.** Include target language, current level, native or strong language, time zone, goals, and preferred format. → *Expect:* potential partners understand the exchange.
3. **Screen for fit and boundaries.** Ask about goals, availability, correction style, and whether they want text, audio, video, or in-person practice. → *Expect:* both people want compatible practice.
4. **Start with a short first session.** Use 20-30 minutes and split time evenly between languages. → *Expect:* the first meeting tests fit without a large commitment.
5. **Use a simple session structure.** Warm up, choose a topic, correct a few recurring errors, and end with new vocabulary. → *Expect:* the conversation produces notes and next topics.
6. **Agree on correction rules.** Decide whether to correct every mistake, only repeated errors, or only after speaking turns. → *Expect:* corrections help without stopping fluency.
7. **Protect personal information.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* strangers may not be who they claim; keep early meetings on-platform or public, and do not share home address, financial details, or identity documents. → *Expect:* contact remains bounded and safe.
8. **Evaluate after two or three sessions.** Continue, adjust format, or politely end the exchange. → *Expect:* the partnership either has a rhythm or closes cleanly.

## Decision points

- Partner dominates one language → reset the time split or end the exchange.
- You need professional correction → hire a tutor rather than relying on a peer exchange.
- In-person meeting is proposed → choose a public place and tell someone where you are going.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unequal exchange:** detect one person receiving all the practice → use a timer or alternate sessions.
- **F2 Conversation dries up:** detect repeated small talk only → prepare topics, articles, pictures, or role-plays.
- **F3 Flirting or pressure:** detect messages shifting away from learning after boundaries → block or report on the platform.
- **F4 Corrections feel discouraging:** detect loss of fluency → ask for fewer corrections during speaking and a summary afterward.

## Verification

You have a partner, a schedule, agreed language split, correction rules, and at least one completed session with vocabulary or error notes.

## Variations

- Online exchange: use platform messaging and video until trust is established.
- Local meetup: check moderation, public location, transport, and whether beginners are welcome.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because you interact with strangers and may share voice, video, schedule, or location. Keep boundaries, use public spaces, and block or report unsafe behavior.
