## Goal

You diagnose why language progress has stalled and adjust practice so measurable improvement resumes.

## Preconditions

- At least several weeks of recent study or use in the target language.
- Notes, app history, tutor feedback, recordings, or test results showing what you have practiced.
- One clear skill target, such as listening, speaking, reading speed, writing accuracy, or exam performance.

## Steps

1. **Define the plateau concretely.** Write what is not improving and how you know. → *Expect:* the issue is measurable, such as "I miss fast speech" rather than "I am bad."
2. **Audit your last two weeks of practice.** List minutes spent on input, output, review, grammar, and feedback. → *Expect:* the pattern shows what you are overusing and neglecting.
3. **Test the weak skill directly.** Record yourself, do a timed reading, take a sample exam task, or transcribe audio. → *Expect:* you have evidence of the bottleneck.
4. **Choose one intervention.** [BRANCH: listening plateau, add transcript-assisted listening | speaking plateau, add recorded monologues and feedback | vocabulary plateau, add spaced repetition from real input | writing plateau, revise one short text with corrections] → *Expect:* the next plan targets the actual bottleneck.
5. **Set a short training block.** Run the intervention for 14-30 days before judging it. → *Expect:* the experiment has a start date, end date, and daily or weekly workload.
6. **Increase feedback quality.** Use a tutor, teacher, exchange partner, writing correction tool, or answer key. → *Expect:* errors are being noticed, not merely repeated.
7. **Retest under similar conditions.** Repeat the original sample task after the training block. → *Expect:* you can compare results instead of relying on mood.
8. **Keep what worked and rotate the next bottleneck.** Continue effective practice and select a new narrow target. → *Expect:* your plan evolves without abandoning the whole language.

## Decision points

- Your study is mostly easy review → add harder input or production with feedback.
- Your study is mostly hard native content → add graded material to rebuild volume and comprehension.
- You are improving but cannot feel it → use recordings, timed tasks, or old materials to make progress visible.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No baseline:** detect that you cannot compare before and after → create a small diagnostic task now and keep it.
- **F2 Too many changes at once:** detect that you cannot tell what helped → test one main intervention per block.
- **F3 Avoiding output:** detect good comprehension but weak speaking or writing → schedule feedback-based production.
- **F4 Burnout mistaken for plateau:** detect fatigue, dread, or skipped sessions → reduce load for one week and use enjoyable input before resuming targeted work.

## Verification

The repeated diagnostic task shows a concrete gain, such as better listening transcription, faster reading with comprehension, fewer corrected writing errors, or a more fluent recording.

## Variations

- `self-study`: use recordings, answer keys, and online correction communities to replace some teacher feedback.
- `classroom`: ask the instructor for one priority error pattern instead of general advice.
- `exam`: use official scoring rubrics and practice tasks so gains transfer to the test.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Be careful when sharing recordings or writing samples online; remove names, addresses, school information, and private stories before asking strangers for feedback.
