## Goal

You prepare for a language proficiency test with a schedule that matches the exam format, scoring rubric, and your current weaknesses.

## Preconditions

- The exact test name, level, date, fee, and registration deadline.
- Official sample questions, scoring rubrics, and timing rules.
- A realistic weekly study schedule.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the test requirements.** Read the official site for sections, timing, allowed identification, retake rules, and score reporting. → *Expect:* you know exactly what will be tested and what logistics can disqualify you.
2. **Take a diagnostic sample.** Complete one official or high-quality practice section under timed conditions. → *Expect:* you have baseline scores by skill area.
3. **Map scores to the rubric.** Identify whether points are lost for accuracy, task completion, fluency, comprehension, organization, or vocabulary range. → *Expect:* each weak area has a cause, not just a low number.
4. **Build a calendar backward from test day.** Assign weekly targets for listening, reading, writing, speaking, vocabulary, and full mock tests. → *Expect:* every week has specific tasks and one rest or catch-up slot.
5. **Practice the exact task types.** Use prompts, audio lengths, answer formats, and timing that match the exam. → *Expect:* the test interface and instructions feel familiar.
6. **Review errors in a log.** Record missed question type, reason, correct answer, and next drill. → *Expect:* repeated errors become visible.
7. **Get feedback on productive skills.** Have a teacher, tutor, trained peer, or rubric-based service review speaking and writing. → *Expect:* you receive specific corrections tied to scoring criteria.
8. **Run at least one full mock test.** Complete it with official breaks and no extra aids. → *Expect:* stamina and pacing problems appear before the real test.
9. **Prepare test-day logistics.** Confirm ID, admission ticket, route, equipment, time zone, and permitted items. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* registration fees and score sends may be nonrefundable, so verify the test date, level, and recipient institutions before paying or submitting. → *Expect:* nothing critical is left to decide on test morning.

## Decision points

- The test is required for immigration, licensure, or admission → use official materials and consider professional instruction because score consequences are significant.
- Speaking or writing is scored by rubric → practice with timed responses and external feedback, not only vocabulary study.
- Your test is computer-based → practice typing, headset use, and on-screen navigation before test day.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Studying general language instead of the exam:** detect improvement in apps but weak practice scores → shift time to official task formats.
- **F2 Running out of time:** detect unfinished sections in mocks → practice pacing checkpoints and skip-return strategies.
- **F3 Productive skills are ungraded in practice:** detect no feedback on essays or speaking → schedule rubric-based reviews.
- **F4 Logistics failure:** detect expired ID, wrong test center, or time-zone confusion → contact the test provider immediately and reschedule if needed.

## Verification

Your latest timed practice scores meet or exceed the target level, you can complete all sections within time, and your test-day documents and registration details are confirmed.

## Variations

- `ielts-toefl`: academic admissions often require score reports sent directly to institutions; confirm each institution's minimum and deadline.
- `dele-delf-goethe-hsk-jlpt`: level selection matters; choose the level required by the program or employer, not just the nearest challenge.
- `remote-proctored`: test your camera, microphone, room rules, and system check before the appointment.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because fees, deadlines, immigration, admission, or employment may be involved. Use only official registration portals for payment, verify recipient codes before sending scores, and protect passport or ID numbers.
