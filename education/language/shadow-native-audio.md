## Goal

You practice speaking by closely imitating native audio so your rhythm, pronunciation, and listening accuracy improve together.

## Preconditions

- A short native or near-native audio clip with transcript, ideally 15-60 seconds.
- Headphones and a quiet place where speaking aloud is acceptable.
- Basic familiarity with the vocabulary or a willingness to look up unknown words.

## Steps

1. **Pick audio slightly below your maximum level.** Choose speech you can mostly understand after reading the transcript. → *Expect:* the clip is challenging but not a blur.
2. **Listen without speaking once.** Focus on melody, pauses, stress, and speed. → *Expect:* you can describe the overall rhythm before imitating it.
3. **Mark the transcript for chunks.** Add slashes for pauses, underline stressed words, and circle sounds you miss. → *Expect:* the text shows where breath groups and emphasis fall.
4. **Repeat one short chunk after the speaker.** Pause after each phrase and imitate it aloud. → *Expect:* you can reproduce the chunk slowly with recognizable sounds.
5. **Speak simultaneously with the audio.** Play the clip and talk along at the same time, staying as close to the speaker as possible. → *Expect:* your speech tracks the speaker's timing without large delays.
6. **Record one attempt.** Compare your recording to the original for timing, vowel quality, consonants, and intonation. → *Expect:* you identify two specific differences to fix.
7. **Repeat with one correction target.** Practice again focusing on only the most important issue. → *Expect:* that feature improves without overwhelming the whole performance.
8. **Finish with a natural-speed run.** Speak through the whole clip once without stopping. → *Expect:* the passage feels smoother than at the start.

## Decision points

- You cannot keep up at all → slow the audio if the player allows it, or shorten the chunk to one phrase.
- You understand the words but sound flat → focus on pitch movement and stress, not individual consonants.
- The clip uses dialect or slang you do not need → choose material closer to your target accent and context.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 You memorize sounds without meaning:** detect that you can mimic but cannot explain the sentence → translate or summarize each chunk before shadowing.
- **F2 You strain your voice:** detect throat pain or hoarseness → stop, lower volume and intensity, and resume later with shorter sessions.
- **F3 Errors fossilize:** detect the same mispronunciation after many repetitions → isolate the sound and get feedback from a teacher or native speaker.
- **F4 Material is too fast:** detect constant lagging → use slower graded audio before returning to native-speed clips.

## Verification

Your final recording matches the original's phrase breaks and stress closely enough that a teacher or native speaker can understand it without seeing the transcript.

## Variations

- `beginner`: shadow single sentences from a course dialogue rather than podcasts or films.
- `advanced`: shadow unscripted interviews, then summarize the content aloud in your own words.
- `tone-languages`: track pitch contours carefully; tone errors can change meaning, so use native feedback when possible.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep recordings private unless you consent to sharing your voice; avoid uploading copyrighted audio clips to public platforms.
