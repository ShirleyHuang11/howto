## Goal

You choose and use language input that you mostly understand, so vocabulary and grammar grow through repeated meaningful exposure.

## Preconditions

- A rough sense of your current level.
- Access to graded readers, learner podcasts, subtitled videos, textbooks, or teacher-selected material.
- A way to save unknown words or useful phrases.

## Steps

1. **Estimate your comprehension.** Sample a page or two minutes of audio without pausing constantly. → *Expect:* you know whether you understand roughly 80-95 percent of the content.
2. **Choose material with context clues.** Prefer stories, dialogues, images, familiar topics, or transcripts over isolated difficult text. → *Expect:* meaning is supported by context, not only dictionary lookups.
3. **Read or listen for meaning first.** Go through once without stopping for every unknown word. → *Expect:* you can summarize the main idea.
4. **Select a few useful unknowns.** Mark only high-frequency or repeated words and phrases. → *Expect:* you have a short review list, not a copied dictionary.
5. **Revisit the same input.** Read again, relisten, or watch the scene a second time after checking key words. → *Expect:* comprehension is noticeably higher on the second pass.
6. **Respond to the content.** Retell the story, answer questions, discuss it, or write a short reaction. → *Expect:* you use new language in a meaningful task.
7. **Increase difficulty gradually.** Move up when current materials feel smooth and predictable. → *Expect:* new input remains understandable with some stretch.

## Decision points

- You understand less than half → switch to easier graded content or add a transcript.
- You understand almost everything → increase length, speed, topic complexity, or remove subtitles.
- You are bored by learner materials → use native material made for children, hobby content, or familiar topics with strong visuals.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Dictionary overuse:** detect that a short page takes an hour → limit lookups to repeated or meaning-blocking words.
- **F2 Passive exposure only:** detect lots of hours but weak recall → add short retells, notes, or discussion.
- **F3 Material is too hard:** detect fatigue and no main-idea understanding → drop one level for two weeks.
- **F4 Narrow vocabulary:** detect improvement only in one topic → rotate themes such as daily life, work, school, food, and news.

## Verification

You can summarize a new piece of level-appropriate input, identify a few new useful phrases, and understand the same material better after one review.

## Variations

- `with-subtitles`: use target-language subtitles first; native-language subtitles can help at the start but should be phased out.
- `reading-focused`: graded readers and repeated extensive reading are more useful than translating every sentence.
- `listening-focused`: transcripts help bridge the gap, but verify progress by listening again without the transcript.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use legal copies of books, videos, and transcripts; do not upload paid course content to public tools or shared decks.
