## Goal

You use a language app as a structured daily practice tool while avoiding the common trap of mistaking app progress for full language ability.

## Preconditions

- A chosen language app and account.
- A language-learning goal or reason for study.
- Headphones or a microphone if the app includes audio or speech.

## Steps

1. **Choose the app for your goal.** Match the app to vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, reading, spaced repetition, or tutoring needs. → *Expect:* the app's strengths fit your target skill.
2. **Take the placement test if offered.** Do not start at zero unless you are truly new. → *Expect:* lessons begin at a productive level.
3. **Set a small daily minimum.** Pick a session length you can keep even on busy days. → *Expect:* the habit is realistic.
4. **Turn on audio and speaking practice.** Listen aloud, repeat, and compare pronunciation where supported. → *Expect:* practice includes sound, not only tapping.
5. **Write down useful phrases.** Move high-value words into a notebook or spaced-repetition deck with your own sentences. → *Expect:* app items become usable outside the app.
6. **Review mistakes immediately.** Repeat missed items and note grammar patterns. → *Expect:* errors become study material.
7. **Add real-world output.** Use app vocabulary in a message, short recording, tutor session, or language exchange. → *Expect:* you can produce language without prompts.
8. **Check progress monthly.** Revisit your goal and test with a conversation, reading passage, or writing sample. → *Expect:* app streaks are connected to real ability.

## Decision points

- App feels too easy → skip ahead, change level, or add harder input.
- You avoid speaking exercises → schedule separate speaking practice because recognition tasks will not build fluency alone.
- Subscription cost is high → compare free alternatives, library access, or focused tutoring before renewing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Streak obsession:** detect doing the shortest lesson only to keep a streak → add one weekly real-use task.
- **F2 Passive recognition:** detect understanding choices but unable to recall words → practice active recall without looking.
- **F3 Bad pronunciation habits:** detect speech recognition passing unclear speech → compare with native audio or ask a speaker.
- **F4 Privacy oversharing:** detect app requests for contacts, voice, or profile data you do not need → limit permissions.

## Verification

You complete regular app sessions, maintain a separate list of usable phrases, and can demonstrate progress in a real task outside the app.

## Variations

- Spaced-repetition apps: quality comes from good cards; use one idea per card and include audio when possible.
- Tutor marketplace apps: vet tutor experience, cancellation rules, and whether lessons match your goals.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use a strong password, limit unnecessary app permissions, and be cautious with public profiles, voice recordings, and messages with strangers.
