## Goal

You learn to recognize, write, and pronounce the symbols in a new writing system well enough to read beginner words without guessing.

## Preconditions

- A reliable chart or textbook for the script, including stroke order or handwriting examples if the script is handwritten in daily use.
- Audio for the language, ideally from a course or dictionary that pairs symbols with sounds.
- A notebook, flashcard app, or spaced-repetition system.

## Steps

1. **Identify what kind of system it is.** Determine whether it is an alphabet, abjad, abugida, syllabary, logographic system, or mixed system. → *Expect:* you know whether each sign maps mainly to a sound, syllable, consonant, word, or meaning.
2. **Learn the full inventory before memorizing details.** Skim all basic characters, diacritics, ligatures, and punctuation. → *Expect:* you can tell which marks are core letters and which modify them.
3. **Group symbols by shape and sound.** Put visually similar characters and sound-related sets together, such as vowel rows or consonant families. → *Expect:* confusing pairs are visible on one comparison sheet.
4. **Practice recognition before production.** Use flashcards that show the symbol and require the name, sound, or example word. → *Expect:* you can identify most symbols quickly without writing them yet.
5. **Add handwriting with correct order.** Copy each symbol slowly, following official or common stroke order where relevant. → *Expect:* your handwritten symbols resemble standard examples and are not built from random strokes.
6. **Attach symbols to real words.** Learn short high-frequency words rather than isolated characters only. → *Expect:* you can read several real beginner words aloud from the script.
7. **Drill the hard contrasts deliberately.** Make a small deck for symbols you confuse, including minimal pairs or side-by-side examples. → *Expect:* your error rate on the confusing set drops over several sessions.
8. **Read graded material daily.** Spend 5-10 minutes reading learner sentences, children's labels, or course dialogues aloud. → *Expect:* recognition shifts from letter-by-letter decoding toward whole-word reading.
9. **Test from both directions.** Alternate reading symbols, writing from dictation, and typing with the correct keyboard/input method. → *Expect:* you can recognize, produce, and enter the script without relying on one skill alone.

## Decision points

- The script has printed and handwritten forms → learn printed recognition first, then add handwriting variants before reading real notes or signs.
- The language uses many ligatures or contextual forms → practice whole syllables or letter combinations, not only isolated characters.
- You are learning a logographic system → prioritize high-frequency characters, radicals/components, and vocabulary context instead of trying to finish the entire inventory first.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Similar symbols keep blending together:** detect repeated swaps on the same pair → make a contrast card that names the visual difference and drill only that pair for a few minutes daily.
- **F2 You can recite the chart but cannot read words:** detect slow decoding in real text → move practice into short words and sentences immediately.
- **F3 Handwriting becomes illegible:** detect symbols that native materials would not recognize → slow down, copy from a standard model, and check stroke order.
- **F4 Pronunciation is detached from the script:** detect that you can name letters but not read aloud → add audio-backed example words to every new symbol.

## Verification

You can read a fresh beginner paragraph aloud, write a short dictated word list in the script, and correctly identify the symbols you previously confused.

## Variations

- `japanese-kana`: learn hiragana first, then katakana, and practice long vowels, small kana, and doubled consonants in full words.
- `arabic-or-hebrew`: treat vowels and diacritics explicitly; unvoweled text requires vocabulary knowledge, not just letter recognition.
- `chinese`: learn components, stroke order, pronunciation, and common words together; a single character often has multiple readings or uses.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. If using online flashcard decks, avoid uploading private class materials or copyrighted textbook scans; create your own cards from licensed or public examples.
