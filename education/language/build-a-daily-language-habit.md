## Goal

You create a repeatable daily language routine that is small enough to survive busy days and focused enough to improve listening, reading, speaking, and vocabulary over time.

## Preconditions

- A target language and a realistic reason for learning it.
- One main course, app, textbook, tutor, or curriculum to avoid constant switching.
- A daily time slot of at least 10-20 minutes.

## Steps

1. **Choose one daily anchor.** Attach language study to an existing routine, such as after breakfast, during a commute, or before bed. → *Expect:* the study time has a specific trigger, not just a vague intention.
2. **Set a minimum viable session.** Define the smallest acceptable day, such as 10 flashcards plus 5 minutes of audio. → *Expect:* you have a routine you can complete even when tired.
3. **Build a balanced weekly menu.** Include vocabulary review, comprehensible input, pronunciation, grammar, and output across the week. → *Expect:* the routine does not become only app taps or only passive listening.
4. **Prepare materials before the session.** Keep the lesson, audio, notebook, and flashcards ready. → *Expect:* you start studying within one minute of the anchor.
5. **Track completion simply.** Mark each day on a calendar or habit tracker without over-recording. → *Expect:* you can see streaks and missed days at a glance.
6. **Use one recovery rule.** If you miss a day, do the minimum session the next day instead of trying to double everything. → *Expect:* one miss does not become a week off.
7. **Schedule a weekly review.** Once a week, check what felt too easy, too hard, or too boring. → *Expect:* the next week has one small adjustment, not a full restart.
8. **Add real use gradually.** Send one message, speak with a tutor, read a short article, or listen to a native clip each week. → *Expect:* the habit connects to actual communication.

## Decision points

- You keep skipping the same time slot → move the anchor to a time with fewer interruptions.
- The routine feels too easy for two weeks → increase duration or difficulty by a small amount.
- You dread the routine → keep the anchor but swap in more engaging material at the same level.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 All streak, no learning:** detect perfect completion but no recall or comprehension gains → add weekly self-tests and more real input.
- **F2 Routine is too ambitious:** detect repeated missed sessions → cut the daily minimum in half for two weeks.
- **F3 App hopping replaces study:** detect frequent tool changes → commit to one core resource for 30 days before switching.
- **F4 Speaking never happens:** detect months of silent study → schedule one low-pressure speaking task each week.

## Verification

After 30 days, you have completed at least 20 sessions, can name what you practiced each week, and can perform a small real-language task that was harder at the start.

## Variations

- `commute`: use audio review and shadowing, then do writing or flashcards later when stationary.
- `busy-parent-or-shift-work`: define a 5-minute emergency version so the habit survives unpredictable days.
- `exam-focused`: replace some open-ended input with timed tasks matching the exam format.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not post recordings, personal messages, or schoolwork publicly unless you are comfortable with them being copied; use private tutor or class channels for feedback.
