## Goal

You define a language-learning goal that is specific enough to guide daily practice and realistic enough to measure.

## Preconditions

- A target language and reason for learning it.
- A rough idea of your current level.
- A calendar showing available study time.

## Steps

1. **Name the real use case.** Decide whether you need travel survival, family conversation, school credit, workplace communication, reading, exams, or relocation. → *Expect:* the goal is tied to an actual situation.
2. **Estimate your current level.** Use a placement quiz, CEFR self-assessment, tutor evaluation, or sample tasks. → *Expect:* a starting level such as beginner, A2, intermediate, or heritage speaker.
3. **Define the target task.** Write a concrete ability, such as "hold a 10-minute restaurant conversation" or "read a news article with a dictionary." → *Expect:* success can be observed.
4. **Choose a timeframe.** Match the target to available hours per week and difficulty of the language for you. → *Expect:* the deadline is plausible, not just desirable.
5. **Select practice modes.** Include input, speaking or writing output, vocabulary, grammar, and review. → *Expect:* the plan covers more than app streaks.
6. **Set weekly metrics.** Track minutes, lessons, words reviewed, conversations, pages read, or recordings made. → *Expect:* progress can be measured every week.
7. **Schedule a checkpoint.** Plan a test, tutor session, language exchange, recording, or writing sample after 2-4 weeks. → *Expect:* the goal has an external check.
8. **Adjust the goal after evidence.** Revise scope if practice shows the goal is too easy, too broad, or unrealistic. → *Expect:* the next version fits real progress.

## Decision points

- You need an exam score → align goals to that exam's sections and scoring scale.
- You mainly need conversation → prioritize listening and speaking earlier than grammar perfection.
- You have only 10 minutes daily → set habit and comprehension goals before fluency goals.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague fluency goal:** detect "be fluent" with no task → rewrite it as a scenario and time limit.
- **F2 App streak without communication:** detect points increasing but no speaking or writing → add output practice.
- **F3 Overloaded plan:** detect missed sessions every week → reduce frequency and protect consistency.
- **F4 No feedback:** detect repeating the same mistakes → add tutor, exchange partner, correction tool, or teacher review.

## Verification

Your goal states the target language, current level, target task, deadline, weekly practice schedule, and checkpoint method.

## Variations

- CEFR-oriented learning: goals can map to A1-C2 descriptors, such as "A2: describe routines and needs in simple sentences."
- Heritage learners: include literacy, formal register, or pronunciation goals depending on what is missing.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Be careful sharing personal details in language communities, and avoid uploading private conversations or student work without permission.
