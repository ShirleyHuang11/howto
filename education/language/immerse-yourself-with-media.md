## Goal

You use books, podcasts, videos, music, news, and social media in the target language to increase comprehension and vocabulary through regular exposure.

## Preconditions

- A target language and approximate level.
- Access to legal media sources in that language.
- A way to save words, phrases, and links.

## Steps

1. **Choose level-appropriate media.** Pick material where you can understand enough context to stay engaged. → *Expect:* you understand the main idea even if some words are unknown.
2. **Mix intensive and extensive exposure.** Use short clips for close study and longer media for relaxed listening or reading. → *Expect:* the plan includes both accuracy and volume.
3. **Use subtitles strategically.** [BRANCH: beginner, use target-language subtitles with slower content | intermediate, try target-language subtitles first, then no subtitles | advanced, use subtitles only to check details] → *Expect:* subtitles support comprehension without replacing listening.
4. **Capture useful language.** Save phrases, collocations, pronunciation notes, and recurring grammar patterns. → *Expect:* notes contain chunks you can reuse.
5. **Rewatch or reread selected material.** Repeat high-value clips or passages after studying vocabulary. → *Expect:* comprehension improves on the second pass.
6. **Shadow short audio.** Repeat after speakers for 30-60 seconds, copying rhythm and intonation. → *Expect:* pronunciation and listening attention improve.
7. **Connect media to output.** Summarize an episode, discuss an article, or write a reaction in the target language. → *Expect:* input becomes speaking or writing practice.
8. **Review and rotate sources.** Keep sources that are useful and enjoyable; replace ones that are too hard, too easy, or boring. → *Expect:* immersion remains sustainable.

## Decision points

- Content is incomprehensible after several minutes → choose easier material or preview vocabulary first.
- You understand but cannot speak → add summaries, shadowing, and conversation practice.
- You keep translating every word → switch some sessions to gist comprehension only.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Media is too hard:** detect frustration and no main idea → use graded readers, learner podcasts, or children's nonfiction.
- **F2 Passive bingeing:** detect hours watched with no recall → add short summaries and phrase capture.
- **F3 Subtitle dependence:** detect understanding only written captions → remove subtitles for short segments.
- **F4 Narrow vocabulary:** detect only one genre or topic → rotate news, conversation, stories, and practical scenarios.

## Verification

You maintain a regular media routine, can summarize recent content in the target language, and have a reviewed list of reusable phrases.

## Variations

- Logographic or unfamiliar scripts: include reading practice and character recognition separately from listening.
- Heritage learners: choose media that expands formal register, literacy, or professional vocabulary.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use legal sources, be careful with public comments in a new language, and avoid sharing personal information in fan or social communities.
