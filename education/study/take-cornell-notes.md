## Goal

You capture class or reading notes in the Cornell format so the page becomes useful for review, self-testing, and summary writing.

## Preconditions

- A notebook, tablet, or document where you can divide the page into sections.
- A lecture, video, textbook section, or meeting to take notes from.
- Enough time after the session to revise and summarize the notes.

## Steps

1. **Set up the page before the session.** Divide it into a narrow cue column on the left, a wider notes column on the right, and a summary area at the bottom. → *Expect:* the page has three clear zones.
2. **Record the topic and date.** Add the course, unit, source, and date at the top. → *Expect:* you can later identify where the notes came from.
3. **Take main notes in the right column.** Write key ideas, examples, formulas, diagrams, and questions, using abbreviations instead of full sentences when appropriate. → *Expect:* the right column captures the substance without trying to transcribe everything.
4. **Mark confusion while listening or reading.** Put a question mark beside unclear points and leave space for answers. → *Expect:* weak spots are visible for follow-up.
5. **Add cues soon after the session.** In the left column, write keywords, prompts, possible quiz questions, vocabulary, or headings that match the notes. → *Expect:* the cue column can trigger recall without exposing the answers.
6. **Write the bottom summary.** In 2-5 sentences, explain the main idea, how the details connect, and what still needs review. → *Expect:* the page ends with a concise synthesis, not copied text.
7. **Review by covering the notes column.** Use the cues to answer aloud or on paper, then uncover the notes to check accuracy. → *Expect:* you know which ideas you can retrieve and which need repair.

## Decision points

- The lecture is too fast → capture headings, examples, and questions first; fill gaps from slides or classmates afterward.
- The material is mostly problem-solving → use the notes column for worked examples and the cue column for "when to use this method" prompts.
- You are using a tablet → create a reusable Cornell template so setup does not waste class time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Transcript-style notes:** detect long paragraphs copied word for word → revise into headings, relationships, and examples.
- **F2 Empty cue column:** detect no prompts after class → add questions before the next review session.
- **F3 Summary repeats details:** detect a bottom section that lists facts but no main point → rewrite it as what the lesson was mainly about.
- **F4 Notes never reviewed:** detect pages filed away untouched → schedule a 5-minute recall pass within 24 hours.

## Verification

The page has a dated topic, useful right-column notes, recall prompts in the left column, a short bottom summary, and at least one covered-note self-test has been completed.

## Variations

- `science`: include diagrams, units, assumptions, and common mistakes in worked examples.
- `humanities`: use cue prompts for claims, evidence, counterarguments, and key quotations with page numbers.
- `digital notes`: tag pages by course and topic so the cue questions remain searchable.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not upload classmates' names, private discussions, disability accommodations, grades, or unpublished course materials to public note-sharing tools without permission.
