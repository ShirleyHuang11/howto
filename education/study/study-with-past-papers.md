## Goal

You use past papers or released exams to understand the exam format, practice under realistic conditions, and fix recurring errors before the real test.

## Preconditions

- Legitimately released past papers, sample exams, or practice tests.
- Mark schemes, rubrics, worked solutions, or answer keys.
- The current syllabus or exam specification to confirm relevance.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the paper is relevant.** Check year, course version, syllabus, exam board, instructor, and format. → *Expect:* the paper matches the current exam closely enough to be useful.
2. **Skim the structure first.** Note sections, timing, marks, allowed tools, question types, and command words. → *Expect:* you know how the exam is built.
3. **Attempt one section under timed conditions.** Put away notes unless the real exam allows them and follow the official time limit. → *Expect:* your attempt reflects real performance pressure.
4. **Mark with the official scheme.** Use the answer key, rubric, or scoring guide and note where points are earned or lost. → *Expect:* errors are tied to marks, not vague impressions.
5. **Create an error log.** Record topic, error type, cause, correct method, and what to practice next. → *Expect:* patterns become visible across questions.
6. **Review the weak topics.** Use notes, textbook explanations, worked examples, or tutoring to repair the causes. → *Expect:* each major error has a study action.
7. **Retake or try a similar question.** After a delay, solve the missed type again without the solution. → *Expect:* the repaired skill transfers to a fresh attempt.

## Decision points

- No official past papers exist → use instructor samples, textbook end-of-chapter mixed reviews, or reputable exam-board samples.
- The syllabus changed → use only matching question types and avoid obsolete content.
- Scores are low across every section → pause full papers and rebuild foundations topic by topic.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Memorizing old answers:** detect improved scores only on repeated papers → switch to unseen papers or changed numbers.
- **F2 Ignoring timing:** detect correct answers that take too long → practice timed sections and learn mark allocation.
- **F3 Mark scheme mismatch:** detect lost points for missing wording or units → study the rubric language and model answers.
- **F4 Unauthorized materials:** detect classmates sharing leaked or current exams → do not use them; ask the instructor for permitted practice materials.

## Verification

You have completed at least one timed past-paper section, marked it with a legitimate scheme, logged errors, and correctly redone the main missed question types.

## Variations

- `essay exams`: compare your outline and thesis to the rubric before writing full timed essays.
- `standardized tests`: use official practice tests when available because third-party difficulty can vary.
- `open-book exams`: practice finding and applying information quickly instead of memorizing page locations only.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use only authorized past papers and do not share leaked exam content, instructor-restricted files, or classmates' answers.
