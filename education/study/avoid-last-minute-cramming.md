## Goal

You spread learning and review over time so exam preparation does not depend on an exhausting, low-retention final night.

## Preconditions

- An exam, deadline, or performance date.
- A topic list, syllabus, study guide, or previous assessments.
- A calendar or planner.

## Steps

1. **Calculate the remaining days.** Count the days until the exam or deadline and mark any days with limited availability. → *Expect:* the true study window is visible.
2. **List exam-weighted topics.** Use the syllabus, review sheet, rubric, or past assessments to identify high-value material. → *Expect:* priorities are based on likely impact.
3. **Schedule first passes early.** Put each major topic on the calendar before the final review period. → *Expect:* every topic appears before the last day.
4. **Add retrieval reviews.** Schedule short self-tests after each topic and again several days later. → *Expect:* review is spaced rather than one-time.
5. **Use practice to find weak spots.** Complete quizzes, problems, outlines, or flashcards before rereading notes. → *Expect:* study time targets actual errors.
6. **Reserve the final day for consolidation.** Plan light review, formula checks, packing materials, sleep, and logistics. → *Expect:* the last day is not carrying the whole workload.
7. **Protect sleep and meals.** Stop late-night study at a set time and prepare what you need for exam day. → *Expect:* you arrive with usable attention and required materials.

## Decision points

- The exam is tomorrow → triage core concepts, formulas, and common problem types; do not attempt to learn everything equally.
- You discover major gaps early → seek office hours, tutoring, or targeted practice while time remains.
- Multiple exams cluster together → interleave subjects and schedule the nearest or hardest exams first.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 False confidence from rereading:** detect familiar notes but poor recall → switch to closed-book questions.
- **F2 Schedule collapse:** detect missed study days → use buffer slots and cut low-weight topics.
- **F3 All-nighter temptation:** detect plan depending on lost sleep → choose the highest-value review and sleep enough to function.
- **F4 Ignored logistics:** detect missing calculator, ID, charger, or location info → prepare materials the day before.

## Verification

Before the final day, all major topics have had at least one first pass and one retrieval review, weak areas are identified, and exam-day materials and logistics are ready.

## Variations

- `cumulative final`: rotate old and new units from the beginning of the plan.
- `skills exam`: schedule repeated practice sessions with feedback rather than long reading blocks.
- `essay exam`: prepare thesis templates, evidence banks, and timed outlines.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid sleep deprivation, excessive caffeine, or unsafe stimulant use; follow school integrity rules and do not seek leaked exam content.
