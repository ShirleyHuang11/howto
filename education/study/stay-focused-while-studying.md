## Goal

You create conditions that make sustained attention easier during a study session and recover quickly when your focus breaks.

## Preconditions

- A specific study task and target outcome.
- Required materials, including notes, book, calculator, or computer.
- A study location where you can control at least some distractions.

## Steps

1. **Write the session target.** State what will be done by the end, such as "finish lab outline" or "review 40 cards." → *Expect:* the session has a finish line.
2. **Clear the workspace.** Keep only the tools and materials needed for the target task. → *Expect:* visual distractions and unrelated materials are reduced.
3. **Silence digital interruptions.** Turn on focus mode, close extra tabs, mute notifications, or use a blocker. → *Expect:* messages and alerts will not interrupt by default.
4. **Choose a focus interval.** Set a timer for a realistic block of 15-45 minutes. → *Expect:* attention is committed for a defined period.
5. **Use a distraction capture list.** When unrelated tasks or worries appear, write them down and return to the work. → *Expect:* interruptions are acknowledged without being followed.
6. **Change strategy when focus fades.** Stand up, switch to active recall, solve a problem, read aloud, or take a short break. → *Expect:* low attention triggers an adjustment instead of drifting.
7. **Close the session deliberately.** Record what was completed and the next action. → *Expect:* restarting later will be easier.

## Decision points

- Noise is the problem → use headphones, move rooms, or choose a library or classroom.
- The task is too vague → split it into a first action that can be completed in one interval.
- Fatigue is the problem → take a real break, eat, hydrate, move, or sleep instead of forcing low-quality work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Hidden multitasking:** detect frequent tab or app switching → close all nonessential tools and use full-screen mode.
- **F2 Task switching:** detect moving among subjects without finishing anything → keep a parking list and finish one small target first.
- **F3 Focus drops from confusion:** detect rereading the same paragraph repeatedly → write the exact question and seek help or a simpler source.
- **F4 Unplanned breaks:** detect breaks starting with no end time → set a break timer before leaving the desk.

## Verification

The study session has a written target, distractions are reduced, at least one focus interval is completed, and progress plus the next action are recorded.

## Variations

- `shared home`: tell others the focus interval and when you will be available again.
- `computer-based work`: use separate browser profiles or windows for study and personal accounts.
- `long reading`: alternate reading sections with brief summaries or recall questions.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not use focus tools that expose private browsing, messages, grades, or school credentials to people who should not see them.
