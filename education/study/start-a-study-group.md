## Goal

You form a small study group with a clear purpose, reliable schedule, and norms that improve learning instead of becoming social drift.

## Preconditions

- A course, exam, project, or shared learning goal.
- Potential members who are enrolled in the course or studying the same material.
- A meeting place, video call link, or chat channel.

## Steps

1. **Define the group's purpose.** Decide whether the group will solve problems, review readings, quiz each other, prepare presentations, or compare drafts. → *Expect:* the invitation states exactly what the group is for.
2. **Invite a small, committed group.** Start with 3-6 people who attend class, attempt work beforehand, and communicate reliably. → *Expect:* enough members for discussion without making scheduling impossible.
3. **Set a recurring time and location.** Choose a predictable meeting slot and a backup plan for remote attendance. → *Expect:* members know when and where the next session happens.
4. **Agree on academic integrity rules.** Clarify what collaboration is allowed by the instructor and what must remain individual work. → *Expect:* everyone knows the boundary between studying together and cheating.
5. **Assign preparation before each meeting.** Send problems, readings, questions, or topics in advance. → *Expect:* members arrive with work to discuss.
6. **Run each session with roles.** Use a facilitator, timekeeper, note-taker, or problem presenter, rotating roles when possible. → *Expect:* the meeting moves through planned tasks.
7. **End with individual next actions.** Each member states what they will review, ask, or practice before the next session. → *Expect:* the group produces follow-up work, not just conversation.

## Decision points

- Members arrive unprepared repeatedly → reduce membership or switch to silent co-study with short check-ins.
- The course forbids collaboration on graded work → discuss concepts and ungraded practice only.
- Scheduling is the biggest barrier → use shorter sessions or asynchronous question threads.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Social drift:** detect most of the session spent chatting → use an agenda and a timer.
- **F2 One-person tutoring:** detect one member explaining everything while others listen → rotate presenters and require everyone to attempt problems first.
- **F3 Integrity violation risk:** detect sharing answers to graded assignments → stop and ask the instructor what collaboration is permitted.
- **F4 Unequal participation:** detect silent or dominating members → assign turns, roles, and specific questions to each person.

## Verification

The group has a purpose, members, schedule, communication channel, academic-integrity rule, first agenda, and a confirmed next meeting.

## Variations

- `exam prep`: use timed practice sections and post-session error logs.
- `writing course`: exchange outlines or drafts using the rubric, but do not rewrite each other's graded submissions.
- `online class`: use shared documents and video calls, with a written agenda to reduce drift.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not share private grades, login credentials, answer keys, or instructor-restricted materials in group chats; confirm collaboration rules before discussing graded work.
