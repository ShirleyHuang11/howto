## Goal

You turn a textbook chapter into a short, accurate summary that captures the main argument, key terms, evidence, and points needing review.

## Preconditions

- The assigned chapter and any instructor learning objectives.
- A notebook, document, or annotation tool.
- Enough time to read actively rather than skim mechanically.

## Steps

1. **Preview the chapter structure.** Read the title, introduction, headings, figures, summary, review questions, and key terms before detailed reading. → *Expect:* you know the chapter's likely main topics.
2. **Write guiding questions.** Turn headings and learning objectives into questions you should be able to answer. → *Expect:* reading has a purpose beyond finishing pages.
3. **Read one section at a time.** After each section, pause and write the main point in your own words. → *Expect:* each section has a short note that is not copied from the book.
4. **Capture essential evidence and examples.** Record formulas, cases, dates, mechanisms, diagrams, or data that support the main points. → *Expect:* the summary includes enough detail to be useful later.
5. **Define key terms accurately.** Write definitions in your own words and keep technical wording only where required. → *Expect:* terms are understandable without the textbook open.
6. **Write the chapter summary.** Combine section notes into one concise paragraph or bullet set that explains the chapter's overall message. → *Expect:* the summary shows how the parts connect.
7. **Add review questions and gaps.** List what you still cannot explain and what to ask in class or study next. → *Expect:* weak areas are visible.

## Decision points

- The chapter is dense or technical → summarize by subsection and include diagrams or worked examples.
- You are summarizing for an essay → include page numbers for evidence you may cite.
- You are summarizing for an exam → convert the summary into active-recall questions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Copying instead of summarizing:** detect long textbook sentences in your notes → close the book and rewrite the idea from memory.
- **F2 Missing the main argument:** detect many facts but no overall point → reread the introduction, conclusion, and section headings.
- **F3 No source location:** detect useful quotes or data with no page number → return to the chapter and add page references.
- **F4 Overlong summary:** detect a summary nearly as long as the chapter section → cut examples that do not support the main idea.

## Verification

You can explain the chapter's main point, define key terms, name the most important evidence or examples, and list the remaining questions without rereading the full chapter.

## Variations

- `science`: include mechanisms, variables, units, and one representative worked example.
- `history`: include chronology, causes, consequences, and contested interpretations.
- `digital textbook`: use bookmarks sparingly and export notes only if permitted by the platform.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Respect copyright when copying textbook text or screenshots, and do not upload restricted course materials to public tools.
