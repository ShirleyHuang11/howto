## Goal

You condense a unit or exam topic onto one organized page that supports review, recall, and quick identification of weak areas.

## Preconditions

- Notes, textbook summaries, assignments, quizzes, and learning objectives.
- One blank page or a digital page with fixed size.
- The exam format or assignment expectations, if available.

## Steps

1. **Define the page scope.** Choose one unit, chapter set, exam section, or theme. → *Expect:* the sheet has boundaries and will not become a miniature textbook.
2. **Collect the highest-value material.** Pull formulas, definitions, timelines, diagrams, mistakes, examples, and instructor-emphasized ideas. → *Expect:* the raw material is gathered before layout begins.
3. **Group related ideas.** Sort content into categories such as concepts, methods, examples, exceptions, and common errors. → *Expect:* the structure reflects how the subject works.
4. **Design the page layout.** Reserve space for headings, tables, diagrams, formulas, and a small "still weak" area. → *Expect:* the page is readable at normal size.
5. **Write compact explanations.** Use your own words, abbreviations you understand, and small examples instead of copying long notes. → *Expect:* every item earns its space.
6. **Add retrieval prompts.** Include questions, blank formulas, or "can I explain..." checks around the page. → *Expect:* the sheet supports active recall, not only recognition.
7. **Test the sheet.** Try a practice problem, outline answer, or self-quiz using the page, then revise unclear parts. → *Expect:* the final page helps performance and exposes gaps.

## Decision points

- The page is overcrowded → remove low-weight details and move them to regular notes.
- The exam allows a note sheet → confirm size, handwritten/typed rules, and allowed content before relying on it.
- The topic is procedural → make flowcharts and worked examples more prominent than definitions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Tiny unreadable text:** detect squinting or cramped writing → enlarge key items and cut less important details.
- **F2 Copy-only sheet:** detect blocks copied from notes → rewrite as formulas, prompts, diagrams, and examples.
- **F3 Missing mistakes:** detect repeated practice errors absent from the page → add a common-errors box.
- **F4 Wrong emphasis:** detect page space spent on trivia not tested → compare with objectives, rubrics, or past exams.

## Verification

The study sheet fits on one page, is readable, covers the defined scope, includes recall prompts or examples, and improves performance on at least one practice task.

## Variations

- `math or science`: include formulas with units, assumptions, and one representative problem.
- `history`: use timelines, cause-effect chains, people, places, and thesis-ready comparisons.
- `open-note exam`: follow the instructor's exact rule; unauthorized sheets can count as academic misconduct.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Confirm exam rules before bringing any study sheet into a test room, and do not include confidential or prohibited material.
