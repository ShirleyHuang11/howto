## Goal

You deepen your understanding by explaining a topic clearly enough that another person, or an imaginary beginner, could follow it and ask questions.

## Preconditions

- A topic you have already encountered in class, reading, or practice.
- Notes or source material for checking accuracy after the explanation.
- A listener, study partner, recording device, whiteboard, or blank page.

## Steps

1. **Choose a focused teaching target.** Pick one concept, problem type, reading argument, or process. → *Expect:* the explanation can fit into 5-15 minutes.
2. **Prepare a beginner-friendly outline.** List the starting idea, needed prerequisites, main steps, example, and common mistake. → *Expect:* the explanation has a clear path.
3. **Teach without reading from notes.** Explain aloud, draw, or write as if speaking to a learner. → *Expect:* gaps and hesitations become obvious.
4. **Use an example.** Work through a concrete problem, case, sentence, diagram, or scenario. → *Expect:* the idea is shown in use, not only defined.
5. **Invite or simulate questions.** Ask "why?", "when does this fail?", and "how is this different from..." → *Expect:* the explanation is tested from multiple angles.
6. **Check accuracy.** Compare your explanation with notes, textbook, rubric, or solution after teaching. → *Expect:* incorrect or missing parts are identified.
7. **Revise and reteach briefly.** Repeat the weak section in simpler, more accurate language. → *Expect:* the second explanation is smoother and more complete.

## Decision points

- No listener is available → record yourself or teach a blank page using the Feynman technique.
- The listener looks confused → slow down, define prerequisites, and use a different example.
- You cannot answer a question → mark it honestly and check a reliable source before answering.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Performance without checking:** detect confident explanations that were never verified → compare against authoritative material.
- **F2 Too much jargon:** detect a listener unable to restate the idea → replace terms with plain language, then reintroduce technical terms.
- **F3 Skipping examples:** detect abstract explanation only → add one complete worked example.
- **F4 Teaching becomes copying:** detect reading notes aloud → close notes and explain from memory, then check afterward.

## Verification

You can explain the topic from memory, work through an example, answer or record follow-up questions, and correct the explanation against reliable source material.

## Variations

- `math`: teach the method, the reason each step is allowed, and a common wrong turn.
- `literature or history`: teach the claim, evidence, context, and counterargument.
- `language learning`: teach a grammar pattern with three original sentences and corrections.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not present guesses as facts when teaching others, and avoid sharing private grades, identifying student details, or restricted course materials.
