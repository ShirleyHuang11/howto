## Goal

You read a textbook chapter for understanding and retention without wasting time copying or rereading everything.

## Preconditions

- The assigned textbook section or chapter.
- Course learning objectives, lecture topics, or review questions if available.
- A notebook, annotation tool, or document for brief notes.

## Steps

1. **Preview the chapter.** Read headings, introduction, summary, figures, key terms, and review questions first. → *Expect:* you know the structure and likely priorities.
2. **Set reading questions.** Turn headings and objectives into questions you need to answer. → *Expect:* reading is guided by specific goals.
3. **Read in short sections.** Complete one heading-level section at a time before taking notes. → *Expect:* notes respond to meaning, not sentence-by-sentence copying.
4. **Pause and recall.** Close or look away from the book and state the section's main point from memory. → *Expect:* comprehension is checked immediately.
5. **Write selective notes.** Record definitions, formulas, mechanisms, examples, diagrams, and confusing points. → *Expect:* notes capture what you must use later.
6. **Work embedded questions or examples.** Attempt practice questions before reading solutions. → *Expect:* you know whether you can apply the reading.
7. **Review the chapter as a whole.** Answer the initial questions and summarize the chapter in a few sentences. → *Expect:* the pieces connect into one understanding.

## Decision points

- The chapter is mostly review → skim familiar parts and spend time on problems or unfamiliar sections.
- The chapter is very technical → slow down for diagrams, definitions, and worked examples.
- You are short on time → read summaries, headings, figures, and assigned problem types first, then return to details.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Linear copying:** detect notes that mirror the book page by page → switch to question-based notes.
- **F2 Skipping examples:** detect understanding definitions but failing problems → work examples actively.
- **F3 Vocabulary pileup:** detect repeated unknown terms → build a short glossary before continuing.
- **F4 No recall check:** detect finishing pages with little memory → pause after each section and summarize from memory.

## Verification

You can answer the chapter's guiding questions, explain the main ideas without the book open, define key terms, and complete representative practice questions or examples.

## Variations

- `science textbook`: prioritize diagrams, units, processes, and end-of-section problems.
- `humanities textbook`: track argument, evidence, chronology, and author perspective.
- `digital textbook`: use search and bookmarks, but still pause for recall rather than scrolling continuously.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Follow copyright and platform rules when downloading, sharing, annotating, or exporting textbook pages.
