## Goal

You use timed focus intervals and planned breaks to start studying, reduce distraction, and measure real work completed.

## Preconditions

- One study task that can be attempted in a short session.
- A timer, watch, phone, app, or browser timer.
- A place where interruptions can be reduced.

## Steps

1. **Choose one concrete task.** Write a task such as "outline chapter 3" or "solve problems 1-10," not a broad subject. → *Expect:* the timer starts with a defined target.
2. **Prepare the workspace.** Open only needed materials, silence notifications, and place distracting devices out of reach if possible. → *Expect:* the first interval can begin without setup delays.
3. **Set a focus timer.** Use 25 minutes as a default, or 10-15 minutes if starting is the main problem. → *Expect:* a visible countdown is running.
4. **Work only on the chosen task.** When unrelated thoughts appear, jot them on a parking list and return to the task. → *Expect:* distractions are captured without taking over.
5. **Take a short break when the timer ends.** Stand, stretch, drink water, or rest your eyes for about 5 minutes. → *Expect:* the break is restorative and has an end time.
6. **Repeat for a small set.** Complete 2-4 focus intervals, then take a longer break of about 15-30 minutes. → *Expect:* work and rest alternate predictably.
7. **Record what was completed.** Note pages read, problems solved, cards reviewed, or questions generated. → *Expect:* progress is visible and the next step is clearer.

## Decision points

- You enter deep flow near the timer end → finish the natural stopping point, then take the break.
- You keep checking your phone → move it to another room or use app blocking.
- The task cannot fit in one interval → split it into smaller outcomes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Timer becomes avoidance:** detect repeated timer setup with no work → start a 5-minute version immediately.
- **F2 Breaks sprawl:** detect a 5-minute break becoming 30 minutes → use a break timer too.
- **F3 Wrong task size:** detect no progress after one interval → redefine the task into a first visible action.
- **F4 Multitasking:** detect switching among classes, tabs, or messages → close extras and restart the interval.

## Verification

At least one timed focus interval is complete, the task has measurable progress, distractions were parked instead of followed, and the next study action is recorded.

## Variations

- `ADHD or low energy`: shorter intervals such as 10/3 or 15/5 may be more sustainable.
- `reading`: use each interval for a defined page range plus a short recall summary.
- `problem sets`: use the break to mark stuck problems for office hours or tutoring.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not use strict timers to skip meals, medication, sleep, or needed movement; protect private study documents if using shared timers or productivity apps.
