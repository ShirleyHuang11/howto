## Goal

You study by retrieving information from memory before looking at the answer, so practice exposes what you actually know.

## Preconditions

- A defined topic, chapter, lecture, vocabulary set, or problem type.
- Notes, textbook sections, slides, or worked solutions to check against after trying.
- Paper, a whiteboard, flashcards, or a quiz app.

## Steps

1. **Choose a narrow target.** Pick one concept set, formula group, reading section, or procedure for the session. → *Expect:* the recall task is specific enough to test in 10-30 minutes.
2. **Close the source material.** Put away notes, tabs, slides, and answer keys before attempting retrieval. → *Expect:* you cannot accidentally read the answer while testing yourself.
3. **Prompt yourself with questions.** Turn headings, learning objectives, diagrams, or formulas into questions. → *Expect:* each prompt requires an answer, explanation, sketch, or solved step.
4. **Answer from memory first.** Write, speak, draw, or solve without checking until you have made a complete attempt. → *Expect:* there is visible evidence of what you could produce unaided.
5. **Check against the source.** Compare your answer with the notes, textbook, rubric, or solution. → *Expect:* errors, omissions, and correct parts are marked clearly.
6. **Repair the miss.** Rewrite the correct explanation in your own words, solve a similar problem, or make a flashcard for the exact gap. → *Expect:* each missed item has a follow-up action.
7. **Retest after a delay.** Return later the same day or the next day and answer the same prompt again without looking. → *Expect:* the corrected idea can be retrieved after time has passed.

## Decision points

- You cannot recall anything → look briefly at a small hint, close the source, and try again instead of rereading the whole section.
- You remember facts but not application → switch to practice problems, examples, or explaining when to use each idea.
- You get bored because prompts are too easy → mix old and new topics and increase difficulty.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Passive checking:** detect yourself thinking "I knew that" after reading the answer → require a written or spoken answer before checking.
- **F2 Memorized wording only:** detect correct definitions but failed examples → add prompts that ask for application and comparison.
- **F3 Too many misses:** detect frustration after repeated blanks → reduce scope, relearn the prerequisite, and test again in smaller chunks.
- **F4 No follow-up:** detect a list of errors with no repair → convert each miss into a flashcard, practice problem, or office-hours question.

## Verification

You can answer the selected prompts with the source closed, check them against reliable material, and correctly redo previously missed items after a delay.

## Variations

- `math`: recall the method, then solve a fresh problem without viewing the worked solution.
- `history`: create prompts that ask for cause, effect, chronology, and evidence, not just dates.
- `language learning`: recall meaning, pronunciation, spelling, and use in a sentence.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. If using an online quiz tool, avoid entering private student information, unpublished exam questions, or copyrighted course materials beyond what your institution permits.
