## Goal

You create memory cues that make ordered lists, unfamiliar terms, procedures, or distinctions easier to recall accurately.

## Preconditions

- A specific set of items to memorize.
- Accurate source material to check the items and order.
- Paper, notes app, flashcards, or a whiteboard.

## Steps

1. **Choose the exact material.** Write the list, sequence, terms, or distinctions exactly as they must be remembered. → *Expect:* the target memory set is complete and correct.
2. **Identify the memory problem.** Decide whether you need order, spelling, categories, paired meanings, steps, or exceptions. → *Expect:* the mnemonic matches the type of recall needed.
3. **Select a mnemonic method.** [BRANCH: ordered list, use an acronym, acrostic, story, or memory palace | paired facts, use images, associations, or keyword links | process, use a flow or first-letter cue] → *Expect:* the chosen method fits the task.
4. **Make the cue vivid but accurate.** Create a phrase, image, story, rhythm, or location path that points to the real information. → *Expect:* the cue is memorable without distorting the content.
5. **Practice recall from the cue.** Cover the source and reconstruct the full answer using only the mnemonic. → *Expect:* you can produce the target items in the right form.
6. **Check and repair errors.** Compare with the source, then adjust the mnemonic where it causes omissions or wrong order. → *Expect:* the cue retrieves accurate information.
7. **Fade the cue with practice.** Review until the actual content comes faster than the mnemonic. → *Expect:* the mnemonic becomes a backup, not the whole memory.

## Decision points

- The mnemonic is harder than the material → simplify it or use spaced repetition instead.
- The material requires understanding → pair the mnemonic with examples and explanations.
- The order matters legally, medically, or technically → verify against the authoritative source every time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Cute but inaccurate cue:** detect remembering the phrase but not the correct facts → rebuild the cue from the exact source.
- **F2 Missing understanding:** detect correct recitation but poor application → add practice questions and examples.
- **F3 Confusable acronyms:** detect the same letter standing for several terms → use an acrostic or story with more context.
- **F4 Forgotten cue:** detect no recall after a few days → schedule spaced reviews and make the image or phrase more distinctive.

## Verification

You can reconstruct the target information from memory, in the required order or pairing, and confirm it against the source without omissions or substitutions.

## Variations

- `anatomy`: use spatial diagrams or body-location cues along with exact terminology.
- `language learning`: connect sound, meaning, and a sentence rather than relying only on a pun.
- `procedures`: use mnemonics only after understanding why each step occurs.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. For safety-critical material, mnemonics should support verified knowledge, not replace official checklists, protocols, or instructor guidance.
