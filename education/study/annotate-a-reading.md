## Goal

You mark and comment on a reading so you can understand it, discuss it, and return later to the most important ideas quickly.

## Preconditions

- The assigned article, chapter, handout, or primary source.
- A pencil, highlighter, sticky notes, PDF tool, or annotation app.
- Any discussion questions, rubric, or learning objectives.

## Steps

1. **Preview before marking.** Read the title, abstract or introduction, headings, conclusion, figures, and author information. → *Expect:* you have a first guess about purpose and structure.
2. **Set an annotation purpose.** Decide whether you are reading for argument, evidence, vocabulary, methods, literary devices, or exam content. → *Expect:* your marks have a focus.
3. **Mark claims and structure.** Highlight or underline thesis statements, section turns, definitions, and conclusions sparingly. → *Expect:* only the most important text is marked.
4. **Write margin comments.** Paraphrase key points, ask questions, note confusion, connect to class, or challenge evidence. → *Expect:* the margins show your thinking, not just colored lines.
5. **Track unfamiliar terms and references.** Circle terms, names, methods, or allusions that need lookup. → *Expect:* unknowns are collected for follow-up.
6. **Summarize each major section.** Write one short note after each section explaining what it did. → *Expect:* you can see the reading's progression.
7. **Finish with a whole-reading takeaway.** Write the main claim, strongest evidence, and one question or critique. → *Expect:* you are ready for discussion or review.

## Decision points

- You are highlighting more than 20-25% of the page → switch to margin notes and underline only claims.
- The reading is for a seminar → add discussion questions and passages worth quoting.
- The reading is for a test → turn annotations into recall prompts after finishing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Over-highlighting:** detect whole paragraphs marked → erase, reduce, or add margin labels showing why they matter.
- **F2 No comprehension check:** detect marks but no summary → write a one-sentence section takeaway.
- **F3 Ignored confusion:** detect repeated question marks with no follow-up → look up terms or ask the instructor.
- **F4 Lost page evidence:** detect useful ideas without page numbers → add page or paragraph markers for citation.

## Verification

The reading has selective marks, margin comments, section summaries, a final takeaway, and a list of questions or terms to resolve.

## Variations

- `PDF`: use a small set of colors consistently, such as claims, evidence, confusion, and vocabulary.
- `primary source`: note author, audience, context, purpose, and bias.
- `literature`: annotate patterns in imagery, diction, structure, character change, and theme.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not share annotated files containing classmates' comments, private instructor notes, or restricted readings outside permitted course systems.
