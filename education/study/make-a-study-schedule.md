## Goal

You build a realistic study calendar that covers every required topic before the due date or exam, with review time and buffers built in.

## Preconditions

- A syllabus, assignment sheet, exam guide, or list of topics to learn.
- Your fixed commitments for the study period, including classes, work, sleep, travel, and family responsibilities.
- A calendar, planner, spreadsheet, or task app you will actually check.

## Steps

1. **Define the target date and outcome.** Write the exam date, submission deadline, or performance goal at the top of the plan. → *Expect:* a clear end date and a measurable goal such as "score 85% on practice exam" or "submit final draft."
2. **List all topics and deliverables.** Break the course or project into chapters, lecture units, problem types, readings, essays, labs, or practice exams. → *Expect:* one complete backlog of study tasks.
3. **Estimate effort honestly.** Assign each item a rough size: small, medium, or large, then convert that into time blocks based on past experience. → *Expect:* a total workload estimate that is larger than your first guess but still schedulable.
4. **Block fixed commitments first.** Put classes, work, meals, sleep, appointments, and commuting into the calendar before study sessions. → *Expect:* only genuinely available time remains open.
5. **Schedule focused study blocks.** Place the hardest topics in high-energy times and limit most blocks to 25-90 minutes with short breaks. → *Expect:* each study block has a specific task, not just "study."
6. **Build in spaced review.** Schedule quick reviews one day, several days, and about one week after first learning important material. → *Expect:* older material reappears before it is forgotten.
7. **Add buffer time and catch-up slots.** Reserve at least 10-20% of the available study time for delays, weak topics, or missed sessions. → *Expect:* the plan can survive one bad day without collapsing.
8. **Check the plan daily and revise weekly.** Mark done items, move unfinished work deliberately, and shorten or split oversized tasks. → *Expect:* the schedule stays current instead of becoming a guilt list.

## Decision points

- Less than three days remain → prioritize exam-weighted topics and active practice over rereading everything.
- The workload exceeds available hours → cut low-value tasks, ask the instructor what to prioritize, or adjust the goal.
- You keep missing the same time slot → move it to a more reliable part of the day.
- A topic is repeatedly weak → replace passive review with problems, self-testing, tutoring, or office hours.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overpacked calendar:** detect back-to-back study blocks with no meals, sleep, or travel time → rebuild the plan around fixed commitments and shorter blocks.
- **F2 Vague tasks:** detect entries like "chemistry" or "read" → rewrite them as concrete actions such as "complete 15 equilibrium problems."
- **F3 No review time:** detect all sessions assigned to new content → convert some sessions to retrieval practice and cumulative review.
- **F4 Falling behind silently:** detect several overdue items with no changes → reschedule them, reduce scope, and tell a teacher or study partner if a deadline is at risk.

## Verification

Every required topic or deliverable appears on the calendar, each study block has a concrete task, at least one buffer slot exists before the deadline, and today's next study action is obvious.

## Variations

- `exam-heavy course`: schedule practice questions and past papers before rereading notes.
- `writing-heavy course`: reserve separate blocks for research, outlining, drafting, revising, citations, and proofreading.
- `mobile-app`: use recurring reminders, but review the full weekly view so small tasks do not hide the real workload.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect grades, student ID numbers, accommodation letters, and private calendar details if sharing the schedule with a tutor, classmate, or app.
