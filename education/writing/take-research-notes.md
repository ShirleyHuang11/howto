## Goal

You take research notes that preserve source details, separate your ideas from borrowed material, and make drafting easier.

## Preconditions

- A research question or assignment prompt.
- Sources you are allowed to use.
- A notebook, document, spreadsheet, citation manager, or notes app.

## Steps

1. **Create a note template.** Include citation, page or location, summary, useful quotations, keywords, and your response. → *Expect:* every note captures the same essential fields.
2. **Record source details first.** Save author, title, date, container, DOI or URL, and access information before reading deeply. → *Expect:* you can cite the source later.
3. **Write a short source summary.** State the source's main claim, method, and relevance in your own words. → *Expect:* you know why the source matters.
4. **Label quotations clearly.** Put exact words in quotation marks and record page numbers. → *Expect:* copied language cannot be mistaken for your own.
5. **Separate paraphrase from response.** Use headings or columns for source idea and your analysis. → *Expect:* your argument stays distinct from the author's.
6. **Tag notes by theme.** Add labels for concepts, methods, evidence types, or essay sections. → *Expect:* related sources can be grouped quickly.
7. **Capture questions and gaps.** Note what the source does not answer or what you need to verify. → *Expect:* future research tasks are visible.
8. **Review notes before drafting.** Sort by theme and choose the most relevant evidence. → *Expect:* drafting starts from organized material rather than a pile of highlights.

## Decision points

- The source is central to your argument → take detailed notes on methods, limitations, and key passages.
- The source is background only → keep notes brief and focus on citation and main idea.
- You are reading many studies → use a spreadsheet or matrix so fields are comparable.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Highlighting without notes:** detect colored passages but no summary → write a one-sentence takeaway after each section.
- **F2 Missing page numbers:** detect quotes or facts without locations → reopen the source and add pages before drafting.
- **F3 Notes mix your words and source words:** detect uncertain authorship → compare to the original and mark quotations clearly.
- **F4 Too many irrelevant notes:** detect notes that do not answer the prompt → archive them and refocus on the research question.

## Verification

Each source note includes citation details, page or location references where needed, a summary in your own words, and clear labels distinguishing quotation, paraphrase, and your analysis.

## Variations

- `source-matrix`: columns can include citation, question, method, evidence, findings, limitations, and relevance.
- `literature-review`: tag by theme and methodology so synthesis is easier.
- `apa-example`: In notes, keep enough information to produce: Lee, H. (2020). Reading strategies in biology courses. Science Education Review, 14(2), 77-92.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not store sensitive interview data, participant identifiers, or private classroom materials in unsecured notes apps; follow any research protocol or school policy.
