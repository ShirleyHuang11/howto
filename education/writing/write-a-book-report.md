## Goal

You write a clear book report that proves you read the book, summarizes it accurately, and responds to the assignment prompt.

## Preconditions

- The book, assignment prompt, and rubric.
- Notes on plot, characters, setting, themes, and important passages.
- Citation requirements if quotations are allowed or required.

## Steps

1. **Read the prompt before drafting.** Identify whether the teacher wants summary, analysis, personal response, character study, or theme discussion. → *Expect:* your report is aimed at the actual task.
2. **Record the book facts.** Note title, author, publication date if needed, genre, setting, and main characters. → *Expect:* the introduction can identify the book accurately.
3. **Write a brief summary.** Cover the main conflict, important events, and resolution without retelling every chapter. → *Expect:* a reader understands the book's basic arc.
4. **Choose a focus for analysis.** Pick one or two themes, character changes, symbols, conflicts, or author choices required by the prompt. → *Expect:* the report goes beyond plot summary.
5. **Select evidence from the text.** Use short quotations or specific scenes, with page numbers when available. → *Expect:* each claim can be tied to the book.
6. **Draft in a simple structure.** Use introduction, summary, analysis paragraphs, and conclusion unless the teacher gives another format. → *Expect:* the report has a beginning, middle, and end.
7. **Explain quotations instead of dropping them in.** After each quotation or scene reference, state what it shows and why it matters. → *Expect:* evidence supports your point instead of replacing your thinking.
8. **Revise for accuracy and clarity.** Check character names, sequence of events, spelling, grammar, and whether spoilers are acceptable for the assignment. → *Expect:* the report is readable and factually correct.

## Decision points

- Teacher asks for "response" → include your reasoned opinion, but still support it with text evidence.
- Teacher asks for "analysis" → reduce plot summary and focus on meaning, choices, and evidence.
- You did not finish the book → finish reading or be transparent with the teacher; do not fake details.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much plot summary:** detect paragraphs that only retell events → add claims about character, theme, or author's choices.
- **F2 Unsupported opinion:** detect "I liked it" without reasons → connect the opinion to a scene, theme, or writing choice.
- **F3 Incorrect details:** detect wrong names, order, or ending → verify against the book before submitting.
- **F4 Overlong quotations:** detect copied blocks of text → trim to short excerpts and explain them in your own words.

## Verification

The final report identifies the book, summarizes the main events accurately, answers the prompt, uses specific textual evidence, and follows the required length and format.

## Variations

- `mla-9`: cite quotations with page numbers when available. EXAMPLE: The narrator describes the house as "silent" (Lee 42).
- `elementary-school`: the report may use a template with boxes for characters, setting, problem, solution, and favorite part.
- `advanced-literature`: replace broad summary with a thesis-driven literary analysis.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not copy summaries from study sites, and avoid uploading a teacher's private prompt or class-only materials into public tools without permission.
