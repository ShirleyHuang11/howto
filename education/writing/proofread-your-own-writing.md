## Goal

You catch grammar, formatting, citation, and clarity problems in your own draft before submitting it.

## Preconditions

- A complete draft, not just notes.
- The assignment prompt and rubric.
- A way to mark changes, such as comments, track changes, or a printed copy.

## Steps

1. **Take a short break before proofreading.** Step away long enough to stop reading from memory. → *Expect:* you notice actual words on the page more easily.
2. **Check the assignment requirements first.** Compare the draft to the prompt, length, citation style, file type, and required sources. → *Expect:* major compliance problems are found before sentence-level edits.
3. **Read for structure.** Confirm the introduction, thesis, paragraphs, transitions, and conclusion appear in a logical order. → *Expect:* each paragraph has a clear job.
4. **Read aloud or use text-to-speech.** Listen for missing words, repeated phrases, awkward sentences, and abrupt transitions. → *Expect:* errors that looked invisible on screen become noticeable.
5. **Proofread one error type at a time.** Check punctuation, verb tense, agreement, spelling, capitalization, and citations in separate passes. → *Expect:* fewer errors slip through than in one general pass.
6. **Verify every citation and quotation.** Match in-text citations to the reference list and check quotation marks, page numbers, and paraphrases. → *Expect:* every borrowed idea is credited and traceable.
7. **Use automated tools cautiously.** Run spellcheck or grammar suggestions, accepting only changes that preserve meaning and meet academic style. → *Expect:* useful corrections are applied without introducing wrong wording.
8. **Format the final file.** Check margins, headings, page numbers, font, spacing, filename, and required file format. → *Expect:* the document looks ready to submit.
9. **Do a final reverse or slow read.** Read sentence by sentence from the end or line by line to catch typos. → *Expect:* remaining surface errors are found before upload.

## Decision points

- Deadline is close → prioritize prompt compliance, thesis, citations, and obvious grammar over style polishing.
- Automated tool rewrites your voice → reject the suggestion and make a smaller manual edit.
- You find a missing source → add it properly or remove unsupported material.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Editing while drafting:** detect major rewrites during proofreading → save a copy, make structural changes, then restart the proofread.
- **F2 Citation mismatch:** detect an in-text citation with no reference entry → add the full reference or remove the citation.
- **F3 Spellcheck false correction:** detect a technical term, name, or quotation changed incorrectly → restore the original and add the term to the dictionary if useful.
- **F4 Formatting shifts after export:** detect page breaks or spacing changes in PDF → reopen the exported file and fix the source document before re-exporting.

## Verification

The draft satisfies the prompt, citations and references match, formatting meets the assignment requirements, and a final slow read finds no obvious errors.

## Variations

- `apa-7`: verify title page, running head if required, headings, DOI format, and reference hanging indents.
- `mla-9`: verify header, heading block, double spacing, Works Cited title, and author-page citations.
- `example-check`: "Its" shows possession; "it's" means "it is" or "it has."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Be careful with private drafts, unpublished research, peer comments, and instructor feedback when using cloud-based proofreading tools.
