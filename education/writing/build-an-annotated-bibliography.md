## Goal

You produce a source list with accurate citations and short annotations that summarize, evaluate, and explain how each source supports your research question.

## Preconditions

- A research question or working topic.
- Assignment requirements for citation style, number of sources, source types, and annotation length.
- Access to library databases, search engines, or course materials.

## Steps

1. **Copy the assignment requirements into a checklist.** Include citation style, minimum scholarly sources, due date, word count per annotation, and whether annotations must be descriptive, evaluative, or reflective. → *Expect:* a checklist that defines what a finished bibliography must contain.
2. **Turn the topic into search terms.** Write 3-5 keyword groups, including synonyms and narrower terms. → *Expect:* searches that return focused results instead of broad background pages.
3. **Collect candidate sources from credible places.** Use library databases, books, government or institutional reports, and relevant peer-reviewed articles before general web pages. → *Expect:* more candidates than the assignment requires.
4. **Screen each source for relevance and credibility.** Check author, publication venue, date, evidence, methods, and whether it directly informs the research question. → *Expect:* weak, outdated, or off-topic sources are removed.
5. **Read strategically and take source notes.** For each source, record the thesis, evidence, method, useful findings, limitations, and one quotation or page reference if permitted. → *Expect:* notes that let you write without rereading the whole source.
6. **Create the citation first.** Use the required style guide or library citation tool, then manually check capitalization, italics, dates, DOI/URL, and hanging indent rules. → *Expect:* a citation that matches the required style rather than an unverified auto-generated entry.
7. **Write the annotation in three moves.** Summarize the source, evaluate its quality or limitations, and explain its use for your project. → *Expect:* each annotation answers "what is it, can I trust it, and why does it matter?"
8. **Arrange and revise the bibliography.** Sort entries alphabetically unless the assignment says otherwise, remove repetition, and make the tone concise and academic. → *Expect:* a clean list with consistent annotation length and style.

## Decision points

- Instructor requires only summaries → omit personal reflection and focus on thesis, method, and findings.
- Sources disagree → keep both if the disagreement is relevant, and name the conflict in the annotations.
- Source is useful but not credible enough → use it for background only, not as a cited source unless allowed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Citation generator error:** detect missing italics, wrong capitalization, or incomplete DOI → compare against the style guide and repair manually.
- **F2 Annotation becomes a review essay:** detect paragraphs that discuss your whole topic instead of the source → rewrite around that source's argument, evidence, and usefulness.
- **F3 Too many similar sources:** detect repeated studies making the same point → keep the strongest or most recent and replace others with sources covering gaps.
- **F4 Source cannot be relocated:** detect a citation with no stable title, author, database record, DOI, or URL → replace it or record enough retrieval information to verify it.

## Verification

Every required source has a complete citation in the assigned style, an annotation that summarizes, evaluates, and connects the source to the research question, and the final list meets the assignment's source-count and formatting requirements.

## Variations

- `apa-7`: use author-date citations and sentence-case article titles. EXAMPLE: Smith, J. A. (2024). Student note-taking habits in online courses. *Journal of Learning Research, 18*(2), 101-119. https://doi.org/10.1234/jlr.2024.18.2.101
- `mla-9`: use Works Cited formatting and include containers such as journal title and database when required.
- `chicago-notes-bibliography`: bibliography entries often use full names, headline-style titles, and publication facts.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not upload copyrighted PDFs or private class materials to public tools, and do not fabricate citations, page numbers, or source claims.
