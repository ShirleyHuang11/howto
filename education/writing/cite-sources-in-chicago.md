## Goal

You cite sources in Chicago style using either notes-bibliography or author-date format, according to the assignment or publication requirement.

## Preconditions

- The required Chicago system: notes-bibliography or author-date.
- Full source metadata and page numbers for quotations or specific claims.
- Access to the Chicago Manual of Style guidance or a trusted library guide.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the Chicago system.** Ask whether the paper needs footnotes/endnotes plus bibliography or author-date citations plus reference list. → *Expect:* you do not mix the two systems accidentally.
2. **Identify the source type.** Classify each source as book, chapter, journal article, webpage, archival item, image, interview, or other format. → *Expect:* you select the correct model.
3. **Collect complete metadata.** Record author, title, publication facts, date, page range, DOI or stable URL, and access date if required. → *Expect:* every citation can be verified.
4. **Create the first note or in-text citation.** For notes-bibliography, include full details in the first note; for author-date, use author, year, and page. → *Expect:* the first citation matches the required system.
5. **Use shortened notes after the first full note.** Include author, shortened title, and page number. → *Expect:* repeated citations are concise but traceable.
6. **Build the bibliography or reference list.** Format entries alphabetically with Chicago punctuation and capitalization. → *Expect:* readers can locate full source details.
7. **Check page-specific claims.** Add page numbers for quotations, close paraphrases, and specific facts. → *Expect:* readers can verify exact evidence.
8. **Proofread for consistency.** Confirm italics, quotation marks, commas, dates, and note numbering. → *Expect:* the final citations use one Chicago system consistently.

## Decision points

- The paper is in history, art history, or many humanities fields → notes-bibliography is often expected.
- The paper is in social sciences or sciences using Chicago → author-date may be required.
- You use archival or primary sources → follow the archive or instructor's preferred citation pattern.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mixed systems:** detect footnotes plus author-date parentheticals → choose the assigned system and revise all citations.
- **F2 Missing page numbers:** detect quotes without specific pages → return to the source and add exact pages.
- **F3 Bibliography copied from notes:** detect note punctuation and author order in bibliography → reformat bibliography entries separately.
- **F4 Unstable web citation:** detect a homepage URL instead of the exact page → use a DOI, permalink, or full page URL.

## Verification

The paper uses one Chicago system consistently, all notes or in-text citations match the bibliography or reference list, and exact quotations include page or location information.

## Variations

- `notes-bibliography-example`: Morrison, Toni. Beloved. New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 1987.
- `first-note-example`: 1. Toni Morrison, Beloved (New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 1987), 42.
- `author-date-example`: Morrison, Toni. 1987. Beloved. New York: Alfred A. Knopf.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not invent archival details, page numbers, or publication facts; confirm sensitive interview citations with the person or protocol governing the research.
