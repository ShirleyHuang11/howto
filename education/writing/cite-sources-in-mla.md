## Goal

You cite sources in MLA style with clear parenthetical citations and a Works Cited page that lets readers locate the original sources.

## Preconditions

- Source metadata: author, title, container, publisher, date, location, URL, DOI, or page range.
- The required MLA version, usually MLA 9th edition.
- The exact passages, ideas, images, or data you used.

## Steps

1. **Identify the source and container.** Determine whether the work appears in a journal, book, website, database, film, anthology, or other container. → *Expect:* you know which MLA elements are needed.
2. **Collect MLA core elements.** Record author, title of source, title of container, contributors, version, number, publisher, date, and location. → *Expect:* the citation can be built from complete metadata.
3. **Create the Works Cited entry.** Put elements in MLA order with correct punctuation, italics, and quotation marks. → *Expect:* the entry follows the MLA template.
4. **Add in-text citations.** Use author's last name and page number when available, with no comma between them. → *Expect:* readers can connect each claim to the Works Cited entry.
5. **Handle sources without pages.** Cite the author or shortened title without inventing page numbers. → *Expect:* web and media citations are accurate.
6. **Alphabetize the Works Cited page.** Sort by the first element of each entry. → *Expect:* readers can find entries quickly.
7. **Apply formatting.** Use double spacing and hanging indents if required by the assignment. → *Expect:* the page follows MLA presentation standards.
8. **Check every quotation and paraphrase.** Confirm wording, page numbers, and source titles against the original. → *Expect:* the paper does not contain unsupported borrowed material.

## Decision points

- The source has two containers, such as an article in a journal in a database → include both if the assignment expects database information.
- The source has no author → start with the title and cite a shortened title in-text.
- You cite poetry, drama, or scripture → use the discipline-specific line, act, scene, or book format requested by the instructor.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Works Cited entry lacks container:** detect only an article title without journal or website → add the larger container.
- **F2 Parenthetical citation has a comma:** detect `(Smith, 24)` → change to MLA form `(Smith 24)`.
- **F3 URL pasted with tracking junk:** detect long links with session parameters → use a stable DOI or clean permalink.
- **F4 Quotation page number is wrong:** detect mismatch with the source → correct the page or remove the quote.

## Verification

The Works Cited page is alphabetized, every in-text citation matches an entry, and quoted or paraphrased material includes accurate MLA location information where available.

## Variations

- `article-example`: Lopez, Maria. "Public Memory and Local Archives." Journal of Cultural History, vol. 15, no. 2, 2024, pp. 88-104.
- `webpage-example`: World Health Organization. "Physical Activity." World Health Organization, 26 June 2024, www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity.
- `no-page`: use `(Lopez)` or a shortened title if no author exists; do not make up paragraph numbers unless the source numbers them.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not cite sources you did not inspect, fabricate page numbers, or upload copyrighted readings to public citation tools.
