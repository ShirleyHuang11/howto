## Goal

You submit work that clearly separates your own ideas from borrowed words, facts, data, images, and arguments.

## Preconditions

- The assignment instructions and academic integrity policy.
- Notes showing which ideas came from which sources.
- The required citation style.

## Steps

1. **Read the integrity rules before drafting.** Check what collaboration, AI assistance, reuse of prior work, and citation practices are allowed. → *Expect:* you know what the course permits.
2. **Track sources while researching.** Record full citation details and page numbers as soon as you take notes. → *Expect:* no note is detached from its source.
3. **Mark exact words as quotations.** Put copied language in quotation marks in your notes immediately. → *Expect:* you can distinguish copied wording from your own paraphrase later.
4. **Paraphrase from understanding.** Close the source, explain the idea in your own structure, then compare for accuracy. → *Expect:* the wording and sentence pattern are genuinely different while the meaning remains correct.
5. **Cite every borrowed idea.** Add citations for quotations, paraphrases, statistics, images, tables, and distinctive claims. → *Expect:* readers can trace all outside material.
6. **Use quotations sparingly and visibly.** Quote only language that needs the original wording and introduce it with context. → *Expect:* quoted material supports your analysis instead of replacing it.
7. **Avoid unauthorized collaboration.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before submitting, confirm that peer help, tutoring, AI tools, translation tools, or shared notes are allowed and acknowledged as required. → *Expect:* your submitted work follows the policy.
8. **Run a final source audit.** Compare the draft against notes and sources for missing citations or too-close paraphrases. → *Expect:* every borrowed element is credited.

## Decision points

- You are unsure whether something is common knowledge → cite it or ask the instructor.
- You reused your own previous work → disclose or cite it if the course requires permission for self-reuse.
- You used AI or a grammar tool → follow the class policy for disclosure and permitted use.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Lost source information:** detect a note without author or page → search your history or source list before using it.
- **F2 Patchwriting:** detect sentence structure that mirrors the source with swapped words → rewrite from memory and cite the idea.
- **F3 Missing image or table credit:** detect visuals without captions or source notes → add attribution and permission information.
- **F4 Accidental collaboration violation:** detect shared wording with a classmate → tell the instructor promptly and clarify what happened before submission if possible.

## Verification

The final draft has citations for every borrowed idea or expression, quotation marks around exact language, and no unapproved collaboration or tool use under the assignment rules.

## Variations

- `apa-example`: Paraphrase citation: Sleep affects memory consolidation during learning (Walker, 2017).
- `mla-example`: Paraphrase citation: Sleep affects memory consolidation during learning (Walker 53).
- `group-project`: agree in writing which sections each person owns and how shared sources will be cited.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because plagiarism can lead to academic penalties. Confirm the rules before submitting, keep research notes with timestamps, and do not share drafts publicly before grades are final.
