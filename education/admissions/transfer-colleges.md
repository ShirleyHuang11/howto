## Goal

You apply to transfer colleges with a realistic credit, cost, and graduation plan instead of discovering after admission that credits or aid do not work.

## Preconditions

- You have completed or in-progress college coursework.
- You can access unofficial transcripts, syllabi, course descriptions, and current financial-aid information.
- You know why you want to transfer and what the new institution must provide.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the transfer reason.** Identify academic fit, cost, location, support needs, major access, or personal constraints. → *Expect:* your school list reflects the real reason for moving.
2. **Research transfer policies.** Check minimum credits, GPA, prerequisites, deadlines, housing, aid, and whether high-school records are required. → *Expect:* each target school has a transfer-requirement checklist.
3. **Estimate credit transfer.** Use transfer equivalency tools and save syllabi for courses that may need review. → *Expect:* you know likely accepted credits and uncertain courses.
4. **Plan major sequencing.** Compare how transferred credits fit the new degree, not just total credit count. → *Expect:* you can estimate remaining semesters.
5. **Prepare application materials.** Order official transcripts, write transfer essays, request recommendations if required, and gather disciplinary or good-standing forms. → *Expect:* every checklist item has an owner and due date.
6. **Submit applications.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* application fees and submitted forms are often final, so confirm term, campus, major, and document uploads before paying. → *Expect:* each portal shows submitted status.
7. **Compare admission, credit, and aid offers.** Review the official transfer-credit evaluation, net cost, housing, and graduation timeline before accepting. → *Expect:* you know which offer is actually viable.
8. **Complete departure and arrival steps.** Accept one offer, decline others, notify your current school if leaving, send final transcript, attend orientation, and register. → *Expect:* records, aid, and enrollment move cleanly.

## Decision points

- Credits transfer as electives only -> decide whether the lost time or cost is acceptable.
- Aid is worse after transfer -> ask for financial-aid review before accepting.
- You are close to finishing current degree -> compare completing first with transferring now.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Credit shock:** detect fewer accepted credits than expected -> appeal with syllabi or choose a school with better articulation.
- **F2 Missed good-standing form:** detect incomplete checklist -> ask your current dean or registrar to submit it quickly.
- **F3 Housing unavailable:** detect no campus housing for transfers -> secure off-campus housing before accepting if relocation is required.
- **F4 Aid gap:** detect unaffordable net cost -> appeal, reduce course plan risk, or decline before deposit deadlines.

## Verification

The transfer destination shows admitted and accepted status, the official credit evaluation is reviewed, final transcripts are requested, and your enrollment plan is financially and academically feasible.

## Variations

- `us`: community-college articulation agreements and guaranteed-transfer pathways can simplify credit transfer, but only if course and GPA rules are met.
- International transfer: credential evaluation, visa timing, and course syllabi may be required earlier.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because transfer decisions affect money, credits, housing, immigration, and graduation timing. Confirm official credit and aid evaluations before paying deposits or withdrawing from your current college.
