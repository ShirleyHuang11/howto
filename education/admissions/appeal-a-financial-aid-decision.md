## Goal

You ask a financial aid office to reconsider an aid decision using clear documentation of changed finances, special circumstances, or competing offers.

## Preconditions

- The current aid offer and cost of attendance.
- Documentation of changed income, medical expenses, unemployment, family change, disaster, unusual dependency circumstance, or competing offer if relevant.
- The college's appeal or special-circumstance process.

## Steps

1. **Identify the reason for appeal.** Decide whether the issue is changed financial circumstances, unusual circumstances, merit reconsideration, or a competing offer. → *Expect:* the appeal has a recognized basis.
2. **Read the college's appeal instructions.** Find required forms, document types, deadline, and submission channel. → *Expect:* you know exactly what the aid office will accept.
3. **Calculate the affordability gap.** Compare cost of attendance, gift aid, loans, family resources, and realistic work income. → *Expect:* you can state the amount needed or the problem clearly.
4. **Gather documentation.** Use tax records, termination letters, medical bills, court documents, benefit statements, or official competing aid offers as appropriate. → *Expect:* claims are backed by records.
5. **Write a concise appeal letter.** Explain the situation, attach documents, state the requested reconsideration, and remain factual and respectful. → *Expect:* the aid officer can understand the request quickly.
6. **Submit through the official process.** Upload or email only through the college's stated channel. → *Expect:* you receive confirmation or a case record.
7. **Respond to follow-up requests promptly.** Provide missing documents or clarification without sending unrelated private material. → *Expect:* the appeal remains active and reviewable.
8. **Review the revised offer.** Compare grants, scholarships, loans, work-study, and renewal terms before deciding. → *Expect:* you know whether the appeal solved the affordability gap.

## Decision points

- Circumstance involves family safety or inability to obtain parent information → ask the aid office about unusual circumstance or dependency override procedures.
- Appeal is denied → ask whether there are payment plans, emergency grants, departmental scholarships, or a later review.
- Revised offer adds loans only → decide whether the debt is acceptable; loans are not the same as grant aid.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unsupported claim:** detect an appeal based only on preference or hardship without documents → gather acceptable evidence or rewrite as a clarification request.
- **F2 Missed appeal channel:** detect email sent when portal upload was required → resubmit through the official process.
- **F3 Too much private detail:** detect unnecessary medical or family narrative → provide concise facts and required documents only.
- **F4 Revised aid is temporary:** detect one-year grant or nonrenewable adjustment → ask whether it applies in future years.

## Verification

The aid office confirms receipt, reviews the appeal, and issues either a written decision or a revised aid offer that you compare against your affordability gap.

## Variations

- `us`: financial aid offices may use professional judgment for special circumstances; FAFSA data alone may not show recent changes.
- `merit-appeal`: some colleges do not negotiate merit aid; ask about scholarship review policy before sending competing offers.
- `international-student`: aid appeal rules may be separate from FAFSA-based processes.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because appeals use tax, medical, legal, and family information. Submit only necessary documents through official college channels and confirm any new loans or enrollment commitments before accepting.
