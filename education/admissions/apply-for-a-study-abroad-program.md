## Goal

You apply for a study-abroad program that fits your degree, budget, health needs, travel documents, and safety requirements.

## Preconditions

- You know your academic term, destination preferences, and graduation requirements.
- You can access your school's study-abroad office or approved provider list.
- You have or can obtain a passport, funding plan, and advisor approval.

## Steps

1. **Define academic and personal constraints.** Identify required credits, language needs, budget, accessibility, health care, and travel timing. → *Expect:* unsuitable programs can be ruled out early.
2. **Choose an approved program.** Compare exchange, faculty-led, provider, internship, and direct-enroll options through your institution. → *Expect:* the program is eligible for credit transfer and school approval.
3. **Confirm credit fit.** Meet the academic advisor or department to pre-approve courses and substitutions. → *Expect:* credits will apply to major, general education, or electives as planned.
4. **Build the real budget.** Include tuition model, program fee, airfare, housing, meals, passport, visa, insurance, local transport, and emergency funds. → *Expect:* you know the net cost after aid and scholarships.
5. **Check passport, visa, and health requirements.** Review passport validity, visa timeline, vaccines, prescriptions, and insurance coverage. → *Expect:* no document or health requirement is impossible by departure.
6. **Prepare application materials.** Complete essays, recommendations, transcript, conduct clearance, course plan, and emergency contacts. → *Expect:* every required item is ready before the deadline.
7. **Submit through the official portal.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* deposits and provider contracts may become nonrefundable, so confirm program, dates, cost, and withdrawal rules before paying. → *Expect:* the portal shows submitted and any deposit status.
8. **Complete post-acceptance tasks.** Attend orientation, register travel, finalize courses, book travel only when advised, and upload required documents. → *Expect:* you are cleared for departure by the study-abroad office.

## Decision points

- Courses do not satisfy requirements -> choose another program or treat the term as elective credit.
- Visa timeline is too short -> defer to a later term or select a destination with feasible processing.
- Health or accessibility support is uncertain -> get written confirmation before committing money.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Credit not approved:** detect advisor refusal or missing equivalency -> submit syllabi for review or switch courses/programs.
- **F2 Passport delay:** detect processing beyond visa or departure deadline -> request expedited service where available and alert the program.
- **F3 Cost surprise:** detect fees not covered by aid -> ask about scholarships, payment plans, or lower-cost programs before deposit deadlines.
- **F4 Conduct or GPA block:** detect ineligibility after starting application -> ask whether appeal, later term, or alternate program is possible.

## Verification

The study-abroad portal shows accepted or submitted status, course approvals are documented, costs are understood, and passport/visa/health tasks are on schedule.

## Variations

- Faculty-led short term: fewer visa and course-transfer issues, but deposits and travel schedules may be stricter.
- Exchange program: tuition may stay at the home institution, while housing, visa, and course registration happen through the host.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from travel, health, money, and identity documents. Use official program channels, confirm refund rules before deposits, keep passport copies secure, and follow institutional travel advisories and emergency-contact requirements.
