## Goal

You build a college application list with realistic admissions odds, affordable options, and schools you would actually attend.

## Preconditions

- A researched college shortlist.
- Your transcript, course rigor, test scores if submitting, activities, budget, and constraints.
- Application limits from time, money, counselor capacity, and recommendation availability.

## Steps

1. **Define what "balanced" means for you.** Include academic fit, selectivity, affordability, location, support needs, and willingness to attend. → *Expect:* balance includes cost and fit, not just acceptance rates.
2. **Estimate admissions category for each school.** Compare your profile to published middle ranges, admit rates, major selectivity, residency rules, and portfolio or audition requirements. → *Expect:* each school is labeled likely, target, or reach with a reason.
3. **Verify affordability category for each school.** Use net price calculators, merit scholarship rules, in-state options, and family contribution limits. → *Expect:* each school is labeled affordable, uncertain, or unaffordable.
4. **Anchor the list with true likely schools.** Include schools where admission is probable, cost is plausible, and you would be willing to enroll. → *Expect:* the list has real safety options.
5. **Add target schools that fit strongly.** Choose schools where your profile is competitive and the academic or personal fit is meaningful. → *Expect:* the middle of the list is not filler.
6. **Limit reaches intentionally.** Keep reach schools only if you can write strong applications and afford the possibility of limited aid. → *Expect:* reach applications do not crowd out better-fit schools.
7. **Check deadline and workload conflicts.** Count essays, recommendations, tests, portfolios, interviews, and scholarship forms. → *Expect:* the list is possible to complete well.
8. **Review the final mix with a counselor or trusted advisor.** Ask specifically about selectivity, cost, and missing local or public options. → *Expect:* obvious blind spots are corrected before application work begins.

## Decision points

- No affordable likely school → add in-state publics, community college transfer paths, or guaranteed-admission options before adding reaches.
- Applying to a capped or direct-admit major → treat admissions odds as harder than the overall university rate.
- Recommendation capacity is limited → reduce the list or prioritize schools using common letters.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 List is all reaches:** detect most schools have very low admit rates or profiles above yours → add likely and target schools you would attend.
- **F2 Likely school is unaffordable:** detect expected net price above family limit → it is not a real safety; replace or confirm aid.
- **F3 Too many essays:** detect more supplemental work than the calendar allows → cut lower-fit schools first.
- **F4 Prestige bias:** detect schools chosen mainly by ranking → revisit fit criteria and outcomes for your major.

## Verification

The final list includes likely, target, and reach schools, at least one affordable likely option, verified deadlines, and a workload you can complete before due dates.

## Variations

- `us`: many students use a mix such as 2-3 likely, 3-5 target, and 2-4 reach schools, adjusted for budget and selectivity.
- `test-optional`: estimate both with and without scores, using the school's latest testing policy.
- `performing-arts`: auditions and portfolios can make selectivity very different from general admission.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid sharing full transcripts, test score reports, Social Security numbers, or family financial documents with unofficial advisors or websites.
