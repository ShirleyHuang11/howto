## Goal

You choose a major that fits your interests, abilities, graduation timeline, finances, and likely next steps after college.

## Preconditions

- You know your school's major declaration deadlines and requirements.
- You can access the catalog, degree audit, advising, and career resources.
- You have at least a rough sense of interests, strengths, and constraints.

## Steps

1. **List your decision criteria.** Include subjects you like, skills you want, careers, graduate-school prerequisites, workload, cost, and time to degree. → *Expect:* a personal rubric rather than a popularity contest.
2. **Read catalog requirements.** Compare prerequisites, required sequences, GPA gates, portfolios, auditions, labs, internships, and capstones. → *Expect:* you know what each candidate major really requires.
3. **Test fit with coursework.** Review syllabi, take an introductory course, or audit lectures when allowed. → *Expect:* you have evidence from actual course content.
4. **Check graduation impact.** Run degree audits or sample plans for each major, including double majors or minors. → *Expect:* you know whether the choice delays graduation or adds cost.
5. **Talk to advisors and students.** Ask about bottleneck courses, faculty support, research, internships, and common pitfalls. → *Expect:* you have practical information beyond brochures.
6. **Connect majors to outcomes.** Use career services and alumni data to see jobs, graduate programs, portfolios, certifications, and skills. → *Expect:* you understand possible paths without assuming one automatic career.
7. **Choose and declare through the official process.** Submit the form, attend required advising, or request admission to restricted majors. → *Expect:* the student record shows the intended major or pending review.
8. **Build the next-term plan.** Register for courses that keep the path open and revisit the choice after key gateway courses. → *Expect:* your schedule supports the declared or exploratory plan.

## Decision points

- Restricted major has a GPA or portfolio gate -> keep a parallel backup major until admitted.
- Major extends graduation significantly -> compare extra cost against career or graduate-school benefits.
- Family pressure conflicts with your evidence -> bring the degree audit and outcome data into the conversation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Choosing from stereotypes:** detect assumptions about easy, lucrative, or prestigious majors -> verify with actual course plans and outcome data.
- **F2 Missing prerequisites:** detect a sequence that starts only once per year -> adjust immediately with an advisor.
- **F3 Ignoring workload fit:** detect repeated poor performance in gateway classes -> consider tutoring, different pacing, or an adjacent major.
- **F4 Late declaration:** detect registration blocks or delayed graduation -> ask advising about exception, summer, or transfer-credit options.

## Verification

Your official student record or advising plan reflects the chosen major, and your next courses satisfy real requirements without unnecessary delay.

## Variations

- Liberal arts colleges: major choice may be flexible but still affects thesis, advising, and course sequencing.
- Professional majors: nursing, engineering, business, education, and arts programs may require competitive admission or accreditation-specific courses.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but choices can affect cost and time to degree. Do not rely only on salary rankings; confirm requirements, debt implications, and licensure or graduate-school prerequisites before committing.
