## Goal

You ask the right recommender early and give them enough information to write a specific, timely academic recommendation.

## Preconditions

- A list of applications requiring recommendations and their deadlines.
- A teacher, counselor, professor, or advisor who knows your academic work well.
- Resume, transcript, draft essays, brag sheet, or project samples if available.

## Steps

1. **Choose a recommender who can be specific.** Prefer someone who taught or supervised you recently and can describe your work, growth, and character. → *Expect:* the person can write more than a generic endorsement.
2. **Check application requirements.** Confirm subject area, counselor versus teacher letter, maximum number of letters, and submission method. → *Expect:* you ask the correct type of recommender.
3. **Ask politely and early.** Request at least several weeks before the first deadline when possible, and give them an easy way to decline. → *Expect:* the recommender can say yes without being pressured.
4. **Provide a complete packet.** Send deadlines, submission links, resume, transcript if appropriate, program list, essay draft, and notes on what you hope they can highlight. → *Expect:* the recommender has enough material to write accurately.
5. **Enter the recommender in the portal.** Use the correct email address and waive or choose FERPA/access rights only after understanding the option. → *Expect:* the recommender receives the official request.
6. **Track submission status.** Check each portal for received, invited, or pending status. → *Expect:* missing letters are visible before deadlines.
7. **Send one respectful reminder.** If the deadline is approaching and the letter is still pending, remind them with the due date and submission link. → *Expect:* the recommender has a clear nudge without repeated pressure.
8. **Thank the recommender.** Send a sincere thank-you and later update them on outcomes if appropriate. → *Expect:* the relationship remains respectful after the application cycle.

## Decision points

- Recommender hesitates or says they are too busy → thank them and ask someone else.
- Portal email never arrives → verify the email address, resend from the portal, or contact admissions support.
- Letter is optional → submit only if it adds meaningful new information.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Generic letter risk:** detect the recommender barely knows you → switch to someone who can provide examples.
- **F2 Missed deadline:** detect pending status close to due date → send a reminder and ask admissions whether late recommendations are accepted.
- **F3 Wrong recommender type:** detect a school requires a core academic teacher → request a qualifying teacher and update the portal.
- **F4 Duplicate or excess letters:** detect too many recommenders added → remove extras if the portal allows or ask admissions how they will handle them.

## Verification

Each application portal shows the recommendation as requested or received from the correct recommender, and the recommender has the deadlines and supporting materials.

## Variations

- `us-ferpa`: many college portals ask whether you waive access to the recommendation; read the language before choosing because it can affect how colleges interpret confidentiality.
- `graduate-school`: provide a statement of purpose, CV, transcript, research summary, and program list.
- `scholarship`: include the scholarship mission and the traits the recommender can address.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because recommendation systems use educational records and private evaluations. Share transcripts and application materials only with trusted recommenders and official portals.
