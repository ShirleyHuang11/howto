## Goal

You identify colleges that fit your academic, financial, personal, and practical needs well enough to investigate further.

## Preconditions

- A rough academic interest, location preference, budget range, and learning environment preference.
- Your transcript, test scores if available, and any non-negotiable needs such as accessibility, commuting, athletics, or family constraints.
- Access to official college websites and trusted comparison tools.

## Steps

1. **Define your fit criteria.** List must-haves and preferences for major, size, location, cost, support services, campus culture, graduation outcomes, and admissions selectivity. → *Expect:* a ranked criteria list instead of a vague search.
2. **Start from official academic offerings.** Check each college's current majors, minors, accreditation if relevant, course catalog, and department pages. → *Expect:* only schools with a plausible academic path remain.
3. **Estimate affordability early.** Use each college's net price calculator and note tuition, fees, housing, travel, and typical aid. → *Expect:* a realistic annual cost estimate for each school.
4. **Check admissions profile and requirements.** Review acceptance rate, middle 50% GPA/test ranges when published, required courses, test policy, portfolio/audition rules, and deadlines. → *Expect:* you understand whether each school is likely, target, or reach.
5. **Research outcomes and support.** Look for retention, graduation rates, career outcomes, advising, disability services, tutoring, mental health resources, and transfer support. → *Expect:* you know whether students like you are likely to be supported.
6. **Gather student-life and logistics evidence.** Check housing, transportation, safety reports, meal plans, clubs, work opportunities, and distance from home. → *Expect:* practical deal-breakers are visible.
7. **Record everything in one table.** Include source links, deadline, cost estimate, academic fit, selectivity, and personal notes. → *Expect:* colleges can be compared side by side.
8. **Shortlist schools for deeper review.** Keep a manageable set that meets your must-haves and deserves a visit, interview, email, or application. → *Expect:* you have a focused shortlist rather than a random list.

## Decision points

- Net price calculator shows unaffordable cost → keep only if merit aid, need-based aid, or in-state alternatives make it realistic.
- Major is unavailable or only a concentration → decide whether the broader department still fits your goals.
- Campus visit is impossible → use virtual tours, current-student panels, course catalogs, and admissions webinars.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Marketing-only research:** detect notes based only on brochures or rankings → verify with official requirements, costs, outcomes, and student support data.
- **F2 Hidden cost surprise:** detect missing travel, housing, fees, or health insurance → rebuild the cost estimate with the full cost of attendance.
- **F3 Deadline missed:** detect a school with an earlier scholarship or honors deadline → add all deadlines to a calendar immediately.
- **F4 Poor academic match:** detect no required major, lab, clinical placement, or licensure pathway → remove the school or ask the department directly.

## Verification

Your shortlist table includes official-source evidence for academic fit, affordability, admissions requirements, deadlines, outcomes, and practical constraints for each college.

## Variations

- `us`: use College Scorecard, Common Data Set, net price calculators, and each school's admissions site.
- `uk`: research UCAS courses, entry requirements, predicted grades, and subject-specific admissions tests.
- `transfer-student`: check credit transfer policies, articulation agreements, major prerequisites, and transfer housing.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not enter sensitive financial or identity information into unofficial tools, and treat rankings or sponsored lists as starting points rather than evidence.
