## Goal

You write a personal statement that connects your background, preparation, goals, and fit for a program or opportunity.

## Preconditions

- The prompt, audience, length limit, and submission rules.
- A current resume, transcript, activity list, or project list.
- Knowledge of the program, scholarship, internship, or institution you are applying to.

## Steps

1. **Identify what the selection committee needs to know.** Extract required themes such as motivation, preparation, goals, leadership, service, research interests, or fit. → *Expect:* a checklist tied to the prompt.
2. **Map evidence to each theme.** Choose experiences, projects, responsibilities, obstacles, and outcomes that prove your claims. → *Expect:* each major point has a concrete example.
3. **Write a central message.** Summarize the statement's main claim about who you are and what you are prepared to do. → *Expect:* the draft has a clear spine.
4. **Open with a relevant moment or direct motivation.** Avoid gimmicks; show the origin or urgency of your interest. → *Expect:* the reader quickly understands why this path matters to you.
5. **Show preparation through specifics.** Discuss courses, research, work, service, skills, or lived experience with outcomes and reflection. → *Expect:* the statement demonstrates readiness instead of merely asserting passion.
6. **Connect goals to the program.** Name features, faculty, curriculum, community, or training opportunities that genuinely fit your plans. → *Expect:* the statement could not be sent unchanged to every program.
7. **Revise for coherence and tone.** Remove resume repetition, generic praise, and unsupported claims. → *Expect:* each paragraph advances the central message.
8. **Proofread against the portal.** Check word count, pasted formatting, special characters, and required acknowledgments. → *Expect:* the submitted text matches your final version.

## Decision points

- Prompt asks for diversity, adversity, or identity → answer only in ways that feel truthful and relevant to the opportunity.
- Applying to many programs → customize the fit paragraph and any program-specific examples.
- There is a gap or weakness → address it briefly if it needs context, then return to growth and preparation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Statement repeats the resume:** detect paragraphs that list achievements only → add motivation, choices, learning, and future direction.
- **F2 Generic fit paragraph:** detect praise that could apply anywhere → add specific program features and why they matter.
- **F3 Too much childhood background:** detect long setup before recent evidence → cut to the experience that directly supports readiness.
- **F4 Sensitive disclosure feels unsafe:** detect information you would regret sharing broadly → revise to a level of detail you can stand behind.

## Verification

The statement answers the prompt, uses specific evidence, explains preparation and goals, names a real fit with the opportunity, and meets the exact length and submission requirements.

## Variations

- `graduate-school`: emphasize research fit, faculty, methods, preparation, and career goals.
- `scholarship`: emphasize mission alignment, impact, leadership, service, and financial or personal context if requested.
- `sample-thesis`: "My work in community health data has prepared me to study epidemiology with a focus on translating local evidence into usable public policy."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because personal statements influence admissions or funding and may disclose private experiences. Do not fabricate credentials, and avoid oversharing medical, family, or legal details unless they are necessary and you choose to include them.
