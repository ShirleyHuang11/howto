## Goal

You write a focused statement of purpose that explains your preparation, fit, and future goals without sounding generic or inflated.

## Preconditions

- You know the prompt, word limit, degree level, and submission deadline.
- You have a current CV or list of academic, professional, and research experiences.
- You have researched the program enough to name specific fit factors.

## Steps

1. **Analyze the prompt and constraints.** Highlight required topics, word count, formatting, and whether personal history belongs in this essay or a separate statement. → *Expect:* a checklist of what the essay must answer.
2. **Choose a central academic goal.** State the field problem, method, professional aim, or research direction you want to pursue. → *Expect:* one clear sentence that controls the essay.
3. **Select evidence of readiness.** Pick two to four experiences that show skills, judgment, and preparation rather than listing everything. → *Expect:* each body paragraph has a concrete example.
4. **Map fit to the program.** Identify faculty, labs, courses, clinics, archives, curriculum features, or outcomes that honestly match your goals. → *Expect:* the draft could not be sent unchanged to every school.
5. **Draft in a logical arc.** Move from goal, to preparation, to fit, to near-term plan. → *Expect:* a complete draft that answers the prompt before polishing style.
6. **Cut vague claims.** Replace "passionate," "world-class," and broad praise with specific evidence and program details. → *Expect:* every major claim is supported by a fact or example.
7. **Check length and formatting.** Trim to the word or page limit and follow file naming and PDF instructions. → *Expect:* the file meets the exact upload rules.
8. **Get feedback and revise.** Ask one field-aware reader and one clarity-focused reader for comments. → *Expect:* the final version is accurate, readable, and free of wrong program names.

## Decision points

- Prompt asks for personal adversity -> include it only when it explains preparation or context, not as a substitute for academic fit.
- You have uncertain research interests -> frame a coherent area and methods you want to explore, not a rigid dissertation topic.
- Word limit is tight -> prioritize goal, preparation, and fit over biographical background.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Generic essay:** detect that only the school name changes -> add program-specific fit and remove empty praise.
- **F2 Resume repetition:** detect paragraphs that merely restate job titles -> explain what you did, learned, and can now contribute.
- **F3 Overclaiming:** detect exaggerated expertise or unsupported impact -> replace with precise scope and evidence.
- **F4 Prompt mismatch:** detect missing required topic -> restructure before polishing.

## Verification

The final statement answers every prompt item, fits the word limit, names the correct program, and gives specific evidence of preparation and fit.

## Variations

- Research SOP: name research questions, methods, and advisor fit. Example thesis sentence: "I want to study how multilingual students use peer feedback in first-year writing courses, combining classroom observation with discourse analysis."
- Professional SOP: connect prior experience to competencies, licensure goals, and supervised practice.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but honesty matters. Do not plagiarize, invent research experience, or disclose trauma, medical information, immigration details, or family history unless it is relevant and you are comfortable having admissions readers evaluate it.
