## Goal

You submit complete graduate-school applications that fit your academic goals, funding needs, and readiness for the program.

## Preconditions

- You have target fields, degree type, and a realistic start term.
- You can obtain transcripts, recommendations, a CV, and required test scores.
- You understand application fees, funding deadlines, and visa or relocation constraints if relevant.

## Steps

1. **Define your fit criteria.** List research area, coursework needs, advisor match, funding, location, degree outcomes, and constraints. → *Expect:* a short rubric for comparing programs.
2. **Build a program spreadsheet.** Record deadlines, requirements, fees, funding deadlines, test rules, faculty matches, and contacts. → *Expect:* every target program has a complete row and deadline.
3. **Contact potential advisors when appropriate.** For research degrees, send concise emails with your interests, CV, and a specific fit question. → *Expect:* useful signals about advisor availability or program fit.
4. **Prepare core materials.** Draft the statement, CV, writing sample, portfolio, transcripts, and test-score plan. → *Expect:* each requirement has a current file or ordering task.
5. **Request recommendations early.** Ask recommenders with deadlines, program list, CV, statement draft, and reminders. → *Expect:* each recommender explicitly agrees and knows how to submit.
6. **Complete applications one by one.** Enter biographical, academic, funding, and program-specific details carefully. → *Expect:* every required field is complete and saved in the portal.
7. **Tailor each statement.** Connect your preparation and goals to the specific program, faculty, methods, curriculum, or clinical/professional track. → *Expect:* no school names, faculty names, or requirements are mismatched.
8. **Review and submit.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* submitted applications and fees are often final, so proofread PDFs, uploads, waiver status, and recommender entries first. → *Expect:* a confirmation page or email for each submitted application.
9. **Monitor status after submission.** Check portals for missing materials and respond quickly to interview, funding, or document requests. → *Expect:* each file is complete or has a specific follow-up task.

## Decision points

- A program cannot offer funding you need -> remove it or classify it as unfunded risk before paying.
- A faculty member says they are not taking students -> decide whether another advisor makes the program still worth applying to.
- A requirement is unclear -> ask the graduate coordinator before submitting an incomplete or wrong document.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Recommender delay:** detect a missing recommendation near deadline -> send a polite reminder and ask the program whether late letters are accepted.
- **F2 Wrong upload:** detect an outdated CV or statement attached -> replace it before submission; after submission, ask the coordinator whether a corrected file can be added.
- **F3 Missed funding deadline:** detect that the admissions deadline differs from funding deadline -> ask about late funding consideration and adjust program priority.
- **F4 Poor program fit:** detect no advisor, course, or outcome match -> drop the program rather than submitting a generic application.

## Verification

Every target application shows submitted and paid or fee-waived, all required materials are marked received, and you have confirmation records for each program.

## Variations

- `us`: PhD programs often combine admission and funding review; master's programs may have separate scholarship or assistantship forms.
- Professional graduate programs: licensing prerequisites, clinical hours, portfolios, or centralized services may matter more than faculty contact.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from fees, immigration implications, personal statements, and education records. Confirm deadlines and funding before paying, do not fabricate achievements, and share sensitive documents only through official portals.
