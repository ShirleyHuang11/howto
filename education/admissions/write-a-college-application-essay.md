## Goal

You write an authentic college application essay that answers the prompt, shows judgment and self-reflection, and fits the required word limit.

## Preconditions

- The exact essay prompt and word or character limit.
- A list of experiences, values, challenges, interests, or changes you can discuss honestly.
- The application platform's policy on assistance, AI, and plagiarism.

## Steps

1. **Read the prompt for the real question.** Underline what it asks you to reveal, such as growth, community, intellectual curiosity, challenge, or contribution. → *Expect:* you know what the reader should learn about you.
2. **Brainstorm specific moments.** List concrete scenes, decisions, conflicts, projects, or responsibilities rather than broad traits. → *Expect:* possible topics have detail and stakes.
3. **Choose a topic that adds new information.** Avoid repeating the activity list unless the essay reveals motivation, context, or growth not shown elsewhere. → *Expect:* the essay has a purpose in the application.
4. **Draft around a focused story or argument.** Start with a specific moment and connect it to reflection, choices, and what changed. → *Expect:* the draft shows evidence of character through action.
5. **Make the reflection explicit.** Explain what you learned, how you think now, and how the experience affects what you will bring to college. → *Expect:* the essay is not just a story.
6. **Revise for voice and specificity.** Replace generic claims with details only you could write, and remove phrases that sound like a brochure. → *Expect:* the essay sounds personal and credible.
7. **Check fit with the prompt and word limit.** Trim repetition, long setup, and extra context before cutting the strongest reflection. → *Expect:* the essay answers the prompt within the exact limit.
8. **Get focused feedback.** Ask a trusted reviewer whether the essay sounds like you and what they learn about you. → *Expect:* feedback identifies clarity, authenticity, and overexplained sections.
9. **Proofread the final version in the application box.** Formatting can change when pasted, so reread line breaks, characters, and special symbols. → *Expect:* the text in the portal matches the final essay.

## Decision points

- Topic depends on trauma or hardship → include only what you are comfortable sharing and emphasize agency, context, and growth.
- Essay could be about any applicant → add specific decisions, images, relationships, and consequences.
- Reviewer wants to rewrite it heavily → preserve your own voice and use feedback selectively.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Essay ignores the prompt:** detect a good story that does not answer the question → rewrite the reflection around the prompt language.
- **F2 Too generic:** detect values like "hard work" without specific evidence → add a scene, choice, or consequence.
- **F3 Over the word limit:** detect the portal truncates or rejects the essay → cut background, repeated adjectives, and throat-clearing first.
- **F4 Inauthentic polish:** detect language you would never use → simplify wording until it sounds like you at your best.

## Verification

The final essay fits the prompt and word limit, reveals something meaningful not obvious elsewhere, uses specific evidence, and has been proofread inside the application platform.

## Variations

- `common-app`: the main essay has a platform word limit; supplements often have separate shorter limits.
- `coalition-or-school-portal`: prompts and character counts may differ, so verify before reusing.
- `sample-thesis`: "I used to treat translation as replacing words; volunteering at the clinic taught me it is also responsibility for context, tone, and trust."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because application essays affect admissions decisions and may include sensitive personal information. Do not fabricate experiences, plagiarize, or let another person or tool write the essay for you.
