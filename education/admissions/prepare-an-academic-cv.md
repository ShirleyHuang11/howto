## Goal

You create an academic CV that presents your education, research, teaching, publications, service, and skills in a clear format for admissions or academic hiring.

## Preconditions

- You have dates, institution names, supervisors, titles, awards, and publication details.
- You know the target program or role and any page or format limits.
- You can verify every item you plan to include.

## Steps

1. **Choose academic CV sections.** Use Education, Research Experience, Publications, Presentations, Teaching, Awards, Service, Skills, and Relevant Professional Experience as applicable. → *Expect:* the document has headings that fit your background.
2. **Put education first.** List degree, institution, location, expected or completed date, thesis title if useful, and advisor if relevant. → *Expect:* readers can immediately see your academic stage.
3. **Describe research and teaching with evidence.** Use concise bullets with methods, populations, tools, responsibilities, and outputs. → *Expect:* each entry shows what you actually did.
4. **Format publications accurately.** Separate peer-reviewed articles, manuscripts under review, presentations, and posters. → *Expect:* publication status is honest and not inflated.
5. **Add awards, service, and skills selectively.** Include items that support the target field; omit routine coursework unless requested. → *Expect:* the CV stays relevant and scannable.
6. **Standardize dates and layout.** Use reverse chronological order within sections, consistent month-year formatting, and readable spacing. → *Expect:* no entry looks pasted from a different template.
7. **Check claims against evidence.** Confirm titles, dates, coauthors, grant names, and software skills. → *Expect:* every item can be defended if asked.
8. **Export as PDF with a clear filename.** Use the required naming convention if the application gives one. → *Expect:* the PDF preserves layout and opens correctly.

## Decision points

- Applying outside academia -> use a resume unless the employer requests a CV.
- No publications yet -> omit the section or list presentations and research experience honestly.
- Strict page limit -> prioritize education, research fit, publications, and required experiences.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Inflated publication status:** detect "publication" for work only drafted -> relabel as manuscript in preparation or omit.
- **F2 Dense unreadable layout:** detect tiny margins or long paragraphs -> use fewer bullets and clearer section breaks.
- **F3 Missing dates:** detect unexplained entries -> add month/year or a brief expected date.
- **F4 Wrong audience:** detect many unrelated jobs crowding academic evidence -> move them to a short relevant experience section or remove them.

## Verification

The CV is a readable PDF, uses consistent formatting, contains only accurate claims, and foregrounds the experiences most relevant to the target academic opportunity.

## Variations

- Humanities: include languages, archival work, conference papers, and teaching fields.
- Sciences: include lab techniques, datasets, software, grants, posters, and publications by status.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but false credentials can have serious consequences. Do not include Social Security numbers, birth dates, full home addresses, or private student information from teaching or research records.
