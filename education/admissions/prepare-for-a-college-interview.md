## Goal

You prepare for a college interview so you can answer clearly, ask informed questions, and represent your interests honestly.

## Preconditions

- Interview date, time, format, interviewer name if provided, and expected length.
- Knowledge of your application, activities, academic interests, and the college.
- A quiet space and reliable technology for virtual interviews.

## Steps

1. **Confirm logistics.** Check time zone, location or video link, dress expectations, and contact method if something goes wrong. → *Expect:* you know where and when to appear.
2. **Research the college beyond rankings.** Review academic programs, student life, values, curriculum, and opportunities tied to your interests. → *Expect:* you can explain why the school fits you.
3. **Prepare your core stories.** Choose examples showing curiosity, responsibility, resilience, collaboration, leadership, or growth. → *Expect:* you have evidence behind common answers.
4. **Practice common questions aloud.** Prepare for "Tell me about yourself," "Why this college," "What do you want to study," and "Describe a challenge." → *Expect:* answers are conversational, not memorized.
5. **Prepare questions for the interviewer.** Ask questions that cannot be answered by the front page of the website. → *Expect:* your questions show real interest.
6. **Set up the interview environment.** For virtual interviews, test camera, microphone, lighting, display name, background, charger, and internet. → *Expect:* technical problems are less likely.
7. **Bring concise materials.** Have your resume, notes, application themes, and questions nearby, but do not read from them. → *Expect:* you can recover details without sounding scripted.
8. **Follow up afterward.** Send a short thank-you if contact information is available and appropriate. → *Expect:* the interaction ends professionally.

## Decision points

- Interview is evaluative → treat it like part of the application and prepare carefully.
- Interview is informational → still prepare, but focus on learning and fit.
- You do not know an answer → be honest, explain your current thinking, and avoid inventing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Robotic answers:** detect memorized paragraphs → practice bullet points and speak naturally.
- **F2 Weak "why us" answer:** detect reasons based only on prestige or location → add specific programs, courses, culture, or opportunities.
- **F3 Technology failure:** detect video or audio trouble → use the backup contact method immediately and offer to reconnect.
- **F4 Oversharing:** detect private details that do not help the interview → redirect to what you learned or how you grew.

## Verification

You can explain your interests, fit with the college, and key experiences aloud in a natural way, and your interview logistics and backup plan are confirmed.

## Variations

- `alumni-interview`: interviewers may know less about current admissions policy; ask about lived experience and community.
- `virtual`: test the exact platform before the interview and keep phone audio as backup if allowed.
- `arts-or-honors`: prepare to discuss portfolio, research, or honors interests in greater detail.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Meet in public or official settings for in-person interviews, do not share unnecessary private information, and verify that communications come from the college or authorized interviewer.
