## Goal

You understand who borrows, how much the loan costs, when repayment begins, and whether the loan is necessary before accepting it.

## Preconditions

- The financial aid offer or loan disclosure.
- Cost of attendance, gift aid, savings, income, and remaining gap.
- Access to official federal, institutional, or lender information.

## Steps

1. **Identify the loan type.** Label each loan as federal subsidized, federal unsubsidized, Parent PLUS, graduate PLUS, private, institutional, or state loan. → *Expect:* you know which rules apply.
2. **Identify the borrower.** Confirm whether the student, parent, or cosigner is legally responsible. → *Expect:* the person taking the debt is explicit.
3. **Record amount, interest rate, and fees.** Include origination fees, fixed or variable rate, annual limit, and total offered amount. → *Expect:* you know the upfront and long-term cost.
4. **Check when interest starts.** Determine whether interest is subsidized while enrolled or accrues immediately. → *Expect:* you can estimate balance growth before repayment.
5. **Review repayment terms.** Note grace period, standard payment estimate, income-driven options if federal, deferment, forbearance, and cosigner release if private. → *Expect:* repayment obligations are not abstract.
6. **Borrow only what is needed.** Compare the remaining gap after grants, scholarships, savings, work, and lower-cost options. → *Expect:* accepted loan amount is no larger than necessary.
7. **Complete required counseling or disclosures.** For federal loans, complete entrance counseling and the Master Promissory Note when required. → *Expect:* the loan can disburse only after required acknowledgments.
8. **Accept or decline deliberately.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* a signed promissory note creates a legal repayment obligation, so confirm amount, borrower, rate, and alternatives before signing. → *Expect:* the portal shows accepted, reduced, or declined loan status.

## Decision points

- Loan is private with variable interest → compare federal options first and understand cosigner risk.
- Parent PLUS fills the gap → confirm parent repayment capacity; it is parent debt, not student debt.
- Offer exceeds actual need → reduce the accepted amount in the aid portal if allowed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Borrower confusion:** detect family assumes student owes a parent loan → clarify loan documents and borrower name before signing.
- **F2 Overborrowing:** detect refund money being used for non-school expenses → reduce future disbursements or return unused funds if allowed.
- **F3 Private loan surprise:** detect variable rate, cosigner liability, or limited hardship options → compare other financing and ask the lender for full disclosures.
- **F4 Disbursement blocked:** detect loan pending after acceptance → complete counseling, promissory note, verification, or enrollment requirements.

## Verification

You have a written summary of loan type, borrower, principal, fees, interest rate, interest accrual, repayment estimate, and accepted amount, and the aid portal reflects your intended decision.

## Variations

- `us-federal-undergraduate`: Direct Subsidized Loans do not accrue interest while enrolled at least half time; Direct Unsubsidized Loans generally do.
- `graduate-student`: unsubsidized and Grad PLUS loans are common, with different limits and rates.
- `non-us`: loan terms vary by country; use the official government or lender disclosure for your locale.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because loans create enforceable debt. Never sign a promissory note or private loan agreement without confirming borrower, amount, rate, fees, repayment start, and alternatives.
