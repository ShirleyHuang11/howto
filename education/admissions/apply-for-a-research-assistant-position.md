## Goal

You apply for a research assistant position with a targeted message, relevant evidence, and a clear understanding of expectations.

## Preconditions

- You know the lab, project, professor, or posting you want to approach.
- You have a resume or academic CV and a short description of your relevant skills.
- You can commit to the expected hours, term, location, and training requirements.

## Steps

1. **Study the project before contacting anyone.** Read the lab page, recent papers, project description, and required skills. → *Expect:* you can name what the group studies and why you fit.
2. **Identify the correct contact path.** [BRANCH: posted job | cold inquiry] Use the application portal for posted roles or email the PI/lab manager for inquiry roles. → *Expect:* you know where the application should go.
3. **Match your evidence to the role.** Select coursework, methods, coding, lab, writing, language, or fieldwork experience that maps to the project. → *Expect:* your materials show fit without exaggeration.
4. **Prepare a concise CV or resume.** Put education, relevant skills, research experience, and availability near the top. → *Expect:* a reviewer can see qualifications in under one minute.
5. **Write a targeted message.** State who you are, why the project interests you, what skills you bring, your availability, and attach the CV. → *Expect:* the message is specific to that lab or posting.
6. **Submit through the requested channel.** Follow file naming, form fields, work-study eligibility, and reference requirements. → *Expect:* the application or email is delivered with the correct attachments.
7. **Prepare for follow-up.** Review the project, be ready to discuss your availability and skills honestly, and prepare questions about training, supervision, pay, authorship, and data handling. → *Expect:* you can interview without guessing.
8. **Follow up once if needed.** After the stated timeline or 7-10 business days, send a short polite follow-up. → *Expect:* you either receive a response or can move on without repeated messages.

## Decision points

- The position requires skills you do not have -> apply only if the posting says training is provided or you can honestly describe beginner readiness.
- The role is unpaid -> ask about course credit, workload, authorship, and whether paid alternatives exist.
- The project handles human subjects or sensitive data -> expect required training before access.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Generic cold email:** detect no project details in your message -> rewrite around one specific paper, method, or research question.
- **F2 Availability mismatch:** detect required hours conflict with classes -> state realistic availability or decline before wasting the lab's time.
- **F3 Missing attachment:** detect a sent email without CV -> reply immediately with an apology and the attachment.
- **F4 Unclear expectations:** detect vague duties or unpaid labor pressure -> ask for written expectations before accepting.

## Verification

The application or inquiry has been sent through the correct channel, your CV is attached, and you have a record of the role, contact, date, and follow-up plan.

## Variations

- Undergraduate RA: faculty may prioritize reliability, curiosity, and schedule fit over prior publications.
- Graduate RA: funding source, assistantship terms, tuition coverage, and advisor expectations matter before accepting.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but research data can be sensitive. Do not claim skills you lack, do not share unpublished data from prior labs, and complete required ethics, safety, or human-subjects training before handling restricted materials.
