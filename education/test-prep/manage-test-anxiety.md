## Goal

You reduce test anxiety enough to prepare, sit for the exam, and recover from stress responses without letting anxiety control performance.

## Preconditions

- A known upcoming exam or recurring test situation.
- Willingness to practice coping techniques before test day.
- Access to medical, counseling, or disability support if anxiety is severe or disabling.

## Steps

1. **Name the anxiety pattern.** Write what happens before and during tests: racing thoughts, nausea, blanking, perfectionism, panic, or avoidance. → *Expect:* a clear description of the problem to target.
2. **Separate preparation gaps from anxiety.** Take a short timed practice set and review whether misses come from knowledge, timing, or panic. → *Expect:* you know what needs studying and what needs stress management.
3. **Create a predictable pre-test routine.** Plan sleep, food, travel, materials, and a 5-minute warmup. → *Expect:* fewer unknowns remain for test day.
4. **Practice controlled breathing.** Use slow exhale breathing or box breathing for 2-5 minutes during study sessions. → *Expect:* your body has a practiced way to downshift.
5. **Use realistic self-talk.** Replace catastrophic predictions with specific task instructions such as "answer the easiest question first." → *Expect:* written phrases you can use during the exam.
6. **Expose yourself gradually to timed pressure.** Start with short timed sets, then longer sections, then full practice tests. → *Expect:* timed work becomes familiar instead of novel.
7. **Make an in-test reset plan.** Choose a brief routine: pause, breathe, relax shoulders, reread the stem, and answer or mark for later. → *Expect:* you know exactly what to do if you freeze.
8. **Seek formal support when anxiety impairs functioning.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* panic attacks, fainting, self-harm thoughts, or inability to attend school or work need professional help; contact a clinician, counselor, or emergency support as appropriate. → *Expect:* severe symptoms are escalated beyond self-help.

## Decision points

- Anxiety improves with practice exposure → continue timed simulations.
- Anxiety remains disabling despite preparation → ask about accommodations, counseling, or medical care.
- The exam is high stakes and soon → simplify the plan to sleep, logistics, breathing, and a few high-yield reviews.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Avoidance disguised as planning:** detect repeated schedule-making without practice → do one small timed set immediately.
- **F2 Breathing technique feels useless on test day:** detect trying it only during panic → practice daily while calm.
- **F3 Overstudying increases anxiety:** detect sleep loss and declining accuracy → schedule recovery and stop late-night cramming.
- **F4 Shame prevents help:** detect hiding symptoms from teachers or family → contact a counselor, disability office, or trusted adult.

## Verification

You have completed timed practice using your reset routine, can describe your anxiety triggers, and have a concrete support plan if symptoms become severe.

## Variations

- School exams: teachers may allow alternate seating, breaks, or counseling referrals.
- Standardized exams: accommodations usually require documentation and advance approval.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because severe anxiety can affect health and safety. Keep health details private except with trusted support people, and seek professional or emergency help for panic that feels unsafe or includes self-harm thoughts.
