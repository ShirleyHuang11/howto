## Goal

You prepare for the GRE by measuring your baseline, targeting Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, and Analytical Writing weaknesses, and making an informed score-reporting plan.

## Preconditions

- A list of programs and whether each requires or accepts GRE scores.
- Access to official ETS practice materials or comparable timed practice.
- A study log for vocabulary, math errors, and writing feedback.

## Steps

1. **Verify whether the GRE is useful for your applications.** Check each program's current admissions page and note required, optional, or not accepted policies. → *Expect:* a program-by-program decision about whether to test.
2. **Schedule backward from deadlines.** Leave time for score reporting and a retake if needed. → *Expect:* a target test date that precedes your earliest deadline by a practical buffer.
3. **Take a timed diagnostic.** Use official-style sections and write the Analytical Writing essay under time. → *Expect:* baseline verbal, quantitative, and writing estimates.
4. **Build an error inventory.** Separate quant misses by topic and verbal misses by vocabulary, sentence logic, reading inference, or time pressure. → *Expect:* a ranked study list instead of a vague score goal.
5. **Rebuild weak quant foundations.** Practice arithmetic, algebra, ratios, rates, geometry, statistics, and data interpretation with written solutions. → *Expect:* missed quant problems have corrected solution notes.
6. **Practice verbal with evidence.** Study high-frequency vocabulary in context and force every reading answer to point back to text evidence. → *Expect:* answer explanations identify why wrong options are wrong.
7. **Draft and review essays.** Practice issue essays with a clear claim, reasons, examples, and concessions. → *Expect:* timed essays have an introduction, organized body paragraphs, and a conclusion.
8. **Run full test simulations.** Include breaks, scratch paper, calculator restrictions, and the current GRE timing. → *Expect:* a score range that is stable enough for application planning.
9. **Decide score recipients deliberately.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* sending scores to programs may expose a result you cannot pull back from their file; confirm recipient names and reporting options before submitting. → *Expect:* scores are sent only to intended institutions.

## Decision points

- Program is GRE-optional → submit only if your score strengthens the file relative to admitted-student ranges.
- Quant-heavy program → prioritize quant accuracy and pacing over broad verbal gains.
- Diagnostic is already above target → shift effort to essays, applications, or recommendation logistics.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vocabulary memorized but not applied:** detect knowing definitions yet missing sentence completions → practice context clues and contrast words.
- **F2 Quant mistakes repeat:** detect the same topic recurring in the log → stop full tests and drill that concept with untimed explanations.
- **F3 Essay feels generic:** detect examples that do not support the claim → build a bank of adaptable academic, work, and civic examples.
- **F4 Score report mistake:** detect wrong institution or department selected → contact ETS immediately, but assume submitted reports may remain visible.

## Verification

You have a current practice score range, an error log with reviewed misses, at least two timed essays, and a confirmed test registration and score-reporting plan aligned with application deadlines.

## Variations

- `us`: ETS administers the GRE and sets ID, retake, and reporting rules; program policies vary widely by graduate field.
- Business programs: some accept either GRE or GMAT; compare which test better fits your strengths before paying for both.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because test fees, deadlines, disability accommodations, and score reporting affect admissions outcomes. Use official accounts, protect login credentials, and confirm all recipients before submitting.
