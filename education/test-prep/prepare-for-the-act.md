## Goal

You build an ACT study plan from a diagnostic score, practice the English, Math, Reading, and Science sections under timed conditions, and arrive ready for test day logistics.

## Preconditions

- An ACT test date or target window.
- Access to official ACT practice materials or a reputable prep source.
- A notebook, spreadsheet, or app for logging misses and timing.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the test format and registration details.** Check whether you are taking paper or online ACT, whether the optional writing section is required, and what ID is accepted. → *Expect:* a saved test date, location or testing mode, admission ticket requirements, and section list.
2. **Take a timed diagnostic.** Use a full official practice test with standard breaks and no pausing. → *Expect:* baseline scores by section and a realistic composite estimate.
3. **Analyze errors by skill and cause.** Label each miss as content gap, careless error, timing issue, misread question, or guessed item. → *Expect:* a ranked list of the 3-5 weaknesses costing the most points.
4. **Set a target score and weekly schedule.** Compare the baseline to your goal score and divide study blocks across weak sections first. → *Expect:* a calendar with practice days, review days, and at least two full-length tests.
5. **Review high-yield ACT content.** Drill punctuation, grammar, algebra, functions, geometry, data interpretation, passage mapping, and science graph reading. → *Expect:* targeted notes and completed practice sets for each weak topic.
6. **Practice ACT timing rules.** English is fast, Math gets harder, Reading rewards passage triage, and Science rewards data-first reading. → *Expect:* section pacing checkpoints written beside your practice timer.
7. **Retake mixed timed sections.** Practice full sections after topic review rather than only untimed drills. → *Expect:* section scores improve or the error log clearly shows what still blocks progress.
8. **Simulate test day at least once.** Take a complete test in one sitting with approved calculator, breaks, and scratch work only. → *Expect:* a reliable score range and fewer surprises about fatigue.
9. **Prepare materials and route.** Print or save the admission ticket, pack accepted ID, calculator, batteries, pencils if needed, snack, and water. → *Expect:* a packed bag and arrival plan for the correct test center or online check-in.

## Decision points

- Writing score required by a college or program → add essay practice and scoring review.
- Math score lags because formulas are forgotten → spend more time on content review before more full tests.
- Scores are stable but below target → change strategy, tutoring, or test date rather than repeating the same drills.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Inflated practice score:** detect frequent pausing or extra time → retake a section with strict timing and use that score for planning.
- **F2 English misses stay random:** detect no pattern in the error log → sort by grammar rule and study one rule at a time.
- **F3 Science timing collapse:** detect unanswered final questions → practice reading graphs and tables before passage text.
- **F4 Wrong test-day materials:** detect missing ID, admission ticket, or calculator policy conflict → fix it before test morning or contact ACT support.

## Verification

You have completed at least one full timed ACT practice test, logged and reviewed every miss, packed required materials, and can state your section pacing plan without looking it up.

## Variations

- `us`: ACT registration, score reporting, fee waivers, and calculator rules are handled through ACT; always check the official account before test day.
- School-day ACT: your school may register you and set different arrival instructions; follow the school's written notice.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect your ACT account login, do not share admission tickets publicly, and use only permitted materials so your score is not canceled.
