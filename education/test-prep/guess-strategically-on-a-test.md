## Goal

You use guessing as a controlled scoring strategy when you do not know an answer, rather than leaving points to chance or panic.

## Preconditions

- You know whether the exam penalizes wrong answers.
- You understand the question type and answer-entry method.
- You have attempted the content you can solve directly.

## Steps

1. **Check the scoring rule.** Confirm whether blank answers, wrong answers, and partial credit are treated differently. → *Expect:* you know whether guessing can help or hurt.
2. **Answer known questions first.** Build confidence and save uncertain items for later. → *Expect:* the questions you can solve are already captured.
3. **Eliminate clearly wrong options.** Cross out choices that contradict the question, use extreme language, mismatch units, or answer a different issue. → *Expect:* the odds improve before you guess.
4. **Use evidence from the test.** Look for definitions, formulas, passage clues, or repeated concepts elsewhere in the exam. → *Expect:* your guess is informed by available information.
5. **Prefer precise fit over familiar wording.** Avoid choosing an answer only because it uses terms from the prompt. → *Expect:* the selected option actually answers the question asked.
6. **Apply probability only after reasoning.** If no penalty exists, fill every remaining answer; if a penalty exists, guess only when eliminated choices make the expected value favorable. → *Expect:* blank and guessed items match the scoring rule.
7. **Mark low-confidence answers for review.** Use the exam's flagging system or a light paper mark if allowed. → *Expect:* you can revisit guesses without losing place.
8. **Lock final guesses before time expires.** Do not leave permitted answers blank while trying to perfect one hard item. → *Expect:* every allowed guess is entered in the answer system.

## Decision points

- No wrong-answer penalty → guess on every unanswered multiple-choice item.
- Wrong-answer penalty and no eliminations → consider leaving blank if the scoring formula makes guessing harmful.
- Partial credit exists → write a formula, outline, or evidence even when the final answer is uncertain.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pattern superstition:** detect choosing based on answer-letter streaks → return to elimination and evidence.
- **F2 Overthinking a first instinct:** detect changing answers without new information → change only when you find a concrete error.
- **F3 Misusing penalty rules:** detect guessing blindly on a penalized exam → calculate whether eliminated options justify it.
- **F4 Blank answer by accident:** detect unanswered bubbles or online flags at final review → enter allowed guesses before time ends.

## Verification

You know the exam's guessing policy, have eliminated options where possible, and have entered every guess that improves expected score under the rules.

## Variations

- Multiple-select questions: guessing all options may be penalized by the item rubric; choose only options you can justify.
- Math exams: estimate magnitude, sign, and units before selecting among choices.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Follow exam rules and do not use unauthorized outside help; strategic guessing is not cheating when based on permitted reasoning.
