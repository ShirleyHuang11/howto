## Goal

You complete a practice test in conditions close enough to the real exam that the result is useful for planning, not just confidence.

## Preconditions

- A practice test aligned to the current exam format.
- A timer, approved tools, scratch paper if allowed, and a quiet place.
- An answer key or scoring guide for review.

## Steps

1. **Choose a representative test.** Prefer official or current-format materials over random question banks. → *Expect:* the practice test matches sections, timing, and question style.
2. **Set up real test conditions.** Clear your desk, silence devices, gather only allowed materials, and set section timers. → *Expect:* the environment resembles the real exam.
3. **Record baseline context.** Note date, sleep, section order, and whether this is a diagnostic or final check. → *Expect:* the score can be interpreted later.
4. **Take the test without pausing.** Do not look up answers, rewind audio unless permitted, or extend time. → *Expect:* a result that reflects actual test performance.
5. **Mark uncertainty while testing.** Circle or flag guessed and time-pressure questions without stopping. → *Expect:* the review will show both wrong answers and lucky guesses.
6. **Score the test promptly.** Use the official conversion table or rubric where available. → *Expect:* section scores and total score are logged.
7. **Review every missed or guessed question.** Write the tested concept, why your answer failed, and the correct method. → *Expect:* an actionable error log.
8. **Choose the next study action.** Convert the top error patterns into drills, content review, or timing practice. → *Expect:* the practice test produces a concrete next plan.

## Decision points

- Score is much lower than expected → check whether timing, format mismatch, fatigue, or content gaps caused it.
- Score is high but many guesses were lucky → treat flagged correct guesses as errors for study purposes.
- Official practice tests are limited → save at least one full test for a final simulation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unreliable score:** detect pauses, interruptions, or extra time → mark the score as invalid and retake later.
- **F2 Review skipped:** detect only a score with no error log → schedule a review block before taking another test.
- **F3 Wrong scoring scale:** detect raw score used as final score when the exam uses conversion → rescore with the official table.
- **F4 Burnout from too many tests:** detect declining focus without new learning → replace one test with targeted review.

## Verification

You have a completed timed practice test, a logged score by section, and an error log that names the next topics or strategies to study.

## Variations

- Essay or speaking exams: include rubric scoring and, when possible, human feedback.
- Adaptive exams: use official adaptive practice because fixed question sets may misrepresent difficulty.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not upload copyrighted test materials to public sites, and keep any student score logs private.
