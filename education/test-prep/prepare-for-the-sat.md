## Goal

You prepare for the SAT with official materials, a score target, timed practice, and test-day logistics handled before the exam date.

## Preconditions

- A College Board account and confirmed or intended SAT test date.
- Access to official SAT practice materials, such as College Board resources and the Bluebook app for digital practice.
- A calculator approved for the Math section and a device that meets digital testing requirements if applicable.

## Steps

1. **Confirm your test requirement and timeline.** Check whether target colleges, scholarships, or programs require, recommend, or ignore SAT scores, and note registration deadlines. → *Expect:* you know why you are taking the SAT and by when scores are needed.
2. **Take a diagnostic practice test.** Use an official digital practice test under timed conditions. → *Expect:* you have baseline section scores and a list of missed question types.
3. **Set a score target.** Compare your baseline with target school middle ranges, scholarship thresholds, or personal goals. → *Expect:* the needed improvement is measurable by section.
4. **Build a study plan.** Schedule Reading and Writing, Math, timed practice, review, and rest days. → *Expect:* the calendar covers weak areas before the test.
5. **Review mistakes deeply.** For each missed question, record skill, reason missed, correct method, and how to recognize the type next time. → *Expect:* errors become teachable patterns.
6. **Practice official question types.** Use College Board-style questions and full sections, increasing timed practice as test day approaches. → *Expect:* timing, navigation, and question format feel familiar.
7. **Prepare test-day logistics.** Confirm admission ticket, acceptable ID, test center or digital setup, approved calculator, snacks, transportation, and arrival time. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* once registration deadlines pass or test day begins, options narrow; confirm official instructions before relying on a plan. → *Expect:* materials and travel or device setup are ready the day before.
8. **Use final days for light review.** Review formulas, common grammar rules, missed-question logs, and pacing strategy; sleep normally. → *Expect:* you enter the test rested with a clear plan.

## Decision points

- Your target schools are test-optional → decide whether your likely score strengthens the application before spending money or sending scores.
- The diagnostic is far below target → extend the timeline if possible and focus on foundational skills before full tests.
- You need accommodations → request them through the College Board process well before the test date.
- Money is a barrier → check eligibility for SAT fee waivers through school counselors or College Board guidance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Practicing with unofficial-only materials:** detect questions that do not match the digital SAT style → anchor practice in official College Board materials.
- **F2 Score plateau:** detect repeated full tests with the same mistakes → reduce full-test frequency and target the specific skills in the error log.
- **F3 Registration or ID problem:** detect missing ticket, wrong name, expired ID, or missed deadline → contact College Board or your school counselor immediately.
- **F4 Timing collapse:** detect many unanswered questions → practice section pacing and learn when to guess and move on.

## Verification

You have a registered or chosen test date, a baseline and target score, a completed study schedule, timed official practice results, an error log with retests, and test-day materials confirmed.

## Variations

- `us`: SAT registration, fee waivers, accommodations, and score sending are handled through College Board processes; school counselors often help with eligibility and deadlines.
- `digital SAT`: practice in Bluebook or official digital tools so navigation, calculator access, and timing match the real test.
- `college admissions`: score policies vary by institution and year; verify each target school's current testing policy before sending scores.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because registration uses personal data and can involve fees and admissions consequences. Use official College Board pages for account actions, protect login credentials, confirm deadlines before paying, and do not use leaked questions or impersonation services.
