## Goal

You turn a graded exam into a concrete learning plan by identifying what went wrong, correcting misunderstandings, and deciding what to change before the next assessment.

## Preconditions

- The graded exam, score report, rubric, or item feedback.
- Course notes, textbook, answer key, or instructor explanations.
- Permission to view or discuss the exam if it is controlled material.

## Steps

1. **Record the score and context.** Note date, topic, preparation time, sleep, timing problems, and any accommodations used. → *Expect:* the result is documented accurately.
2. **Separate score categories.** Divide lost points by content, process, timing, directions, presentation, or grading dispute. → *Expect:* mistakes are grouped by cause.
3. **Correct each missed item.** Rewrite the correct solution or answer in full, including the rule or source that supports it. → *Expect:* every miss has a corrected version.
4. **Find repeated patterns.** Look for the same concept, question type, or careless habit appearing more than once. → *Expect:* the top 2-4 patterns are visible.
5. **Ask for clarification when needed.** Bring specific questions to the instructor, TA, tutor, or study group. → *Expect:* confusing grading or concepts are explained.
6. **Check for legitimate grading errors.** Compare the rubric to your answer respectfully and only raise clear issues. → *Expect:* any regrade request is evidence-based.
7. **Convert patterns into practice tasks.** Choose drills, reading, flashcards, office hours, or timed practice based on the error causes. → *Expect:* the next study plan targets actual misses.
8. **Protect controlled exam materials.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* posting, sharing, or copying restricted exam content can violate academic rules; confirm what is allowed before sharing anything. → *Expect:* review stays within course policy.

## Decision points

- Most lost points are careless errors → add checking routines and slow first-pass reading.
- Most lost points are content gaps → revisit instruction and practice from simpler examples.
- A regrade may lower the score → ask about regrade policy before submitting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Only reading the grade:** detect no written corrections → schedule a review session and make an error log.
- **F2 Blaming the grader too soon:** detect no rubric evidence → gather exact line references before asking.
- **F3 Studying everything equally:** detect a broad vague plan → focus on repeated high-point losses first.
- **F4 Losing access to feedback:** detect an online review window closing soon → download or record permitted feedback immediately.

## Verification

Every missed or weak item has a cause label, corrected answer, and linked study action, and any regrade question is supported by the rubric.

## Variations

- Standardized tests: you may receive only a score report, so review by skill category rather than item.
- Writing exams: compare comments to the rubric and revise one paragraph as practice.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Respect academic integrity rules, keep grades private, and do not share controlled exam questions without permission.
