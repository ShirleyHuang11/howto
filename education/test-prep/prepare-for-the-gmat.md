## Goal

You prepare for the GMAT with a business-school-focused score target, timed practice, and a reporting plan that supports your applications.

## Preconditions

- A list of target MBA or business programs and their test policies.
- Access to official GMAT practice exams or reputable adaptive practice.
- A calculator policy and scratch-work setup matching the test you will take.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the required test and version.** Check whether each program accepts GMAT, GRE, waiver requests, or no test. → *Expect:* a written testing requirement for every target program.
2. **Set a target score range.** Compare admitted-student score data with your application profile. → *Expect:* a score goal that is ambitious but tied to actual programs.
3. **Take an official-style diagnostic.** Complete Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning, and Data Insights under timed conditions. → *Expect:* baseline section scores and pacing notes.
4. **Audit missed questions.** Label misses by concept, reasoning trap, time sink, or careless reading. → *Expect:* a prioritized weakness list.
5. **Drill quantitative reasoning.** Practice number properties, algebra, word problems, rates, probability, and data sufficiency-style reasoning where relevant. → *Expect:* corrected solutions show the fastest valid method.
6. **Practice verbal precision.** Work on critical reasoning, reading comprehension, and argument evaluation with written justification. → *Expect:* answers are selected from evidence, not intuition alone.
7. **Train Data Insights workflows.** Practice tables, charts, multi-source reasoning, and two-part analysis with strict time checkpoints. → *Expect:* you can decide quickly when to calculate, estimate, or skip.
8. **Simulate adaptive testing.** Take full practice exams without pausing or looking up explanations mid-test. → *Expect:* a stable practice score range and known fatigue pattern.
9. **Finalize test-day and score-report choices.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* once scores are sent or accepted according to the testing service's rules, programs may receive them; confirm recipients and policies first. → *Expect:* registration, ID, accommodations, and reporting choices are saved.

## Decision points

- GRE practice score is stronger than GMAT practice score → consider using the GRE if target schools accept both.
- Data Insights is the weakest section → add mixed data interpretation practice rather than more pure math drills.
- Application deadline is near → prioritize official practice exams and score reporting logistics over broad content review.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adaptive practice score swings:** detect large score changes between tests → compare timing and question difficulty, then run focused section practice.
- **F2 Quant work is too slow:** detect correct answers that take too long → learn estimation, backsolving, and faster algebraic setup.
- **F3 Critical reasoning traps repeat:** detect attraction to extreme or out-of-scope answers → write the conclusion and assumption before choosing.
- **F4 Deadline missed for score delivery:** detect a reporting date after application deadline → contact the program and consider submitting unofficial scores if allowed.

## Verification

You have completed official-style full practice tests, reviewed every miss, reached or revised your target score range, and confirmed registration, ID, accommodations, and score-reporting instructions.

## Variations

- Online GMAT: room scan, equipment checks, and whiteboard rules differ from a test center.
- Executive MBA or waiver programs: work experience or prior coursework may matter more than a test score.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because fees, admissions deadlines, accommodations, and score reporting can affect applications. Use official portals, confirm school codes, and do not use unauthorized test content.
