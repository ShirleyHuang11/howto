## Goal

You prepare to answer oral exam questions clearly, support claims with evidence, and handle follow-up questions without freezing.

## Preconditions

- The oral exam format, topic list, language, time limit, and grading rubric.
- Course notes, readings, slides, or approved source materials.
- A way to record practice answers.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the exam format.** Ask whether it is a presentation, viva, language interview, defense, or question bank. → *Expect:* you know timing, allowed notes, and evaluation criteria.
2. **Create topic prompts.** Convert the syllabus or rubric into likely questions beginning with explain, compare, defend, apply, or critique. → *Expect:* a realistic practice question list.
3. **Build answer outlines.** For each major topic, prepare a claim, 2-3 supporting points, evidence, and a concise close. → *Expect:* outlines fit the expected answer length.
4. **Practice aloud under time.** Record responses without reading full scripts. → *Expect:* answers sound spoken, organized, and within time.
5. **Prepare follow-up handling.** Practice clarifying the question, pausing, and answering from first principles when surprised. → *Expect:* you have phrases for thinking without rambling.
6. **Get feedback from another person.** Ask a teacher, peer, tutor, or advisor to ask unpredictable questions. → *Expect:* feedback identifies clarity, accuracy, and delivery issues.
7. **Tune delivery.** Work on volume, pace, eye contact or camera focus, and pronunciation if relevant. → *Expect:* recordings are understandable and not rushed.
8. **Prepare exam materials and setting.** Bring permitted notes, ID, slides, or equipment; test microphone and camera if online. → *Expect:* logistics do not distract from answering.

## Decision points

- The exam is a language oral → prioritize fluency, repair phrases, and interactive listening.
- The exam is a thesis defense or viva → know methods, limitations, and why choices were justified.
- Notes are allowed → make cue cards with headings only, not a script you will read.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Memorized script fails:** detect freezing when wording changes → practice outlines and paraphrasing.
- **F2 Answers are too long:** detect running over time → use claim, evidence, significance, stop.
- **F3 Weak evidence:** detect unsupported opinions → attach each claim to a reading, data point, or example.
- **F4 Panic after a hard question:** detect silence or rambling → ask for clarification, restate what you know, and answer the nearest defensible point.

## Verification

You can answer representative questions aloud within the time limit, support claims with evidence, and recover from at least one unexpected follow-up in practice.

## Variations

- Online oral exam: test internet, camera, microphone, screen sharing, and backup contact method.
- Group oral exam: practice handoffs and avoid repeating another speaker's answer.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect unpublished research, patient/client data, and exam prompts if the institution treats them as confidential.
