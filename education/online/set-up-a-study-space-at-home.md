## Goal

You create a home study space that reduces friction, supports focus, and has the tools needed for online or independent learning.

## Preconditions

- You have a place you can use consistently, even if it is shared or temporary.
- You know what devices, books, supplies, and internet access your study requires.
- You can make basic changes without blocking other household needs.

## Steps

1. **Choose the most reliable location.** Prioritize consistent access, lighting, seating, outlet availability, and low interruption over appearance. → *Expect:* one spot is chosen for regular study.
2. **Clear the work surface.** Remove unrelated items and keep only current study tools, water, and necessary supplies. → *Expect:* the surface is ready for one focused task.
3. **Set up ergonomics.** Adjust chair, screen height, keyboard, lighting, and notebook placement to reduce strain. → *Expect:* you can sit or stand comfortably for a study block.
4. **Stabilize internet and power.** Place chargers, test Wi-Fi, and use headphones or Ethernet if needed. → *Expect:* video calls, downloads, and course platforms work from the spot.
5. **Create a supply station.** Keep pens, paper, calculator, textbooks, flashcards, and device chargers nearby. → *Expect:* you do not leave the space for routine materials.
6. **Add distraction controls.** Move entertainment devices, silence notifications, use website blockers if needed, and tell household members your study times. → *Expect:* interruptions are reduced before studying begins.
7. **Prepare a reset routine.** At the end of each session, file papers, plug in devices, and set out the next task. → *Expect:* the space is ready for the next session.

## Decision points

- Shared home is noisy -> use headphones, a library, a school space, or a scheduled quiet period.
- Space must be packed away daily -> create a portable study kit in a box or backpack.
- Video meetings are required -> test camera angle, microphone, lighting, and background privacy.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Uncomfortable setup:** detect neck, wrist, or back strain -> adjust height, add support, or shorten sessions with movement breaks.
- **F2 Constant interruptions:** detect repeated household disruptions -> post a schedule or move to a more predictable location.
- **F3 Supply scavenging:** detect lost time finding materials -> restock the study station after every session.
- **F4 Digital distraction:** detect tabs or apps pulling attention -> use a separate browser profile or blocker during study blocks.

## Verification

You can start a scheduled study block in under two minutes with needed materials, working internet, power, and minimal predictable interruptions.

## Variations

- Small apartment or shared room: use a folding tray, lap desk, or portable kit instead of a permanent desk.
- Family study space: create separate bins and headphones for each learner to reduce conflict.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep cords out of walkways, avoid overloaded outlets, protect private documents from shared spaces, and position screens so sensitive school or work information is not visible to others.
