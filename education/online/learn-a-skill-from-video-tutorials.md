## Goal

You turn video tutorials into actual skill practice by choosing a path, doing the work alongside the video, and producing independent output.

## Preconditions

- You know the skill you want and the level you are starting from.
- You have the required tools, materials, software, or practice environment.
- You can pause, rewind, and take notes while practicing.

## Steps

1. **Define a concrete target.** Choose an output you can make or task you can perform after the tutorials. → *Expect:* a practical finish line, not an endless playlist.
2. **Select a coherent tutorial sequence.** Prefer one instructor or structured playlist for the basics before mixing sources. → *Expect:* videos build on each other in a sensible order.
3. **Set up the practice environment.** Install software, gather materials, create files, or prepare workspace before watching. → *Expect:* you can follow along without constant setup breaks.
4. **Watch actively and pause often.** Recreate each demonstrated step yourself before moving on. → *Expect:* your screen, project, or physical result matches the lesson checkpoint.
5. **Take action notes.** Record commands, settings, mistakes, and principles in your own words. → *Expect:* notes help you repeat the process without replaying the whole video.
6. **Do a variation without copying.** Change the prompt, dataset, design, recipe, song, or problem while using the same technique. → *Expect:* you can transfer the skill beyond the exact tutorial.
7. **Build a small independent project.** Make something from memory, using notes only when needed. → *Expect:* a finished artifact shows real learning.
8. **Review gaps and choose the next lesson.** Identify what still required copying and study that specific gap. → *Expect:* the next tutorial is targeted, not random.

## Decision points

- Tutorial skips explanations -> use it for demonstration only and find a concept-focused source.
- Tool version differs -> check official documentation or comments for updated steps.
- You keep copying without understanding -> slow down and practice smaller variations.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Tutorial bingeing:** detect hours watched with no output -> stop and reproduce the last lesson from scratch.
- **F2 Version mismatch:** detect menus, commands, or interfaces that no longer exist -> search the tool's current documentation.
- **F3 Hidden prerequisite:** detect terms or steps you cannot follow -> learn that prerequisite first.
- **F4 No feedback:** detect repeated mistakes you cannot diagnose -> ask a community, teacher, or peer with screenshots and exact steps.

## Verification

You can perform the target skill or produce a small independent artifact without replaying the tutorial step by step.

## Variations

- Software tutorials: keep a changelog of tool versions and commands that differ from the video.
- Physical skills: record yourself practicing and compare safely against the demonstration.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most skills. For tools, downloads, or plugins, use trusted sources; for physical, electrical, food, health, or fitness skills, follow qualified safety guidance rather than relying on an entertainer's video.
