## Goal

You create a self-study roadmap with a clear outcome, ordered topics, resources, practice tasks, and checkpoints.

## Preconditions

- You know the subject or skill area and why you want to learn it.
- You have a realistic weekly time budget.
- You can access books, courses, documentation, exercises, mentors, or communities.

## Steps

1. **Define the end capability.** Write what you should be able to do, solve, write, build, or explain. → *Expect:* a concrete outcome that can be tested.
2. **Assess your starting point.** Take a diagnostic quiz, attempt a sample task, or list what you already know. → *Expect:* obvious gaps and strengths are visible.
3. **Break the subject into modules.** Order foundations, core concepts, practice domains, and advanced topics. → *Expect:* the roadmap has a sequence rather than a pile of links.
4. **Choose one primary resource per module.** Add secondary references only for stuck points or alternate explanations. → *Expect:* each module has a main path.
5. **Add practice and retrieval.** Pair every reading or video block with exercises, projects, flashcards, explanations, or quizzes. → *Expect:* the plan tests recall and application.
6. **Schedule checkpoints.** Set dates for mini-projects, mock exams, tutor review, or portfolio review. → *Expect:* progress can be measured before the final goal.
7. **Limit the first cycle.** Plan 2-6 weeks in detail and leave later topics flexible. → *Expect:* the roadmap is useful without pretending to predict everything.
8. **Revise from evidence.** After each checkpoint, adjust pace, resources, or module order. → *Expect:* the roadmap improves based on performance.

## Decision points

- Goal requires certification or credit -> align the roadmap to the official exam blueprint or syllabus.
- You lack prerequisites -> add a short prerequisite module before the main path.
- You need accountability -> add a study group, tutor, public project, or deadline.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Resource hoarding:** detect many saved links and no practice -> pick one primary resource and start module one.
- **F2 Overambitious schedule:** detect repeated missed blocks -> reduce scope or extend dates.
- **F3 No feedback loop:** detect uncertainty about progress -> add quizzes, projects, or expert review.
- **F4 Skipping foundations:** detect advanced material failing repeatedly -> return to prerequisite concepts.

## Verification

The roadmap lists the target outcome, starting level, ordered modules, primary resources, practice tasks, checkpoints, and a first scheduled study block.

## Variations

- Exam prep: start from the official test outline and schedule full-length practice tests.
- Creative skills: use finished pieces, critique, and revision cycles as checkpoints.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid sharing private notes or work files in public communities without review, and verify paid mentorship, bootcamp, or credential claims before spending money.
