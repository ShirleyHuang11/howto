## Goal

You turn a video lecture into usable notes that capture the argument, examples, and questions to review later.

## Preconditions

- Access to the video lecture and any slides, transcript, or readings.
- A note-taking tool with timestamps or headings.
- A quiet enough environment to pause and replay short sections.

## Steps

1. **Preview the lecture.** Read the title, objectives, slides, chapter headings, or transcript summary before starting. → *Expect:* you know the main topics before taking notes.
2. **Set up a note structure.** Create sections for key ideas, examples, questions, and action items. → *Expect:* the page is ready before the lecture starts.
3. **Watch the first pass for meaning.** Play at normal speed or slightly faster, pausing only for major points and unclear terms. → *Expect:* notes capture the lecture's structure instead of a transcript.
4. **Timestamp difficult moments.** Add the video time next to confusing explanations, formulas, demos, or claims to verify. → *Expect:* you can jump back to exact points later.
5. **Write concepts in your own words.** After each major section, pause and summarize the idea without copying the instructor's wording. → *Expect:* each section has a short explanation you understand.
6. **Capture examples and worked steps.** For calculations, diagrams, or procedures, note the inputs, method, and result. → *Expect:* you can reproduce the example without replaying the whole lecture.
7. **Mark questions and follow-ups.** Flag anything unclear, missing, or contradicted by readings. → *Expect:* open questions are visible for office hours, forums, or later study.
8. **Review and condense within 24 hours.** Rewrite the notes into a short summary and convert key facts into flashcards or practice prompts. → *Expect:* the final notes are shorter, clearer, and study-ready.

## Decision points

- Lecture is dense or technical → slow playback and pause after each worked example.
- Transcript is available → use it to check missed wording, not as a substitute for understanding.
- Lecture is mostly demonstration → take process notes with screenshots only where allowed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Notes become a transcript:** detect long copied paragraphs → rewrite as bullets that state claims, evidence, and examples.
- **F2 Missing context:** detect formulas or terms with no definitions → add the surrounding concept and source timestamp.
- **F3 Too many screenshots:** detect images with no explanation → add captions describing what each screenshot proves or demonstrates.
- **F4 No review happens:** detect notes that are never reopened → schedule a 10-minute review immediately after the lecture block.

## Verification

You can explain the lecture's main point, find any important timestamp quickly, and answer or generate practice questions from your notes.

## Variations

- STEM lecture: include definitions, assumptions, units, and each step of worked problems.
- Humanities lecture: track thesis, evidence, counterarguments, and quoted passages with timestamps.
- Mobile viewing: use shorter shorthand notes during the video and clean them up on a keyboard afterward.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not upload copyrighted lecture videos, private class recordings, or classmates' comments to public tools without permission.
