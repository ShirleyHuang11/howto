## Goal

You keep a visible record of what you are learning, what still needs practice, and what to study next.

## Preconditions

- A course, textbook, skill plan, or syllabus to track.
- A notebook, spreadsheet, learning app, or task manager.
- A regular study schedule you can review at least weekly.

## Steps

1. **List the learning outcomes.** Copy the course modules, exam objectives, or skill milestones into one tracker. → *Expect:* every major topic appears as a row or checklist item.
2. **Choose simple status labels.** Use labels such as `not started`, `learning`, `practice`, `review`, and `mastered`. → *Expect:* each topic can be marked without inventing a new label every time.
3. **Record evidence, not feelings only.** For each topic, note quiz scores, completed exercises, projects, flashcard accuracy, or a short explanation you wrote from memory. → *Expect:* progress is tied to observable work.
4. **Add next actions.** Write the next concrete task for weak or unfinished topics, such as "redo lesson 3 exercise 4" or "make 10 flashcards." → *Expect:* the tracker tells you what to do next without rereading the whole course.
5. **Update after each study session.** Spend two minutes marking completed work, questions, and blockers. → *Expect:* the tracker reflects your latest session.
6. **Review weekly.** Sort or scan for stale, weak, or deadline-sensitive topics and schedule the next study blocks. → *Expect:* the coming week has focused study tasks.
7. **Archive completed milestones.** Move mastered topics or finished modules to a done section while keeping scores and notes. → *Expect:* completed work is preserved but no longer clutters daily planning.

## Decision points

- Tracker takes longer than studying → reduce it to topic, status, evidence, and next action.
- Scores are high but recall is weak → add closed-book retrieval practice before marking mastered.
- Many topics stay blocked → ask an instructor, peer, tutor, or study community for help.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague progress labels:** detect many items marked "done" with no proof → add a score, deliverable, or recall note for each completed item.
- **F2 Tracker becomes a chore:** detect skipped updates for a week → simplify the fields and update only at the end of study sessions.
- **F3 Easy topics dominate:** detect repeated work on comfortable modules → schedule the weakest high-value topics first.
- **F4 Deadlines surprise you:** detect assignments or exams missing from the tracker → add due dates and sort by date during the weekly review.

## Verification

Your tracker shows every required topic, its current status, evidence of progress, and the next study action for anything not yet mastered.

## Variations

- Spreadsheet: use filters for status and due date, plus conditional formatting for overdue items.
- Learning management system: copy grades and module completion into your own tracker so you are not limited by the platform view.
- Certification study: align rows to the official exam objective list rather than to course video titles.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep grades, school IDs, and private feedback out of public templates or shared screenshots unless you intentionally remove identifying details.
