## Goal

You maintain progress in self-study by designing a system that works even when motivation is low.

## Preconditions

- You have a subject, course, or roadmap you want to continue.
- You can schedule regular study time.
- You are willing to adjust the plan based on evidence rather than guilt.

## Steps

1. **Reconnect the goal to a real use.** Write what the learning will let you do and when you want to use it. → *Expect:* the study has a purpose beyond "I should."
2. **Set a small weekly target.** Choose hours, modules, exercises, pages, or project milestones that fit your actual calendar. → *Expect:* the target is challenging but believable.
3. **Make the next action obvious.** End each session by writing the exact next task. → *Expect:* starting again does not require re-planning.
4. **Use visible progress tracking.** Track completed sessions, practice problems, pages, flashcards, or project commits. → *Expect:* progress is observable even when results feel slow.
5. **Build accountability.** Tell a peer, join a study group, book a tutor, or schedule a check-in. → *Expect:* someone or something expects evidence of progress.
6. **Alternate input and output.** Pair videos or reading with recall, exercises, writing, teaching, or projects. → *Expect:* study feels active and produces feedback.
7. **Plan for low-energy days.** Create a minimum task such as five flashcards, one problem, or ten minutes of review. → *Expect:* a bad day does not break the chain completely.
8. **Review and adjust weekly.** Keep what worked, remove friction, and resize goals instead of quitting silently. → *Expect:* the plan changes before motivation collapses.

## Decision points

- You consistently avoid one task -> diagnose whether it is too hard, too vague, or not valuable.
- Progress matters for a deadline -> add external accountability and more frequent checkpoints.
- You feel burned out -> reduce intensity, sleep, and return with a smaller plan rather than forcing long sessions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 All-or-nothing plan:** detect skipped study after one missed day -> restart with the minimum task immediately.
- **F2 Vague goals:** detect "study more" with no action -> define the next lesson, exercise, or output.
- **F3 No reward or feedback:** detect boredom and uncertainty -> add quizzes, projects, public notes, or mentor review.
- **F4 Overloaded schedule:** detect chronic missed blocks -> reduce commitments or move study to a protected time.

## Verification

You have a weekly target, visible tracker, next-action list, accountability mechanism, and a minimum task for low-energy days.

## Variations

- Language learning: track streaks cautiously and prioritize speaking, listening, reading, and spaced repetition.
- Technical learning: use small projects and tests as feedback instead of only counting videos watched.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid public accountability systems that expose private goals or personal data, and treat prolonged exhaustion or distress as a sign to reduce load or seek support rather than as a discipline problem.
