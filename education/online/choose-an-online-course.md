## Goal

You choose an online course that matches your learning goal, level, schedule, budget, and need for credentials.

## Preconditions

- You can describe the skill, topic, or credential you want.
- You know your available time, budget, and preferred learning format.
- You can evaluate course previews, syllabi, or reviews.

## Steps

1. **Write the learning outcome.** State what you want to be able to do after the course, not just the topic name. → *Expect:* a measurable goal such as "build a basic React app" or "read Spanish news articles."
2. **Set constraints.** Decide budget, weekly hours, deadline, language, accessibility needs, and whether a certificate matters. → *Expect:* courses outside your constraints are easy to reject.
3. **Compare syllabi and previews.** Look for prerequisites, projects, assessments, instructor credentials, update date, and sample lessons. → *Expect:* you know what the course actually teaches.
4. **Check level fit.** Match beginner, intermediate, or advanced content to your current skill by trying a preview quiz or lesson. → *Expect:* the course feels challenging but not opaque.
5. **Evaluate proof of learning.** Prefer courses with exercises, projects, feedback, labs, or graded work over passive video only. → *Expect:* the course includes ways to practice and verify skill.
6. **Read recent reviews critically.** Focus on comments about content accuracy, support, pacing, and outdated material. → *Expect:* known weaknesses are acceptable or manageable.
7. **Choose and record a finish plan.** Pick one course and schedule start date, weekly blocks, and completion criteria. → *Expect:* you have a chosen course and a realistic plan to finish it.

## Decision points

- You need academic credit -> verify accreditation and transfer approval before enrolling.
- You need job evidence -> choose a project-based course with portfolio output rather than only a certificate.
- Reviews say content is outdated -> choose a newer course for fast-changing fields like software, law, or platform tools.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Course too hard:** detect repeated confusion in the first module -> switch to prerequisite material before refund deadline.
- **F2 Passive learning trap:** detect many videos but no practice -> add exercises or choose a project-based alternative.
- **F3 Certificate mismatch:** detect that the credential is not recognized by your target school or employer -> verify before paying.
- **F4 Time underestimation:** detect missed sessions in week one -> reduce course load or extend deadline.

## Verification

You have selected one course whose syllabus, level, cost, schedule, and evidence of learning match your stated outcome.

## Variations

- Career reskilling: prioritize projects, feedback, and employer-recognized credentials.
- Hobby learning: prioritize instructor clarity, enjoyment, and sustainable pace over certificates.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but avoid overpaying for vague promises. Do not share unnecessary personal data, and verify refund policies, recurring subscriptions, and credential claims before payment.
