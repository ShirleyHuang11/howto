## Goal

You choose extracurricular activities that fit the child's interests, schedule, wellbeing, and family constraints.

## Preconditions

- A list of available school, community, sports, arts, service, or club options.
- The child's interests, temperament, and current workload.
- Budget, transportation, and caregiver availability.

## Steps

1. **Ask the child what interests them.** Discuss activities they want to try, skills they want to build, and what they do not enjoy. → *Expect:* the child's voice guides the search.
2. **List practical constraints.** Note cost, schedule, location, equipment, transportation, and parent volunteer requirements. → *Expect:* impossible options are visible early.
3. **Check safety and supervision.** Review adult supervision, background-check practices where applicable, emergency procedures, and physical risks. → *Expect:* safety questions are answered before enrollment.
4. **Balance the weekly schedule.** Add schoolwork, sleep, meals, chores, family time, and downtime before committing. → *Expect:* the activity does not crowd out basic needs.
5. **Try before committing when possible.** Attend an open house, trial class, practice, or meeting. → *Expect:* the child experiences the real environment.
6. **Choose a manageable number.** Start with one or two meaningful commitments rather than filling every afternoon. → *Expect:* participation is sustainable.
7. **Register and record details.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* fees or team commitments may be nonrefundable, so confirm cost, schedule, refund policy, and required forms before paying. → *Expect:* registration, calendar dates, and contact information are saved.
8. **Review after the trial period.** Ask the child about enjoyment, stress, friendships, and workload. → *Expect:* you know whether to continue, adjust, or stop.

## Decision points

- Child wants to quit quickly → distinguish normal adjustment from persistent distress, unsafe coaching, or overload.
- Activity affects sleep or grades → reduce commitments before performance or health suffers.
- Cost is a barrier → ask about scholarships, used equipment, school clubs, or community programs.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overscheduling:** detect fatigue, late homework, or irritability → drop or pause the lowest-priority activity.
- **F2 Parent-driven choice:** detect the child attending only to please adults → revisit the child's interests.
- **F3 Hidden costs:** detect surprise uniforms, travel, or competition fees → request a full cost list before continuing.
- **F4 Unsafe environment:** detect bullying, harsh coaching, poor supervision, or ignored injuries → remove the child and report concerns if needed.

## Verification

The child is registered for an activity they want to try, the family can meet the time and cost commitment, and safety, schedule, and communication details are documented.

## Variations

- Competitive sports: ask about concussion protocols, rest, travel, and coach qualifications.
- Arts programs: confirm recital, supply, costume, and practice expectations.
- High school college planning: depth and authentic commitment matter more than a long activity list.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because activities involve money, transportation, child supervision, and possible injury. Confirm adult contacts, pickup rules, medical forms, and photo permissions before participation.
