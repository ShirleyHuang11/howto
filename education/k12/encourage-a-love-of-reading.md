## Goal

You make reading a regular, enjoyable part of a child's life without turning every book into a test.

## Preconditions

- Access to books, audiobooks, comics, magazines, library materials, or ebooks at the child's level and interests.
- A routine time when reading can happen without pressure.
- Openness to different formats and genres.

## Steps

1. **Learn the child's interests.** Ask about favorite topics, shows, games, animals, sports, mysteries, humor, or real-world questions. → *Expect:* book choices connect to genuine curiosity.
2. **Offer varied formats.** Include picture books, chapter books, nonfiction, graphic novels, audiobooks, poetry, and magazines. → *Expect:* the child sees reading as broader than school books.
3. **Visit a library or browse together.** Let the child choose some books freely and choose a few stretch options together. → *Expect:* the child leaves with appealing choices.
4. **Create a pleasant reading routine.** Set a short daily or frequent reading time with comfortable seating and few distractions. → *Expect:* reading has a predictable low-pressure place.
5. **Read aloud and discuss naturally.** Share funny, surprising, or interesting moments without constant comprehension questions. → *Expect:* reading feels social and enjoyable.
6. **Respect abandon decisions.** Let the child stop a book that is too hard, boring, or upsetting after giving it a fair try. → *Expect:* the child trusts that choice matters.
7. **Connect books to life.** Pair reading with activities, conversations, library events, recipes, maps, or projects. → *Expect:* books lead to real engagement.
8. **Model reading.** Let the child see adults reading for information, work, or pleasure. → *Expect:* reading looks useful and normal.

## Decision points

- Child says reading is hard → check level fit and consider screening for vision, decoding, or language issues.
- Child only wants one genre → allow it while occasionally offering nearby options.
- Audiobooks are preferred → use them; they build vocabulary and story knowledge, especially when paired with print when possible.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Reading becomes punishment:** detect reading assigned only after misbehavior → separate reading from discipline.
- **F2 Books are too difficult:** detect avoidance or guessing → choose easier high-interest texts.
- **F3 Adult controls all choices:** detect the child rejecting every selection → give real choice within acceptable options.
- **F4 Progress pressure crowds out joy:** detect constant tracking or quizzing → replace some sessions with shared read-aloud time.

## Verification

The child voluntarily engages with reading material, can name books or topics they like, and has a repeatable routine for accessing new reading choices.

## Variations

- Reluctant reader: start with short, funny, visual, or interest-based texts.
- Advanced reader: provide variety, discuss themes, and protect time for deeper books.
- Multilingual family: reading in any home language supports literacy and identity.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use age-appropriate materials, respect sensitive topics, and protect library account, school login, and ebook account information.
