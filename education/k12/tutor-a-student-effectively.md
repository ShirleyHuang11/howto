## Goal

You run a tutoring session that diagnoses the student's needs, teaches actively, and leaves the student with a clear next step.

## Preconditions

- Permission from the parent, guardian, school, or program as appropriate.
- The student's assignment, curriculum topic, or target skill.
- A safe tutoring setting with appropriate supervision and communication boundaries.

## Steps

1. **Set the session objective.** State the skill or task you will work on today and how success will be checked. → *Expect:* the student knows the purpose of the session.
2. **Check prior understanding.** Ask the student to solve a warm-up problem or explain the topic in their own words. → *Expect:* you see the current gap before teaching.
3. **Teach one small chunk.** Explain or model only the next needed concept, using age-appropriate language. → *Expect:* the student can repeat the idea or step back to you.
4. **Guide practice.** Work through a similar example with prompts, gradually reducing help. → *Expect:* the student begins doing more of the thinking.
5. **Switch to independent practice.** Give a fresh problem, passage, or prompt for the student to complete without immediate hints. → *Expect:* you can assess whether learning transferred.
6. **Give specific feedback.** Name what worked, correct one or two priority errors, and have the student revise. → *Expect:* the student knows exactly what to keep or change.
7. **Document progress.** Record the objective, work completed, errors noticed, and next recommended practice. → *Expect:* the parent, teacher, or next session has a usable note.
8. **Close safely and professionally.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* maintain agreed supervision and communication boundaries; do not move to private channels without guardian or program approval. → *Expect:* the session ends with clear next steps and no boundary ambiguity.

## Decision points

- Student lacks prerequisite knowledge → reteach the prerequisite before pushing the current assignment.
- Student is anxious or ashamed → reduce difficulty, normalize mistakes, and rebuild with a small success.
- Parent asks for guaranteed grades → explain what tutoring can support and what depends on attendance, practice, and classroom expectations.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overhelping:** detect the student completing work only when prompted → switch to easier independent practice.
- **F2 Misaligned content:** detect methods that conflict with the teacher's approach → ask for class notes or coordinate with the teacher.
- **F3 Weak documentation:** detect no record of progress → write a brief session note immediately after tutoring.
- **F4 Boundary problem:** detect private messaging, secrecy, or unsupervised changes → stop and restore the approved communication plan.

## Verification

The student can complete at least one aligned task more independently than at the start, and the session note records objective, evidence, and next practice.

## Variations

- Reading tutoring: include decoding, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension checks.
- Math tutoring: require the student to explain both the procedure and why it works.
- Online tutoring: use screen share, shared whiteboards, and guardian-approved recording or chat settings.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because tutoring involves children and education records. Follow program safeguarding rules, keep sessions observable, and protect student data and contact information.
