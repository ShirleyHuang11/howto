## Goal

You arrive at a parent-teacher conference ready to understand the child's progress, ask useful questions, and agree on next steps.

## Preconditions

- The conference date, time, format, and teacher name.
- Recent grades, assignments, behavior notes, test scores, or school messages.
- Questions or concerns from the child and caregiver.

## Steps

1. **Review school information.** Check grades, attendance, teacher comments, assignments, and assessment reports. → *Expect:* you know the main patterns before the meeting.
2. **Ask the child for input.** Ask what is going well, what feels hard, and what they want the teacher to know. → *Expect:* the child's perspective is included.
3. **Choose priority questions.** Write 3-5 questions about learning, behavior, social adjustment, or support. → *Expect:* the meeting has a focused agenda.
4. **Gather examples.** Bring work samples, dates of concerns, messages, or notes from home. → *Expect:* discussion is based on specifics.
5. **Plan a collaborative tone.** Frame concerns as shared problem solving, such as "What can we try next?" → *Expect:* the meeting is more likely to produce practical steps.
6. **Confirm logistics.** Check whether the meeting is in person, phone, or video, and whether interpretation or accessibility support is needed. → *Expect:* you can attend without avoidable barriers.
7. **Take notes during the conference.** Record strengths, concerns, teacher recommendations, and agreed actions. → *Expect:* you leave with a written record.
8. **Follow up afterward.** Share age-appropriate takeaways with the child and send any promised information to the teacher. → *Expect:* next steps are understood at home and school.

## Decision points

- Concerns may involve disability or accommodations → ask whether a 504 or IEP evaluation or meeting is appropriate.
- Meeting time is too short → ask for a follow-up conference focused on the priority concern.
- Language access is needed → request an interpreter before the conference.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too many topics:** detect a rushed meeting with no decisions → pick the top two concerns and schedule follow-up.
- **F2 Emotional escalation:** detect blame or defensiveness → pause, restate the shared goal, and return to evidence.
- **F3 No action plan:** detect general comments only → ask who will do what by when.
- **F4 Missing child perspective:** detect adult-only assumptions → talk with the child afterward and update the teacher if needed.

## Verification

You have written notes that identify the child's strengths, concerns, agreed next steps, responsible people, and any follow-up date.

## Variations

- Secondary school: the student may attend part of the conference and help set goals.
- Virtual conference: test the link, microphone, and camera before the appointment.
- Special education: bring the current IEP, 504 plan, progress reports, and service questions.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect student records and discuss sensitive behavior, health, disability, or family issues only with appropriate school staff.
