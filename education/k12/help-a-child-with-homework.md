## Goal

You help a child complete homework while building independence, confidence, and honest learning.

## Preconditions

- The assignment instructions, due date, and required materials.
- A quiet workspace with basic supplies.
- An agreement that the adult helps the child think, but does not do the work for them.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the assignment.** Ask the child to explain what they need to do and check the teacher's instructions together. → *Expect:* both of you understand the task and due date.
2. **Set a short work block.** Choose a realistic timer, usually 10-30 minutes depending on age and attention. → *Expect:* the child knows when the next break will happen.
3. **Start with what the child knows.** Ask them to try the first problem, sentence, or step while you observe. → *Expect:* you can see whether the issue is understanding, attention, or confidence.
4. **Prompt instead of answering.** Use questions such as "What did the teacher do in class?" or "What information is given?" → *Expect:* the child takes the next step themselves.
5. **Model one similar example if needed.** Make a new example with different numbers or words, then have the child return to the assignment. → *Expect:* the child can transfer the method to their own work.
6. **Check for effort and accuracy.** Review completed work together, asking the child to explain corrections. → *Expect:* mistakes become learning moments rather than adult edits.
7. **Record unresolved questions.** Write a note for the teacher or mark questions to ask in class. → *Expect:* blockers are documented instead of hidden.
8. **End with organization.** Put the assignment, book, device, and supplies where they need to go for school. → *Expect:* the homework is ready to turn in.

## Decision points

- Child is stuck for more than 10 minutes → step back to a simpler example or contact the teacher.
- Child is upset or exhausted → take a break, then resume or write a teacher note if work cannot be completed.
- Assignment requires independent work → limit help to clarifying directions and encouraging effort.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adult takes over:** detect yourself giving answers or rewriting work → stop and switch to prompts or examples.
- **F2 Power struggle:** detect arguing or tears → pause, validate frustration, and restart with a smaller task.
- **F3 Missing instructions:** detect confusion about requirements → check the class portal, folder, or message the teacher.
- **F4 Repeated difficulty:** detect the same skill causing problems across assignments → request teacher guidance or extra support.

## Verification

The child can explain what they completed, unresolved questions are written down, and the work is packed or submitted according to the teacher's instructions.

## Variations

- Early elementary: use short sessions, manipulatives, and reading directions aloud.
- Middle school: shift responsibility to the student by having them manage the planner and materials.
- Online homework: confirm the platform shows submitted or saved status before closing.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect student login credentials and avoid uploading a child's work, image, or school information to public tools without permission.
