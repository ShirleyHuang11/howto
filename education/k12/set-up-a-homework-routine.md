## Goal

You create a predictable homework routine that helps a child start, finish, and turn in schoolwork with less conflict.

## Preconditions

- The child's typical homework load and due-date system.
- A workspace, supplies, and device access if needed.
- A caregiver who can check the routine consistently at first.

## Steps

1. **Choose a consistent homework window.** Pick a time after school, activity, snack, or dinner that fits the child's energy. → *Expect:* homework has a regular place in the day.
2. **Set up the workspace.** Put pencils, paper, chargers, books, and allowed devices in one spot. → *Expect:* the child does not need to hunt for materials.
3. **Make a start checklist.** Include unpack folder, check planner or portal, list assignments, and choose first task. → *Expect:* the child knows how to begin.
4. **Use short work blocks.** Match timers and breaks to the child's age and attention span. → *Expect:* work proceeds in manageable chunks.
5. **Rank tasks by urgency and effort.** Start with due-soon or harder tasks when energy is best. → *Expect:* important assignments are not left until bedtime.
6. **Limit distractions.** Silence nonessential notifications, remove unrelated tabs, and keep toys or entertainment out of reach. → *Expect:* attention stays on the assigned task.
7. **Build in a finish routine.** Check completion, pack papers, submit online work, charge devices, and reset supplies. → *Expect:* finished work is ready for school.
8. **Review weekly.** Adjust timing, workload, and support based on what worked and what caused conflict. → *Expect:* the routine improves instead of becoming a rigid punishment.

## Decision points

- Homework regularly exceeds teacher expectations → contact the teacher with time spent and examples.
- Child resists starting → make the first step tiny, such as opening the planner or doing one problem.
- Online portal is unreliable → keep a written backup assignment list.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Routine starts too late:** detect bedtime pressure → move homework earlier or split it into two sessions.
- **F2 Supplies missing:** detect repeated searches → create a dedicated homework kit.
- **F3 Caregiver checks too much:** detect dependence on adult reminders → shift checklist ownership to the child gradually.
- **F4 Assignments not submitted:** detect completed work still in backpack or drafts → add submission confirmation to the finish routine.

## Verification

For one school week, homework starts at the planned time, assignments are tracked, and completed work is packed or submitted with fewer last-minute problems.

## Variations

- Elementary school: use visual checklists and caregiver-led setup.
- Middle or high school: use planners, calendars, and portal checks with increasing student responsibility.
- Shared custody or multiple homes: duplicate supplies and use one shared assignment tracker.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect school portal passwords and avoid leaving student devices unlocked or unsupervised in shared spaces.
