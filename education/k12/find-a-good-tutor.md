## Goal

You choose a tutor who matches the child's learning needs, communicates clearly, and works safely with appropriate adult oversight.

## Preconditions

- The subject, grade level, goals, and current concerns.
- Recent assignments, grades, teacher feedback, or assessment information.
- A budget and preferred format: in person, online, individual, or small group.

## Steps

1. **Define the tutoring goal.** Write the target skill, timeline, and evidence of success, such as improved fraction fluency or stronger essay structure. → *Expect:* you can describe the need clearly to candidates.
2. **Ask for qualified referrals.** Contact teachers, school counselors, other parents, reputable tutoring centers, or vetted platforms. → *Expect:* you have candidates from traceable sources.
3. **Screen credentials and experience.** Check subject knowledge, grade-level experience, background checks where available, references, and special education experience if needed. → *Expect:* unqualified candidates are removed.
4. **Interview the tutor.** Ask how they diagnose gaps, plan sessions, communicate progress, and handle frustration. → *Expect:* their method is specific and child-centered.
5. **Confirm safety arrangements.** For in-person tutoring, use a visible common area; for online tutoring, keep sessions observable and use approved platforms. ⚠️ *Hazard:* do not leave a child isolated with an unvetted adult; confirm supervision and communication boundaries first. → *Expect:* safety rules are explicit before sessions begin.
6. **Run a trial session.** Have the tutor work with the child on real material while you observe enough to assess fit. → *Expect:* the child is engaged and the tutor identifies next steps.
7. **Agree on logistics and payment.** Put schedule, rate, cancellation rules, materials, and progress updates in writing. → *Expect:* there are no vague money or attendance expectations.
8. **Review progress after several sessions.** Compare the goal with work samples, confidence, teacher feedback, and tutor notes. → *Expect:* you know whether to continue, adjust, or stop.

## Decision points

- Child needs disability support → prefer tutors experienced with IEP/504 accommodations and coordinate with school staff.
- Tutor promises guaranteed grades or test scores → treat as a warning sign and ask for realistic evidence.
- Budget is tight → ask the school, library, community center, or nonprofit programs about free options.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Poor fit:** detect dread, confusion, or no progress after several sessions → discuss changes once, then switch tutors if needed.
- **F2 Tutor does homework for the child:** detect perfect work the child cannot explain → reset expectations or end the arrangement.
- **F3 Unsafe boundaries:** detect private messaging, secrecy, or unsupervised meetings → stop sessions and report if appropriate.
- **F4 Hidden costs:** detect surprise fees or required packages → pause before paying and require written terms.

## Verification

The tutor has appropriate experience, written logistics are agreed, safety boundaries are set, and the first sessions produce clear notes on the child's needs and progress.

## Variations

- Online platform: review recording, chat, privacy, and cancellation settings before booking.
- Test prep: ask for diagnostic results and a score-improvement plan tied to official test content.
- Specialized learning needs: coordinate tutoring goals with the child's school plan and service providers.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a child is working with an outside adult and school records may be shared. Share only necessary records, supervise appropriately, and keep payment through traceable methods.
