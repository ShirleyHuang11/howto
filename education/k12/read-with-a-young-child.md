## Goal

You share a book with a young child in a way that builds language, attention, comprehension, and enjoyment.

## Preconditions

- An age-appropriate book the child can see and handle.
- A comfortable, well-lit place to sit.
- Enough time to read without rushing.

## Steps

1. **Let the child choose or help choose.** Offer two or three appropriate books if the child is overwhelmed. → *Expect:* the child has some ownership of the reading time.
2. **Preview the cover.** Read the title, look at the picture, and ask what the child thinks the book may be about. → *Expect:* the child is ready to listen and predict.
3. **Read with expression.** Use a clear voice, natural pacing, and pauses for pictures or repeated phrases. → *Expect:* the child stays engaged with the story.
4. **Ask open questions sparingly.** Use prompts such as "What do you notice?" or "Why do you think that happened?" → *Expect:* the child talks about meaning, not just labels.
5. **Follow the child's attention.** Pause for comments, reread favorite pages, or shorten the session if the child is restless. → *Expect:* reading remains positive.
6. **Connect the story to life.** Briefly relate a character, place, or event to the child's experience. → *Expect:* the child links book language to real meaning.
7. **Invite participation.** Let the child turn pages, finish repeated lines, point to details, or retell a page. → *Expect:* the child is actively involved.
8. **Close with a recap.** Ask what happened first, next, and last, or what the child liked best. → *Expect:* the child recalls part of the story.

## Decision points

- Child wants the same book repeatedly → reread it; repetition supports language and confidence.
- Book is too hard → paraphrase some sentences and focus on pictures and big ideas.
- Child is learning to decode → let them read manageable words while you support fluency.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Reading becomes a quiz:** detect the child withdrawing from too many questions → return to enjoying the story.
- **F2 Book mismatch:** detect boredom or frustration → choose a shorter, more familiar, or more interesting book.
- **F3 Adult rushes:** detect skipped pages or impatient corrections → slow down and make the goal shared reading.
- **F4 Screen distraction:** detect attention drifting to devices → put devices away during the reading block.

## Verification

The child participated by listening, looking, talking, turning pages, predicting, retelling, or asking for more reading.

## Variations

- Toddler: use board books, naming, repetition, and short sessions.
- Early reader: alternate pages or sentences and praise accurate strategy use.
- Bilingual home: read in any language the adult speaks comfortably; comprehension and conversation still support literacy.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Use sturdy books for very young children, avoid small loose pieces that could be mouthed, and do not post identifiable reading videos without guardian consent.
