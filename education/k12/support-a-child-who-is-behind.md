## Goal

You identify why a child is behind academically and build a coordinated support plan that is realistic, kind, and measurable.

## Preconditions

- Recent grades, assessments, work samples, teacher comments, and attendance information.
- A conversation with the child about what feels hard.
- Willingness to coordinate with school staff, tutors, or health professionals if needed.

## Steps

1. **Define the gap specifically.** Name the skill or area, such as decoding, multiplication facts, essay planning, attendance, or assignment completion. → *Expect:* the concern is more precise than "behind."
2. **Look for patterns.** Compare work samples, grades, missed assignments, test domains, and teacher feedback. → *Expect:* recurring weak areas are visible.
3. **Talk with the child calmly.** Ask what they understand, what is confusing, and what makes school harder. → *Expect:* the child shares clues without feeling blamed.
4. **Contact the teacher.** Ask what the teacher observes, what grade-level expectations are, and what support is already available. → *Expect:* home and school have the same facts.
5. **Choose a small intervention.** Pick targeted practice, tutoring, reading time, homework routine changes, attendance support, or school-based help. → *Expect:* the plan has one or two concrete actions.
6. **Set a review date and measure.** Decide how progress will be checked, such as weekly fluency, assignment completion, quiz scores, or teacher notes. → *Expect:* improvement can be measured.
7. **Escalate when progress is limited.** If the child does not improve after targeted support, ask about evaluation, intervention teams, 504, IEP, or health screening. → *Expect:* persistent concerns move to formal support.
8. **Protect confidence.** Keep practice short, praise strategy and effort, and avoid comparing the child to siblings or classmates. → *Expect:* the child can work on gaps without shame.

## Decision points

- Gap is sudden → check for attendance, illness, stress, bullying, vision, hearing, sleep, or family changes.
- Gap has lasted across years → ask the school about formal evaluation or intervention.
- Child is multilingual → distinguish language acquisition from disability by using qualified school staff.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too broad a plan:** detect many goals at once → pick the highest-impact skill for the next two weeks.
- **F2 Blame replaces diagnosis:** detect comments about laziness without evidence → return to patterns, barriers, and supports.
- **F3 No school coordination:** detect home practice that does not match class expectations → ask the teacher for priority skills and materials.
- **F4 Progress is not measured:** detect vague improvement claims → choose a simple weekly data point.

## Verification

The child has a written support plan with a specific skill gap, action steps, responsible adults, review date, and a measurable progress indicator.

## Variations

- Reading delay: ask about phonemic awareness, decoding, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension separately.
- Math delay: separate fact fluency, conceptual understanding, problem solving, and language demands.
- Attendance-related gap: prioritize make-up plan, routines, transportation, and school reengagement.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because academic struggles may involve disability, health, bullying, or family stress. Share sensitive information only with appropriate school or care professionals and avoid shaming the child.
