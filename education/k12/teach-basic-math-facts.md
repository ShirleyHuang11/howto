## Goal

You help a child learn basic addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division facts with understanding, accuracy, and steady recall.

## Preconditions

- The fact set to practice, such as sums within 10 or multiplication by 6.
- Counters, number lines, arrays, flashcards, or a practice app.
- Short practice windows repeated over days.

## Steps

1. **Choose a small fact set.** Work on 5-10 related facts instead of a full table at once. → *Expect:* practice feels manageable.
2. **Show the meaning visually.** Use counters, dots, arrays, fingers, or a number line to represent each fact. → *Expect:* the child sees what the fact means.
3. **Teach a strategy.** Use doubles, make-ten, skip counting, fact families, or commutative pairs. → *Expect:* the child has a method besides guessing.
4. **Practice with low pressure.** Ask facts in mixed order and allow thinking time. → *Expect:* the child attempts recall without panic.
5. **Correct errors immediately and calmly.** Return to the visual model or strategy, then retry the missed fact later. → *Expect:* mistakes are fixed before they become habits.
6. **Track mastered and missed facts.** Keep a short list of facts that are quick, slow, or incorrect. → *Expect:* practice targets the facts that need it.
7. **Use brief retrieval sessions.** Practice for 5-10 minutes over multiple days rather than one long drill. → *Expect:* recall becomes faster and more stable.
8. **Apply facts in problems.** Use word problems, games, or mental math where facts support a larger task. → *Expect:* the child can use facts outside flashcards.

## Decision points

- Child counts every answer slowly → strengthen strategies before speed drills.
- Errors cluster around one set → isolate that set for a few days.
- Timed drills cause distress → measure accuracy first, then build fluency gradually.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Memorizing without meaning:** detect correct answers but no explanation → return to arrays, counters, or fact families.
- **F2 Too many facts at once:** detect random errors across the set → reduce the number of facts.
- **F3 Speed pressure backfires:** detect freezing or guessing → remove the timer and rebuild confidence.
- **F4 Practice is irregular:** detect no review for several days → attach a 5-minute drill to a daily routine.

## Verification

The child answers the target facts accurately, explains at least one strategy, and uses the facts correctly in a simple problem.

## Variations

- Addition/subtraction: emphasize make-ten, doubles, near doubles, and fact families.
- Multiplication/division: use arrays, equal groups, skip counting, and inverse relationships.
- App-based practice: confirm the app gives mixed review and error feedback, not only speed games.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid public leaderboards or apps that collect unnecessary child data, and keep practice encouraging rather than punitive.
