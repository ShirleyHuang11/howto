## Goal

You formally request a school meeting to discuss disability-related supports, accommodations, evaluation, or services for a child.

## Preconditions

- Concerns about the child's learning, access, behavior, health, attention, communication, or school participation.
- Relevant records such as grades, work samples, medical notes, teacher emails, discipline records, or prior evaluations.
- Contact information for the teacher, counselor, principal, 504 coordinator, or special education office.

## Steps

1. **Identify the concern clearly.** Write the specific school impact, such as reading below grade level, diabetes care needs, anxiety-related attendance, or attention barriers. → *Expect:* the request is tied to educational access or performance.
2. **Gather supporting evidence.** Collect dated examples, reports, teacher comments, and work samples. → *Expect:* the school receives concrete information.
3. **Decide what to request.** [BRANCH: possible accommodations, request a 504 meeting | possible specialized instruction or evaluation, request an evaluation for special education/IEP eligibility] → *Expect:* the request matches the type of support being considered.
4. **Write the request in plain language.** State the child's name, school, grade, concerns, requested meeting or evaluation, and your contact information. → *Expect:* the school can route the request.
5. **Send it to the right people.** Email or deliver the request to the principal, teacher, 504 coordinator, or special education coordinator and keep a copy. → *Expect:* you have a dated record of the request.
6. **Ask for timeline and next steps.** Request written confirmation of how the school will proceed and when you should expect a response. → *Expect:* the process has a trackable next date.
7. **Prepare for the meeting.** Bring records, questions, proposed accommodations, and the child's perspective when appropriate. → *Expect:* you can participate meaningfully.
8. **Review anything before signing.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* consent forms, evaluation plans, or service decisions affect legal rights and timelines; read them and ask questions before signing. → *Expect:* you understand what you are approving or declining.

## Decision points

- School suggests informal help only → ask whether a formal 504 or IEP evaluation is being refused and request the decision in writing.
- Concern involves medical access but not specialized instruction → a 504 plan may be the right starting point.
- Child may need specially designed instruction → request an IDEA special education evaluation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Verbal request disappears:** detect no response after a conversation → send a written dated request.
- **F2 Evidence is too vague:** detect general statements like "struggling" → add specific examples, dates, grades, and work samples.
- **F3 Meeting has no decision:** detect unclear next steps → ask for written action items and responsible staff.
- **F4 Parent feels pressured to sign:** detect forms presented without explanation → ask for time to review and consult an advocate if needed.

## Verification

You have a dated written request, confirmation from the school, and either a scheduled 504/IEP meeting, evaluation plan, or written explanation of the school's decision.

## Variations

- `us`: Section 504 covers disability-related access accommodations; IDEA governs IEP eligibility and special education services.
- Private school: legal obligations and available services differ; ask the local education agency about evaluation rights.
- Non-US: terminology and rights vary; contact the local school authority or disability education office.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because disability, medical, and education records are sensitive and decisions affect legal rights. Share records only with appropriate school staff and read consent or waiver forms before signing.
