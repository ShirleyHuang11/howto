## Goal

You request a payment plan that you can realistically afford and get the creditor, provider, landlord, or agency to confirm the terms in writing.

## Preconditions

- Account number, balance, due date, creditor contact, and any collection or shutoff deadline.
- A realistic budget showing what you can pay now and monthly.
- Knowledge of hardship, financial aid, or fee-waiver options that may apply.

## Steps

1. **Verify the debt and deadline.** Confirm the amount, creditor, account number, and any immediate consequence. → *Expect:* you are negotiating the correct balance.
2. **Calculate an affordable offer.** Choose a payment amount you can sustain without missing essentials. → *Expect:* the proposed plan is realistic.
3. **Ask about assistance first.** For medical, utility, tax, tuition, or rent balances, ask about hardship programs, charity care, or fee reductions. → *Expect:* you know whether the balance can be reduced before installment terms.
4. **Write the request clearly.** Include the balance, reason for hardship if needed, proposed payment amount, start date, and payment method. → *Expect:* the recipient can approve a specific plan.
5. **Request written confirmation before paying.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not rely on a phone promise for fees, interest, credit reporting, eviction, shutoff, or collections; confirm terms first. → *Expect:* you receive written terms or a formal agreement.
6. **Review the terms.** Check due dates, interest, fees, autopay, default consequences, credit reporting, and whether collection activity pauses. → *Expect:* you understand the full cost and risk.
7. **Make the first payment only under confirmed terms.** Use a traceable payment method and save the receipt. → *Expect:* the account shows the plan active and the payment posted.
8. **Calendar every due date.** Set reminders before each payment and keep records. → *Expect:* you can avoid accidental default.

## Decision points

- The amount is wrong or not yours → dispute validation before agreeing to pay.
- The proposed payment is unaffordable → offer less or ask for hardship review instead of overpromising.
- The account is already in collections or court → get advice about rights and settlement wording before paying.
- Autopay is required → confirm withdrawal dates and how to cancel or change the plan.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Verbal-only agreement:** detect no written confirmation → send a recap and do not assume protections apply until confirmed.
- **F2 Plan default:** detect a missed or late payment → contact the creditor immediately and ask to reinstate before penalties escalate.
- **F3 Hidden fees or interest:** detect balance not decreasing as expected → request an amortization or itemized statement.
- **F4 Collection continues:** detect calls, notices, shutoff, or eviction despite plan → send the written agreement and escalate to supervisor or regulator.

## Verification

You have a written payment-plan agreement showing balance, payment amount, due dates, fees or interest, consequences of default, and confirmation that the first payment posted correctly.

## Variations

- `medical-us`: ask for itemized bill, insurance review, and hospital financial assistance or charity care before agreeing to installments.
- `tax`: government agencies often have formal installment agreements with specific forms, fees, and penalties.
- Template:
  "I am requesting a written payment plan for account [number], balance [amount].
  I can pay [amount] on [date] and [amount] each [month/week] starting [date].
  Please confirm in writing whether fees, interest, collections, shutoff, or credit reporting will be paused while I comply."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because payment promises can affect credit, housing, utilities, lawsuits, or collections. Verify the creditor before paying, avoid giving bank details to unverified callers, and keep written proof.
