## Goal

You send a clear repair request that identifies the problem, documents its impact, and asks the landlord to fix it by a reasonable date.

## Preconditions

- Your lease, tenant portal access or landlord email address, and the correct maintenance-request channel.
- Photos or video of the problem, dates it occurred, and any steps you took to reduce damage.
- Local tenant-rights rules if the issue affects heat, water, locks, pests, mold, or habitability.

## Steps

1. **Document the problem before writing.** Photograph the issue, save receipts if you paid for temporary supplies, and write down when you first noticed it. → *Expect:* a dated evidence set that supports the request.
2. **Check the lease and local notice rules.** Confirm whether requests must go through a portal, email, certified mail, or emergency phone number. → *Expect:* the correct delivery method and landlord contact are identified.
3. **Write a factual subject line.** Use the unit address and problem, such as "Repair request: leaking kitchen sink at Unit 4B." → *Expect:* the landlord can route the message without reading the whole note.
4. **State the repair needed and its impact.** Include where it is, what is happening, when it began, and whether it affects safety or habitability. → *Expect:* a reader understands the urgency and scope.
5. **Ask for a specific next step and deadline.** Request inspection or repair and give a reasonable response date; use shorter timing for urgent issues. → *Expect:* the message contains a concrete ask, not only a complaint.
6. **Attach evidence and send through the required channel.** Keep the tone calm and preserve a copy of the sent message. → *Expect:* the landlord or portal shows the request was submitted.
7. **Track the response.** Record ticket numbers, appointment windows, and any promises made. → *Expect:* you have a timeline if follow-up or escalation is needed.

## Decision points

- Emergency condition such as gas smell, fire, flooding, or loss of heat in dangerous weather → call emergency services or the building emergency line before sending routine correspondence.
- Lease requires a maintenance portal → submit there first, then send a short email noting the ticket number if needed.
- No response by the requested deadline → send a dated follow-up and check local tenant remedies before withholding rent or arranging repairs yourself.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong recipient:** detect no acknowledgement or a bounced email → resend through the lease-specified channel and confirm the contact by phone or portal.
- **F2 Vague request ignored:** detect a generic "we will look into it" with no date → reply with the location, severity, photos, and a requested inspection window.
- **F3 Access dispute:** detect repair staff cannot enter → provide lawful access instructions and available times, or follow the lease's entry-notice process.
- **F4 Retaliation concern:** detect threats, rent increases, or harassment after a protected request → preserve all messages and contact a local tenant organization or attorney.

## Verification

The landlord has acknowledged the request with a ticket number, email reply, or appointment, and the repair is either completed or scheduled with a specific date and access plan.

## Variations

- `us`: habitability rules are state and city specific; heat, hot water, pests, and locks may have faster statutory deadlines.
- `uk`: use "landlord" or "letting agent" and keep records for the council's private-rented-sector team if hazards are not addressed.
- Example:
  "Hello, I am requesting repair of the leaking kitchen sink in Unit 4B. The leak began on August 24 and is dripping into the cabinet below; photos are attached. Please confirm an inspection or repair appointment by August 29."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because repair requests can affect housing rights, money, and habitability. Do not exaggerate facts, do not threaten unlawful rent withholding, and get explicit confirmation before granting entry outside normal lease procedures.
