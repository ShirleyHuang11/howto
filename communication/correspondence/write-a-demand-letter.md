## Goal

You write and send a factual demand letter that states the dispute, the remedy requested, and the deadline while preserving proof of delivery.

## Preconditions

- Names and addresses of the sender and recipient.
- Contract, invoice, receipt, photos, messages, account statements, or other evidence.
- A legally realistic remedy and deadline.

## Steps

1. **Identify the legal relationship.** Determine whether the dispute is about a contract, unpaid invoice, property damage, deposit, warranty, debt, landlord-tenant issue, or consumer purchase. → *Expect:* the letter is framed around the correct obligation.
2. **Build a document timeline.** List dates, amounts, promises, payments, missed deadlines, and prior attempts to resolve the issue. → *Expect:* every claim can be supported by evidence.
3. **Choose the remedy.** Request a specific action, such as payment, refund, repair, return of property, cancellation, or written correction. → *Expect:* the recipient can satisfy the demand without guessing.
4. **Check required pre-suit rules.** Some disputes require statutory notice language, cure periods, or specific delivery methods before filing in court. → *Expect:* the letter meets any known notice requirement or you know legal advice is needed.
5. **Draft the letter calmly.** State facts, cite the agreement or law only if you are confident, attach copies of key documents, and avoid threats you will not carry out. → *Expect:* the tone is firm, factual, and professional.
6. **Set a clear deadline.** Give a reasonable response date and say what you may do next, such as file a complaint, start small claims, or pursue other remedies. → *Expect:* the deadline is calendar-specific.
7. **Review before sending.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm facts, amounts, addresses, attachments, and legal statements before sending because the letter may become evidence. → *Expect:* the final letter is accurate and complete.
8. **Send with proof.** Use certified mail, tracked courier, email with receipt, platform message, or the contract-required method. → *Expect:* you have proof of delivery or attempted delivery.
9. **Track the deadline and response.** Save all replies and do not negotiate by phone without written follow-up. → *Expect:* the record shows whether the recipient complied, refused, or ignored the demand.

## Decision points

- The dispute involves injury, eviction, discrimination, immigration, criminal accusations, or large sums → consult a lawyer before sending.
- A contract specifies notice method → follow that method exactly.
- The recipient offers partial settlement → decide whether to accept, counter, or require payment before releasing claims.
- The deadline passes with no response → move to the planned escalation, such as agency complaint or small claims.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Letter too emotional:** detect insults, exaggeration, or accusations you cannot prove → rewrite as facts, documents, and requested remedy.
- **F2 Wrong recipient:** detect mail returned or entity name mismatch → identify registered agent, billing address, property manager, or legal notice address and resend.
- **F3 No proof of delivery:** detect only an unsaved email or ordinary mail → resend using a trackable method allowed by the contract or law.
- **F4 Overbroad legal claim:** detect uncertain statutes or penalties → remove unsupported legal conclusions or get legal advice.
- **F5 Recipient ignores letter:** detect no response after the deadline → escalate using the saved evidence packet.

## Verification

The final demand letter has been sent to the correct recipient with proof of delivery, includes the amount or remedy demanded, a specific deadline, and copies of the key supporting documents.

## Variations

- `payment demand`: "I request payment of $1,250 for Invoice 1042, due March 15, 2026. Please send payment by September 15, 2026. If payment is not received by that date, I may pursue available remedies, including small claims court."
- `consumer refund`: "I request a refund of $389.99 for Order AB123 because the product was returned on July 8, 2026 and no refund has been issued."
- `landlord-tenant`: notice periods and required wording vary by jurisdiction; check local law before sending.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a demand letter can become legal evidence. Use only accurate statements, avoid threats of criminal action to collect money, and get legal advice for high-value or regulated disputes.
