## Goal

You write a respectful eulogy that tells the truth with care, honors the person who died, and supports the people gathered to remember them.

## Preconditions

- The service format, time limit, and any religious or family expectations.
- Accurate basic facts: full name, relationship, major dates if needed, and close survivors if they will be mentioned.
- One to three memories that show the person's character.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the time and tone.** Ask the organizer how long you should speak and whether the service is formal, religious, informal, or private. → *Expect:* the eulogy fits the setting.
2. **Gather accurate details.** Verify names, relationships, and dates with family or the obituary. → *Expect:* factual details are correct.
3. **Choose a few truthful themes.** Focus on qualities shown through actions, such as generosity, humor, discipline, faith, curiosity, or devotion. → *Expect:* the eulogy has a coherent portrait.
4. **Write in plain language.** Use short sentences and concrete memories rather than trying to sound literary. → *Expect:* the draft sounds like a person speaking honestly.
5. **Handle complexity gently.** If the relationship was difficult, avoid false perfection and speak about what can be honored truthfully. → *Expect:* the eulogy does not lie or settle scores.
6. **Close with gratitude or farewell.** End with a final sentence addressed to the person or the gathered mourners. → *Expect:* the ending gives the room a clear place to land.
7. **Practice aloud and prepare a printed copy.** Mark pauses and keep water nearby if delivering it. → *Expect:* you can read it even if emotional.
8. **Share with a close family reviewer if appropriate.** → *Expect:* sensitive details are checked before the service.

## Decision points

- The death involved trauma, suicide, addiction, or legal issues → use careful, non-graphic language and follow the family's wishes.
- Multiple speakers are planned → keep yours narrow rather than covering the whole life.
- You are too emotional to deliver it → ask someone else to read your words.
- Family facts are disputed → omit the detail or use neutral wording.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Fact error:** detect a wrong name, date, or relationship → correct all copies and tell the organizer before the service.
- **F2 Too long:** detect a draft beyond the assigned time → cut chronology and keep only the strongest memories.
- **F3 Private disclosure:** detect medical, financial, family, or legal details not publicly shared → remove them.
- **F4 Emotional overwhelm:** detect that you cannot continue while practicing → arrange a backup reader.

## Verification

The eulogy has been read aloud within the time limit, facts have been checked, sensitive details are approved or omitted, and a readable final copy is ready for the service.

## Variations

- `religious-service`: coordinate with clergy about scripture, prayers, and what belongs in the homily versus the eulogy.
- `celebration-of-life`: a warmer or more humorous tone may fit, while still avoiding embarrassment.
- Template:
  "I knew [Name] as [relationship]. When I think of them, I think first of [quality].
  One memory that shows this is [brief story].
  We will miss [Name], and we are grateful for [specific legacy]."

## Safety & privacy

Low procedural risk but high emotional sensitivity. Do not disclose private diagnoses, circumstances of death, family conflict, or secrets unless the immediate family has explicitly approved it.
