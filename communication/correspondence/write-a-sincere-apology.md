## Goal

You write an apology that clearly accepts responsibility, names the harm, and offers repair without pressuring the other person to forgive you.

## Preconditions

- You understand what you did or failed to do.
- You are willing to change behavior or make a concrete repair.
- The message will not violate a legal, HR, school, or safety process where counsel or policy guidance is needed.

## Steps

1. **Name the action plainly.** Write down the specific behavior you are apologizing for without excuses. → *Expect:* the apology can be understood without background argument.
2. **Acknowledge the impact.** State how the action affected the other person, using what they told you when possible. → *Expect:* the apology recognizes harm rather than only regret.
3. **Take responsibility.** Use "I" statements and avoid "if," "but," or blaming circumstances. → *Expect:* the message does not shift fault.
4. **Offer repair.** Name a concrete action: replace an item, correct a record, reimburse money, redo work, stop a behavior, or give space. → *Expect:* the recipient can see what will change.
5. **Avoid demanding forgiveness.** Do not ask them to reassure you, respond immediately, or accept your intent as the main issue. → *Expect:* the recipient has room to decide what they need.
6. **Keep it concise.** Remove long explanations that make the message about your feelings. → *Expect:* the apology is direct and readable.
7. **Send through the least intrusive channel.** Use the channel the relationship and situation can support. → *Expect:* the message is delivered without cornering the recipient.

## Decision points

- Money, injury, employment, school discipline, or legal liability is involved → get appropriate advice before sending written admissions.
- The person asked for no contact → respect that boundary unless a formal process requires a response.
- You do not understand the harm yet → ask once for clarification, then listen without debating.
- You already apologized repeatedly → focus on changed behavior instead of more messages.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Non-apology wording:** detect "I'm sorry if you felt" or "but" → replace with direct responsibility.
- **F2 Overexplaining:** detect a long backstory → cut it to one short context sentence or remove it.
- **F3 Repair missing:** detect no next action → add what you will do differently.
- **F4 Pressure for forgiveness:** detect requests like "please tell me we're okay" → remove them and let the recipient respond freely.

## Verification

The apology states the specific action, acknowledges the impact, accepts responsibility, offers a realistic repair, and does not pressure the recipient for forgiveness.

## Variations

- `personal`: "I am sorry I shared your news before you were ready. That was yours to tell, and I understand it put you in an unfair position. I have told Sam I should not have said anything, and I will not discuss it with anyone else."
- `workplace`: "I am sorry I missed the deadline without warning you. It created avoidable pressure for the team. I have sent the completed draft and added earlier status checks so this does not happen again."
- `brief text`: "I was wrong to snap at you earlier. I am sorry. You did not deserve that, and I will step away next time instead of taking frustration out on you."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk in ordinary personal situations, but written apologies can matter in disputes. Do not send admissions about legal, employment, school, or safety matters without understanding the consequences.
