## Goal

You prepare a short, warm wedding toast that honors the couple, fits the room, and can be delivered confidently.

## Preconditions

- The requested speech length, event tone, and whether other people are speaking.
- One appropriate story about the person or couple.
- A written draft and time to practice aloud.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the assignment.** Ask the couple or planner how long the toast should be and when it will happen. → *Expect:* you know the time limit and setting.
2. **Choose one central point.** Decide what quality or moment you want guests to remember. → *Expect:* the toast has a clear emotional center.
3. **Pick a safe story.** Use a story that is affectionate, brief, and understandable to people who were not there. → *Expect:* the story makes the couple look good.
4. **Write a simple structure.** Open with thanks, tell the story, connect it to the marriage, and end with a toast. → *Expect:* the draft has a beginning, middle, and ending.
5. **Cut anything risky.** Remove exes, sex, drunkenness, private conflict, inside jokes, and jokes at the couple's expense. → *Expect:* nothing would embarrass the couple or older relatives.
6. **Practice aloud with a timer.** Aim for two to four minutes unless asked otherwise. → *Expect:* you can deliver it smoothly within the limit.
7. **Print or save a readable copy.** Use large text or cards, not a dense paragraph on a phone. → *Expect:* you can glance down without losing your place.
8. **Deliver slowly and end by inviting the toast.** Raise the glass and use the couple's names. → *Expect:* guests know when to join in.

## Decision points

- You are nervous → write shorter and use bullet cards.
- You do not know one spouse well → focus on what you have learned through the person you know and welcome them warmly.
- The couple asked for no speeches → do not give an unscheduled toast.
- Alcohol is involved → deliver before drinking much.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too long:** detect a draft over five minutes → cut to one story and one blessing.
- **F2 Inside jokes:** detect references only a few guests understand → replace with a universal observation.
- **F3 Embarrassing material:** detect anything the couple would not want recorded → remove it.
- **F4 Lost place while speaking:** detect a pause or skipped line → breathe, look at your cards, and continue with the closing toast.

## Verification

The final toast is practiced aloud under the requested time limit, includes one appropriate story, names both partners warmly, and ends with a clear invitation for guests to raise a glass.

## Variations

- `best-man-or-maid-of-honor`: one playful line is fine, but the speech should still honor both partners.
- `parent`: focus on welcome, pride, and blessing rather than a full life history.
- Template:
  "For those I have not met, I am [name]. I have known [person] for [time], and one thing I have always admired is [quality].
  I saw that same quality when [brief story].
  Please raise a glass to [Name] and [Name]: may your life together be full of [wish]."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but reputationally sensitive. Do not reveal private stories, pregnancies, family conflict, past relationships, medical details, or anything the couple has not chosen to share.
