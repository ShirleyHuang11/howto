## Goal

You write a polished formal invitation that gives guests all essential details and makes the requested response easy.

## Preconditions

- Event host, honoree, purpose, date, time, venue, dress code, RSVP deadline, and contact method.
- Guest list and any plus-one, child, accessibility, or security constraints.
- Delivery format: printed card, email, letter, or event platform.

## Steps

1. **Define the host and occasion.** Decide who is inviting whom and what event is being held. → *Expect:* the opening line names the host and purpose clearly.
2. **Write the essential event details.** Include date, start time, venue name, full address, and virtual link if applicable. → *Expect:* a guest can put the event on a calendar without asking follow-up questions.
3. **State formality cues.** Add dress code, ceremony/reception distinction, parking, security check-in, or arrival window if relevant. → *Expect:* guests know how to prepare.
4. **Set RSVP instructions.** Provide deadline, response method, and any required meal choice or guest count. → *Expect:* guests know exactly how and when to respond.
5. **Use formal but natural wording.** Keep sentences clean and avoid exaggerated language. → *Expect:* the invitation sounds respectful and readable.
6. **Proofread names and dates.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm spelling, titles, venue address, date, time zone, and RSVP contact before printing or sending broadly. → *Expect:* no visible errors remain.
7. **Send using the chosen format.** Mail, email, or publish through the event platform with enough lead time. → *Expect:* invitees receive the invitation and can respond.

## Decision points

- The event has limited capacity → make the named invitees explicit and avoid ambiguous plus-one wording.
- The venue has security requirements → include full legal names, ID requirements, or arrival instructions.
- Guests are international → include time zone, country, and date format that cannot be misread.
- The invitation is ceremonial → use titles and host names more formally.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong date or address sent:** detect the mistake after sending → send a correction immediately with a clear subject line.
- **F2 RSVP responses incomplete:** detect missing meal or guest details → follow up with a short checklist.
- **F3 Guest list ambiguity:** detect people asking whether partners or children are invited → clarify directly and update wording for future recipients.
- **F4 Overly stiff wording:** detect language that sounds unnatural → simplify while keeping titles and event details formal.

## Verification

The invitation includes host, event purpose, date, time, location, RSVP deadline, response method, and any required dress, meal, access, or guest-limit information.

## Variations

- `formal dinner`: "Dr. and Mrs. Patel request the pleasure of your company at a dinner honoring Professor Elena Ruiz on Friday, October 16, 2026, at seven o'clock in the evening, The Meridian Room, 42 Lake Street. RSVP by September 25 to events@example.com."
- `business reception`: "The Board of Directors invites you to a reception celebrating the opening of the North Campus Office on Thursday, May 14, 2026, from 6:00 to 8:00 p.m. Business attire. Please respond by May 1."
- `digital`: include a calendar link, map link, and RSVP button, but keep the formal wording concise.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not publish private addresses, access codes, or guest lists more broadly than needed, and confirm details before mass sending or printing.
