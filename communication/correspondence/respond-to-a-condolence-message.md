## Goal

You acknowledge a condolence message in a way that is simple, sincere, and manageable during grief.

## Preconditions

- The condolence message or list of people to thank.
- Your preferred channel: text, email, card, social post, or no immediate reply.
- Any details about services, donations, or family privacy that you are willing to share.

## Steps

1. **Decide whether a reply is needed now.** It is acceptable to wait or send a very short acknowledgment. → *Expect:* you choose a response level you can handle.
2. **Thank the person plainly.** A simple "thank you for thinking of us" is enough. → *Expect:* the sender knows their care was received.
3. **Name the loss only if you want to.** You may mention the person's name or keep it general. → *Expect:* the reply respects your own privacy and energy.
4. **Share practical information only if ready.** Include service details, donation preferences, or family updates only when appropriate. → *Expect:* the message does not create more obligations.
5. **Use a group update for many messages.** If overwhelmed, post or send one general thank-you. → *Expect:* you acknowledge support without replying individually to everyone.
6. **Let close people help.** Ask a trusted person to track messages, send service details, or draft replies if needed. → *Expect:* responses are manageable.

## Decision points

- You are too overwhelmed → send no reply yet, or use a one-line acknowledgment.
- The sender offered specific help → reply with one concrete need if you have one.
- The message contains painful language → you do not need to correct it immediately.
- Public comments are numerous → one public thank-you may be enough.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overcommitting:** detect promises to call or visit everyone → replace with gratitude and no timeline.
- **F2 Forced emotional detail:** detect an explanation you do not want to share → shorten it.
- **F3 Missed practical offers:** detect offers of meals, rides, or childcare buried in messages → ask a helper to organize them.
- **F4 Reply pressure:** detect guilt about delayed responses → send a simple belated thanks when able.

## Verification

The condolence sender or group has received a brief acknowledgment, or you have intentionally deferred replies and recorded any practical offers that need follow-up.

## Variations

- `formal-card`: "Thank you for your kind words and for remembering [Name]. Your support means a great deal to our family."
- `text`: one or two sentences is normal and enough.
- Template:
  "Thank you for your kind message. We miss [Name] deeply, and it helps to know people are thinking of us.
  I may be slow to reply, but I am grateful."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not share service location, family schedules, cause of death, or donation links publicly unless the family has agreed.
