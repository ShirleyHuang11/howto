## Goal

You give a landlord or tenant a clear written notice that ends a tenancy on a specific date, with proof that the notice was delivered.

## Preconditions

- The lease, rental agreement, or local tenancy rules showing the required notice period.
- The property address, unit number, names of the parties, and intended move-out or termination date.
- A delivery method allowed by the lease or local law, such as certified mail, portal upload, personal delivery, or email if accepted.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the required notice period.** Read the lease and local landlord-tenant rules before choosing the date. → *Expect:* you know whether the notice must be 30, 60, 90, or another number of days.
2. **Identify the legal parties and premises.** Use the full names from the lease and the exact rental address. → *Expect:* the notice cannot be confused with another person or unit.
3. **State the termination date plainly.** Write the last day of tenancy and, if you are the tenant, the date you expect to return possession. → *Expect:* the reader can identify the final date without interpreting vague wording.
4. **Include required lease details.** Add forwarding address, deposit-return address, inspection request, or statutory language if the lease or jurisdiction requires it. → *Expect:* the notice contains every required administrative detail.
5. **Keep the tone factual.** Do not argue about disputes in the notice; put only what is needed to end the tenancy. → *Expect:* the document reads as a formal notice, not a complaint.
6. **Sign and date the notice.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before sending, confirm the date is legally valid and that you intend to end the tenancy. → *Expect:* the final notice has a signature, printed name, and date.
7. **Deliver using an accepted method.** [BRANCH: certified mail, send to the lease notice address and keep the receipt | portal/email, upload or send to the official notice channel and save confirmation | hand delivery, bring a witness or request a signed receipt] → *Expect:* you have a delivery record tied to the notice.
8. **Save the evidence package.** Store the notice, lease clause, delivery proof, and any reply together. → *Expect:* you can prove what was sent, when, and to whom.

## Decision points

- Lease requires a specific form or notice address → use that form or address exactly.
- Month-to-month tenancy → local law often controls the notice period; verify before sending.
- Fixed-term lease ending automatically → still send a written confirmation if the lease asks for notice of nonrenewal.
- You are breaking a lease early → get legal or tenant-union advice before sending, because fees and mitigation rules vary.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too-short notice:** detect that the termination date falls before the required period ends → resend with a valid date and keep both copies.
- **F2 Wrong recipient:** detect that the notice went to a property manager but the lease names another notice address → send again to the required address immediately.
- **F3 No proof of delivery:** detect that email, mail, or portal confirmation is missing → redeliver by a trackable accepted method.
- **F4 Disputed move-out date:** detect a landlord or tenant saying the date is unclear → reply with the exact date and attach the original notice.

## Verification

The notice names the parties and property, states the termination date, is signed and dated, and you have a saved delivery receipt or portal/email confirmation showing it was sent to an accepted address.

## Variations

- `us`: notice periods and required language vary by state and sometimes city; check the state housing agency, local court self-help site, or lease notice clause.
- `uk`: tenants and landlords use different notice types, such as section 21 or section 8 in England; use the correct statutory form where required.
- Template:
  "I am giving notice that the tenancy for [address/unit] will end on [date]. I will return possession on or before that date.
  Please send deposit correspondence to [forwarding address].
  Signed, [name], [date]."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because an invalid notice can affect housing rights, deposits, and rent liability. Confirm the lease, local notice period, recipient address, and final date before signing or sending.
