## Goal

You write a birthday message that feels personal because it names a real quality, memory, or wish for the recipient.

## Preconditions

- The recipient's birthday and preferred communication channel.
- One specific detail: a shared memory, trait, current season of life, or interest.
- Awareness of any sensitive age, grief, illness, or family context.

## Steps

1. **Choose the relationship tone.** Decide whether the message should be affectionate, funny, formal, romantic, or brief. → *Expect:* the style fits the relationship.
2. **Pick one specific anchor.** Use a memory, quality, inside-but-kind reference, or current milestone. → *Expect:* the message could not be sent unchanged to anyone else.
3. **Add a genuine wish.** Name something you hope for them in the coming year. → *Expect:* the message looks forward, not just backward.
4. **Avoid lazy jokes if risky.** Skip age jokes, appearance jokes, and jokes about being late unless you know they will land. → *Expect:* the message does not make the recipient absorb a dig.
5. **Keep the length appropriate.** Use two lines for casual contacts and a fuller note for close relationships. → *Expect:* the message feels intentional without being overwrought.
6. **Send it on time or acknowledge lateness simply.** → *Expect:* the recipient receives a warm note without needing to reassure you.

## Decision points

- The person had a difficult year → acknowledge strength or presence without forcing cheer.
- You are posting publicly → avoid private memories or sensitive milestones.
- The message is late → say "belated happy birthday" once and continue warmly.
- You know them professionally → keep praise specific but not intimate.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Generic wording:** detect "hope it's great" with no detail → add one real observation.
- **F2 Public overshare:** detect private information in a social post → move it to a private message or remove it.
- **F3 Backhanded joke:** detect age, weight, relationship, or career teasing → cut it.
- **F4 Apology takeover:** detect a late-message apology longer than the birthday wish → shorten the apology.

## Verification

The birthday message includes one specific personal detail, one genuine wish, matches the channel, and contains no private or teasing content that could embarrass the recipient.

## Variations

- `professional`: "Wishing you a happy birthday and a year full of good work, good health, and good people."
- `close-family`: include a concrete memory or gratitude.
- Template:
  "Happy birthday, [Name]. I keep thinking about [specific memory/quality], and it still makes me smile.
  I hope this next year brings you more [specific wish]. I am grateful for you."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Be careful with public posts, sensitive ages, grief anniversaries, fertility, illness, job loss, or relationship status.
