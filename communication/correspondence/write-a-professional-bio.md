## Goal

You write a professional bio that accurately explains who you are, what you do, and why your work is credible for a specific audience.

## Preconditions

- The intended use: website, conference, LinkedIn, grant, press, internal profile, or speaker introduction.
- Required length, person perspective, and any style guidelines.
- Current title, organization, credentials, achievements, and contact or link preferences.

## Steps

1. **Identify the audience and length.** Decide what the reader needs to know and whether the bio should be 50, 100, or 200 words. → *Expect:* the scope is clear before drafting.
2. **Choose first or third person.** Match the platform's convention. → *Expect:* the bio uses one consistent point of view.
3. **Lead with current role and value.** State your title, organization, field, and the problem or audience you serve. → *Expect:* the first sentence answers who you are professionally.
4. **Add proof selectively.** Include credentials, awards, publications, clients, projects, or years of experience only if relevant. → *Expect:* the bio supports credibility without becoming a resume.
5. **Include a human detail only if appropriate.** Add a research interest, community role, or personal detail when it fits the context. → *Expect:* the bio has personality without oversharing.
6. **Cut stale or inflated claims.** Remove buzzwords, unverifiable superlatives, and outdated roles. → *Expect:* the bio is accurate and current.
7. **Check names, links, and permissions.** Confirm employer names, trademarks, degrees, and client mentions. → *Expect:* no sensitive or unauthorized affiliation is included.
8. **Save versions by length.** Keep short, medium, and long versions for reuse. → *Expect:* you can provide the right version quickly.

## Decision points

- The bio is for a conference → emphasize topic authority and speaking relevance.
- The bio is for an employer site → follow company style and disclosure rules.
- You are changing careers → lead with transferable expertise and current focus.
- You have limited experience → focus on current work, training, and concrete interests rather than padding.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Resume dump:** detect a list of every job and credential → cut to the items relevant to this audience.
- **F2 Empty jargon:** detect phrases like "dynamic thought leader" → replace with specific work and outcomes.
- **F3 Outdated details:** detect old title, old employer, or expired certification → update or remove.
- **F4 Unauthorized claims:** detect client names, revenue, or confidential projects → remove unless approved.

## Verification

The bio matches the requested length and perspective, states your current role, includes relevant proof, has verified names and credentials, and is ready to paste into the target platform.

## Variations

- `speaker`: include topic area, current role, and one credibility marker.
- `academic`: include appointment, research area, selected publications or grants, and institutional affiliation.
- Template:
  "[Name] is a [role] at [organization], where [they/he/she] [core work].
  [Name]'s work focuses on [areas], and [they/he/she] has [selected proof].
  Outside of work, [optional appropriate detail]."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but protect confidential employer, client, salary, security, and personal information. Confirm permission before naming clients, patients, students, or sensitive projects.
