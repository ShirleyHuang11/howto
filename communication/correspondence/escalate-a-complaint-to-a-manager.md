## Goal

You escalate an unresolved complaint to someone with authority while preserving evidence, staying factual, and asking for a specific resolution.

## Preconditions

- The original complaint, dates, names, ticket numbers, receipts, policies, and prior responses.
- A clear desired remedy such as refund, repair, correction, apology, replacement, or deadline.
- The correct escalation channel: manager, supervisor, executive support, regulator, or platform dispute.

## Steps

1. **Assemble the timeline.** List what happened, who you contacted, and what each person promised. → *Expect:* the complaint can be understood quickly.
2. **Identify the policy or standard.** Find the warranty, contract, service promise, law, or published policy that supports your request. → *Expect:* the escalation has a basis beyond dissatisfaction.
3. **Choose the right manager-level contact.** Use the company's support escalation path, store manager, department head, or written complaint address. → *Expect:* the message reaches someone with authority.
4. **Write a concise summary.** State the issue, prior attempts, and current impact in chronological order. → *Expect:* the reader does not need to reconstruct the case.
5. **Request a specific remedy and deadline.** Ask for what would resolve the complaint and when you need a response. → *Expect:* the manager can approve or counter a concrete request.
6. **Attach evidence without overload.** Include the most relevant receipts, photos, screenshots, and ticket numbers. → *Expect:* the claim is supported but readable.
7. **Send and log the escalation.** Save the message, attachments, timestamp, and any case number. → *Expect:* you have a complete record.
8. **Escalate externally only if needed.** [BRANCH: financial product, use the appropriate regulator or ombudsman | consumer purchase, use platform dispute, card issuer, or consumer agency | workplace issue, follow HR or legal process] → *Expect:* outside escalation is grounded in the written record.

## Decision points

- The issue involves discrimination, retaliation, safety, or legal rights → consider formal complaint channels or legal advice before informal escalation.
- The company offers a reasonable remedy → accept in writing and confirm timing.
- The manager calls instead of writing → take notes and send a recap email.
- The deadline passes → follow up once, then use the next documented escalation route.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Emotional but unclear complaint:** detect insults or long venting → rewrite as timeline, evidence, remedy.
- **F2 Wrong authority:** detect replies saying they cannot approve remedy → ask who can and forward the record.
- **F3 Evidence gap:** detect missing receipt, ticket, or policy → gather records before escalating further.
- **F4 Retaliation risk:** detect workplace, housing, or service access vulnerability → seek advice and preserve records before sending more.

## Verification

The escalation message is sent to a manager-level or formal complaint channel, includes a timeline and evidence, states the requested remedy and deadline, and is saved with proof of delivery or ticket number.

## Variations

- `consumer-retail`: include receipt, order number, product photos, and policy quote.
- `healthcare-billing`: include account number, explanation of benefits, dates of service, and request for billing review.
- Template:
  "I am escalating complaint [ticket/order number] because it remains unresolved after contacts on [dates].
  The issue is [brief facts], and the supporting evidence is attached.
  I am requesting [specific remedy] by [date]. Please confirm the next step in writing."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because complaints can involve money, access, legal rights, or employment. Keep sensitive data limited to what the recipient needs, and avoid threats unless you are invoking a real formal right.
