## Goal

You send a respectful cold email that quickly explains why you are writing, why the recipient is relevant, and what small action you are requesting.

## Preconditions

- A legitimate reason to contact this person and a professional email address.
- One or two facts showing why this recipient is a good fit.
- A small, specific ask that can be answered or declined easily.

## Steps

1. **Define the single ask.** Decide whether you want advice, a short call, a referral, a source quote, or permission to send more detail. → *Expect:* the email has one clear purpose.
2. **Research lightly but specifically.** Find a recent role, project, publication, company page, or public talk that explains your reason for contacting them. → *Expect:* the opening can be personalized without flattery.
3. **Write a direct subject line.** Use a plain subject such as "Question about [topic]" or "Request for 15 minutes on [topic]." → *Expect:* the recipient can identify the topic before opening.
4. **Open with context in one sentence.** State who you are and why you chose them. → *Expect:* the recipient understands the relevance immediately.
5. **Make the ask small and optional.** Request a bounded action and make it easy to decline or redirect. → *Expect:* the email does not impose open-ended work.
6. **Add only necessary credibility.** Include one line about your role, project, or connection to the topic. → *Expect:* the recipient can judge legitimacy without reading a biography.
7. **Close with thanks and contact details.** → *Expect:* the recipient has everything needed to reply.
8. **Send one thoughtful follow-up if needed.** Wait about a week unless there is a real deadline. → *Expect:* you make one respectful attempt without spamming.

## Decision points

- You need a favor from someone senior → ask for a small pointer or resource, not a large time commitment.
- You are contacting many people → customize each opening and comply with anti-spam rules.
- You have a mutual connection → mention it only if the connection would recognize and approve the reference.
- The request involves sensitive or proprietary information → ask for public guidance, not confidential details.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too long:** detect multiple paragraphs of background before the ask → cut to five to eight sentences.
- **F2 Generic personalization:** detect vague praise that could apply to anyone → replace it with a specific public detail.
- **F3 Large ask:** detect requests like "review my plan" or "mentor me" → narrow to one question or a 15-minute call.
- **F4 No reply:** detect silence after one follow-up → stop and try a more relevant contact.

## Verification

The sent email has a specific subject, a personalized reason, one bounded ask, a short credibility line, and no attachments or private information the recipient did not request.

## Variations

- `academic`: include your affiliation, research question, and why their work is relevant.
- `business-development`: include the likely benefit to them, not only your own goal.
- Template:
  "Hi [Name], I am [role], and I saw your work on [specific topic].
  I am trying to understand [narrow question]. Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week, or is there someone better to ask?
  Thanks, [Your name]"

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but respect privacy and anti-spam laws. Do not scrape private addresses, misrepresent relationships, or pressure someone after they decline or do not respond.
