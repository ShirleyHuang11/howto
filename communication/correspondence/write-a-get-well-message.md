## Goal

You write a kind, appropriate message to someone who is ill, injured, recovering, or going through treatment without prying or minimizing what they face.

## Preconditions

- The person's situation as much as they have chosen to share.
- The relationship and communication channel they normally prefer.
- A specific offer of help only if you can actually provide it.

## Steps

1. **Match the closeness of the relationship.** Decide whether the tone should be warm, formal, brief, religious, humorous, or practical. → *Expect:* the message sounds appropriate coming from you.
2. **Acknowledge without prying.** Refer generally to recovery, treatment, or a hard time unless they disclosed specifics. → *Expect:* the message respects privacy.
3. **Offer care, not advice.** Avoid medical suggestions, comparisons, or "everything happens for a reason." → *Expect:* the recipient does not have to defend their feelings or choices.
4. **Make help specific if offered.** Say "I can drop dinner Tuesday" instead of "let me know if you need anything." → *Expect:* the offer is easy to accept or ignore.
5. **Keep it short.** Sick people may not have energy for long replies. → *Expect:* the message can be read quickly.
6. **Send through a suitable channel.** [BRANCH: close relationship, text or card may work | workplace acquaintance, email or group card may be better] → *Expect:* the message arrives without creating pressure.

## Decision points

- The condition is serious or uncertain → say you are thinking of them, not that they will definitely recover.
- The person is private → avoid naming the illness or treatment.
- You share faith and know it is welcome → include a prayer or blessing; otherwise keep it secular.
- You want to help materially → offer one concrete task with a date or option.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Accidental advice:** detect treatment suggestions or health claims → remove them.
- **F2 Forced optimism:** detect "you will beat this" when the outcome is unknown → replace with steady support.
- **F3 Vague help:** detect "anything at all" → offer a specific errand, meal, ride, or check-in.
- **F4 Privacy breach:** detect details copied into a group thread → move to a private message or keep details general.

## Verification

The message is sent in an appropriate channel, is brief and supportive, contains no unsolicited medical advice, and includes only privacy-safe details.

## Variations

- `coworker`: mention that there is no need to reply and that you wish them a smooth recovery.
- `close-friend`: a concrete offer and a personal memory may be welcome.
- Template:
  "I am so sorry you are going through this. I am thinking of you and hoping today is a little easier.
  No need to reply. I can drop soup on Tuesday or run an errand this week if that would help."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Protect health privacy, avoid diagnosis or treatment advice, and do not share someone's condition with others unless they asked you to.
