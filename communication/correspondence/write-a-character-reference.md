## Goal

You write a truthful, specific character reference that helps a decision-maker understand someone's reliability, conduct, and growth without exaggeration.

## Preconditions

- The recipient, deadline, submission method, and purpose of the reference.
- The person's full name, relevant role or case context, and any length or format limits.
- Direct knowledge of the person that you can honestly describe.

## Steps

1. **Clarify the purpose and audience.** Ask whether the letter is for court, housing, employment, school, licensing, or another decision. → *Expect:* you know what traits matter to the reader.
2. **Confirm you can speak honestly.** Decline or keep it narrow if you lack direct knowledge. → *Expect:* every claim in the letter is something you can personally support.
3. **Open with your relationship and duration.** State how you know the person and for how long. → *Expect:* the reader can judge the basis of your opinion.
4. **Give two or three concrete examples.** Describe observed behavior, not broad praise alone. → *Expect:* the letter includes specific evidence of responsibility, kindness, reliability, or accountability.
5. **Address the context carefully.** [BRANCH: court or discipline matter, acknowledge awareness of the situation only if true and focus on observed accountability | job or housing matter, focus on dependability and trustworthiness] → *Expect:* the letter is relevant without pretending to know facts you do not know.
6. **Close with a measured recommendation.** Say what you can recommend and provide contact information if appropriate. → *Expect:* the reader knows your conclusion and can follow up.
7. **Review for accuracy and consent.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before submitting, confirm the person is comfortable with the facts you included and that nothing is false or misleading. → *Expect:* the final letter is truthful, signed, and ready to submit.

## Decision points

- You are asked to lie or omit material facts → decline to write the letter.
- The letter is for court, immigration, or licensing → keep it factual and consider whether the person should review it with counsel.
- You have only known the person casually → write a limited reference or do not write one.
- Submission must be confidential → send it directly rather than giving the person an editable copy.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague praise:** detect phrases like "great person" with no examples → add concrete incidents you personally observed.
- **F2 Overclaiming:** detect claims about events you did not witness → remove or rephrase as limited to your own experience.
- **F3 Wrong tone for legal setting:** detect jokes, emotional pressure, or attacks on others → revise to calm factual language.
- **F4 Missed deadline or format:** detect late submission or a portal rejection → submit again in the required format and notify the requester.

## Verification

The reference states your relationship, gives specific truthful examples, matches the requested format, is signed or submitted by the deadline, and contains no claim you cannot personally support.

## Variations

- `court`: include the case caption or defendant name if requested, and avoid telling the judge what sentence to impose unless counsel asked for that exact framing.
- `employment`: emphasize work habits, judgment, collaboration, and reliability.
- Template:
  "I have known [Name] for [time] as [relationship]. In that time, I have personally seen [specific example].
  Based on my experience, I believe [Name] is [measured trait] and would [specific recommendation].
  I can be reached at [contact] if more information is needed."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because false references can damage legal, housing, job, or immigration outcomes. Do not include private medical, criminal, financial, or family details unless they are necessary, true, and authorized.
