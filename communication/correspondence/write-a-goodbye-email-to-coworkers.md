## Goal

You send a professional goodbye email that thanks coworkers, leaves a good final impression, and shares appropriate contact information.

## Preconditions

- Your final workday and any company policy about departure messages.
- A recipient list approved by norms or your manager if needed.
- Personal contact details you are comfortable sharing.

## Steps

1. **Confirm timing and policy.** Check whether departure communications need manager or HR approval. → *Expect:* the message will not conflict with company process.
2. **Choose the recipient list.** Send broadly only if that is normal; otherwise send team or individual notes. → *Expect:* the audience matches your relationships.
3. **Keep the tone positive and brief.** Thank people for the work and avoid grievances, gossip, or confidential details. → *Expect:* the email reads professionally.
4. **Mention transition only lightly.** If useful, name your last day and who owns key responsibilities next. → *Expect:* coworkers know any practical next step.
5. **Share personal contact if desired.** Include LinkedIn or personal email only if you want future contact. → *Expect:* people can stay in touch without using company systems.
6. **Send near the end of your last day or final week.** → *Expect:* the message arrives after transition plans are mostly settled.
7. **Send private thanks separately.** Write individual notes to mentors, close teammates, or direct reports. → *Expect:* important relationships are acknowledged personally.

## Decision points

- Departure is involuntary or tense → keep it shorter and neutral.
- You are moving to a competitor → avoid naming confidential future work if restricted.
- You manage people → include transition reassurance and appreciation for the team.
- Company disables email quickly → prepare the note earlier and coordinate timing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much detail:** detect explanations about why you are leaving → cut to thanks and contact.
- **F2 Wrong audience:** detect clients, vendors, or sensitive groups included unintentionally → revise recipients before sending.
- **F3 Confidential disclosure:** detect project names, strategy, or internal issues → remove them.
- **F4 Missed people:** detect important coworkers not included → send individual follow-ups.

## Verification

The goodbye email has been sent to the intended recipients, states your last day if appropriate, thanks coworkers, shares only approved contact information, and contains no complaints or confidential information.

## Variations

- `small-team`: a warmer note with one or two specific thanks may fit.
- `large-company`: keep the broad email short and send personal notes separately.
- Template:
  "Hi everyone, as some of you know, my last day at [Company] is [date].
  Thank you for the collaboration, support, and good work we have shared.
  I would be glad to stay in touch at [contact]. Wishing you all the best."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but employment reputation matters. Do not air grievances, disclose future confidential plans, export company contact lists improperly, or share personal contact details you do not want circulated.
