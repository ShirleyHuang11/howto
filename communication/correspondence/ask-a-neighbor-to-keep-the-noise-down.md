## Goal

You ask a neighbor to reduce disruptive noise in a calm, specific way that gives them a fair chance to fix the problem.

## Preconditions

- A clear pattern of the noise: dates, times, duration, and type.
- Building quiet hours, lease rules, HOA rules, or local noise ordinance if available.
- A safe communication channel such as a note, message, or daytime conversation.

## Steps

1. **Document the pattern first.** Write down dates, times, and what you heard for several incidents. → *Expect:* you can describe the issue without exaggeration.
2. **Check the applicable rule.** Review lease quiet hours, building rules, or local ordinance before contacting them. → *Expect:* you know whether you are asking for courtesy or rule compliance.
3. **Choose a low-conflict channel.** [BRANCH: you know them, speak briefly during daytime | you do not know them, leave a short note or message | you feel unsafe, contact management instead] → *Expect:* the first contact does not escalate unnecessarily.
4. **Describe the impact specifically.** Name the noise and time window, not their character or motives. → *Expect:* the neighbor knows what to change.
5. **Make a practical request.** Ask for a concrete adjustment such as lowering bass after 10 p.m. or moving workouts away from a shared wall. → *Expect:* the request is actionable.
6. **Thank them and leave room for dialogue.** Invite them to tell you if noise from your unit is also a problem. → *Expect:* the tone stays neighborly.
7. **Escalate only if needed.** If the pattern continues, send documentation to landlord, HOA, or local non-emergency services according to severity. → *Expect:* escalation includes facts and prior attempt to resolve.

## Decision points

- You hear violence, threats, or immediate danger → call emergency services instead of writing a note.
- The neighbor responds aggressively → stop direct contact and use management or authorities.
- The noise is daytime living noise → ask politely but recognize ordinary use may be allowed.
- The issue is recurring late-night parties → document and escalate under quiet-hour rules.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague complaint:** detect "you are always loud" → revise with dates, times, and specific noise.
- **F2 Unsafe confrontation:** detect anger, intoxication, or threats → leave and use a safer channel.
- **F3 Retaliation or harassment:** detect increased noise or hostile contact → save evidence and involve management or local authorities.
- **F4 No improvement:** detect repeated incidents after a reasonable request → submit the log and prior message to the responsible party.

## Verification

The neighbor has received a specific written or spoken request, you have kept a dated noise log, and either the noise decreases during the requested hours or you have evidence ready for formal escalation.

## Variations

- `apartment`: building management often requires a written log before acting.
- `single-family`: local noise ordinances usually set decibel or quiet-hour standards and may require non-emergency reporting.
- Template:
  "Hi, I am your neighbor in [unit/address]. I wanted to ask if you could lower the [music/bass/footsteps] after [time].
  It has been carrying into my [bedroom/office] on [examples], and I would really appreciate the help.
  Please let me know if noise from my place is ever an issue too."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because neighbor disputes can escalate. Do not threaten, insult, record where illegal, or confront someone who seems intoxicated or aggressive; use management or authorities for safety concerns.
