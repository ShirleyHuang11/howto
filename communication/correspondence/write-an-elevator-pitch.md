## Goal

You create a short spoken or written pitch that explains what you do, who it helps, and what conversation you want next.

## Preconditions

- A target audience: employer, investor, customer, collaborator, admissions reviewer, or networking contact.
- The thing being pitched: yourself, a project, product, service, or idea.
- A clear next action, such as a meeting, introduction, feedback, or follow-up.

## Steps

1. **Define the listener's interest.** Write what this audience cares about and what problem they recognize. → *Expect:* the pitch starts from their context, not yours.
2. **State the one-sentence core.** Say what you do or offer in plain language. → *Expect:* a stranger can repeat the main idea.
3. **Add the benefit or evidence.** Include a concrete outcome, differentiator, credential, or traction point. → *Expect:* the pitch has a reason to believe it.
4. **Make it conversational.** Use words you would actually say out loud. → *Expect:* the pitch does not sound memorized from a brochure.
5. **End with a next step.** Ask for a meeting, advice, contact, trial, or permission to send details. → *Expect:* the listener knows how to respond.
6. **Time it aloud.** Aim for 20 to 45 seconds unless a longer format is requested. → *Expect:* the pitch fits a real brief encounter.
7. **Prepare variants.** Create one version for speaking and one for email or profile text. → *Expect:* you can adapt without rambling.

## Decision points

- Pitching yourself for jobs → connect your background to the role's needs.
- Pitching a product → name the customer, problem, and measurable value.
- Pitching an idea without traction → emphasize insight, prototype, or next validation step.
- Listener looks rushed → use the 10-second version and ask permission to follow up.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too broad:** detect a pitch that lists many audiences or features → choose one listener and one outcome.
- **F2 No ask:** detect an ending that trails off → add a clear next step.
- **F3 Jargon overload:** detect acronyms or internal terms → replace with plain language.
- **F4 Overlong delivery:** detect a pitch over one minute → cut background and keep only problem, value, proof, ask.

## Verification

The pitch can be delivered aloud in under 45 seconds, names the audience or problem, states the value, includes one proof point, and ends with a specific next action.

## Variations

- `networking`: make the ask advice-oriented and low pressure.
- `sales`: include the buyer pain and a measurable result.
- Template:
  "I help [audience] solve [problem] by [approach].
  The result is [benefit/proof].
  I am looking for [next step], and I would value [specific ask]."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Do not disclose confidential employer plans, client names, unreleased products, or misleading performance claims to make the pitch sound stronger.
