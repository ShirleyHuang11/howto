## Goal

You write a brief, sincere note that acknowledges the loss, offers comfort without cliches, and does not add burden to the grieving person.

## Preconditions

- The recipient's name and relationship to the person who died.
- The deceased person's name, if appropriate to use.
- A card, email, message, or letter format suitable for the relationship.

## Steps

1. **Choose the right level of closeness.** Match the tone to your relationship: warm and personal for close friends, simple and respectful for coworkers or acquaintances. → *Expect:* the note feels appropriate rather than intrusive.
2. **Open with direct sympathy.** Say you are sorry for the loss and name the person if you knew them. → *Expect:* the first sentence clearly acknowledges what happened.
3. **Share one true memory or quality if you have one.** Keep it specific and brief. → *Expect:* the note offers a real human detail, not a generic phrase.
4. **Offer practical support only if you can follow through.** Name one concrete help, such as a meal, ride, errand, or quiet check-in. → *Expect:* the recipient is not asked to manage vague offers.
5. **Avoid explanations and comparisons.** Do not say everything happens for a reason, compare grief, or ask for details. → *Expect:* the note stays focused on the recipient's loss.
6. **Close gently.** Use a simple signoff such as "With sympathy," "Thinking of you," or "With love." → *Expect:* the note ends without demanding a response.
7. **Send it without over-editing.** A short sincere note is better than a delayed perfect one. → *Expect:* the recipient receives a kind message in a reasonable timeframe.

## Decision points

- You did not know the deceased → focus on care for the recipient, not invented memories.
- The loss was traumatic or public → avoid speculation, details, or comments about blame.
- You are offering help → propose a specific action and time window.
- You are late sending it → acknowledge briefly, but still send the note.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much about yourself:** detect many sentences starting with "I" → revise to center the recipient and the person who died.
- **F2 Vague support offer:** detect "let me know if you need anything" alone → add one concrete thing you can do.
- **F3 Accidental platitude:** detect phrases that explain or minimize grief → replace with simple sympathy.
- **F4 Relationship uncertainty:** detect not knowing family names or details → keep the wording plain and avoid assumptions.

## Verification

The note names the loss, includes one sincere sentence of care or memory, avoids burdensome requests, and has been sent or prepared in the chosen format.

## Variations

- `close friend`: "I am so sorry about Maya. I keep thinking about how she made every room feel warmer. I can bring dinner Thursday or take the kids to practice Friday if either helps. I love you and am here."
- `coworker`: "I am very sorry for your loss. Please accept my sincere condolences to you and your family. I am thinking of you and wishing you comfort in the days ahead."
- `late note`: "I am sorry I am writing late, but I wanted you to know I have been thinking of you since hearing about your father's death."

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but grief is sensitive. Do not share private details, speculate about cause of death, or offer help you are not prepared to provide.
