## Goal

You provide an honest, specific tenant reference that helps a future landlord verify rental history without disclosing unnecessary private information.

## Preconditions

- The tenant's written permission to provide a reference.
- Accurate records of lease dates, rent payment history, property condition, and rule compliance.
- The recipient's name, organization, email address, and any required reference form.

## Steps

1. **Confirm consent and recipient identity.** Ask the tenant to authorize the reference and verify the landlord, agent, or screening company requesting it. → *Expect:* you know the request is legitimate and permitted.
2. **Review objective records.** Check tenancy dates, rent amount if relevant, late payments, notices, maintenance issues, and move-out condition. → *Expect:* the reference is based on records rather than memory alone.
3. **Limit the scope to tenancy facts.** Do not mention protected characteristics, family status, disability, medical issues, religion, race, nationality, or rumors. → *Expect:* the draft avoids discriminatory or irrelevant content.
4. **Write a concise recommendation.** State your relationship, dates, payment reliability, care of the unit, communication, and whether you would rent to them again. → *Expect:* the recipient receives useful rental information.
5. **Use careful language for mixed history.** Describe verified facts neutrally, such as "rent was paid after reminders in three months," not insults or speculation. → *Expect:* negative facts are accurate and defensible.
6. **Send through the requested channel and save a copy.** Include your contact details only if you are comfortable with follow-up. → *Expect:* the reference is delivered and archived.

## Decision points

- Tenant has not given consent → do not provide a reference; ask the requester to have the tenant contact you.
- Records are incomplete → say what you can verify and omit uncertain claims.
- You would not recommend the tenant → provide a factual reference only, or decline if your jurisdiction or policy favors neutral references.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Privacy over-disclosure:** detect medical, family, immigration, or other irrelevant details in the draft → remove them before sending.
- **F2 Inaccurate dates or amounts:** detect conflict with lease records → correct the reference and, if already sent, send a brief correction.
- **F3 Pressure to exaggerate:** detect a request from the tenant to hide material facts → decline to make false statements.
- **F4 Fake requester:** detect a personal email, odd payment demand, or unverified company → verify independently before responding.

## Verification

The reference has been sent to the verified recipient, matches your records, and contains no protected or unnecessary personal details.

## Variations

- `us`: fair-housing risk is significant; keep references objective and consistent across applicants.
- Property manager: company policy may require a neutral verification form instead of a narrative recommendation.
- Example:
  "I rented 12 Oak Street, Unit 2, to Jordan Lee from June 2023 through May 2025. Rent was paid on time, communication was prompt, and the unit was returned in good condition. Based on that tenancy, I would rent to Jordan again."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because false or discriminatory references can affect housing and legal exposure. Provide only accurate tenancy facts with the tenant's consent, and confirm before sending any negative or sensitive information.
