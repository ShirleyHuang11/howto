## Goal

You send a polite follow-up that makes the original request easy to answer and gives the recipient a clear next action.

## Preconditions

- The original message, date sent, and any promised response timeline.
- A concise subject line and the specific decision, answer, or action you need.
- Enough time has passed for a reasonable reply unless the matter is urgent.

## Steps

1. **Check the original thread.** Confirm the message was sent to the right person and did not already receive a reply. → *Expect:* you know exactly what is outstanding.
2. **Choose the right timing.** Wait two to five business days for ordinary work messages, longer for low-priority requests, shorter for deadlines. → *Expect:* the follow-up feels reasonable rather than impatient.
3. **Reply in the same thread.** Keep the subject and context attached. → *Expect:* the recipient can see the original request below your note.
4. **Restate the ask in one sentence.** Make the needed response obvious. → *Expect:* the recipient can answer without rereading a long exchange.
5. **Add a deadline only if real.** State the date or consequence neutrally. → *Expect:* urgency is clear without sounding like a threat.
6. **Offer an easy out or alternate contact.** If appropriate, ask whether someone else is better placed to handle it. → *Expect:* the recipient can redirect you instead of ignoring the message.
7. **Send and set a reminder.** Note when you will follow up again or use another channel. → *Expect:* the message is sent and a next check date exists.

## Decision points

- The request is time-sensitive → include the real deadline in the first sentence.
- The recipient may be away → add a line asking who can cover in their absence.
- This is a second follow-up → be more direct and consider phone, chat, or escalation.
- The original ask was unclear → rewrite the request rather than merely saying "checking in."

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Buried ask:** detect a follow-up that says only "just following up" → add the specific decision or answer needed.
- **F2 Wrong recipient:** detect bounced mail or no connection to the topic → forward to the correct person with context.
- **F3 Too many nudges:** detect multiple unanswered follow-ups → stop repeating the same email and choose a different path.
- **F4 Defensive tone:** detect blame or sarcasm → revise to assume good intent and focus on the next action.

## Verification

The follow-up is sent in the correct thread, contains the specific ask and any real deadline, and you have a reminder or next escalation date recorded.

## Variations

- `job-application`: wait at least one week after the stated timeline unless the employer gave another window.
- `sales-or-networking`: keep it especially short and include a graceful close if there is no interest.
- Template:
  "Hi [Name], I wanted to follow up on my note below about [specific request].
  Could you let me know [answer/action needed] by [date], or point me to the right person?
  Thanks, [Your name]"

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but avoid forwarding confidential threads broadly. If the topic involves money, legal deadlines, medical details, or employment status, verify recipients before sending.
