## Goal

You send a written billing dispute that identifies the error, preserves deadlines, and creates a record the company, collector, insurer, or regulator can act on.

## Preconditions

- The bill, statement, invoice, explanation of benefits, or collection notice.
- Account number, service dates, disputed amount, and reason for dispute.
- Proof such as receipts, cancellation confirmations, contract terms, insurance documents, or prior correspondence.

## Steps

1. **Read the bill and deadline.** Identify the due date, dispute deadline, account number, charge dates, and where written disputes must be sent. → *Expect:* you know the last safe date to dispute and the required address or portal.
2. **Separate undisputed from disputed amounts.** Decide whether to pay any undisputed portion to avoid late fees or service interruption. → *Expect:* the disputed amount is isolated.
3. **Gather evidence.** Collect receipts, contract pages, cancellation numbers, screenshots, delivery records, medical EOBs, or prior credits. → *Expect:* every disputed charge has supporting evidence.
4. **Write the dispute clearly.** State that you dispute the bill, identify each charge, explain why it is wrong, and request correction, itemization, investigation, or validation. → *Expect:* a billing agent can understand and investigate the issue.
5. **Request account protection while investigated.** Ask the company not to assess late fees, send the disputed amount to collections, or report it as delinquent while the dispute is pending, if applicable. → *Expect:* the request is explicit in the letter.
6. **Review before sending.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm the account number, disputed amount, addresses, and attachments before sending because deadlines and credit reporting may depend on the written record. → *Expect:* the packet is accurate and complete.
7. **Send through a traceable method.** Use the portal, certified mail, fax confirmation, secure message, or email address specified for disputes. → *Expect:* you have proof of submission.
8. **Monitor the account and credit impact.** Watch for responses, adjusted statements, collection notices, or credit-report changes. → *Expect:* the account shows investigation, correction, or a written denial.
9. **Escalate if needed.** If denied or ignored, use the regulator, insurer appeal, card issuer dispute, state consumer office, CFPB, or small claims route appropriate to the bill type. → *Expect:* escalation includes the original dispute and proof of delivery.

## Decision points

- Credit card billing error → use the card issuer's formal billing-error process and deadline.
- Medical bill → compare the provider bill to the insurer explanation of benefits and request itemized coding review.
- Debt collector notice → request debt validation in writing within the legal deadline for your jurisdiction.
- Utility or telecom shutoff risk → contact the utility commission or emergency billing assistance process promptly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sent to wrong address:** detect no acknowledgment or returned mail → resend to the dispute address on the bill and keep both records.
- **F2 Deadline missed:** detect that the formal dispute period passed → still request correction, but escalate through customer service, regulator, or negotiated settlement.
- **F3 Evidence incomplete:** detect a denial citing lack of proof → send the specific missing receipt, cancellation, EOB, or contract term.
- **F4 Collections continue:** detect collector contact or credit reporting while disputed → send the dispute record to the collector and regulator or credit bureau as applicable.
- **F5 Verbal promise not honored:** detect no written adjustment after a phone call → send a written follow-up naming the date, representative, and promised correction.

## Verification

You have written proof that the dispute was submitted, the account identifies the disputed amount, and the bill is corrected, under investigation, or denied in writing with stated next steps.

## Variations

- `credit-card`: many jurisdictions have strict written-dispute deadlines; send to the billing inquiries address, not the payment address.
- `medical`: include patient name, date of service, provider, insurer claim number, and EOB discrepancy.
- `template`: "I dispute the $148.22 charge dated August 4, 2026 on Account 1234. The service was canceled on July 28, 2026, confirmation C-8891 attached. Please remove the charge, waive related late fees, and confirm the corrected balance in writing."

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because billing disputes can affect money, service, collections, and credit. Send disputes in writing, keep proof, redact unrelated personal data, and do not ignore separate legal or collection deadlines.
