## Goal

Find and use a reasonably clean restroom while traveling abroad without getting stuck by payment, access codes, toilet style, or missing supplies.

## Preconditions

- You have small coins or a contactless payment method for paid toilets where common.
- You carry tissues or toilet paper, hand sanitizer, and any needed menstrual or medical supplies.
- You can identify restroom signs, local words, or map symbols for toilets.

## Steps

1. **Use predictable locations first.** Look for major train stations, department stores, museums, hotels, shopping malls, libraries, or chain cafes. → *Expect:* you find a staffed or maintained facility more reliably than a random street toilet.
2. **Check access requirements.** Look for payment gates, receipt codes, customer-only signs, gender labels, and accessible-stall symbols before queuing. → *Expect:* you know whether you need coins, a purchase, or a code.
3. **Carry supplies in.** Bring tissue, sanitizer, and a small bag for trash if plumbing signs say not to flush paper. → *Expect:* missing paper or soap does not become a problem.
4. **Inspect before committing.** Check lock, cleanliness, toilet type, paper, disposal bin, water, and floor condition. → *Expect:* you can choose another stall or leave before unpacking.
5. **Use local disposal rules.** Flush only what signs allow and put paper or hygiene products in bins where plumbing requires it. → *Expect:* you leave without clogging or violating posted rules.
6. **Wash or sanitize and reset.** Wash hands if possible, sanitize after touching doors or payment gates, and restock tissues later. → *Expect:* you leave clean and prepared for the next need.

## Decision points

- Paid restroom ahead → pay if it is staffed and clean; the fee often buys maintenance.
- No public toilets nearby → buy a small item at a cafe or ask hotel lobby staff politely.
- Squat toilet only → face the correct direction, secure loose items, and use handrails or walls carefully.
- Accessibility need → prioritize museums, malls, transit hubs, and hotels over older small cafes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No coins:** payment gate accepts only coins → ask nearby staff for change or find a card-accepting facility.
- **F2 No supplies:** stall lacks paper or soap → use carried tissue and sanitizer, then restock at the next shop.
- **F3 Dirty or unsafe stall:** lock, floor, or cleanliness is unacceptable → leave and use the next predictable location rather than forcing it.
- **F4 Code required:** door needs a receipt or keypad code → ask the cashier or make a small purchase if appropriate.

## Verification

You used a facility with acceptable cleanliness and privacy, followed local payment and disposal rules, and still have basic supplies for the next restroom stop.

## Variations

- `europe`: paid station toilets and cafe receipt codes are common in many cities.
- `japan`: public restrooms are often clean, but controls and bidet panels may be unfamiliar.
- Rural travel: carry more paper and sanitizer because facilities may be sparse or basic.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep your bag off wet floors, do not leave valuables on hooks or shelves, and avoid isolated facilities when a staffed option is nearby.
