## Goal

Turn a missing checked bag into a documented claim with a tracking number, delivery plan, and reimbursement record.

## Preconditions

- You have your baggage claim tag, boarding pass or booking reference, and delivery address for the next 24 to 72 hours.
- You know what the bag looks like: brand, color, size, hard/soft shell, wheels, tags, and distinctive contents.
- You have not left the airport baggage hall without checking whether an airline baggage desk is still open.

## Steps

1. **Check the nearby exceptions first.** Look at oversized baggage, late-arrival belts, priority piles, and bags removed from the carousel by staff. → *Expect:* the bag is either found nearby or clearly absent from the baggage area.
2. **File before leaving the airport.** Go to the operating airline's baggage desk and give the bag tag, route, connection details, and delivery address. → *Expect:* you receive a written Property Irregularity Report or claim number.
3. **Describe the bag precisely.** Provide brand, color, dimensions, number of wheels, luggage tag text, AirTag or tracker status, and two or three unique contents. → *Expect:* the claim record distinguishes your bag from similar black rollers.
4. **Ask what expenses are covered.** Request the airline's delayed-bag allowance, claim deadline, receipt requirements, and delivery process. → *Expect:* you know what essentials can be bought and how to claim reimbursement.
5. **Buy only immediate essentials.** Purchase toiletries, underwear, weather-appropriate basics, medications only through proper channels, and trip-critical clothing at reasonable prices. → *Expect:* receipts show necessary interim purchases tied to the delay.
6. **Track twice daily.** Use the claim portal, airline app, and tracker location if available, then update the delivery address when you move hotels or cities. → *Expect:* the claim stays current and the bag route is visible or escalated.
7. **Close the claim after delivery.** Inspect the bag, note damage or missing contents before signing if possible, and submit reimbursement with receipts. → *Expect:* the bag is back or a separate damage/loss claim is opened within the airline deadline.

## Decision points

- Bag tracker shows the bag at the airport → give the exact terminal or carousel area to the baggage desk but let staff retrieve it.
- You have a cruise, tour, or remote transfer soon → ask for pickup at the airport or delivery to the next reachable hotel instead of today's address.
- The bag is delayed beyond the airline's threshold → convert from delayed-bag tracking to formal lost-bag compensation with an itemized contents list.
- Essential medication is missing → contact a clinician, pharmacy, insurer, or embassy channel instead of waiting for the airline.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Desk closed:** no baggage agent is present → file through the airline app or airport service phone immediately and return to the desk when it opens.
- **F2 No claim number:** staff says to wait without paperwork → insist politely on a written report because reimbursement usually requires it.
- **F3 Delivery missed:** courier arrives after you changed hotels → update the claim portal and call the baggage service with the new address and date window.
- **F4 Bag arrives damaged:** new cracks, broken wheels, or missing contents are visible → photograph before use and file a damage report within the stated deadline.

## Verification

You have a claim number, baggage tag number, delivery address, airline coverage rules, saved receipts for essentials, and either the returned bag or an active lost/damage claim.

## Variations

- International arrivals: customs rules may require you to be present or authorize delivery for the bag to clear.
- Codeshare flights: file with the airline that operated the final flight into the airport, even if you bought from a partner.
- Group travel: file each missing bag under its own tag, even when all bags are on one booking.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because passports, hotel addresses, tracker locations, and receipt details may be shared. Do not put home addresses on public luggage tags; use a phone or email and keep valuables, medication, documents, and chargers out of checked bags.
