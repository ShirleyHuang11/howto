## Goal

Cross an international land border legally with documents, transport, customs declarations, money, and onward plans in order.

## Preconditions

- You have passport, visa or eTA if required, entry forms, vehicle papers if driving, and proof of onward travel or lodging if required.
- You know opening hours, official crossing point, transport drop-off and pickup locations, and local currency needs.
- You understand customs limits for cash, alcohol, tobacco, food, medicine, pets, and restricted goods.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the official crossing.** Check government or operator sources for hours, closures, visa-on-arrival availability, and pedestrian or vehicle rules. → *Expect:* the crossing is open for your nationality and transport mode.
2. **Stage documents before arrival.** Put passport, visa, entry forms, vehicle registration, rental permission, insurance, and onward proof in one reachable folder. → *Expect:* inspection can happen without unpacking luggage.
3. **Exit the first country properly.** Get exit stamp, electronic exit confirmation, or vehicle export processing where required. → *Expect:* your departure is recorded before entering the next country.
4. **Enter the next country at the official post.** Present documents, answer itinerary questions briefly, and pay only posted official fees at marked counters. → *Expect:* entry is stamped, electronically approved, or otherwise documented.
5. **Clear customs and biosecurity.** Declare cash, goods, food, medication, equipment, or vehicle contents as required. → *Expect:* customs releases you without undeclared restricted items.
6. **Reset practical basics after entry.** Get local cash or eSIM if needed, confirm onward transport, check time zone, and save emergency numbers. → *Expect:* the next leg works under the new country's rules.

## Decision points

- Visa-on-arrival uncertain → obtain the visa in advance because border staff may not solve missing eligibility.
- Taxi or bus swaps at the border → keep luggage with you through both exit and entry procedures.
- Driving a rental car → confirm cross-border permission and insurance in writing before reaching the checkpoint.
- Officials ask unusual questions or fees → stay calm, ask for a receipt and posted fee schedule, and avoid arguing at the window.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing exit stamp:** you skipped or missed exit control → return to the exit office immediately before attempting entry.
- **F2 Visa problem:** entry officer says your visa or stay allowance is invalid → ask about correction, return, or embassy contact before leaving the controlled area.
- **F3 Unofficial helper pressure:** a tout offers forms, shortcuts, or fee handling → decline and use only signed official counters.
- **F4 Prohibited item found:** customs flags food, medicine, or gear → declare, surrender, or provide prescription and permits as instructed.

## Verification

Your passport or electronic record shows legal exit and entry, customs has released you, luggage and transport are intact, and you know your allowed stay length.

## Variations

- Schengen-style borders: internal checks may be light, but external entry and stay-day counting remain strict.
- Remote crossings: hours, cash, and onward transport can be limited, so backup plans matter more.
- Vehicle crossings: insurance, temporary import permits, and road taxes can be as important as passports.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because immigration errors can lead to denial, fines, detention, or illegal overstay. Use official crossings, protect documents from touts, and do not carry goods for strangers.
