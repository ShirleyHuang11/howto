## Goal

Drive in another country with legal documents, a suitable vehicle, local-road awareness, and a plan for navigation, fuel, tolls, and emergencies.

## Preconditions

- You have a valid driver's license, passport, rental agreement, insurance proof, and an International Driving Permit if required.
- You know whether traffic drives on the left or right, common road signs, speed units, and local blood-alcohol limits.
- You can navigate offline and contact roadside assistance.

## Steps

1. **Confirm legal eligibility.** Check license validity, minimum age, IDP rules, border-crossing permission, and insurance before accepting the vehicle. → *Expect:* documents satisfy police, rental, and insurance requirements.
2. **Inspect the car before leaving.** Photograph all sides, wheels, windshield, fuel level or battery charge, spare tire or repair kit, safety vest, warning triangle, and child seats. → *Expect:* existing damage and required equipment are documented.
3. **Set navigation while parked.** Load offline maps, route, toll settings, fuel stops, and hotel parking destination before moving. → *Expect:* the first drive starts without phone handling at speed.
4. **Rehearse local controls.** Find headlights, wipers, mirrors, gear selector, parking brake, fuel cap, and lane position cues. → *Expect:* basic controls are muscle-ready before traffic.
5. **Drive defensively for the first hour.** Use slower lanes, avoid city centers at rush hour, keep longer following distance, and let locals pass. → *Expect:* you adapt to signage, lane discipline, and roundabouts without pressure.
6. **Handle tolls, fuel, and parking early.** Learn the toll tag or ticket system, fuel type, pump payment flow, and legal parking signs before you are nearly empty or late. → *Expect:* no avoidable fines, wrong-fuel risk, or unpaid tolls.
7. **Park and secure the car.** Remove visible luggage, lock the car, note the parking level or street rules, and save the rental emergency number. → *Expect:* the vehicle is legal, findable, and not advertising baggage.

## Decision points

- Left-side driving after a long flight → sleep first or use transit to the first hotel because fatigue compounds lane mistakes.
- Mountain, snow, or rural route → confirm tires, chains, fuel range, daylight, and mobile coverage before departure.
- City has congestion zones or ZTL-style restricted areas → park outside and use transit unless your hotel provides explicit entry instructions.
- Border crossing planned → get rental-company permission in writing before leaving the country.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong-side drift:** you clip lane markings or enter a roundabout awkwardly → slow down, follow a vehicle, and use a passenger as a lane-position caller.
- **F2 Wrong fuel risk:** nozzle or fuel label does not match the rental agreement → stop before pumping and ask station staff or the rental company.
- **F3 Toll missed:** no payment booth or tag beep occurs → use the official online post-payment system quickly with plate number and rental dates.
- **F4 Accident or breakdown:** warning lights, collision, or tire failure occurs → move to safety, wear required vest, call emergency services or roadside assistance, and document the scene.

## Verification

You are legally documented, the rental condition is photographed, route and controls are understood, and the first drive is completed without traffic violations, wrong-fuel incidents, or unsafe phone use.

## Variations

- `uk` and many former Commonwealth countries: left-side driving changes roundabout direction, lane position, and mirror habits.
- `eu`: vignettes, environmental zones, safety vests, and warning triangles may be mandatory.
- Island rentals: roads may be narrow, livestock or scooters common, and full insurance can be worth the cost.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because driving errors can injure people and create large liability. Do not drive impaired or sleep-deprived, keep documents private, and treat dashcam, GPS, and rental telematics as location records.
