## Goal

Buy travel insurance that matches the trip's medical, cancellation, interruption, baggage, and evacuation risks without relying on vague brochure promises.

## Preconditions

- You know trip dates, destinations, traveler ages, prepaid nonrefundable costs, activities, and existing coverage from health insurance or credit cards.
- You can access policy documents, not just comparison snippets.
- You understand that exclusions and claim documentation matter more than the headline coverage amount.

## Steps

1. **Define the risks worth insuring.** List nonrefundable flights, tours, cruises, medical exposure, rental cars, adventure activities, and weather season. → *Expect:* you know which losses would hurt enough to insure.
2. **Check existing coverage.** Review credit-card travel benefits, health insurance abroad, employer coverage, and homeowner baggage limits. → *Expect:* you know what is already covered and what gaps remain.
3. **Compare policies by wording.** Open the certificate or policy PDF and check cancellation reasons, medical limits, evacuation, pre-existing-condition waiver, baggage limits, and activity exclusions. → *Expect:* at least one policy covers the actual trip rather than only generic delays.
4. **Buy within waiver windows if needed.** If you need pre-existing-condition or cancel-for-any-reason options, purchase within the required days after initial trip deposit. → *Expect:* time-sensitive benefits are either secured or knowingly unavailable.
5. **Enter traveler and trip costs accurately.** Use legal names, birth dates, destination countries, travel dates, and prepaid cost totals that match receipts. → *Expect:* the confirmation reflects the real travelers and insured trip value.
6. **Save claim instructions offline.** Store the policy number, emergency assistance phone, covered reasons, claim deadlines, and receipt requirements with your itinerary. → *Expect:* you can start a claim during the trip without searching the web.

## Decision points

- International trip with limited medical coverage → prioritize medical and evacuation limits over small baggage benefits.
- Expensive prepaid cruise or tour → insure the full nonrefundable cost and cancellation/interruption terms carefully.
- Risky activity planned → confirm the activity is covered by name or buy a specific rider.
- Credit-card coverage looks sufficient → verify it applies only if the trip was paid with that card and meets duration limits.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Exclusion discovered late:** the policy excludes the main activity or condition → cancel within the free-look period if available and buy appropriate coverage.
- **F2 Underinsured trip cost:** prepaid costs exceed the insured amount → update coverage before departure if the insurer allows increases.
- **F3 Claim denied for missing proof:** insurer requests receipts or delay letters you lack → collect airline letters, medical records, police reports, and itemized receipts before leaving the location.
- **F4 Emergency number unreachable:** the assistance line fails abroad → use local emergency services first, then contact the insurer through alternate numbers or email.

## Verification

You hold a policy confirmation showing correct travelers, destinations, dates, coverage limits, insured trip cost, emergency assistance contact, and claim documentation rules.

## Variations

- Annual policy: better for frequent travelers but check per-trip duration and maximum trip-cost limits.
- Cruise travel: missed-port, medical evacuation, and interruption coverage can matter more than ordinary flight-delay coverage.
- Domestic trip: cancellation and interruption may matter, while medical evacuation may be less central depending on health coverage.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because medical details, ages, trip costs, and passport-adjacent identity information may be entered. Buy from licensed insurers or brokers, read exclusions, and do not send medical records except through official claim channels.
