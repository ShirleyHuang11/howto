## Goal

Tip appropriately while traveling by matching local norms, service context, bill structure, and payment method without overexposing cash.

## Preconditions

- You know the country or city, the service type, and whether the bill includes service charge or gratuity.
- You have small local notes or coins for cash situations.
- You can read receipts well enough to identify tax, service charge, cover charge, and optional tip lines.

## Steps

1. **Check the local norm before service.** Look up the country and service type from a reliable current travel source or ask hotel staff what locals do. → *Expect:* you know whether tipping is expected, optional, rounded, or unusual.
2. **Read the bill before adding anything.** Look for service charge, gratuity, coperto, couvert, tip line, or included-service wording. → *Expect:* you know whether a tip is already included.
3. **Match the service context.** Use restaurant, taxi, hotel porter, guide, spa, or delivery norms rather than one universal percentage. → *Expect:* the amount fits the role and local custom.
4. **Use small money deliberately.** Carry small notes for porters, housekeeping, restroom attendants, and guides where cash tipping is normal. → *Expect:* you can tip without asking for awkward change.
5. **Confirm card tip handling.** If paying by card, ask whether the server receives card tips or whether cash is preferred. → *Expect:* the tip reaches the intended worker when local practice allows.
6. **Leave politely and move on.** Hand cash discreetly, add the card tip clearly, or say thanks without tipping where that is the norm. → *Expect:* the interaction ends without confusion or pressure.

## Decision points

- Service charge is mandatory and high → do not add another full tip unless service was exceptional or local norm says to.
- Tipping is culturally uncommon → avoid forcing cash on someone who is refusing it.
- Tour guide or driver served you all day → tip based on local day-rate norms, group size, and quality.
- Cash is scarce → prioritize people who cannot receive tips through card systems.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Double tipped:** service charge and extra tip both appear → treat it as a lesson unless the duplicate was accidental and you can correct the receipt immediately.
- **F2 No small bills:** you only have large notes → ask for change from the cashier before the moment or skip cash tipping where inappropriate.
- **F3 Aggressive demand:** someone pressures for an invented fee or tip → decline calmly, move to a staffed area, and ask official staff if the charge is real.
- **F4 Card tip uncertainty:** the terminal shows no tip option or unfamiliar percentages → ask the server what is customary before tapping.

## Verification

The final amount paid matches local expectations, no included service charge was accidentally duplicated, and any intended cash or card tip was clearly received.

## Variations

- `us`: restaurant tips are often a major wage component and percentages are expected unless service charge is included.
- `japan`: routine tipping can confuse or embarrass staff; excellent service is usually built into the price.
- `europe`: service may be included, with rounding or small additional amounts common in many places.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid flashing large cash, do not photograph receipts with full card details, and resist pressure from unofficial helpers who convert confusion into money.
