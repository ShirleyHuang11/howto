## Goal

You claim any delay compensation, reimbursement, or duty-of-care payment you are entitled to after arriving substantially late.

## Preconditions

- Your booking reference, ticket number, flight number, scheduled times, actual arrival time, and passenger names.
- Receipts for meals, transport, hotel, or calls caused by the delay.
- Access to the airline account or email used for the booking.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the actual arrival delay.** Compare scheduled arrival with the time the aircraft door opened or the airline's official arrival record. → *Expect:* a documented arrival delay in hours and minutes.
2. **Identify the governing rule.** [BRANCH: EU/UK-covered flight, check EU261/UK261 compensation thresholds | other route, check the airline contract, local regulator, and travel insurance benefits] → *Expect:* a clear rule source for whether cash compensation, expenses, or only goodwill applies.
3. **Collect disruption evidence.** Save boarding passes, delay notifications, screenshots of status pages, and written staff explanations. → *Expect:* a claim folder with proof of the flight, delay, and stated reason.
4. **Separate compensation from expense reimbursement.** Compensation is a fixed statutory amount where eligible; reimbursement covers reasonable care costs with receipts. → *Expect:* two claim amounts: statutory compensation if applicable and itemized expenses.
5. **Check for disqualifying causes.** Look for extraordinary circumstances such as severe weather, security closure, or air traffic restrictions; technical or crew issues may still qualify in many regimes. → *Expect:* a claim position that explains why the delay is eligible or why only expenses are claimed.
6. **Submit the airline claim form.** Use the airline's official compensation/refund page, enter each passenger, flight, bank or payment details, and attach evidence. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm bank details and passenger names before submitting because wrong details can delay payment. → *Expect:* a submitted claim with reference number or confirmation email.
7. **Calendar the response deadline.** Note the airline's promised response time and the regulator's complaint escalation window. → *Expect:* a follow-up date and saved claim reference.
8. **Escalate if refused or ignored.** Reply with evidence and the specific rule; if unresolved, file with the relevant aviation regulator, ADR body, ombudsman, or card/travel-insurance channel. → *Expect:* an escalation case number or a revised airline decision.
9. **Confirm payment.** Match the received payment against the claimed amount and passenger count. → *Expect:* funds posted to your account or a written final denial to use for further escalation.

## Decision points

- Delay is under the legal threshold → claim only documented expenses or insurance benefits.
- Airline says "extraordinary circumstances" without detail → request the specific cause and operational evidence.
- You accepted miles or a voucher → verify whether it waived cash rights before relying on it.
- Multiple passengers on one booking → file for every eligible passenger, not just the purchaser.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing actual arrival time:** detect no reliable delay record → use airport flight history, airline status emails, and photos from the gate or baggage claim.
- **F2 Airline rejects with a generic reason:** detect a template denial with no facts → reply asking for the exact disruption cause and cite the applicable rule.
- **F3 Expense receipts are incomplete:** detect missing itemized proof → submit card statements plus photos, but expect partial reimbursement.
- **F4 Claim sent through a fee-taking agency by mistake:** detect a commission agreement → cancel within any cooling-off period or continue only if the net payout is acceptable.
- **F5 Payment sent as voucher:** detect credit instead of cash → reject it promptly if cash is required and allowed.

## Verification

The airline or escalation body has issued a written decision and either cash compensation/reimbursement has posted to your account for the expected amount, or a final denial with case reference is saved.

## Variations

- `eu`: EU261 generally covers eligible EU departures and many EU arrivals on EU carriers; fixed compensation depends on distance and delay.
- `uk`: UK261 is similar but enforced through UK channels.
- `us`: cash compensation for delays is usually not statutory; focus on refunds when you choose not to travel, airline commitments, expenses, and insurance.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because bank details, passport-like identity data, and money are involved. Use only official airline, regulator, insurer, or card issuer portals, and confirm before accepting vouchers that may waive cash rights.
