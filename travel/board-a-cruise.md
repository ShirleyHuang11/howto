## Goal

Board a cruise ship with documents accepted, luggage handed off correctly, onboard account active, and muster requirements completed.

## Preconditions

- You have passport or approved ID, visas if required, cruise boarding pass, health forms, luggage tags, and payment card for onboard charges.
- You know terminal address, arrival window, prohibited items, alcohol rules, and whether luggage is checked curbside.
- You have essential medication, documents, swimwear or first-day items in a carry-on because checked bags may arrive later.

## Steps

1. **Complete online check-in.** Upload required identity, payment, photo, emergency contact, and arrival-time information before embarkation day. → *Expect:* the cruise app or portal shows ready-to-board documents.
2. **Tag checked luggage.** Attach cruise luggage tags with cabin number, ship, sailing date, and passenger name before reaching the port. → *Expect:* porters can route bags to the correct stateroom.
3. **Arrive inside the assigned window.** Use the correct terminal and keep documents out before dropping luggage. → *Expect:* staff accept your arrival time and direct you to security or check-in.
4. **Hand bags only to official porters.** Confirm ship name and tip locally if appropriate, keeping documents, medication, valuables, and first-day essentials with you. → *Expect:* checked bags leave with tagged port staff and your carry-on stays with you.
5. **Clear security and document check.** Present ID, boarding pass, health paperwork, and any minors' consent documents. → *Expect:* you receive a key card, wearable, or app status authorizing boarding.
6. **Complete muster instructions.** Watch the safety briefing, visit the assigned muster station if required, and scan in. → *Expect:* the app, crew, or station scan marks muster complete.
7. **Verify the cabin and onboard account.** Check key access, dining time, packages, shore excursions, and bag delivery status. → *Expect:* the cruise account and stateroom setup match your booking.

## Decision points

- Passport vs birth certificate allowed → use the passport when possible because emergency disembarkation and international returns are simpler.
- Flying in same day → avoid it when possible; missed sailings are expensive and ships rarely wait.
- Medication needs refrigeration → ask guest services or cabin steward early, before sailaway.
- Luggage not delivered by evening → check the naughty-room or guest services for prohibited-item inspection.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Document rejected:** check-in will not accept your ID or visa → contact cruise staff immediately and escalate before the boarding window closes.
- **F2 Bag missing onboard:** luggage has not arrived after general delivery → use the tag photo and porter location to report it to guest services.
- **F3 Muster incomplete:** app or crew says safety briefing is pending → finish the video and station scan before venues or sailaway privileges are restricted.
- **F4 Prohibited item held:** security flags an iron, power strip, alcohol, or tool → surrender, ship-store, or retrieve after sailing according to cruise policy.

## Verification

You are physically aboard, your onboard account works, muster is marked complete, cabin access works, and checked luggage is delivered or formally tracked by guest services.

## Variations

- Closed-loop sailings: some itineraries accept limited ID combinations, but passports still reduce emergency risk.
- Luxury or small-ship cruises: luggage and check-in may happen at a hotel or lounge rather than a large terminal.
- River cruises: boarding windows can be looser, but low bridges and water levels may alter embarkation ports.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because identity documents, payment accounts, and medical information are processed, and missed boarding can strand travelers. Keep documents in hand, verify official porters, and complete safety requirements promptly.
