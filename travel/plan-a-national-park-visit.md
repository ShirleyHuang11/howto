## Goal

Plan a national park visit with the right permits, timing, route, supplies, weather awareness, and backup options.

## Preconditions

- You know the park, season, party size, transport, fitness level, and whether lodging or camping is needed.
- You can access official park alerts, reservation systems, trail maps, and road-condition updates.
- You have suitable clothing, water capacity, sun or cold protection, and emergency communication appropriate to the terrain.

## Steps

1. **Start with official alerts.** Check the park website for closures, timed-entry reservations, fire restrictions, wildlife warnings, road work, and shuttle rules. → *Expect:* current constraints are known before booking the day.
2. **Choose an achievable focus.** Pick one primary area or trail and one nearby backup instead of trying to cross the whole park. → *Expect:* the day has a realistic anchor and fallback.
3. **Reserve required access.** Book timed entry, parking, campsite, shuttle, ferry, or backcountry permit where required. → *Expect:* confirmations show correct date, vehicle, party size, and entry window.
4. **Plan the travel clock.** Add gate queues, shuttle waits, trail time, sunset, altitude, heat, and the drive back to lodging. → *Expect:* the schedule leaves daylight and recovery margin.
5. **Pack the park kit.** Bring water, food, layers, rain shell, map, headlamp, first aid, trash bag, charger, and any required traction or bear-safe storage. → *Expect:* common delays or weather shifts do not strand you unprepared.
6. **Check conditions the night before.** Recheck weather, smoke, road status, trail reports, and reservation QR codes. → *Expect:* the go/no-go decision uses current conditions.
7. **Start early and reassess on site.** Enter near opening, ask rangers about the route, and turn around if weather, fatigue, crowds, or trail conditions exceed the plan. → *Expect:* the visit completes without racing darkness or ignoring hazards.

## Decision points

- Timed entry is sold out → use shuttle access, less crowded entrances, cancellation releases, or a nearby public land alternative.
- Heat or storms are forecast → move hikes to dawn, shorten exposure, or choose scenic drives and visitor centers.
- Wildlife is active near the route → follow ranger closures and distance rules rather than trying for photos.
- Children or mixed fitness levels are present → choose shorter loops with bathrooms, shade, and exit options.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Permit missing:** the gate or trailhead requires a reservation you lack → switch to a legal non-reserved area or monitor official cancellations.
- **F2 Parking full:** trailhead lots are closed or overflowing → use shuttle, overflow lots, or the backup area rather than roadside parking illegally.
- **F3 Weather turns:** thunder, flash-flood risk, smoke, or heat becomes unsafe → turn back, shelter appropriately, and use visitor centers or scenic stops.
- **F4 Someone struggles:** pace, altitude, heat, or injury overwhelms a traveler → stop, hydrate, descend or shorten the route, and seek ranger help if symptoms persist.

## Verification

You have valid access or permit confirmations, a realistic route and backup, current condition checks, required supplies, and a completed visit that returns before unsafe weather or darkness.

## Variations

- `us`: some parks use timed-entry or trail-specific lotteries, and reservation windows can open months ahead.
- Alpine parks: snow and road closures can persist into summer.
- Desert parks: water, shade, and heat timing dominate the plan more than mileage.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from weather, terrain, wildlife, dehydration, and remote roads. Share your route with someone not on the trip, keep location data private when posting rare wildlife or fragile sites, and obey closures.
