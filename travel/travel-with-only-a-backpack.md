## Goal

Pack and travel with only one backpack that fits transport limits, covers the itinerary, and stays comfortable enough to carry all day.

## Preconditions

- You know airline or bus personal-item and carry-on dimensions, weather, laundry access, dress requirements, and trip length.
- You have a backpack with comfortable straps, a secure document pocket, and capacity appropriate to your body.
- You are willing to do laundry or repeat outfits.

## Steps

1. **Set the bag limit first.** Use the strictest carrier's size and weight limits, then choose a backpack that fits those limits when full. → *Expect:* the bag can board without forced checking.
2. **Build a capsule clothing list.** Pack layers and neutral combinations for three to five days, including one worn outfit, underwear, socks, sleepwear, and weather shell. → *Expect:* every clothing item works with multiple outfits.
3. **Shrink toiletries and liquids.** Use travel-size containers, solids where practical, and only products you actually use daily. → *Expect:* toiletries fit the security and leakage constraints.
4. **Limit electronics.** Bring phone, charger, adapter, power bank within airline rules, and only trip-critical laptop or camera gear. → *Expect:* electronics are useful enough to justify their weight.
5. **Pack by access frequency.** Put documents, medicine, jacket, liquids, and charger near the top or outer pockets, with laundry and spare clothes deeper. → *Expect:* airport and transit needs are reachable without unpacking.
6. **Test carry the loaded bag.** Walk stairs or around the block for ten minutes and remove the least useful items if straps bite or posture changes. → *Expect:* the pack is carryable without pain or awkward imbalance.
7. **Plan laundry before departure.** Identify sink-wash items, laundromats, hotel machines, or service wash days in the itinerary. → *Expect:* clean-clothing supply is scheduled rather than hoped for.

## Decision points

- Formal event or special gear required → consider renting, shipping, or wearing the bulky item instead of packing extras.
- Cold weather trip → prioritize thin layers and compression, but do not sacrifice warmth for size.
- Souvenirs likely → leave space or pack a foldable tote for the return.
- Airline weighs carry-ons → wear heavier layers and keep dense items minimal.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bag too heavy:** test carry is uncomfortable or over limit → remove duplicate clothing, extra shoes, books, and full-size toiletries.
- **F2 Liquids problem:** security rejects containers → transfer to compliant sizes or replace with solids before the checkpoint.
- **F3 Laundry unavailable:** planned machine or sink washing fails → buy one small clothing item or use a service wash at the next stop.
- **F4 Weather mismatch:** forecast changes after packing → add or buy one targeted layer rather than overhauling the whole bag.

## Verification

The packed backpack meets the strictest size and weight limit, carries comfortably for ten minutes, and contains documents, medicine, clothing, toiletries, electronics, and laundry plan for the full trip.

## Variations

- Business travel: use wrinkle-resistant clothing and one compact shoe strategy.
- Tropical travel: quick-dry fabrics and rain protection matter more than outfit count.
- Winter city travel: wear coat and boots in transit to keep the backpack smaller.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Keep documents and medication on your body or in a hidden pocket, avoid overloading your back, and do not pack prohibited blades, liquids, or batteries.
