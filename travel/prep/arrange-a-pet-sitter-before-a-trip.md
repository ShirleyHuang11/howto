## Goal

You arrange reliable pet care before travel, with clear instructions, emergency authority, and access logistics handled.

## Preconditions

- Trip dates, pet routine, veterinarian contact, medication needs, and emergency contact.
- Candidate sitter, boarding facility, friend, or family member.
- House access method if the pet stays home.

## Steps

1. **Choose the care type.** Compare in-home visits, overnight sitting, boarding, daycare boarding, or care by a trusted friend based on the pet's needs. → *Expect:* the care model fits the pet's health, temperament, and routine.
2. **Screen the sitter or facility.** Check references, reviews, insurance or bonding if applicable, experience with the species, and emergency procedures. → *Expect:* the caregiver is credible and available.
3. **Confirm dates, visits, and price.** Specify start and end times, number of visits, overnight expectations, feeding, walks, litter, play, and total cost. → *Expect:* both sides agree on schedule and payment terms.
4. **Prepare written care instructions.** Include food amounts, medication, behavior notes, hiding places, leash rules, vet contact, and what counts as an emergency. → *Expect:* a caregiver could follow the routine without guessing.
5. **Authorize emergency care.** Provide vet authorization, spending limit, microchip information, and emergency contacts. → *Expect:* the caregiver can get timely veterinary help if needed.
6. **Arrange secure access.** Set up keys, smart-lock code, alarm instructions, parking, building entry, and backup access. → *Expect:* the sitter can enter without exposing permanent access unnecessarily.
7. **Do a trial or handoff.** Have the sitter meet the pet, practice medication if needed, and walk through supplies. → *Expect:* the sitter and pet can interact safely before the trip.
8. **Confirm updates during travel.** Agree on photo or text frequency and what requires immediate contact. → *Expect:* you know when and how status updates will arrive.

## Decision points

- Pet needs injections, complex medication, or monitoring → use a vet technician, medical boarding, or experienced sitter.
- Pet has separation anxiety or aggression → disclose it and choose a caregiver equipped for that behavior.
- Smart lock or alarm access is unreliable → leave a physical backup key with a trusted nearby person.
- Sitter cancels → activate a backup sitter, boarding facility, or local trusted contact immediately.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sitter no-shows:** detect missed check-in or camera/activity evidence → contact backup caregiver or emergency contact to visit.
- **F2 Pet refuses food or medication:** detect sitter report of refusal → provide approved alternatives and call the vet if medically important.
- **F3 Lockout:** detect sitter cannot enter → use backup key holder, building manager, or locksmith according to your plan.
- **F4 Pet becomes ill:** detect vomiting, injury, breathing distress, or abnormal behavior → authorize vet care and keep records for reimbursement if insured.

## Verification

The sitter has confirmed dates, access, written care instructions, emergency authorization, vet contact, and update schedule before you depart.

## Variations

- `boarding`: confirm vaccine requirements, drop-off windows, bedding rules, and medication fees.
- `cats`: prioritize litter instructions, hiding spots, water, and indoor-only rules.
- `dogs`: specify leash, harness, reactivity, walk route, feeding timing, and weather limits.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because pet welfare and home access are involved. Use trusted caregivers, limit access permissions to the travel window, disclose behavior risks, and give explicit emergency-care authority and spending limits.
