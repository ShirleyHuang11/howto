## Goal

You carry medication through air travel legally and safely, with enough supply, correct labeling, and temperature protection.

## Preconditions

- Current medications, prescription labels, medical devices, and dosing schedule.
- Flight itinerary, destination country, and trip length.
- Prescriber or pharmacy contact if refills or letters are needed.

## Steps

1. **List every medication and device.** Include prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicine, controlled substances, syringes, inhalers, injectors, pumps, and refrigerated items. → *Expect:* you have a complete medication inventory for the trip.
2. **Check destination restrictions.** Review official destination rules for controlled substances, stimulants, opioids, cannabis products, injectables, and quantity limits. → *Expect:* no medication is being carried in violation of known entry rules.
3. **Keep medication in original labeled containers.** Use pharmacy labels showing the passenger name, drug name, dose, and prescriber when possible. → *Expect:* each prescription can be matched to the traveler.
4. **Request documentation if needed.** Ask the prescriber for a letter listing diagnosis if appropriate, generic drug names, dosage, and medical necessity for devices or controlled medication. → *Expect:* a signed letter or prescription copy is ready.
5. **Pack medication in carry-on.** Put essential medication, devices, and documentation in a personal item, not checked luggage. → *Expect:* medication remains with the traveler throughout the flight.
6. **Plan temperature control.** Use an insulated pouch, approved cold pack, and manufacturer storage guidance for refrigerated medication. → *Expect:* medication will stay within the required range during door-to-door travel.
7. **Prepare security screening.** Separate liquids, injectables, and medical devices if required and tell screening staff before inspection. → *Expect:* screeners can inspect medication without confiscating properly documented supplies.
8. **Set dose reminders across time zones.** Ask a clinician or pharmacist how to adjust timing for critical medications. → *Expect:* the traveler has a dosing plan for travel days and local time.

## Decision points

- Medication is a controlled substance → verify import rules through official government or embassy sources before travel.
- Medication requires refrigeration → confirm flight duration, hotel refrigerator access, and backup cooling.
- Trip may be extended → carry extra days of supply where legal and permitted by insurance.
- Screening staff question medication → calmly request supervisor review and show labels or documentation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Medication packed in checked bag:** detect it before bag drop → move essentials to carry-on immediately.
- **F2 Destination bans the drug:** detect official prohibition → ask prescriber about legal alternatives or obtain required permits before travel.
- **F3 Cold chain breaks:** detect thawed cold packs or excessive heat exposure → contact the pharmacy or manufacturer before using the medication.
- **F4 Medication lost abroad:** detect missing supply → contact travel insurer, prescriber, local pharmacy, or embassy for replacement guidance.

## Verification

All essential medication is in carry-on, labeled or documented, legal for the destination as checked against official rules, and sufficient for the trip plus a delay buffer.

## Variations

- `us-tsa`: medically necessary liquids and gels are allowed in reasonable quantities but must be declared for screening.
- `international`: drug legality can differ sharply by country, including common ADHD, pain, sleep, and anxiety medications.
- `refrigerated`: use manufacturer storage limits rather than guessing from general cooler performance.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because confiscation, missed doses, or temperature damage can affect health. Do not stop or adjust medication without clinician guidance, protect medical privacy, and confirm controlled-substance rules before departure.
