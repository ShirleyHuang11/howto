## Goal

You register your international trip with your government so you can receive security alerts and make it easier for consular staff to contact you in a crisis.

## Preconditions

- Citizenship country, passport information if requested, destination, travel dates, lodging address, and emergency contact.
- A reliable email address and phone number.
- Official government traveler-enrollment website for your country.

## Steps

1. **Find the official enrollment program.** Use your government's travel or foreign affairs website, such as the US State Department STEP site for US citizens. → *Expect:* you are on an official government domain, not a travel agency or paid intermediary.
2. **Create or access the account if required.** Sign in or register with your email and verify it if prompted. → *Expect:* the enrollment portal opens under your account.
3. **Enter traveler details.** Provide name, citizenship, contact information, and passport details only where the official portal requests them. → *Expect:* the traveler profile is complete enough to enroll a trip.
4. **Add the trip itinerary.** Enter destination country, arrival and departure dates, lodging or local contact, and cities or regions to be visited. → *Expect:* the trip appears as a draft or pending enrollment.
5. **Add emergency contact information.** Use someone not traveling with you when possible. → *Expect:* the portal has a reachable person to contact if needed.
6. **Review alert preferences.** Choose email or SMS alerts if available and confirm the destination country is included. → *Expect:* alert settings match how you can receive messages abroad.
7. **Submit the enrollment.** ⚠️ *Privacy confirmation:* confirm the trip dates and contact details before submitting to the government system. → *Expect:* the portal confirms the trip registration or enrollment.
8. **Save consular contact information.** Save the nearest embassy or consulate phone number, emergency line, and address offline. → *Expect:* consular contact details are available without internet.

## Decision points

- Visiting multiple countries → register each country or add every destination to the same trip if the portal supports it.
- Trip details change → update the enrollment rather than relying on the original itinerary.
- Dual citizen traveling to a country of other citizenship → understand that consular assistance may be limited there.
- No formal registration program → sign up for official travel advisories and save embassy contacts manually.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong website:** detect fees, ads, or non-government branding → leave the site and use the official foreign ministry or embassy website.
- **F2 Email verification fails:** detect no confirmation email → check spam, use another email, or register alerts without an account if available.
- **F3 Itinerary changes after submission:** detect different dates or lodging → log back in and update the trip.
- **F4 Alerts not received abroad:** detect no SMS or email access → rely on saved embassy contacts, local news, and travel companions.

## Verification

The official portal shows the trip as enrolled or registered, and embassy or consulate emergency contact details are saved offline.

## Variations

- `us`: use the Smart Traveler Enrollment Program, commonly called STEP, through the US State Department.
- `canada`: use Registration of Canadians Abroad.
- `uk`: the UK generally provides travel advice and consular contact information rather than a universal trip registration program.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because the enrollment contains identity and itinerary data. Use only official government sites, share accurate emergency contacts, and update or remove trip details when plans change if the system allows it.
