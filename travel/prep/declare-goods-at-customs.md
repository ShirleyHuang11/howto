## Goal

You accurately declare goods, cash, food, alcohol, tobacco, gifts, and purchases at the border so customs can assess duties and admissibility.

## Preconditions

- Receipts for purchases, gifts, repairs, and high-value items bought abroad.
- Knowledge of what you packed, including food, plants, animal products, medicines, and currency.
- Customs declaration form, kiosk, app, or officer interview process for the destination country.

## Steps

1. **Review declaration rules before arrival.** Check the destination customs authority's rules for duty-free allowance, prohibited items, food, agriculture, currency, and commercial goods. → *Expect:* you know what must be declared.
2. **Organize receipts and values.** Total purchases, gifts, alcohol, tobacco, repairs, and items bought duty-free. → *Expect:* you can state approximate values in the required currency.
3. **Separate risky items.** Keep food, seeds, plants, animal products, medicines, cash, and high-value goods easy to inspect. → *Expect:* declared items can be shown without unpacking everything.
4. **Complete the declaration honestly.** Use the paper form, kiosk, app, or oral declaration and answer every question truthfully. → *Expect:* the declaration lists all required categories, even if you think the item is allowed.
5. **Declare uncertain items.** If unsure, say so and show the item to the officer. → *Expect:* customs decides admissibility rather than treating it as concealment.
6. **Pay duties or surrender prohibited items if required.** ⚠️ *Legal confirmation:* confirm the assessment, receipt, and item disposition before paying or abandoning property. → *Expect:* duties are paid with receipt or prohibited goods are surrendered according to instructions.
7. **Keep customs paperwork.** Save stamped forms, tax receipts, seizure or abandonment records, and officer instructions. → *Expect:* you have proof of declaration and payment.
8. **Do not repack until cleared.** Keep declared items accessible until customs releases you. → *Expect:* you leave the customs area only after clearance.

## Decision points

- Carrying more cash than the reporting threshold → file the required currency report; reporting is usually not a tax by itself.
- Bringing food or agricultural items → declare them even if packaged or homemade.
- Goods are for resale or business → use commercial declaration procedures rather than personal exemption.
- Unsure whether something counts as a purchase → declare it and show receipts.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Receipt missing:** detect no proof of value → provide best estimate and supporting evidence such as card statements or product listings.
- **F2 Item prohibited:** detect officer says it cannot enter → surrender, export, or follow official treatment options; do not conceal it.
- **F3 Duty higher than expected:** detect assessment exceeds estimate → ask for itemized calculation and keep the receipt for review or refund process if available.
- **F4 Forgot to declare:** detect an omission before leaving customs → tell an officer immediately and correct the declaration.

## Verification

Customs has accepted your declaration, assessed or waived duties, and released you with receipts or records for any paid, surrendered, or inspected goods.

## Variations

- `us`: CBP requires declaration of many foods and agricultural products, and currency or monetary instruments over $10,000 must be reported.
- `eu`: allowances and VAT or duty rules vary depending on arrival from inside or outside the EU customs area.
- `canada`: personal exemptions depend on length of absence and item categories.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because false declarations can lead to fines, seizure, or immigration consequences. Be truthful, declare uncertain items, keep receipts, and do not carry goods for someone else unless you know exactly what they are.
