## Goal

You pack the under-seat personal item so essential documents, medicine, electronics, comfort items, and one-delay backup stay accessible throughout the trip.

## Preconditions

- Airline personal-item size limit and itinerary.
- Bag that fits under the seat for the strictest airline on the route.
- Critical items gathered before packing.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the size limit.** Check the airline's personal-item dimensions for your fare and aircraft where available. → *Expect:* the chosen bag fits the strictest published limit.
2. **Choose the right bag.** Use a soft-sided backpack, tote, or small shoulder bag that can compress under the seat. → *Expect:* the bag can close fully and fit without blocking your feet excessively.
3. **Pack identity and travel documents first.** Put passport, ID, boarding pass backup, visas, wallet, keys, and insurance cards in a secure easy-access pocket. → *Expect:* documents can be reached without unpacking the whole bag.
4. **Add health and safety essentials.** Pack medication, glasses, contacts, medical devices, masks if wanted, sanitizer, snacks, and empty water bottle. → *Expect:* essentials needed during delays remain with you.
5. **Pack electronics for screening and use.** Place phone, charger, power bank, laptop or tablet, headphones, and adapters where they are protected and accessible. → *Expect:* electronics can be removed or used without disturbing fragile items.
6. **Add a delay kit.** Include one change of underwear or shirt, basic toiletries within liquid rules, and any child or pet essentials for 24 hours. → *Expect:* a checked-bag delay would be manageable for one day.
7. **Balance weight and access.** Put heavy items low and frequently used items near the top or outside pocket. → *Expect:* the bag carries comfortably and does not spill when opened.
8. **Do a final no-check test.** Remove anything prohibited in cabin baggage and confirm lithium batteries and valuables are not in checked luggage. → *Expect:* the personal item is airport-ready and contains all irreplaceable items.

## Decision points

- Traveling on a basic-economy or low-cost fare → treat the personal item as your only guaranteed cabin bag.
- Checked bag contains clothing only → personal item should still hold medicine, documents, valuables, and a delay kit.
- Long flight → prioritize comfort items, chargers, and items needed without opening the overhead bin.
- Traveling with children → keep snacks, diapers, wipes, medication, and a change of clothes accessible.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bag too large at gate:** detect sizer failure → remove compressible items into pockets or pay to check a larger bag if required.
- **F2 Essential packed in checked bag:** detect missing medication, ID, or charger before bag drop → retrieve it immediately.
- **F3 Liquids rejected at security:** detect oversized toiletries → discard, transfer to checked bag before screening, or replace after security.
- **F4 Bag becomes too heavy:** detect shoulder or back strain → move nonessential items to carry-on roller if allowed or reduce duplicates.

## Verification

The personal item fits the airline limit, closes fully, and contains identity documents, medication, valuables, chargers, and a 24-hour delay kit.

## Variations

- `international`: include entry documents, lodging address, pen if forms are used, and offline copies of approvals.
- `work-trip`: keep laptop, charger, badge, presentation materials, and one meeting-ready shirt with you.
- `outdoor-trip`: keep prescription items and irreplaceable gear with you, but verify tools and liquids are cabin-legal.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but poor packing can expose identity documents or separate you from medication. Keep valuables and medical essentials on your body or under the seat, and do not leave the bag unattended.
