## Goal

You create secure backup copies of important travel documents so you can prove identity, recover bookings, and replace lost documents faster.

## Preconditions

- Original passport, visas, travel authorizations, driver's license, insurance card, booking confirmations, and emergency contacts.
- A scanner, phone camera, or scanning app.
- A secure cloud account or encrypted storage you can access while traveling.

## Steps

1. **Gather every critical document.** Include passport photo page, visas, residence permits, travel authorizations, flight and hotel confirmations, insurance, prescriptions, and emergency contacts. → *Expect:* all essential documents are in one place.
2. **Scan or photograph clearly.** Capture each document straight-on in good light with all corners visible. → *Expect:* names, numbers, dates, barcodes, and contact details are readable without zooming excessively.
3. **Name files consistently.** Use names like `passport-jordan-2029.pdf` or `travel-insurance-policy.pdf`. → *Expect:* each file can be identified without opening it.
4. **Save a secure digital set.** Store the files in an encrypted password manager, secure cloud folder, or device folder protected by a passcode. → *Expect:* you can open the copies from your phone after unlocking the device.
5. **Make one offline backup.** Download the files for offline access or save them inside a password manager that works without data. → *Expect:* airplane mode does not prevent you from viewing the documents.
6. **Print a minimal paper set.** Print passport photo page, visa or authorization, insurance, and emergency contacts; keep it separate from the originals. → *Expect:* a compact paper backup exists in a different bag.
7. **Share access with a trusted person.** Give a trusted contact either the files, a sealed envelope, or instructions to access them in an emergency. → *Expect:* someone not traveling can help if your phone and wallet are lost.
8. **Protect and remove unnecessary copies.** Delete scans from unsecured camera roll, printer memory where possible, and shared downloads. → *Expect:* copies remain only in intended secure locations.

## Decision points

- Traveling internationally → include passport, visa, entry authorization, onward-ticket proof, lodging address, and consular contact.
- Carrying medication → include prescription labels and a clinician letter when needed.
- Concerned about theft → keep paper copies separate from originals and avoid carrying full identity bundles together.
- Traveling with children → include custody or consent documents if required by the route or border authority.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Blurry scan:** detect unreadable numbers or barcodes → rescan in brighter light and verify before deleting the original image.
- **F2 Cloud account inaccessible abroad:** detect login blocked by 2FA or roaming problems → enable offline access and carry a paper emergency set.
- **F3 Paper copy lost:** detect missing backup papers → rely on secure digital copies and print replacements at lodging or a business center.
- **F4 Sensitive files overshared:** detect copies sent to the wrong person or stored in email → revoke links, delete messages where possible, and replace exposed document numbers if authorities advise it.

## Verification

With the phone in airplane mode, you can open readable digital copies, and a paper backup set is packed separately from the original documents.

## Variations

- `us`: include the nearest US embassy or consulate contact for international travel; lost US passports are replaced through consular services abroad.
- `eu`: national identity cards may be accepted for some regional travel, but keep passport copies if carrying a passport.
- `business-travel`: include invitation letters, conference registration, and employer emergency contacts.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because these copies expose identity and travel details. Store them in encrypted or access-controlled locations, avoid sending them over unsecured chat, and remove temporary scans from the camera roll and shared devices.
