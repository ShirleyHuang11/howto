## Goal

You get rebooked after a missed flight connection, protect your baggage and lodging plans, and document what the airline owes or offers.

## Preconditions

- Airline app, booking reference, ticket number if available, and identification.
- Current flight status, boarding passes, and connection details.
- Payment card for temporary expenses if needed.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the connection is actually missed.** Check gate screens, airline app, and staff updates before leaving the gate area. → *Expect:* you know whether the onward flight departed, closed, or is still reachable.
2. **Get into multiple support queues.** Use the airline app, phone line, chat, customer-service desk, and lounge desk if eligible. → *Expect:* you have at least one active path to an agent.
3. **Ask for protected rebooking.** State that you missed a connection on the same ticket and ask for the earliest reasonable rebooking to the final destination. → *Expect:* the agent searches alternative flights and partner options.
4. **Evaluate routing before accepting.** Check arrival time, airport changes, overnight stays, visa or transit requirements, and baggage implications. → *Expect:* the proposed itinerary is workable before you accept it.
5. **Confirm baggage handling.** Ask whether checked bags will be transferred, held, rerouted, or need to be collected. → *Expect:* you know where your bags are expected to go.
6. **Ask about meals, hotel, and compensation.** Policies depend on cause, airline, route, and local law; request written vouchers or instructions. → *Expect:* you receive benefits if eligible or a clear denial reason.
7. **Save documentation.** Screenshot delay notices, new itinerary, boarding passes, receipts, and agent names or chat transcripts. → *Expect:* you have evidence for reimbursement or insurance claims.
8. **Update downstream plans.** Notify lodging, transfer providers, hosts, tours, and travel companions of the new arrival. → *Expect:* critical reservations are adjusted or protected from no-show cancellation.

## Decision points

- Same-ticket connection missed due to airline delay → airline usually handles rebooking; ask for protected alternatives.
- Separate tickets → the second airline may treat it as a no-show; contact them immediately and expect fewer protections.
- Overnight delay → request hotel and meal support, then check travel insurance and card benefits.
- International reroute changes transit country → verify visa and entry requirements before accepting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 App shows no options:** detect no self-service rebooking → call, chat, stand in line, and search plausible flights to suggest.
- **F2 Agent offers worse routing:** detect avoidable long delay or airport change → ask about partner airlines, nearby airports, or waitlist options.
- **F3 Checked bag goes missing:** detect bag not at final destination → file a baggage report before leaving the airport.
- **F4 Hotel reservation at risk:** detect arrival after check-in cutoff → call the hotel and ask them to note late arrival in writing.

## Verification

You have a confirmed replacement itinerary to the destination, baggage instructions, and saved evidence of the disruption and any vouchers or reimbursement promises.

## Variations

- `eu-uk`: passenger-rights rules may provide compensation or care depending on cause, delay length, and route.
- `us`: airlines publish customer-service commitments; compensation for delays is more policy-based than automatic.
- `award-ticket`: rebooking may require the operating airline and loyalty program to coordinate, especially on partners.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because rebooking affects money, legal entry, and lodging. Do not accept a route that creates visa problems, keep receipts, and avoid sharing booking codes publicly because they can expose travel details.
