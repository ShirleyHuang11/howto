## Goal

Get usable local cash abroad at a fair rate while avoiding avoidable exchange fees, card traps, and counterfeit-risk situations.

## Preconditions

- You know the destination currency, a rough live exchange rate, and whether cards are widely accepted for your route.
- You have a debit card with a PIN, a backup card, and small emergency cash in a major currency if appropriate.
- Your bank travel controls, withdrawal limits, and foreign ATM fees are understood before leaving.

## Steps

1. **Estimate actual cash need.** List cash-only items such as taxis, markets, tips, rural buses, toilets, deposits, or visa-on-arrival fees. → *Expect:* you have a target amount instead of exchanging your whole budget.
2. **Find a bank ATM or reputable exchange office.** Prefer ATMs inside banks, airport arrival halls for a small starter amount, or licensed exchange counters with posted buy/sell rates. → *Expect:* the provider is identifiable, staffed or bank-branded, and not a street approach.
3. **Decline dynamic currency conversion.** When the ATM or card terminal asks to charge in your home currency, choose local currency. → *Expect:* your own bank handles conversion rather than the merchant's marked-up rate.
4. **Check the fee and limit screen.** Read ATM operator fees, withdrawal amount, and receipt option before confirming. → *Expect:* the total local-currency withdrawal and fixed fee are known.
5. **Count and split the cash discreetly.** Count near the machine or counter, inspect obvious note condition, then split cash between wallet and a hidden backup location. → *Expect:* the amount matches the receipt and no single loss takes all cash.
6. **Keep one receipt until reconciliation.** Save the ATM or exchange receipt until the card transaction posts and you leave the country. → *Expect:* you can dispute wrong amounts or prove legal exchange if questioned.

## Decision points

- Airport exchange counter vs ATM → use the airport only for a small first-day amount unless the rate is surprisingly competitive.
- ATM offers a poor fixed fee → larger, less frequent withdrawals can reduce per-withdrawal fees, but only if you can secure the cash.
- Cash-heavy country → bring cleaner, newer backup notes in a major currency because torn or marked bills may be refused.
- Leftover currency near departure → spend coins and small notes before security because many exchanges refuse coins.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Card retained:** the ATM keeps your card → enter the bank branch if open, call the card issuer, and switch to the backup card.
- **F2 Bad rate accepted:** the receipt shows a costly conversion → stop using that provider and treat the loss as capped by the small test withdrawal.
- **F3 Counterfeit concern:** a note looks wrong or is refused by merchants → take it to a bank for assessment and avoid street exchangers.
- **F4 Cash shortage:** cards fail where cash is needed → use a bank ATM, hotel desk referral, or licensed exchange counter before entering cash-only transport or markets.

## Verification

You have enough local cash for known cash-only needs, the withdrawal or exchange receipt matches the counted amount, and your card statement shows a local-currency transaction without unwanted home-currency conversion.

## Variations

- `japan`: cash is still useful for small restaurants, temples, and rural transit despite strong card acceptance in cities.
- `eu`: card acceptance is broad, but coins are useful for toilets, lockers, and small tips.
- High-inflation destinations: exchange smaller amounts more often and avoid holding excess local currency.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk due to cash theft, card skimming, and exchange fraud. Shield your PIN, avoid isolated ATMs at night, never hand your card to a stranger offering help, and split cash immediately.
