## Goal

Shift your body clock after eastbound travel so you can fall asleep and wake closer to local time within the first few days.

## Preconditions

- You know the origin and destination time zones, arrival time, first-night bedtime target, and next-morning obligations.
- You can access daylight, meals, hydration, and a quiet sleep environment.
- You avoid sleep medication or melatonin unless it is safe for you and used according to medical guidance.

## Steps

1. **Set the destination clock early.** Change phone and watch to destination time when boarding or after takeoff. → *Expect:* all sleep and meal decisions reference the arrival time zone.
2. **Sleep by destination night.** On the flight, try to sleep when it is night at the destination and use eye mask, earplugs, and reduced screens. → *Expect:* rest is aligned with the time zone you are entering.
3. **Get morning light after arrival.** If arriving before midday, spend 20 to 60 minutes outside without sunglasses if comfortable. → *Expect:* bright light signals local daytime to your body.
4. **Avoid long naps.** If you must nap, keep it short and before mid-afternoon local time. → *Expect:* tiredness is reduced without stealing pressure from local bedtime.
5. **Eat and move on local schedule.** Take meals at local times, walk outside, hydrate, and avoid heavy alcohol. → *Expect:* digestion and alertness begin matching the destination day.
6. **Protect the first local bedtime.** Dim lights, reduce screens, keep the room cool, and go to bed near the target time even if sleep is imperfect. → *Expect:* the first night anchors the new rhythm.
7. **Repeat morning light for two days.** Get outdoor light soon after waking and avoid bright light late at night. → *Expect:* wake time shifts earlier and daytime sleepiness fades.

## Decision points

- Arrival is late evening → skip sightseeing, eat lightly if needed, and go straight into the bedtime routine.
- Arrival is early morning after little sleep → use daylight and movement, then take only a short controlled nap.
- Critical meeting next morning → arrive a day earlier if possible and avoid alcohol on the flight.
- Melatonin considered → use only if appropriate for you and avoid combining with alcohol or sedatives.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Long nap:** you sleep for hours in the afternoon → wake, get light and movement, and keep the next bedtime reasonable rather than staying up all night.
- **F2 Awake at 3 a.m.:** you cannot fall back asleep → keep lights dim, avoid phone scrolling, and get morning light at the planned wake time.
- **F3 Evening second wind:** you feel alert near bedtime → dim the environment and avoid late caffeine or intense exercise.
- **F4 Digestive mismatch:** hunger arrives at origin times → eat small local-time meals until appetite shifts.

## Verification

Within two to three local mornings, you wake within about one hour of the target time, avoid long daytime naps, and can function through the local day.

## Variations

- Short trips: staying partly on home time may be better if obligations allow it.
- Business travel: schedule high-stakes work after the first local morning when possible.
- Children: use meals, light, and bedtime routines, but expect slower adjustment.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid driving or making major decisions while sleep-deprived, use sleep aids only with medical confidence, and keep hotel-room privacy controls set for uninterrupted sleep.
