## Goal

You set an asking price supported by recent comparable sales, current competition, property condition, and your selling timeline.

## Preconditions

- You know the home's size, condition, features, upgrades, defects, and location constraints.
- You have recent comparable sales and current active listings from an agent, appraiser, or reliable property database.
- You know your minimum net proceeds target.

## Steps

1. **Define the comparable property set.** Use recent nearby sales with similar size, age, condition, property type, school zone, and lot features. → *Expect:* a short list of relevant sold comparables.
2. **Adjust for meaningful differences.** Account for condition, renovations, bedrooms, baths, garage, view, lot, basement, HOA, and location drawbacks. → *Expect:* a supported value range rather than one guessed number.
3. **Compare active and pending competition.** Check homes buyers can choose today and what recently went under contract. → *Expect:* you know where your home sits against current alternatives.
4. **Calculate seller net proceeds.** Estimate mortgage payoff, agent compensation, taxes, transfer fees, credits, repairs, staging, and closing costs. → *Expect:* a net sheet for several price scenarios.
5. **Choose a pricing strategy.** [BRANCH: hot market, price near market to attract multiple offers | slower market, price competitively to avoid stale listing | unique home, allow more room for longer exposure] → *Expect:* the asking price matches the market and timeline.
6. **Set review triggers before listing.** Decide when to reduce price or adjust strategy based on showings, saves, feedback, and offer activity. → *Expect:* a written plan for the first 7 to 21 days.
7. **Approve the final list price in writing.** Confirm price, included items, and any seller credits or concessions you are willing to consider. → *Expect:* the listing can go live with a defensible price.

## Decision points

- Few relevant comparable sales → widen geography cautiously or consult an appraiser.
- High showing traffic but no offers → price may be close but terms, condition, or presentation need adjustment.
- Low traffic from launch → price or photos may be missing the buyer pool.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Anchoring to desired profit:** detect price based on what you need rather than market data → rebuild the price from comps and net sheets.
- **F2 Overpricing:** detect few showings and repeated negative price feedback → reduce quickly enough to regain attention.
- **F3 Ignoring condition:** detect buyer objections to repairs → adjust price, repair, or offer credits.
- **F4 Bad comparable set:** detect comps from unlike neighborhoods or property types → replace them with closer true substitutes.

## Verification

The list price is supported by comparable sales, current competition, a seller net sheet, and a written adjustment plan if market response is weak.

## Variations

- `us`: public sale-price availability varies by state, so agent MLS data may be more complete than consumer websites.
- Appraisal-sensitive market: pricing far above likely appraisal can create financing problems for buyers.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because pricing affects major financial outcomes and negotiation leverage. Do not rely only on automated estimates, and keep mortgage payoff and financial hardship details private from buyers.
