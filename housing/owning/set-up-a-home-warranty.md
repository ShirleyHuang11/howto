## Goal

You choose and activate a home warranty with realistic expectations about coverage, exclusions, service fees, waiting periods, and claim procedures.

## Preconditions

- Property address, closing date if recently purchased, and age or condition of major systems and appliances.
- Any seller-paid warranty offer or existing warranty contract.
- Budget for premium, service call fees, and uncovered repairs.

## Steps

1. **List what you want covered.** Include HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water heater, kitchen appliances, washer/dryer, pool, septic, well pump, or roof leak add-ons. → *Expect:* coverage needs are written before comparing plans.
2. **Read sample contracts, not just marketing pages.** Check exclusions, dollar caps, maintenance requirements, pre-existing condition clauses, and replacement rules. → *Expect:* you understand what the warranty will not pay for.
3. **Compare total cost.** Add annual premium, service call fee, add-ons, cancellation fees, and coverage limits. → *Expect:* each plan has an estimated best-case and worst-case cost.
4. **Check contractor and claims process.** Confirm whether the company chooses the technician, response times, emergency handling, and whether outside contractors need preapproval. → *Expect:* you know who controls repairs.
5. **Review waiting periods and start date.** Many plans do not cover failures immediately after purchase unless tied to a real-estate transaction. → *Expect:* coverage start date is clear.
6. **Purchase through a verified channel.** Use the company site, real-estate closing paperwork, or documented broker channel. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before paying, confirm term, property, covered items, service fee, and cancellation rules. → *Expect:* the contract is active or scheduled to activate.
7. **Save policy documents and account access.** Store the contract, receipt, claim phone number, portal login, and covered equipment list. → *Expect:* you can file a claim without searching later.
8. **Set maintenance reminders.** Keep filter changes, HVAC service, and appliance maintenance records. → *Expect:* you can show required maintenance if a claim is challenged.

## Decision points

- Major systems are old but still functioning → warranty may help with uncertainty, but caps and exclusions may limit value.
- You prefer choosing your own contractor → many warranties are a poor fit unless they allow preapproved reimbursement.
- Seller is paying → still read the contract because service fees and exclusions remain your responsibility.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Claim denied as pre-existing:** detect denial based on age or condition → provide inspection reports, maintenance records, and proof the item worked at coverage start.
- **F2 Low replacement cap:** detect payout far below actual replacement cost → ask for contract citation and decide whether to pay difference or cancel renewal.
- **F3 Contractor delay:** detect missed appointments or no parts → escalate through warranty support and document each contact.
- **F4 Duplicate coverage:** detect overlap with manufacturer warranty or insurance → use the coverage that applies best and avoid paying for unnecessary add-ons at renewal.

## Verification

You have the active contract, start date, service fee, coverage list, exclusions, claims contact, and maintenance records stored where you can retrieve them during a breakdown.

## Variations

- `real-estate-transaction`: seller-paid plans may start at closing and have different waiting periods.
- `non-us`: home warranty products and consumer protections vary; use local contract and insurance rules.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from contract cost, denied claims, and sharing property details. Do not treat a warranty as homeowners insurance, and confirm all terms before paying.
