## Goal

You negotiate sale terms that protect your net proceeds, timing, and legal position while keeping a viable buyer moving toward closing.

## Preconditions

- You have an offer, counteroffer, inspection request, appraisal issue, or closing issue to respond to.
- You know your minimum acceptable net proceeds and timing constraints.
- You can communicate through the contract-required method and deadline.

## Steps

1. **Identify the exact issue being negotiated.** Separate price, credits, repairs, closing date, possession, appraisal gap, contingencies, and personal property. → *Expect:* one clear negotiation agenda.
2. **Calculate the financial impact.** Convert repairs, credits, price changes, and timing costs into net proceeds. → *Expect:* you know the cost of each concession.
3. **Rank your priorities.** Decide what is must-have, tradeable, and unacceptable before responding. → *Expect:* you can counter without improvising under pressure.
4. **Use written counters and addenda.** Put every agreed change into the correct contract form or attorney-drafted language. → *Expect:* verbal understandings are converted to enforceable written terms.
5. **Trade terms deliberately.** [BRANCH: buyer wants repairs, offer credit instead if cleaner | buyer wants lower price, ask for stronger deposit or fewer contingencies | seller needs time, negotiate rent-back or later closing] → *Expect:* concessions buy something useful.
6. **Watch deadlines.** Track inspection response, appraisal objection, financing, title, and closing deadlines. → *Expect:* no right expires unnoticed.
7. **Sign only the final agreed terms.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* signed amendments can change price, credits, and obligations, so confirm the complete revised deal before signing. → *Expect:* the contract file reflects the negotiated agreement.

## Decision points

- Buyer asks for excessive inspection concessions → offer limited repairs, credit, or decline with evidence.
- Appraisal comes in low → negotiate price, appraisal gap cash, cancellation, or backup buyer strategy.
- Negotiation threatens closing deadline → decide whether an extension protects you or adds risk.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Verbal agreement dispute:** detect disagreement over what was promised → rely on written contract language and document future changes.
- **F2 Concession exceeds value:** detect giving price cut plus credit for same issue → recalculate net impact before signing.
- **F3 Deadline lapse:** detect missed response period → ask attorney or agent what rights remain and request written extension if needed.
- **F4 Emotional counter:** detect a response driven by offense rather than outcome → pause and compare against your priorities.

## Verification

All negotiated terms are in signed written documents, deadlines are tracked, and the revised deal meets your minimum net, timing, and risk requirements.

## Variations

- `us`: attorney review periods and standard contract addenda differ by state.
- Seller's market: stronger backup demand may support fewer concessions; buyer's market may require more flexibility.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because negotiation changes binding legal and financial terms. Keep financial distress private, confirm all terms in writing, and use an attorney for unclear legal language or possession arrangements.
