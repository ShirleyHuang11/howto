## Goal

You arrange the home so buyers can understand room purpose, scale, light, and flow without being distracted by clutter or personal items.

## Preconditions

- The home is clean and basic repairs are complete.
- You know whether the home will be occupied, vacant, or digitally staged.
- You have listing photos or showings scheduled far enough out to stage first.

## Steps

1. **Choose the buyer profile and room purpose.** Decide whether each room should read as bedroom, office, dining, playroom, or flex space. → *Expect:* every visible room has one clear function.
2. **Remove excess furniture and personal items.** Keep enough furniture to show scale and remove anything that blocks paths, windows, or focal points. → *Expect:* rooms feel open and easy to move through.
3. **Use neutral, clean surfaces.** Clear counters, minimize decor, use fresh linens, and remove strong style statements. → *Expect:* buyers notice the room, not the owner's belongings.
4. **Improve light and sightlines.** Open blinds, replace dim bulbs with consistent color temperature, clean windows, and angle furniture toward natural focal points. → *Expect:* photos and tours show bright, readable spaces.
5. **Stage key rooms first.** Prioritize living room, kitchen, primary bedroom, dining area, entry, and outdoor living space if present. → *Expect:* the rooms that drive buyer decisions look complete.
6. **Check photo framing.** Stand where listing photos will be taken and remove cords, bins, pet items, toiletries, and visual clutter. → *Expect:* camera views are clean from every main angle.
7. **Reset before each showing.** Make beds, empty trash, hide laundry, secure valuables, turn on lights, and set temperature comfortably. → *Expect:* the home presents consistently for each buyer.

## Decision points

- Home is vacant → consider renting furniture for main rooms or using accurate virtual staging disclosed as such.
- Room is small → use fewer pieces and expose floor area rather than adding decor.
- Strong personal style → edit toward neutral finishes and simple textures.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overstaging:** detect rooms that look like props instead of livable spaces → remove decor and keep functional furniture.
- **F2 Misleading virtual staging:** detect furniture or finishes that do not exist → label images accurately and keep unstaged photos available.
- **F3 Clutter returns during occupancy:** detect messy counters or laundry before showings → create a daily reset checklist.
- **F4 Poor traffic flow:** detect blocked doors or tight walkways → remove or reposition furniture.

## Verification

Each main room has a clear purpose, photo angles are free of clutter, and the home can be reset quickly before showings.

## Variations

- Luxury listing: professional staging may be worth the cost when buyer expectations are high.
- Budget staging: focus on cleaning, decluttering, light, linens, and furniture placement before buying decor.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but secure valuables, medications, mail, family photos, and identifying documents before showings or photography. Do not block exits, stairs, or electrical panels with staging.
