## Goal

You attend an HOA meeting prepared, understand what the board is deciding, and make useful owner comments without missing follow-up actions.

## Preconditions

- Meeting notice, agenda, date, time, location or video link, and owner sign-in requirements.
- Current governing documents if you plan to ask about rules or board authority.
- Any written materials, budget packet, violation issue, or architectural request relevant to your concern.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the meeting type.** Identify whether it is a board meeting, annual meeting, budget meeting, special meeting, or hearing. → *Expect:* you know what business can be conducted.
2. **Read the agenda before attending.** Mark items that affect assessments, rules, repairs, elections, contracts, or your property. → *Expect:* you can follow the meeting without improvising.
3. **Prepare one concise comment or question.** Tie it to the agenda, a document, or a specific request for action. → *Expect:* your point fits within the owner-comment limit.
4. **Sign in correctly.** Use the required owner name, unit or lot number, proxy, ballot, or video display name. → *Expect:* your attendance and voting eligibility are recognized.
5. **Listen for motions and decisions.** Note who made motions, votes, tabled items, deadlines, and assigned follow-up. → *Expect:* you can distinguish discussion from actual board action.
6. **Speak during the allowed comment period.** State your name, property, issue, evidence, and requested next step calmly. → *Expect:* the board or manager understands what response you are asking for.
7. **Respect executive-session boundaries.** Personnel, legal, collections, and owner-discipline matters may be closed. → *Expect:* you know which topics will not be discussed publicly.
8. **Request records or follow-up in writing.** After the meeting, email the manager or board with any document request or promised action. → *Expect:* your follow-up has a timestamp and clear ask.

## Decision points

- You want to vote → confirm quorum, ballot, proxy, and eligibility rules before the meeting.
- Your issue is a personal violation or fine → use the hearing or dispute process, not only open comment.
- Board makes a decision you question → ask for minutes, motion text, and governing authority before escalating.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong meeting expectations:** detect that owner comments are limited or no vote occurs → ask where the proper agenda or hearing process is handled.
- **F2 Missed sign-in:** detect your attendance or vote is not counted → contact management immediately with proof you attended.
- **F3 Comment runs too long:** detect the chair cuts you off → send the full written version after the meeting.
- **F4 No follow-up:** detect promised action not completed → reply with date, agenda item, and specific requested status.

## Verification

You attended or submitted a proxy correctly, recorded the key decisions and follow-up items, and sent any needed written request after the meeting.

## Variations

- `virtual-meeting`: test the link, display your owner name, and know how to request speaking time.
- `annual-meeting`: elections, quorum, proxies, and budget ratification may matter more than routine board reports.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but HOA meetings can involve private disputes and financial information. Keep notes factual, follow recording rules, and avoid sharing another owner's personal matter publicly.
