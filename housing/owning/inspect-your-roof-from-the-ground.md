## Goal

You identify visible roof problems from safe ground level and decide whether routine monitoring, minor maintenance, or a professional roof inspection is needed.

## Preconditions

- Daylight, dry weather, and a clear view of all roof slopes from your yard, sidewalk, or street.
- Binoculars or a phone camera with zoom.
- Recent storm history, roof age if known, and any attic or ceiling leak observations.

## Steps

1. **Walk the full perimeter from the ground.** Stand back far enough to see each roof plane without climbing. → *Expect:* you can view shingles, ridges, valleys, gutters, flashing areas, and penetrations.
2. **Scan for missing or lifted roof covering.** Look for bare patches, curled shingles, cracked tiles, displaced metal panels, or uneven edges. → *Expect:* any damaged areas are noted by roof side and approximate location.
3. **Check roof lines for sagging or dips.** Compare ridges and eaves against straight visual lines. → *Expect:* roof planes appear straight, or suspicious sagging is documented.
4. **Inspect valleys, flashing, and penetrations.** Use zoom to check around chimneys, skylights, vents, satellite mounts, and wall intersections. → *Expect:* flashing appears intact, with no gaps, rust streaks, or lifted edges.
5. **Look at gutters and downspouts.** Note sagging gutters, overflowing sections, plant growth, granules, or detached downspouts. → *Expect:* water-control issues are listed separately from roof-covering issues.
6. **Check the ground and attic clues.** Look for shingle pieces, nails, granules at downspouts, damp attic sheathing, or ceiling stains. → *Expect:* exterior and interior clues either agree or show where follow-up is needed.
7. **Photograph concerns with context.** Take a wide shot and a zoomed shot of each issue. → *Expect:* photos can show a roofer exactly where to look.
8. **Decide the next action.** [BRANCH: no issues, set a seasonal reminder | minor debris or gutter issue, schedule cleaning | missing covering, sagging, active leak, or damaged flashing, call a licensed roofer] → *Expect:* each finding has a next step and priority.

## Decision points

- Active leak or wet ceiling → treat as urgent and contact a roofer immediately.
- Damage appeared after hail or wind → photograph before cleanup and check your insurance reporting deadline.
- Roof cannot be seen safely from public or yard areas → use a professional inspection rather than climbing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Hidden leak missed:** detect stains or musty attic odor despite a normal exterior view → inspect attic during daylight and after rain, then call a roofer.
- **F2 Misidentified normal wear:** detect uncertainty about staining, algae, or granule loss → send photos to a qualified roofer for a paid opinion.
- **F3 Unsafe temptation to climb:** detect a need for closer viewing → stop; hire someone with fall protection and roof access equipment.
- **F4 Insurance evidence incomplete:** detect post-storm damage but no dated photos → take dated photos immediately and keep weather reports and contractor notes.

## Verification

You have dated photos of every roof side, a written list of any visible defects, and a clear decision to monitor, clean gutters, request repair, or schedule a professional inspection.

## Variations

- `tile-roof`: cracked or slipped tiles matter; avoid walking on tile because it breaks easily.
- `flat-or-low-slope`: ground inspection is less revealing; professional inspection of membrane seams and drains is more important.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if performed from the ground. Do not climb ladders, step onto the roof, or enter unsafe attic areas; avoid photographing neighbors' windows or private spaces.
