## Goal

You select a real estate agent whose experience, communication, local knowledge, and representation terms fit your purchase or sale.

## Preconditions

- You know whether you are buying, selling, or both.
- You have a target area, property type, and approximate timeline.
- You are ready to compare representation agreements before signing.

## Steps

1. **Define what you need from an agent.** Identify location, property type, price range, timeline, negotiation help, and special needs such as condos, relocation, or investment property. → *Expect:* a short selection checklist.
2. **Find candidates from several sources.** Use referrals, recent local transactions, brokerage sites, open houses, and licensing records. → *Expect:* three to five candidates to interview.
3. **Interview each candidate.** Ask about recent comparable clients, local market strategy, availability, offer or pricing approach, and who on their team handles daily work. → *Expect:* clear differences in expertise and service style.
4. **Check credentials and reputation.** Verify active license, disciplinary history if available, recent reviews, and transaction experience in your target market. → *Expect:* no unresolved licensing or trust concerns.
5. **Discuss compensation and representation.** Ask how the agent is paid, what you may owe, and whether they represent only you or could have dual-agency conflicts. → *Expect:* compensation and agency duties are explained in writing.
6. **Review the agreement before signing.** Check term length, cancellation rights, exclusivity, commission, admin fees, and buyer-broker or listing obligations. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* an exclusive agreement can limit who you may work with, so read the term and cancellation clause before signing. → *Expect:* you understand your obligations.
7. **Select the agent and set working norms.** Agree on communication channel, response expectations, document process, and decision deadlines. → *Expect:* a signed agreement only with the agent you intentionally selected.

## Decision points

- Agent pressures you to sign immediately → slow down and compare terms.
- Dual agency is proposed → decide whether reduced advocacy is acceptable or ask for separate representation.
- Selling a specialized property → favor proven local sale history over general popularity.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Poor communication:** detect slow replies before signing → choose another agent or set written expectations.
- **F2 Unclear fees:** detect vague commission or admin charges → require a written fee explanation before signing.
- **F3 Wrong market fit:** detect few recent transactions in your area or property type → interview agents with better local experience.
- **F4 Bad agreement terms:** detect long exclusivity with no cancellation path → negotiate the term or decline.

## Verification

You have a signed representation agreement, if any, with clearly understood compensation, term, cancellation rights, and communication expectations.

## Variations

- `us`: buyer-agent compensation and required agreements vary by state, brokerage, MLS practice, and current industry rules.
- For-sale-by-owner support: some agents offer limited-service or flat-fee arrangements instead of full representation.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because agent agreements can create legal and payment obligations. Do not sign unread forms, share sensitive financial details beyond what is needed, or accept conflicted representation without understanding the tradeoff.
