## Goal

You keep heating and cooling equipment running safely and efficiently through routine owner maintenance and timely professional service.

## Preconditions

- You know the type of system: furnace, boiler, heat pump, central air, ductless mini-split, or another system.
- You can access filters, thermostats, vents, and outdoor equipment safely.
- You have the model information or prior service records if available.

## Steps

1. **Identify the system and service interval.** Read the equipment label or manual and note fuel type, filter size, age, and recommended service schedule. → *Expect:* a system record with model, filter, and service needs.
2. **Replace or clean filters.** Turn the system off if required, install the correct filter direction, and set a reminder based on use and household conditions. → *Expect:* airflow is unobstructed and the filter date is recorded.
3. **Clear vents and returns.** Move furniture, rugs, and curtains away from supply and return vents. → *Expect:* air can circulate through occupied rooms.
4. **Inspect outdoor units safely.** Clear leaves, weeds, and debris around the condenser or heat pump without bending fins or opening electrical panels. → *Expect:* the unit has clear airflow around it.
5. **Test heating and cooling before peak season.** Run heat before winter and cooling before summer, listening for unusual noises and checking temperature response. → *Expect:* the system starts, runs steadily, and changes indoor temperature.
6. **Schedule professional maintenance.** Have a licensed technician inspect combustion safety, refrigerant-side issues, electrical components, condensate drainage, blower, coils, and controls as applicable. ⚠️ *Hazard:* gas, combustion, refrigerant, and high-voltage components can injure or kill; leave sealed and electrical service work to qualified technicians. → *Expect:* a service report with readings, issues, and recommendations.
7. **Track repairs and replacement planning.** Save invoices, monitor repeated failures, and estimate replacement timing for aging equipment. → *Expect:* you can distinguish routine service from a system nearing end of life.

## Decision points

- System uses gas or oil → prioritize combustion safety and carbon monoxide protection.
- Cooling performance is weak → check filter and outdoor airflow first, then call a technician.
- Repair quote is large on old equipment → compare repair cost, efficiency, refrigerant type, and replacement options.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No heat or cooling:** detect system not starting → check thermostat, breaker, switch, and filter, then call service if unresolved.
- **F2 Condensate leak:** detect water near indoor unit → shut down if leaking actively and clear or service the drain.
- **F3 Carbon monoxide alarm:** detect alarm activation or combustion concern → leave the home and call emergency services or the utility.
- **F4 Repeated filter clogging:** detect frequent airflow restriction → check filter rating, return leaks, pets, dust sources, and duct condition.

## Verification

Filters are current, vents and outdoor units are clear, the system operates in both relevant modes, and a professional service report is saved when maintenance is due.

## Variations

- Heat pump: outdoor unit must stay clear in both heating and cooling seasons, and auxiliary heat settings matter.
- Boiler: service focuses on pressure, expansion tank, pumps, valves, combustion, and radiators rather than air filters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from electricity, gas, combustion products, refrigerants, and water leaks. Do not bypass safety switches, open sealed refrigerant systems, or ignore carbon monoxide alarms; verify contractors before giving home access.
