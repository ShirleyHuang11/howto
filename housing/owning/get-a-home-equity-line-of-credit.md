## Goal

You evaluate, apply for, and close a HELOC only if the credit line's cost, variable-rate risk, and lien against your home fit the purpose.

## Preconditions

- Mortgage balance, estimated home value, property insurance, tax status, income, debts, and credit profile.
- A clear borrowing purpose, repayment plan, and maximum draw amount.
- Competing offers from banks, credit unions, or mortgage lenders.

## Steps

1. **Define the borrowing need and exit plan.** Write the amount, use, repayment source, and deadline for paying it down. → *Expect:* the HELOC is tied to a plan, not open-ended spending.
2. **Estimate available equity.** Subtract current mortgage and other liens from conservative home value, then apply lender combined-loan-to-value limits. → *Expect:* you know the likely maximum credit line.
3. **Compare HELOC terms.** Review margin, index, introductory rate, rate caps, draw period, repayment period, minimum payment, annual fees, closing costs, and early-closure fees. → *Expect:* offers are comparable beyond the teaser rate.
4. **Stress-test payment changes.** Calculate payment at today's rate and at a higher rate. → *Expect:* you know whether the payment remains affordable if rates rise.
5. **Apply through a trusted lender channel.** Provide income, asset, mortgage, insurance, and property information through a secure portal. → *Expect:* the lender confirms application receipt and required documents.
6. **Review appraisal or valuation results.** Confirm property value, credit limit, lien position, and any conditions. → *Expect:* approval terms match the equity estimate or differences are explained.
7. **Read closing documents carefully.** Check APR disclosures, variable-rate terms, draw rules, fees, prepayment or early termination terms, and lien language. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* signing creates a lien that can put your home at risk; confirm all terms and right-to-cancel timing before signing. → *Expect:* you understand the legal obligation before closing.
8. **Set draw and repayment controls.** Use the line only for the planned purpose, disable unnecessary checks/cards if possible, and schedule payments above interest-only minimums. → *Expect:* borrowing and repayment are controlled.

## Decision points

- Purpose is discretionary spending → consider saving first; home-secured debt is usually a poor fit.
- Rate is variable and budget is tight → consider a fixed-rate home equity loan or not borrowing.
- Loan-to-value is high → preserve equity for sale, emergency, or market decline risk.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Teaser-rate shock:** detect payment increase after intro period → refinance, convert to fixed segment if offered, or accelerate principal repayment.
- **F2 Overdrawing for unrelated expenses:** detect draws beyond plan → freeze access, remove checks/cards, and create payoff schedule.
- **F3 Closing cost surprise:** detect fees not in early quotes → compare final documents and pause if terms changed materially.
- **F4 Lien blocks future sale or refinance:** detect payoff or subordination issue → request payoff statement early and coordinate with future lender or closing agent.

## Verification

You have signed only after reviewing final HELOC terms, confirmed any rescission period, received the credit line details, and set a repayment plan that does not rely on minimum interest-only payments.

## Variations

- `us-primary-residence`: many HELOCs secured by a primary residence include a three-business-day right to cancel after signing.
- `fixed-rate-draw-option`: some HELOCs allow part of the balance to convert to fixed repayment; compare fees and rate before using it.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because a HELOC is home-secured debt and can lead to foreclosure if unpaid. Confirm every rate, fee, lien, and repayment term before signing or drawing funds.
