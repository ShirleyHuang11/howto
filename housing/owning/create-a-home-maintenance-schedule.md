## Goal

You build a recurring maintenance calendar that prevents avoidable damage, keeps systems efficient, and gives you a record of completed work.

## Preconditions

- You have access to the home, appliance manuals if available, and prior service records.
- You know the age or approximate condition of major systems.
- You have a calendar, task app, spreadsheet, or paper binder.

## Steps

1. **Inventory major systems.** List roof, gutters, HVAC, water heater, plumbing, electrical panel, appliances, sump pump, exterior drainage, smoke alarms, and filters. → *Expect:* a home system checklist.
2. **Add monthly tasks.** Include HVAC filter check, leak check under sinks, test GFCI outlets, inspect sump pump if present, and walk exterior drainage. → *Expect:* a short monthly routine.
3. **Add seasonal tasks.** Schedule gutter cleaning, HVAC service, hose bib winterizing, sprinkler startup or shutdown, exterior caulk checks, and yard drainage checks. → *Expect:* tasks appear in the right season.
4. **Add annual safety tasks.** Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, replace batteries as needed, review fire extinguishers, and clean dryer vent ducting. → *Expect:* safety tasks recur at least yearly.
5. **Schedule professional service.** Add HVAC tuneups, chimney cleaning if used, pest checks, septic pumping, roof inspection, or generator service as applicable. → *Expect:* outsourced tasks have target months and vendors.
6. **Create a repair reserve.** Estimate yearly maintenance savings, often based on home age, size, and system condition. → *Expect:* a budget line exists for routine and surprise work.
7. **Record completed work.** Save dates, receipts, photos, model numbers, warranties, and contractor notes. → *Expect:* a maintenance history that helps troubleshooting and resale.

## Decision points

- Older home or deferred maintenance → schedule more frequent inspections and a larger reserve.
- You cannot safely reach a component → hire a professional rather than skipping it.
- Repeated issue appears → replace the underlying part instead of repeatedly patching symptoms.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Calendar too ambitious:** detect many missed tasks → reduce to critical safety and water-damage prevention tasks first.
- **F2 No documentation:** detect forgotten service dates → start a simple log from today and attach future receipts.
- **F3 Ignored water signs:** detect stains, musty smell, or dampness → investigate promptly before mold or structural damage grows.
- **F4 Missed filter changes:** detect weak airflow or dust buildup → replace filters and set shorter reminders.

## Verification

Your calendar includes monthly, seasonal, annual, and professional tasks, with reminders and a place to store receipts and service records.

## Variations

- Cold climate: add freeze prevention, snow load, ice dam, and heating system checks.
- Hot humid climate: add dehumidification, pest, drainage, and air-conditioning tasks more often.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk overall, but falls, electricity, gas, combustion appliances, and ladders can be hazardous. Hire licensed professionals for unsafe work and keep contractor access limited to needed areas.
