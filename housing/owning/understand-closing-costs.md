## Goal

You know what cash is due at closing, why each charge exists, and which fees can still be questioned before signing.

## Preconditions

- You have a Loan Estimate, Closing Disclosure, settlement statement, or closing cost worksheet.
- You know the purchase price, loan amount, down payment, and target closing date.
- You can contact your lender, closing agent, escrow officer, or attorney.

## Steps

1. **Collect the current cost documents.** Use the latest Loan Estimate and, when available, the Closing Disclosure or settlement statement. → *Expect:* one current document set rather than old estimates.
2. **Separate down payment from closing costs.** Down payment reduces the loan balance; closing costs pay lender, title, escrow, government, prepaid, and insurance charges. → *Expect:* you can identify the total cash to close.
3. **Review lender charges.** Check origination, discount points, underwriting, processing, and rate-lock costs. → *Expect:* you know which costs came from the lender and whether points are optional.
4. **Review third-party and title charges.** Check appraisal, credit report, title search, title insurance, settlement, recording, transfer tax, survey, and attorney fees. → *Expect:* each third-party fee has a service provider and purpose.
5. **Review prepaids and escrow deposits.** Check homeowners insurance, prepaid interest, property taxes, mortgage insurance, and escrow reserve deposits. → *Expect:* you know which amounts are future expenses being collected early.
6. **Compare estimates to final numbers.** Look for large changes from the Loan Estimate to the Closing Disclosure and ask for explanations. → *Expect:* all material changes are explained or corrected.
7. **Confirm the wire or cashier's check instructions independently.** Call the closing office at a verified phone number before sending funds. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* wire transfers can be unrecoverable, so verify instructions using a trusted phone number, not an email thread. → *Expect:* you have verified cash-to-close instructions and a final amount.

## Decision points

- Cash to close is higher than expected → ask whether the change is a timing issue, escrow deposit, rate point, tax proration, or error.
- Discount points appear → decide whether buying down the rate is worth the upfront cost.
- Seller credits exceed allowed limits → ask the lender how excess credits will be handled.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wire fraud attempt:** detect new instructions by email or urgency language → stop and call the closing office using a verified number.
- **F2 Last-minute fee increase:** detect unexplained changes on the Closing Disclosure → ask the lender to document the reason and correct tolerance violations.
- **F3 Cash shortage:** detect that available funds are below cash to close → ask about lender credits, seller credits, gift documentation, or delayed closing.
- **F4 Misread escrow deposit:** detect confusion between monthly payment and reserves → ask for a line-by-line explanation of prepaid taxes and insurance.

## Verification

You can explain every major line item, the lender or closing agent has confirmed final cash to close, and wire or cashier's check instructions have been independently verified.

## Variations

- `us`: buyers with mortgages receive a Closing Disclosure before closing; local transfer taxes and title customs vary by state and county.
- No-loan purchase: there is no lender fee section, but title, escrow, taxes, insurance, and recording costs may still apply.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from large payments and wire fraud. Confirm all payment instructions by trusted phone call, protect loan documents containing personal data, and do not sign final documents until unexplained charges are resolved.
