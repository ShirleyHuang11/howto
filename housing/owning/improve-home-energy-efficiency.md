## Goal

You reduce energy waste in a home through correctly prioritized fixes, starting with low-risk measures and using contractors where safety, code, or building science requires it.

## Preconditions

- You have permission to alter the home and can access utility bills, thermostat settings, attic/basement areas, and mechanical equipment.
- You know whether the home uses gas, oil, propane, electric resistance, heat pump, or district heating.
- You have basic safety gear for simple work: gloves, eye protection, dust mask, flashlight, and stable ladder where needed.

## Steps

1. **Establish the baseline.** Record 12 months of energy use, rates, square footage, thermostat settings, and comfort problems. → *Expect:* a baseline you can compare against after changes.
2. **Fix controls and behavior first.** Program thermostats, turn off unused loads, set water-heater temperature appropriately, and use efficient schedules without creating unsafe indoor temperatures. → *Expect:* obvious waste is reduced without construction.
3. **Replace inefficient lighting and plug loads.** Install LED bulbs in high-use fixtures and retire old standby-heavy devices where practical. → *Expect:* lower electric use with no change in comfort.
4. **Seal easy air leaks.** Weatherstrip doors, seal obvious gaps around penetrations, and use outlet gaskets on drafty exterior walls. Avoid sealing around flues or combustion air openings. → *Expect:* fewer drafts at doors, windows, and penetrations.
5. **Service existing equipment.** Replace filters, clean vents, clear outdoor units, schedule tuneups, and verify ducts or radiators deliver heat/cooling to occupied rooms. → *Expect:* equipment operates cleanly and airflow or heat distribution improves.
6. **Use an audit for bigger envelope work.** Before major insulation or HVAC spending, get diagnostic guidance if you do not already have it. → *Expect:* upgrades are based on measured leaks, insulation gaps, and mechanical needs.
7. **Insulate and air seal in the correct order.** Seal attic bypasses before adding attic insulation; address moisture, knob-and-tube wiring, recessed light safety, and ventilation details first. ⚠️ *Hazard:* covering unsafe wiring, active leaks, or non-rated fixtures can create fire or mold risk; confirm conditions before insulating. → *Expect:* insulation is continuous, dry, and code-compatible.
8. **Upgrade water heating and appliances when replacement makes sense.** Compare lifecycle cost, rebates, fuel type, capacity, venting, and electrical-panel limits. → *Expect:* replacement choices fit the home rather than just the sticker efficiency.
9. **Plan HVAC replacement last.** After air sealing and insulation, ask for a load calculation and compare heat pump, high-efficiency furnace/boiler, ductless, or hybrid options. → *Expect:* new equipment is right-sized for the improved house.
10. **Track results.** Compare weather-adjusted bills, comfort, humidity, and maintenance notes for at least one full season. → *Expect:* documented savings or a clear reason to investigate further.

## Decision points

- You smell gas, see soot, or suspect backdrafting → stop and call the utility, emergency service, or a qualified technician before efficiency work.
- The home has moisture, mold, roof leaks, or pest damage → solve those defects before adding insulation.
- Payback is long but comfort or resilience matters → decide based on comfort, health, and future equipment needs, not bills alone.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sealing creates poor ventilation:** detect stale air, high humidity, condensation, or odors → consult an auditor or HVAC professional about ventilation.
- **F2 Contractor oversizes HVAC:** detect no load calculation and same-size replacement by habit → request Manual J or local equivalent sizing.
- **F3 Rebate denied:** detect missing preapproval, model number, invoice detail, or contractor certification → collect documents and appeal quickly.
- **F4 DIY insulation mistake:** detect blocked soffit vents, compressed batts, or gaps → remove and reinstall correctly or hire remediation.

## Verification

You have completed prioritized upgrades, retained invoices and rebate records, and can show reduced energy use or improved comfort compared with the baseline under similar weather.

## Variations

- `us`: ENERGY STAR, utility programs, state energy offices, and federal or state tax credits may require specific equipment ratings and documentation.
- Heat-pump climates: cold-climate models and backup strategy matter more than generic efficiency ratings.
- Historic homes: preserve vapor behavior, drainage, and original materials; consult specialists before aggressive air sealing.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from ladders, insulation fibers, wiring, combustion appliances, contractor contracts, and energy-use data. Confirm safety conditions before sealing or insulating, get permits where required, and read financing or rebate terms before signing.
