## Goal

You and your roommates agree on practical daily rules for shared living so expectations are visible, fair, and easy to revisit.

## Preconditions

- All current roommates can attend or respond.
- Any lease, HOA, building, or landlord rules that already control the household.
- A shared place to store the final rules, such as a document, group chat, or printed kitchen copy.

## Steps

1. **Start with non-negotiable rules.** Bring lease, HOA, safety, pet, parking, smoking, trash, and quiet-hour restrictions. → *Expect:* house rules do not contradict binding property rules.
2. **Ask each roommate for top friction points.** Limit the list to repeated issues such as dishes, noise, guests, temperature, bathrooms, or shared supplies. → *Expect:* the meeting focuses on real pain points.
3. **Set cleaning responsibilities.** Assign tasks, frequency, and standard of done for kitchen, bathroom, floors, trash, recycling, and shared clutter. → *Expect:* nobody has to guess who cleans what.
4. **Agree on noise and guest expectations.** Define quiet hours, overnight guest limits, party notice, shared-space use, and work/sleep needs. → *Expect:* private life and household comfort are balanced.
5. **Define food and shared supplies.** Decide what is communal, what is labeled, how replacement costs are split, and what cannot be used without asking. → *Expect:* fridge, pantry, and supply rules are clear.
6. **Set communication channels.** Choose what belongs in group chat, what requires an in-person conversation, and how much notice is needed for disruptions. → *Expect:* routine issues have a normal channel.
7. **Write rules as observable actions.** Use "wipe counters after cooking" instead of "be clean." → *Expect:* each rule can be followed and checked.
8. **Schedule a review.** Revisit after two to four weeks and then as needed. → *Expect:* the rules can improve without turning every issue into a conflict.

## Decision points

- Rule affects money, deposit, or move-out → put it in a signed roommate agreement, not only a casual list.
- One roommate has medical, accessibility, or work constraints → adapt rules so they are realistic and respectful.
- Someone refuses any shared expectations → decide whether the living arrangement is viable before renewing or adding them.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rules are too vague:** detect repeated arguments about interpretation → rewrite as specific actions, times, or limits.
- **F2 One person becomes manager:** detect all reminders coming from one roommate → rotate reminders or use a shared checklist.
- **F3 Group chat escalates conflict:** detect sarcasm or pile-ons → move sensitive issues to a scheduled conversation.
- **F4 Rules go stale:** detect ignored tasks after schedule changes → hold a review and revise assignments.

## Verification

The household has a written rules list covering cleaning, noise, guests, shared supplies, communication, and review timing, and every roommate has acknowledged it.

## Variations

- `family-roommates`: include childcare, elder care, and family privacy boundaries if relatives share the home.
- `short-term-sublet`: keep rules shorter and focus on access, cleaning, guests, and checkout condition.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, mostly interpersonal. Do not use house rules to override legal tenant rights, invade bedrooms or private devices, or shame roommates in shared channels.
