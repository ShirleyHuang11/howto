## Goal

You understand the homeowners association rules that apply to your property, including what you can change, what fees you owe, and how enforcement or approvals work.

## Preconditions

- Current HOA governing documents: declaration or CC&Rs, bylaws, rules and regulations, architectural guidelines, budget, fee schedule, and recent notices.
- Access to the HOA portal, management company, or board contact.
- Your property address, lot/unit number, and closing documents if you recently purchased.

## Steps

1. **Collect the current document set.** Download governing documents from the HOA portal or request them from management. → *Expect:* you have dated copies, not only seller-provided old versions.
2. **Identify the hierarchy of rules.** Read the declaration or CC&Rs first, then bylaws, rules, policies, and architectural standards. → *Expect:* you know which document controls if two rules conflict.
3. **Mark rules affecting daily life.** Note parking, pets, rentals, trash, noise, exterior storage, landscaping, and amenity use. → *Expect:* common violation areas are summarized for your household.
4. **Review architectural approval requirements.** Find what projects require approval before work, such as fences, paint, windows, solar, decks, landscaping, or cameras. → *Expect:* you know when to submit an application before spending money.
5. **Understand assessments and fines.** Record regular dues, special assessment authority, late fees, interest, collection process, and hearing rights. → *Expect:* payment obligations and enforcement timelines are clear.
6. **Check meeting and record-access rights.** Review notice requirements, owner comment periods, election rules, and how to inspect records. → *Expect:* you know how owners can participate or request information.
7. **Clarify ambiguous rules in writing.** Ask the manager or board for written interpretation before acting on a gray area. → *Expect:* any informal advice is documented by email or portal message.
8. **Create a household compliance checklist.** Put recurring tasks and restrictions into a short reference. → *Expect:* everyone living there knows the practical rules.

## Decision points

- Rule affects a planned improvement → pause the project and submit approval before ordering materials.
- You bought recently and documents differ → ask for the latest adopted version and board minutes showing changes.
- Rule may conflict with local law → consult a qualified local attorney or owner advocacy resource before relying on assumptions.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Outdated documents:** detect rules different from recent notices → request the current official set from the HOA or management company.
- **F2 Verbal permission only:** detect approval given casually by a board member → ask for written approval in the required format.
- **F3 Missed fine deadline:** detect a violation notice with response window → calendar the hearing or cure deadline immediately.
- **F4 Misread rental or pet restrictions:** detect a lease or animal arrangement that may violate rules → seek written clarification before committing.

## Verification

You have current HOA documents, a written summary of key restrictions and fees for your property, and a clear approval path for any planned exterior or restricted activity.

## Variations

- `condominium`: master deed, unit boundaries, limited common elements, and insurance responsibilities are especially important.
- `planned-community`: exterior changes, landscaping, fencing, and vehicle rules often dominate.
- `non-us`: terminology and owner rights differ; use the local equivalent governing documents and statutes.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because HOA rules can create fines, legal disputes, liens, and sale complications. Do not rely on verbal exceptions, and avoid sharing personal disputes in public owner forums when formal channels are required.
