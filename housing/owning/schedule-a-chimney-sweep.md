## Goal

You hire a qualified chimney professional to inspect and clean the flue before unsafe creosote, blockage, or appliance problems create a fire or carbon-monoxide risk.

## Preconditions

- A fireplace, wood stove, pellet stove, gas fireplace, or fuel-burning appliance vented through a chimney.
- Appliance type, fuel type, approximate last cleaning date, and any symptoms such as smoke, odor, or poor draft.
- Access to the fireplace, stove, basement cleanout, attic if needed, and roof exterior.

## Steps

1. **Define the service needed.** Request chimney inspection and sweeping for the exact appliance and fuel type. → *Expect:* the scheduler knows whether this is wood, gas, pellet, oil, or another venting system.
2. **Find qualified providers.** Look for local licensing where required, insurance, and recognized chimney credentials or strong documented experience. → *Expect:* you have two or three legitimate companies to compare.
3. **Ask what inspection level is included.** Routine annual service is usually a visual Level 1 inspection; changes, damage, or property sale may require a more detailed inspection. → *Expect:* the quote states what will be inspected and cleaned.
4. **Confirm access and preparation.** Ask whether furniture must be moved, ashes removed, pets secured, and roof access provided. → *Expect:* you know how to prepare the work area.
5. **Schedule before peak season if possible.** Book before cold weather or heavy fireplace use. → *Expect:* you receive a date, arrival window, price range, and cancellation policy.
6. **Prepare the area on service day.** Let ashes cool for at least several days, remove fragile items, and clear a path. ⚠️ *Hazard:* hot embers can remain hidden in ash; never vacuum or bag warm ash. → *Expect:* the technician can work cleanly and safely.
7. **Review findings before approving repairs.** Ask for photos of cracked liners, missing caps, water damage, or heavy creosote. → *Expect:* cleaning and any recommended repairs are documented separately.
8. **Save the report and set the next reminder.** Keep invoices, photos, and inspection notes. → *Expect:* you know when the next cleaning or repair follow-up is due.

## Decision points

- You smell smoke, see soot falling, or have a chimney fire event → stop using the appliance until inspected.
- Technician recommends major liner or masonry work → get photos and consider a second estimate.
- Gas fireplace only → cleaning may focus on venting, logs, glass, pilot assembly, and carbon-monoxide safety rather than creosote.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Unqualified cleaner:** detect vague credentials, no insurance, or pressure sales → cancel and hire a documented professional.
- **F2 Mess in living space:** detect poor containment → pause work and require drop cloths, vacuum containment, and cleanup.
- **F3 Hidden liner damage:** detect smoke issues after sweeping → request camera inspection and stop using the fireplace.
- **F4 Missed appointment in peak season:** detect long delays → call multiple providers and avoid using the appliance until serviced if symptoms exist.

## Verification

You have a completed sweep or inspection report, any safety defects are documented with photos, and the appliance is either cleared for use or marked out of service pending repair.

## Variations

- `wood-burning`: creosote buildup and flue blockage are primary concerns.
- `gas-fireplace`: carbon monoxide, vent integrity, glass seals, and burner condition are primary concerns.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from chimney fires, carbon monoxide, ladder work, and in-home contractor access. Confirm the technician's identity, keep valuables secure, and do not use an appliance that has been flagged unsafe.
