## Goal

You buy homeowners insurance that satisfies your lender and realistically protects the home, belongings, liability exposure, and required hazards.

## Preconditions

- You know the property address, closing date, lender mortgagee clause, and loan number if available.
- You have basic home details: year built, roof age, square footage, construction type, safety features, and claims history if known.
- You know whether flood, earthquake, windstorm, or wildfire coverage is a separate concern in the area.

## Steps

1. **Collect property and lender requirements.** Ask the lender for coverage minimums, deductible limits, effective date, and mortgagee clause. → *Expect:* a written insurance requirement list.
2. **Request several quotes.** Get quotes from insurers or an independent agent using the same dwelling limit, deductible, and endorsements. → *Expect:* comparable quotes rather than mismatched prices.
3. **Set dwelling coverage by rebuild cost.** Do not use purchase price alone; ask for replacement cost estimate and ordinance or law coverage if older systems may need code upgrades. → *Expect:* coverage reflects likely rebuild cost.
4. **Review exclusions and separate policies.** Check flood, earthquake, sewer backup, wind, hail, wildfire, mold, and vacancy limitations. → *Expect:* you know what the policy does not cover.
5. **Choose liability, personal property, and deductible levels.** Balance premium savings against cash you can actually pay after a loss. → *Expect:* selected limits fit your risk and budget.
6. **Bind coverage for the correct effective date.** Pay the required premium or authorize escrow billing, and send the declarations page to the lender. → *Expect:* the lender accepts proof of insurance before closing.
7. **Store policy documents and schedule a review.** Save the declarations page, full policy, claims contact, and renewal date. → *Expect:* you can find coverage details quickly after a loss.

## Decision points

- Property is in a flood zone or flood-prone area → price National Flood Insurance Program or private flood coverage before closing.
- Premium is unexpectedly high → ask about roof age, claims history, credit-based insurance score where legal, and deductible options.
- Lender rejects the policy → correct mortgagee clause, effective date, deductible, or coverage amount.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Underinsured dwelling:** detect coverage below rebuild estimate → increase dwelling limit or add replacement-cost endorsements.
- **F2 Missing flood coverage:** detect flood exclusion in a flood-prone area → buy separate flood coverage and note waiting periods.
- **F3 Wrong mortgagee clause:** detect lender rejection → copy the exact clause from the lender and reissue proof.
- **F4 Deductible too high:** detect a deductible you could not pay after a loss → lower the deductible before binding if affordable.

## Verification

You have a bound policy effective no later than closing, the lender has accepted the declarations page, and you understand major deductibles and exclusions.

## Variations

- `us`: flood insurance may be required by lenders in FEMA special flood hazard areas, but flood risk can exist outside mapped zones.
- Condo: the association master policy may cover the building; you still need an HO-6 or equivalent unit policy.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because gaps can create major financial loss. Share personal and property data only with legitimate insurers or licensed agents, and confirm coverage is bound before relying on it.
