## Goal

You understand what your mortgage escrow account pays, how the monthly amount is calculated, and how to respond to shortages or refunds.

## Preconditions

- You have a mortgage statement, escrow disclosure, Closing Disclosure, or annual escrow analysis.
- Your loan either requires escrow or lets you choose whether to waive it.
- You know your property tax and homeowners insurance billing cycles if available.

## Steps

1. **Identify what escrow covers.** Check whether the account pays property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, flood insurance, or other required items. → *Expect:* a list of bills paid by the servicer.
2. **Separate principal and interest from escrow.** Principal and interest repay the loan; escrow is a reserve for future bills. → *Expect:* your monthly payment components are clear.
3. **Review the initial deposit.** At closing, the lender may collect several months of taxes and insurance to seed the account. → *Expect:* you know why prepaid and reserve amounts were collected.
4. **Read the annual escrow analysis.** Check projected bills, required cushion, monthly escrow payment, shortage, surplus, or refund. → *Expect:* you can trace the new payment amount to projected bills.
5. **Verify bills were paid.** Check county tax records and insurer status after due dates. → *Expect:* taxes and insurance show paid or the servicer is investigating.
6. **Handle shortages or surpluses.** [BRANCH: shortage, pay lump sum or spread it over payments if offered | surplus, accept refund or reduced payment] → *Expect:* the servicer confirms the payment plan.
7. **Update the servicer after insurance or tax changes.** Send new declarations pages or tax bills when premiums, exemptions, or assessments change. → *Expect:* escrow projections can be recalculated.

## Decision points

- Escrow waiver is available → compare convenience against the discipline required to pay large tax and insurance bills yourself.
- Payment jumps after analysis → determine whether taxes, insurance, shortage repayment, or cushion caused the increase.
- Servicer misses a payment → escalate immediately and request penalties be covered if servicer error caused them.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Tax bill unpaid:** detect county records showing delinquent taxes → call the servicer, upload the bill, and request written resolution.
- **F2 Insurance lapse warning:** detect insurer notice of nonpayment → contact servicer and insurer the same day.
- **F3 Escrow shortage shock:** detect a large payment increase → ask for the analysis worksheet and shortage repayment options.
- **F4 Duplicate insurance billing:** detect you paid a bill the servicer also paid → ask insurer or servicer to refund or credit the duplicate.

## Verification

Your mortgage statement shows the escrow component, covered bills match your records, and taxes and insurance are current with no unexplained shortage.

## Variations

- `us`: escrow rules and cushion limits are regulated for many mortgages, but servicer practices and waiver availability vary.
- Non-escrow loan: you pay taxes and insurance directly and should maintain a separate savings reserve.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because missed tax or insurance payments can create liens, lapse coverage, or default. Store statements securely and verify payment status directly with the county and insurer when something looks wrong.
