## Goal

You challenge an incorrect property assessment or tax bill with evidence before the local deadline and preserve appeal rights if the assessor denies relief.

## Preconditions

- Current assessment notice or tax bill, parcel number, assessed value, exemptions, tax rate, and protest deadline.
- Recent sale price, appraisal, photos, repair estimates, comparable sales, or property-record errors.
- Local assessor or appraisal district instructions and required protest form.

## Steps

1. **Separate assessment from tax rate.** Identify whether you are challenging market value, classification, exemptions, acreage, improvements, or the tax calculation. → *Expect:* the protest targets a correct issue.
2. **Calendar the deadline.** Use the date on the notice and local rules; late protests are often rejected. → *Expect:* filing deadline and hearing window are saved.
3. **Check the property record.** Verify square footage, bedrooms, baths, lot size, condition, improvements, exemptions, and ownership. → *Expect:* factual errors are listed with evidence.
4. **Gather market evidence.** Use recent comparable sales, appraisal reports, closing statement, photos of defects, repair bids, or income data for rental property if allowed. → *Expect:* evidence supports a lower assessed value or corrected record.
5. **File the protest correctly.** Use the assessor portal, mail, or form; choose the protest reason and attach evidence if required. → *Expect:* you receive filing confirmation before the deadline.
6. **Prepare for informal review or hearing.** Organize a one-page value argument with comparable sales adjusted for size, condition, and location. → *Expect:* your requested value is clear and evidence-based.
7. **Attend review and respond to evidence.** Ask how the assessor calculated value and address differences factually. → *Expect:* the assessor or board understands your requested correction.
8. **Accept, appeal, or pay under protest as required.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* missing payment or appeal deadlines can create penalties or lose rights; confirm the written order, payment deadline, and next appeal step. → *Expect:* you know the final value and payment obligation.

## Decision points

- Exemption missing → file exemption correction separately if the protest form does not handle it.
- Assessment is high but tax bill rose because rates changed → attend budget/rate hearings; valuation protest may not solve it.
- Evidence is weak → consider whether the time, risk, or professional fee is worth the likely reduction.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Protest rejected as late:** detect portal refusal or notice → ask about good-cause or correction procedures, but expect limited options.
- **F2 Comparables not comparable:** detect assessor dismisses sales → replace with closer sales in time, location, size, and condition.
- **F3 Tax still due during appeal:** detect pending case but payment deadline remains → pay required amount to avoid penalties while appeal continues.
- **F4 Exemption omitted again:** detect next bill still missing it → file exemption documents and request corrected tax bill.

## Verification

You have filed before the deadline, received confirmation, presented evidence, and saved the written decision showing corrected value, exemptions, tax amount, and any appeal or payment deadlines.

## Variations

- `us-texas`: county appraisal districts use formal protest and appraisal review board processes with strict deadlines.
- `us-general`: deadlines, terminology, homestead exemptions, and appeal boards vary by state and county.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from tax penalties, liens, and financial disclosure. Use official assessor channels, redact sensitive financial details when not required, and confirm deadlines in writing.
