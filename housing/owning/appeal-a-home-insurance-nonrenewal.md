## Goal

You respond to a homeowners insurance nonrenewal notice before the deadline, correct fixable issues, preserve evidence, and either restore coverage or replace it without a lapse.

## Preconditions

- You have the written nonrenewal notice, policy number, effective nonrenewal date, and reason stated by the insurer.
- You can document the property's condition with dated photos, invoices, permits, inspections, and correspondence.
- You know your mortgage lender's insurance requirements if the home has a loan.

## Steps

1. **Read the notice immediately.** Identify the nonrenewal date, appeal deadline, reason, contact method, and whether the policy is being canceled midterm or simply not renewed. → *Expect:* a calendar deadline and exact stated reason.
2. **Check state rules and policy terms.** In the US, look up your state insurance department's homeowners nonrenewal rules and complaint process; requirements vary by state. → *Expect:* you know whether the notice period and reason appear compliant.
3. **Request the underwriting file or evidence.** Ask the insurer or agent for photos, inspection notes, claim history, aerial-imagery findings, or risk scoring used for the decision. → *Expect:* a specific defect list rather than a vague risk label.
4. **Fix correctable property issues.** Trim vegetation, repair roof defects, remove debris, update handrails, address peeling paint, clear brush, or correct pool/trampoline hazards as applicable. → *Expect:* dated before-and-after proof and invoices.
5. **Write the appeal package.** Include policy number, property address, nonrenewal reason, concise rebuttal, repair evidence, contractor invoices, permits, and a request for reinstatement or reinspection. → *Expect:* a complete packet that directly addresses each reason.
6. **Submit before the deadline and preserve proof.** Use the insurer's required channel and keep email confirmations, certified-mail receipts, upload screenshots, and names of representatives. → *Expect:* evidence the appeal was received on time.
7. **Ask for temporary underwriting review or reinspection.** Request written confirmation that the insurer will reconsider after repairs, especially if the problem was based on old photos or remote imagery. → *Expect:* a review date or written refusal.
8. **Shop replacement coverage in parallel.** Contact independent agents, admitted insurers, surplus-lines brokers if needed, and any state residual market or FAIR Plan equivalent. → *Expect:* at least one backup path before the nonrenewal date.
9. **Notify the mortgage servicer only with confirmed replacement details.** If coverage changes, provide the declarations page promptly to avoid force-placed insurance. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* allowing a lapse can trigger costly lender-placed coverage and leave the home uninsured; confirm the new policy is bound before the old one ends. → *Expect:* the lender has accepted proof of insurance.
10. **Get the outcome in writing.** Save reinstatement, renewed terms, denial, complaint number, or replacement declarations page. → *Expect:* written proof of continuous coverage or a documented appeal record.

## Decision points

- Notice appears legally defective → file a complaint with the state insurance department and still shop replacement coverage.
- The reason is wildfire, coastal, claims history, or market withdrawal → repairs may not solve it; prioritize backup coverage immediately.
- The insurer offers renewal only after expensive repairs → compare repair cost, coverage quality, deductible, and replacement alternatives.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed appeal deadline:** detect a denial based on lateness → file a regulator complaint if notice was unclear, but bind replacement coverage first.
- **F2 Remote imagery is wrong:** detect outdated roof, tree, or debris photos → submit dated current photos and request human underwriting review.
- **F3 No standard insurer will quote:** detect repeated declinations → ask an independent agent about residual-market, FAIR Plan, surplus-lines, or higher-deductible options.
- **F4 Mortgage force-placed insurance:** detect lender notice or escrow charge → send declarations page and request cancellation/refund of overlapping force-placed coverage.

## Verification

Before the original nonrenewal date, you have written reinstatement/renewal from the insurer or a bound replacement policy with declarations page accepted by the mortgage servicer.

## Variations

- `us`: appeal rights, notice periods, FAIR Plans, and complaint channels are state-specific; use the state insurance department as the authority.
- Condo or HOA property: coordinate roof, exterior, trees, and common-area issues with the association if the cited risk is not solely yours.
- Paid-off home: no lender will force coverage, but a lapse still leaves you exposed to total loss and liability.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a coverage lapse can create major financial loss and insurance documents contain personal and property data. Confirm policy binding in writing, do not rely on verbal promises, and redact sensitive information before sending documents to anyone other than insurer, agent, regulator, or lender.
