## Goal

You file a home warranty claim correctly, preserve evidence, manage the assigned contractor, and challenge denials or delays with contract-based documentation.

## Preconditions

- Active home warranty contract, account login, claim phone number or portal, and service fee amount.
- Covered item details: brand, model, age, symptoms, error codes, and maintenance records.
- Photos or video showing the failure and any urgent safety issue.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the item appears covered.** Read the contract section for that system or appliance, including exclusions and caps. → *Expect:* you know the clause you will rely on.
2. **Stop causing further damage.** Shut off water, power, or use of the failed item when safe. ⚠️ *Hazard:* for gas smell, electrical burning, sewage, or active flooding, use emergency services or qualified professionals first. → *Expect:* the situation is stabilized before the warranty process continues.
3. **Open the claim through the required channel.** Use the portal or claim phone number and describe symptoms factually, not guessed causes. → *Expect:* you receive a claim number and assigned next step.
4. **Pay only the required service fee.** Confirm amount and whether it is due online or to the technician. → *Expect:* the fee matches the contract.
5. **Coordinate with the assigned contractor.** Provide access, model numbers, and symptom notes; ask for the diagnosis in writing. → *Expect:* an appointment is scheduled and the contractor has enough information.
6. **Require authorization before outside work.** Do not hire your own repair person unless the warranty company gives written authorization. → *Expect:* reimbursement rights are preserved.
7. **Track all communication.** Record dates, names, claim numbers, photos, invoices, diagnosis, parts delays, and authorization decisions. → *Expect:* you have a claim timeline.
8. **Challenge denials with contract language.** If denied, ask for the specific exclusion and submit maintenance records or contrary evidence. → *Expect:* appeal or escalation is documented.

## Decision points

- Emergency threatens health or major property damage → mitigate immediately, then notify the warranty company as soon as possible.
- Contractor diagnosis seems wrong or incomplete → request a second opinion through the warranty company before paying for independent work.
- Repair cost exceeds cap → decide whether to accept cash-out, pay difference, or replace independently.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Denied for lack of maintenance:** detect request for records → provide filter changes, service invoices, photos, or inspection reports.
- **F2 Contractor no-shows:** detect missed appointment → report to warranty company and request reassignment.
- **F3 Unauthorized repair not reimbursed:** detect work done before approval → ask for exception, but expect limited recovery; get written authorization next time.
- **F4 Repeated failed repair:** detect same issue returns → reopen under workmanship guarantee or request escalation to replacement review.

## Verification

The claim number, diagnosis, authorization decision, completed repair or payout, service fee, and any remaining warranty guarantee are documented in your records.

## Variations

- `hvac-claim`: keep filter and annual service records; failures during extreme weather may have longer scheduling delays.
- `appliance-claim`: serial and model numbers are often required before parts authorization.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from repair delays, denied coverage, and home access. Do not let warranty procedures delay emergency shutoff or safety response; share access codes only when necessary and temporary.
