## Goal

You make the home cleaner, safer, easier to show, and less likely to trigger avoidable buyer objections before listing.

## Preconditions

- You have permission to sell the property and know your target listing window.
- You can access utility areas, storage areas, exterior spaces, and documents for major repairs.
- You have a realistic repair and cleaning budget.

## Steps

1. **Walk through as a buyer would.** Start at the curb and note smell, clutter, damage, lighting, safety issues, and deferred maintenance. → *Expect:* a prioritized punch list.
2. **Handle obvious maintenance problems.** Fix leaks, loose railings, missing covers, broken switches, sticking doors, and visible hazards. → *Expect:* common inspection and showing objections are reduced.
3. **Deep clean and deodorize.** Clean kitchens, baths, floors, windows, appliances, vents, pet areas, and trash spaces. → *Expect:* the home smells neutral and looks cared for.
4. **Declutter and depersonalize.** Remove excess furniture, personal photos, valuables, medications, documents, and crowded storage. → *Expect:* rooms and closets look spacious and safe for showings.
5. **Improve curb appeal.** Mow, trim, weed, repair exterior lights, clean entry areas, and remove debris. → *Expect:* the first view supports the asking price.
6. **Gather documents buyers may ask for.** Collect permits, warranties, appliance ages, utility averages, HOA documents if applicable, and repair receipts. → *Expect:* disclosure and buyer questions can be answered accurately.
7. **Decide what not to repair.** Avoid expensive remodels unless an agent or market data shows a likely return. → *Expect:* budget goes to work that improves saleability, not vanity projects.

## Decision points

- Major defect discovered → decide whether to repair, disclose, price accordingly, or get a pre-listing inspection.
- Occupied home with children or pets → set showing rules and secure hazards before listing.
- Tight timeline → prioritize cleaning, safety, curb appeal, and access over cosmetic upgrades.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Over-improvement:** detect spending on upgrades buyers may not value → stop and compare expected return with agent feedback.
- **F2 Hidden clutter:** detect packed closets or garage during photos → rent storage or box items offsite.
- **F3 Disclosure omission:** detect a known defect not documented → update disclosures before listing or accepting offers.
- **F4 Unsafe showing condition:** detect loose steps, exposed wiring, or unsecured valuables → repair or restrict access before showings.

## Verification

The home is clean, safe to tour, decluttered, documented for disclosures, and ready for professional photos or listing preparation.

## Variations

- `us`: seller disclosure forms and required property condition statements vary by state.
- Tenant-occupied home: coordinate notice, access, and photography according to the lease and local law.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because disclosures and defects can affect legal liability. Do not hide known problems, leave personal documents visible, or let visitors access unsafe areas without warning.
