## Goal

You create a written roommate agreement that clarifies money, chores, guests, shared spaces, conflict handling, and move-out expectations before disputes arise.

## Preconditions

- Lease, deed, HOA rules, house rules, or landlord permission terms that apply to occupants.
- Names and contact details of all roommates.
- Shared understanding of rent, utilities, deposits, bedrooms, parking, pets, and move-in date.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the legal baseline.** Read the lease, HOA rules, or owner requirements before promising anything. → *Expect:* the agreement does not conflict with superior housing documents.
2. **List all payments.** Include rent, mortgage contribution if applicable, utilities, internet, subscriptions, deposits, furniture, supplies, and due dates. → *Expect:* each cost has an amount or formula and payer.
3. **Assign rooms and shared areas.** Define bedrooms, bathrooms, storage, parking, kitchen space, and any off-limits areas. → *Expect:* space expectations are concrete.
4. **Set chores and maintenance duties.** Cover trash, dishes, cleaning, yard work, snow, filters, pest prevention, and reporting repairs. → *Expect:* recurring work has owners and frequency.
5. **Define guests, quiet hours, pets, smoking, and parties.** Use specific limits instead of vague "be respectful" language. → *Expect:* common lifestyle conflicts have clear rules.
6. **Create conflict and change procedures.** Include how to raise issues, how decisions are made, and how amendments must be approved. → *Expect:* disagreements have a process.
7. **Write move-out terms.** Cover notice period, replacement roommate approval, deposit return, unpaid bills, keys, cleaning, and abandoned property. → *Expect:* exit obligations are known before someone leaves.
8. **Review and sign together.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before signing, confirm all roommates understand payment duties and that the agreement does not override tenant-law or lease rights. → *Expect:* every roommate has the same dated copy.

## Decision points

- One roommate is not on the lease → clarify occupant status and landlord permission before move-in.
- Owner lives with roommate → local lodger or tenant rules may differ; get local advice for eviction or notice terms.
- A term affects legal rights or large money → consider a local attorney-reviewed template.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague payment language:** detect "split utilities" without dates or accounts → amend with exact percentages, due dates, and payment method.
- **F2 Lease conflict:** detect agreement allows pets or sublets the lease bans → follow the lease and revise the roommate agreement.
- **F3 Chores ignored:** detect repeated missed tasks → use the conflict process and convert chores to a rotating schedule or paid cleaner split.
- **F4 Move-out dispute:** detect unpaid bills or unclear deposit deductions → use photos, invoices, and the signed move-out section to settle.

## Verification

All roommates have signed and dated the agreement, the terms match the lease or property rules, and payment, guest, chore, conflict, and move-out sections are complete.

## Variations

- `college-housing`: campus housing contracts may prohibit private roommate terms that conflict with residence-life rules.
- `owner-occupied`: include access, maintenance, and notice expectations carefully because the owner has separate property obligations.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from money disputes, housing instability, and personal-data sharing. Do not include illegal waivers, and store copies where roommates can access them without exposing bank details unnecessarily.
