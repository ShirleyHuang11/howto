## Goal

You complete the lender-required appraisal, understand the appraised value, and respond correctly if the value affects financing or negotiations.

## Preconditions

- You are under contract to buy or refinance a home.
- Your lender has ordered or is ready to order the appraisal.
- The property can be accessed by the appraiser.

## Steps

1. **Ask the lender when the appraisal will be ordered.** Confirm the fee, payment method, and estimated turnaround. → *Expect:* the appraisal is ordered through the lender's approved appraisal process.
2. **Coordinate property access.** Make sure the seller, listing agent, tenant, or property manager knows the appointment window. → *Expect:* the appraiser can enter and observe required areas.
3. **Provide factual property information when allowed.** Send permits, upgrades, square footage documentation, and recent comparable sales through the lender or agent without pressuring the appraiser. → *Expect:* relevant facts are available for the valuation file.
4. **Wait for lender review.** The appraiser sends the report to the lender, and the lender reviews it before releasing a copy to you. → *Expect:* the lender confirms whether value supports the loan.
5. **Read the appraisal report.** Check appraised value, condition notes, comparable sales, required repairs, and whether the appraisal is "as-is" or "subject to" work. → *Expect:* you know whether the property meets loan requirements.
6. **Respond to a low value or repair condition.** [BRANCH: low appraisal, renegotiate price, add cash, dispute with evidence, or cancel if protected | repair condition, require completion and reinspection] → *Expect:* your loan file has a documented path forward.
7. **Confirm financing status after appraisal.** Ask whether underwriting needs anything else related to value or condition. → *Expect:* the lender confirms the appraisal condition is satisfied or identifies the remaining issue.

## Decision points

- Appraisal meets or exceeds price → continue underwriting.
- Appraisal is below price → decide who covers the gap before removing financing or appraisal protections.
- Appraisal requires repairs → decide whether repairs must happen before closing or the loan product can allow an escrow holdback.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Access failure:** detect a missed appointment or locked property → reschedule quickly and confirm access in writing.
- **F2 Low appraisal:** detect value below contract price → send a reconsideration package with factual comparable sales or negotiate a price change.
- **F3 Incorrect property facts:** detect wrong bedroom count, square footage, or condition notes → ask the lender for a formal correction or reconsideration of value.
- **F4 Repair condition delays closing:** detect "subject to" repairs → assign responsibility, complete work, and schedule final inspection.

## Verification

The lender has accepted the appraisal, any required repairs or value disputes are resolved, and your loan file shows the appraisal condition cleared.

## Variations

- `us`: federal lending rules generally keep lenders from letting borrowers choose or influence the appraiser directly.
- Cash purchase: an appraisal is optional unless you want an independent value check.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because value affects financing, contract rights, and cash needed to close. Do not misrepresent property facts, pressure the appraiser, or remove appraisal protections before understanding the financing impact.
