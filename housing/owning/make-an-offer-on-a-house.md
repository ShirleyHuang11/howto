## Goal

You submit a competitive, informed purchase offer on a home while understanding the price, contingencies, deadlines, and legal commitments.

## Preconditions

- A property you have toured or evaluated enough to consider buying.
- Mortgage preapproval or proof of funds.
- A real estate agent or attorney if customary or required in your market.
- Funds available for earnest money and inspections if the offer is accepted.

## Steps

1. **Review comparable sales and market conditions.** Look at recent similar sales, active competition, days on market, and seller disclosures. → *Expect:* your offer price is grounded in evidence.
2. **Set your maximum walk-away number.** Include purchase price, estimated payment, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, repairs, and closing costs. → *Expect:* you know the highest total commitment you can accept.
3. **Choose contingencies carefully.** Decide on inspection, financing, appraisal, sale-of-current-home, HOA, title, and insurance contingencies with your agent or attorney. → *Expect:* the offer protects the issues that matter most.
4. **Define earnest money and deadlines.** Set deposit amount, delivery deadline, inspection period, financing deadline, closing date, and possession terms. → *Expect:* every major obligation has a date and amount.
5. **Review disclosures and known defects.** Read seller disclosures, HOA documents available so far, flood or hazard information, and visible repair concerns. → *Expect:* known risks are reflected in price or contingencies.
6. **Write the offer contract.** Have your agent or attorney prepare the jurisdiction-appropriate purchase agreement and addenda. → *Expect:* the written offer matches your intended terms.
7. **Read before signing.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* a signed and accepted offer can become a binding purchase contract; confirm price, contingencies, deadlines, included items, and default consequences before signing. → *Expect:* you understand the commitment you are making.
8. **Submit the offer and track response time.** Send the offer through the proper channel and note the expiration deadline. → *Expect:* the seller or listing agent has received it.
9. **Respond to acceptance, rejection, or counteroffer.** [BRANCH: accepted, follow contract deadlines immediately | countered, compare changes against your walk-away limits | rejected, keep records and continue searching] → *Expect:* your next action matches the seller's response.

## Decision points

- Multiple-offer situation → decide whether to improve price, terms, or certainty without waiving protections you cannot tolerate.
- Inspection contingency waiver requested → consider preinspection, repair budget, and worst-case defects before agreeing.
- Appraisal gap likely → decide whether you can bring extra cash if lender value is lower than price.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Offer exceeds sustainable budget:** detect payment or cash needed above your limit → lower the offer or walk away.
- **F2 Missed contingency deadline:** detect inspection or financing deadline approaching → request extension in writing or decide before rights expire.
- **F3 Ambiguous included items:** detect uncertainty about appliances, fixtures, or personal property → specify them in the contract.
- **F4 Wire fraud attempt:** detect changed wiring instructions by email → call a verified phone number for escrow or closing office before sending funds.

## Verification

The offer package has been submitted with the correct price, contingencies, deadlines, signatures, proof of funds or preapproval, and documented receipt by the seller's side.

## Variations

- `us`: customs vary by state; some markets use agents, some require attorneys, and earnest money is usually held by escrow, title, broker, or attorney.
- Cash offer: replace financing terms with proof of funds and confirm appraisal or inspection choices.
- Condo or HOA property: include time to review association documents, budget, reserves, rules, and pending assessments.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because a purchase offer can become a binding legal and financial obligation. Read all documents before signing, verify wiring instructions by trusted phone call, and consult licensed local professionals for legal, tax, or financing advice.
