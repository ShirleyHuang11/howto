## Goal

You clean and reseal a wood deck so water beads on the surface, the finish is even, and the wood is protected from weathering.

## Preconditions

- A structurally sound wood deck with no loose railings, rotten boards, or protruding fasteners.
- Dry forecast in the product's temperature range, usually with no rain for 24-48 hours.
- Deck cleaner, brush or low-pressure washer, sealer or stain, applicators, gloves, eye protection, and drop cloths.

## Steps

1. **Test whether resealing is needed.** Sprinkle water on several boards; if it soaks in within a few minutes, resealing is due. → *Expect:* the deck fails the bead test in enough areas to justify the work.
2. **Repair obvious hazards first.** Tighten fasteners, replace rotten boards, and secure rails before coating. ⚠️ *Hazard:* do not hide structural decay under stain; confirm the walking surface and rails are sound. → *Expect:* the deck is safe to walk and ready for surface work.
3. **Clear and protect the area.** Remove furniture, cover nearby siding and plants, and sweep debris from gaps. → *Expect:* the deck is open and surrounding surfaces are protected.
4. **Clean the wood thoroughly.** Apply deck cleaner as directed, scrub traffic areas, and rinse without gouging the wood. → *Expect:* dirt, mildew, gray oxidation, and old loose finish are removed.
5. **Let the deck dry fully.** Follow the coating label; many products require 24-48 hours of dry wood. → *Expect:* the wood is dry to the touch and within the product's moisture requirement if measured.
6. **Stir and test the finish.** Stir, do not shake, and apply a small test patch in an inconspicuous area. → *Expect:* color and absorption look acceptable after drying.
7. **Apply thin, even coats.** Work with the grain, avoid puddles, and back-brush lap marks. → *Expect:* the coating penetrates evenly without glossy sticky spots unless the product is film-forming.
8. **Respect cure time.** Keep people, pets, furniture, and rain off the deck until the label says it is ready. → *Expect:* the finish dries evenly and does not track or peel from early use.

## Decision points

- Existing coating is peeling or opaque → strip or sand according to product guidance before resealing.
- Boards are damp, shaded, or recently pressure-washed → wait longer; sealing wet wood traps moisture.
- Deck is composite, PVC, or painted → use the manufacturer's cleaning procedure instead of wood sealer.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sticky finish:** detect tacky boards after cure time → remove excess with mineral spirits if compatible or follow manufacturer cleanup guidance.
- **F2 Lap marks:** detect dark overlap stripes → work smaller wet edges next time; lightly clean and recoat only if the product allows.
- **F3 Peeling after sealing:** detect film lifting → surface was dirty, wet, or incompatible; strip failed areas and restart with compatible product.
- **F4 Plant or siding damage:** detect discoloration from cleaner or stain → rinse immediately and use better masking before continuing.

## Verification

After curing, water beads on multiple boards, the color is even, walking surfaces are not sticky, and rails, stairs, and nearby siding are free of coating spills.

## Variations

- `transparent-sealer`: shows wood grain but needs more frequent renewal.
- `semi-transparent-stain`: adds UV protection and color while still showing grain.
- `humid-climate`: allow extra drying time after washing and before rain exposure.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from slippery surfaces, cleaners, solvents, and fall hazards on stairs. Wear eye and skin protection, ventilate solvent products, and confirm structural repairs before coating.
