## Goal

You obtain a mortgage preapproval that reflects your real finances and helps you shop for homes within a sustainable budget.

## Preconditions

- Stable income, savings, and a basic target purchase area or price range.
- Recent pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, bank statements, debt information, and ID.
- Permission to let lenders review credit when you are ready.

## Steps

1. **Estimate your own budget first.** Review income, debts, down payment, closing costs, emergency savings, taxes, insurance, and HOA dues. → *Expect:* you know the payment you can tolerate, not only the loan maximum.
2. **Check credit reports and scores.** Review reports for errors and avoid opening new credit before applying. → *Expect:* major credit issues are known before lender review.
3. **Compare several lenders.** Contact banks, credit unions, mortgage brokers, or online lenders and ask about loan types, fees, rates, and service. → *Expect:* you have more than one option.
4. **Submit complete financial documents.** Provide income, asset, employment, debt, and identity documents through secure lender channels. → *Expect:* the lender can underwrite a real preapproval.
5. **Authorize the credit pull deliberately.** Confirm whether the lender will do a hard inquiry and when rate-shopping windows apply in your credit scoring context. → *Expect:* credit review happens intentionally.
6. **Review the preapproval terms.** Check loan amount, estimated rate, loan type, down payment, expiration date, assumptions, and conditions. → *Expect:* you understand what the letter does and does not guarantee.
7. **Ask what could change approval.** Discuss job changes, new debt, large deposits, credit changes, property type, appraisal, and condo or HOA issues. → *Expect:* you know what actions to avoid while shopping.
8. **Choose the letter amount strategically.** Ask for a letter matching your offer strategy rather than automatically showing your maximum approval. → *Expect:* your agent has a usable letter for offers.

## Decision points

- Debt-to-income ratio is high → reduce debt, lower target price, or ask about eligible loan programs.
- Down payment funds include gifts → ask the lender for gift-letter and sourcing requirements before moving money.
- Self-employed income is complex → prepare tax returns, profit-and-loss statements, and business bank records early.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Prequalification mistaken for preapproval:** detect no document review or credit pull → ask for a fully documented preapproval.
- **F2 Budget exceeds comfort:** detect approval far above your desired payment → shop by your own payment ceiling.
- **F3 Large unexplained deposits:** detect lender questions about funds → document source before transferring or spending.
- **F4 Rate assumptions stale:** detect expired letter or changed rates → refresh preapproval before making offers.

## Verification

You have a written preapproval letter with lender name, borrower name, loan type or assumptions, approved amount, expiration date, and known conditions, and the payment fits your budget.

## Variations

- `us`: preapproval commonly involves credit review, income and asset verification, and later underwriting after a property is under contract.
- Non-US: mortgage-in-principle, agreement-in-principle, or broker processes vary by country.
- New construction or condos: lenders may require additional property, project, or HOA review.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because credit, identity, bank, tax, and employment documents are involved. Use secure upload portals, verify lender identity, and do not wire or transfer money based only on email instructions.
