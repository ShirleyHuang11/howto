## Goal

You clean, patch, and seal an asphalt driveway so minor cracks are protected and the surface cures without tracking into the house or street.

## Preconditions

- Asphalt driveway in sound condition; concrete driveways use different products.
- Dry weather, moderate temperatures, and no expected rain during the product's cure window.
- Cleaner, crack filler, patch material if needed, squeegee or brush, sealer, gloves, eye protection, and barricade tape.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the surface is asphalt and worth sealing.** Look for flexible black pavement, not concrete slabs; large potholes or alligator cracking need repair first. → *Expect:* the driveway is a good candidate for maintenance sealing.
2. **Check the product label and forecast.** Verify temperature, rain-free time, and curing requirements. → *Expect:* conditions match the sealer's instructions.
3. **Clean the driveway.** Sweep, remove weeds, scrub oil spots with degreaser, and rinse thoroughly. → *Expect:* the surface is free of loose dirt, vegetation, and oily residue.
4. **Let the pavement dry.** Wait until cracks and low spots are dry unless the product specifically allows damp application. → *Expect:* no standing water or dark wet patches remain.
5. **Fill cracks and patch holes.** Use crack filler for narrow cracks and asphalt patch for holes; compact and cure as directed. → *Expect:* defects are filled flush enough that sealer will not pool deeply.
6. **Mix and stage the sealer.** Turn pails upside down briefly if allowed, then stir to uniform consistency. → *Expect:* the sealer has even color and texture.
7. **Apply from the highest end downward.** Pour small ribbons and spread thinly with a squeegee or brush, keeping a wet edge. → *Expect:* coverage is even without thick ridges or puddles.
8. **Block traffic until fully cured.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* wet sealer is slippery and can track onto roads, shoes, and floors; barricade the driveway clearly. → *Expect:* people, vehicles, and deliveries stay off until the label's cure time passes.
9. **Inspect after cure.** Touch a hidden edge and walk lightly before driving on it. → *Expect:* the surface is dry, firm, and does not mark shoes or tires.

## Decision points

- Concrete driveway → use concrete sealer procedures, not asphalt driveway sealer.
- New asphalt less than the product's waiting period → delay sealing so oils can cure out.
- Deep base failure, drainage problems, or repeated potholes → get pavement repair estimates before coating.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sealer washes away:** detect rain damage or streaks → let dry, clean loose material, and recoat only after stable weather.
- **F2 Tire marks or pickup:** detect black transfer onto tires → extend cure time and avoid parking; thick application may need professional advice.
- **F3 Oil spots reject sealer:** detect bare circles over stains → degrease thoroughly and spot-prime if product supports it.
- **F4 Cracks reopen quickly:** detect movement after filling → larger pavement or drainage issue may need hot crack fill or resurfacing.

## Verification

The driveway has an even cured coat, cracks are filled, no sealer transfers to shoes or tires, and the driveway remains barricaded until vehicle-safe cure time has passed.

## Variations

- `coal-tar-restricted-area`: many places restrict coal-tar sealers; use locally compliant asphalt-emulsion products.
- `steep-driveway`: apply thinner coats and watch for runoff; traction additives may be appropriate if allowed.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from chemicals, slippery pavement, runoff, and vehicle access. Keep sealer out of storm drains, wear protection, and confirm cure before allowing traffic.
