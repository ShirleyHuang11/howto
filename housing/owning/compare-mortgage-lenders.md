## Goal

You compare mortgage offers on total cost, reliability, loan fit, and service quality instead of choosing only the lowest advertised rate.

## Preconditions

- You know your approximate purchase price, down payment, credit profile, income type, and target closing date.
- You can provide the same scenario to each lender.
- You understand that formal applications may require credit checks and personal financial data.

## Steps

1. **Define the loan scenario.** Set purchase price, down payment, property type, occupancy, loan term, and whether you want points. → *Expect:* every lender quotes the same scenario.
2. **Gather lender candidates.** Include a bank, credit union, mortgage broker, online lender, and any trusted local lender recommended by your agent. → *Expect:* at least three realistic options.
3. **Request written Loan Estimates or detailed worksheets.** Ask for rate, APR, points, lender fees, credits, lock period, and estimated cash to close. → *Expect:* comparable written offers.
4. **Compare rate and points together.** Calculate whether paying points makes sense for how long you expect to keep the loan. → *Expect:* you know the break-even period for any buydown.
5. **Compare execution risk.** Ask about underwriting speed, appraisal timing, weekend availability, local closing experience, and communication process. → *Expect:* you know who can meet your contract deadline.
6. **Check loan program fit.** Compare conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, ARM, or portfolio options only if they match your eligibility and risk tolerance. → *Expect:* the recommended product matches your finances and property.
7. **Choose and document the lender.** Save the selected offer, lock terms if applicable, and contact path for your loan team. → *Expect:* you have a lender selected on written terms.

## Decision points

- Lowest rate has high points → compare total cost over your expected ownership period.
- Fast closing matters → favor a lender with proven underwriting and appraisal timelines.
- Income or property is complex → choose a lender experienced with self-employment, condos, jumbo, or renovation loans.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mismatched quotes:** detect different down payments, lock periods, or points → ask lenders to reissue quotes using identical assumptions.
- **F2 Bait rate:** detect a low verbal rate but no written fee detail → require a written estimate before relying on it.
- **F3 Slow underwriting:** detect missed document reviews or unclear conditions → escalate to the loan officer or switch before contract deadlines.
- **F4 Over-sharing documents:** detect requests from an unknown lender → verify licensing and domain before uploading tax returns or bank statements.

## Verification

You have written offers for the same loan scenario and a selected lender whose rate, fees, timeline, and loan program fit your contract and budget.

## Variations

- `us`: Loan Estimates use a standardized federal format after a formal mortgage application.
- Rate-shopping window: credit scoring models commonly treat multiple mortgage inquiries in a short window as one shopping event, but timing rules vary by model.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from debt decisions and sensitive financial records. Verify lender identity before uploading documents, compare written terms, and do not choose a loan you cannot afford under realistic taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs.
