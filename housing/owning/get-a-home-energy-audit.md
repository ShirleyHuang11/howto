## Goal

You schedule and complete a credible home energy audit, then receive a prioritized report showing where the house wastes energy and which upgrades are worth doing first.

## Preconditions

- You own the home or have the owner's permission to inspect insulation, mechanical equipment, attic, basement, and utility bills.
- You can access 12 months of electric, gas, oil, propane, or district-energy bills if available.
- Combustion appliances are operating normally, and you know where the electrical panel, thermostat, attic hatch, and mechanical room are.

## Steps

1. **Collect utility and house information.** Gather recent bills, square footage, year built, HVAC type, water-heater type, attic/basement access, and known comfort problems. → *Expect:* one folder or note with the data an auditor will ask for.
2. **Check for utility or government programs.** Search your electric/gas utility, city, state/province, or national energy office for subsidized audits, approved contractors, and rebate requirements. → *Expect:* a short list of eligible audit types and any forms needed before work starts.
3. **Choose the audit level.** [BRANCH: basic walkthrough | diagnostic audit with blower-door and infrared testing] Pick diagnostic testing if you have high bills, drafts, moisture concerns, or plan expensive upgrades. → *Expect:* a clear scope and estimated fee before scheduling.
4. **Vet the auditor.** Confirm training or certification, insurance, diagnostic equipment, sample report quality, and whether the auditor sells installation work. → *Expect:* you understand any conflict of interest and have a qualified provider.
5. **Schedule under usable conditions.** Ask how long the visit takes, what areas must be accessible, and whether windows, fireplaces, pets, or combustion appliances need special handling. → *Expect:* an appointment with preparation instructions.
6. **Prepare the home.** Clear attic and crawlspace access, unlock rooms, replace burned-out bulbs only if relevant, and avoid masking normal drafts with temporary fixes. → *Expect:* the auditor can inspect the house as it normally performs.
7. **Walk through problem areas first.** Show cold rooms, ice dams, moisture stains, noisy ducts, uneven temperatures, and past repair records. → *Expect:* the auditor targets the real comfort and efficiency complaints.
8. **Allow diagnostic testing safely.** If the auditor uses a blower door, combustion safety testing, infrared camera, or duct leakage test, follow their instructions and keep children and pets away from equipment. → *Expect:* measured air leakage, thermal defects, or appliance safety findings are recorded.
9. **Review preliminary findings before they leave.** Ask which fixes are urgent, which are low-cost, which require permits, and which should happen before HVAC replacement. → *Expect:* you can distinguish maintenance, safety, comfort, and investment items.
10. **Save the final report and rebate paperwork.** Keep photos, test numbers, modeled savings, contractor recommendations, and incentive forms. → *Expect:* a written priority list you can use for quotes or DIY work.

## Decision points

- Auditor finds combustion backdrafting, gas odor, exposed wiring, or active moisture → pause efficiency work and address the safety or building defect first.
- Report recommends replacing HVAC before air sealing/insulation → ask whether load calculations should be repeated after envelope improvements.
- Rebates require preapproval or approved contractors → do not start installation work until the program confirms eligibility.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sales visit disguised as an audit:** detect no testing, no calculations, and only one product pitch → get an independent second opinion before buying.
- **F2 Incomplete access:** detect skipped attic, crawlspace, or mechanical spaces → reschedule the missing inspection or document why access was impossible.
- **F3 Unusable report:** detect vague advice without photos, priorities, or measurements → ask for a corrected report with specific recommended actions.
- **F4 Rebate missed:** detect work started before approval → contact the program immediately; if ineligible, decide based on economics without the rebate.

## Verification

You have a written audit report with utility baseline, observed defects, recommended measures, priority order, estimated savings or rationale, safety findings, and any rebate next steps.

## Variations

- `us`: look for utility rebates, state energy-office programs, ENERGY STAR Home Upgrade guidance, and Inflation Reduction Act-related state rebate programs where active.
- Rental property: get written owner permission before intrusive inspections or upgrades.
- Newer high-performance home: prioritize commissioning, ventilation settings, duct balancing, and controls over simple insulation assumptions.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because the audit may lead to expensive contractor work and can involve combustion appliances, electrical panels, ladders, attics, and personal utility data. Confirm scope, cost, and permission before the visit; do not sign upgrade contracts during the audit unless you have reviewed alternatives.
