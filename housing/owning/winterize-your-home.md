## Goal

You prepare the home for freezing weather so pipes, heating systems, exterior water lines, and weather-exposed areas are less likely to fail.

## Preconditions

- Freezing temperatures are expected or seasonal shutdown is approaching.
- You know where the main water shutoff, hose bibs, thermostat, and breaker panel are.
- You can safely access exterior faucets, gutters, and heating equipment.

## Steps

1. **Locate and test shutoffs.** Find the main water shutoff and any interior shutoffs for exterior faucets. → *Expect:* you can shut off water quickly in an emergency.
2. **Disconnect and drain exterior water.** Remove hoses, drain hose bibs, shut off interior valves if present, and install insulated covers where useful. → *Expect:* exterior fixtures are not holding water that can freeze.
3. **Protect vulnerable pipes.** Insulate pipes in crawlspaces, garages, exterior walls, and unheated basements; seal drafts near plumbing. → *Expect:* exposed pipes have insulation and less cold air movement.
4. **Service the heating system.** Replace filters, test heat before severe weather, and schedule professional service if due. → *Expect:* heat runs normally before cold weather arrives.
5. **Clean gutters and manage drainage.** Remove leaves, extend downspouts away from the foundation, and clear drains. → *Expect:* meltwater and rain move away from the house.
6. **Check weather sealing.** Inspect door sweeps, window locks, caulk, attic hatch seals, and obvious drafts. → *Expect:* major air leaks are sealed or scheduled for repair.
7. **Set safe winter operating rules.** Keep heat on, open cabinet doors during extreme cold where plumbing is vulnerable, and know when to drip faucets. ⚠️ *Hazard:* space heaters and combustion appliances can cause fire or carbon monoxide poisoning; use only listed heaters as directed and never use grills or ovens for heat. → *Expect:* household members know freeze and heat safety steps.

## Decision points

- Home will be vacant → shut off water if appropriate, set heat safely, and arrange monitoring.
- Pipes froze before → add heat tape rated for the use, insulation, or plumbing relocation by a professional.
- Ice dams are common → improve attic air sealing, insulation, and ventilation rather than only clearing ice.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Frozen pipe:** detect no water flow or bulging pipe → keep faucet open, warm slowly, and shut off water if a leak starts.
- **F2 Heating failure:** detect no heat or unsafe operation → call HVAC service and use safe temporary heat only.
- **F3 Ice dam leak:** detect ceiling stains after snow → reduce attic heat leakage and call a roofer for safe mitigation.
- **F4 Gutter overflow:** detect water spilling near foundation → clean gutters and extend downspouts once safe.

## Verification

Exterior water is disconnected and drained, vulnerable pipes are protected, heat works, gutters drain, and everyone knows the main water shutoff location.

## Variations

- Mild climate: focus on rare-freeze pipe protection and irrigation shutdown.
- Vacation home: professional winterization may include draining plumbing, antifreeze in traps, and monitoring systems.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from freezing damage, ladders, fire, and carbon monoxide. Do not climb icy roofs or ladders, do not use outdoor combustion devices indoors, and confirm smoke and carbon monoxide alarms work.
