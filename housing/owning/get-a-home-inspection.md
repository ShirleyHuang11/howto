## Goal

You hire a qualified inspector, attend or review the inspection carefully, and use the findings to make an informed purchase decision before your inspection contingency expires.

## Preconditions

- You have an accepted offer or are otherwise allowed to inspect the property.
- You know the inspection contingency deadline, if your contract has one.
- You have access instructions from the seller, agent, or listing representative.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the deadline and inspection scope.** Read the contract for the inspection period, cancellation rights, and any required notice method. → *Expect:* a calendar deadline and a written list of inspections you can still order.
2. **Choose a qualified inspector.** Look for state licensing where required, professional credentials, sample reports, insurance, and strong recent reviews. → *Expect:* an inspector who can explain defects clearly and deliver a written report before your deadline.
3. **Book the inspection and request access.** Coordinate the time with both agents or the seller, and ask whether utilities will be on. → *Expect:* a confirmed appointment with the property accessible and utilities available if possible.
4. **Add specialty inspections when warranted.** [BRANCH: older home, add sewer scope, pest, roof, chimney, or electrical | rural home, add well and septic | visible moisture, add mold or drainage evaluation] → *Expect:* all high-risk systems are assigned to an appropriate specialist.
5. **Attend the inspection when allowed.** Walk with the inspector near the end, ask how serious each issue is, and do not distract them during detailed testing. → *Expect:* you understand which findings are safety issues, major costs, routine maintenance, or cosmetic defects.
6. **Read the full report.** Separate urgent defects from normal wear, and ask the inspector to clarify anything unclear in writing. → *Expect:* a defect list with photos, locations, and practical next steps.
7. **Decide before the contingency expires.** Discuss repair requests, credits, price changes, further inspections, or cancellation with your agent or attorney. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* after the contingency deadline passes, your right to cancel or renegotiate may shrink, so confirm the deadline before waiving anything. → *Expect:* a written decision sent through the contract-required channel.

## Decision points

- Major safety or structural defect found → get contractor estimates before negotiating.
- Seller refuses repairs or credits → decide whether the price still makes sense with the defect risk.
- Inspector cannot access a system → request reinspection, specialist access, or a written concession.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missed contingency deadline:** detect that the report arrived late or no response was sent → ask your agent or attorney whether an extension can be signed immediately.
- **F2 Vague report:** detect phrases like "recommend further evaluation" without detail → call the inspector and get written clarification or a specialist estimate.
- **F3 Utilities off:** detect that plumbing, HVAC, or appliances could not be tested → require utilities on and reinspect before waiving the contingency.
- **F4 Seller disputes findings:** detect pushback on defects → use report photos and licensed contractor estimates rather than opinions.

## Verification

You have the signed inspection report, any specialty reports, written clarification for unclear defects, and a documented decision before the inspection contingency deadline.

## Variations

- `us`: licensing rules vary by state; some states regulate home inspectors and others rely on professional associations and contract terms.
- Pre-listing inspection: the seller orders the inspection before listing to identify repairs and disclosure items.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because inspection results affect a major purchase and legal deadlines. Do not waive inspection rights casually, do not enter unsafe areas yourself, and confirm written deadlines before signing any waiver or amendment.
