## Goal

You evaluate a buyer's offer by net proceeds, certainty, contingencies, timing, and legal obligations before accepting, rejecting, or countering.

## Preconditions

- You have the full written offer and all addenda.
- Seller disclosures and listing terms are available for comparison.
- You can consult your agent, attorney, or tax adviser if needed.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the offer is complete.** Check buyer names, property address, purchase price, earnest money, financing type, closing date, contingencies, expiration, and signatures. → *Expect:* a valid offer package ready to evaluate.
2. **Calculate net proceeds.** Subtract mortgage payoff, commissions or concessions, closing costs, repair credits, taxes, transfer fees, and requested seller-paid costs. → *Expect:* the offer's real net value is clear.
3. **Assess buyer financing strength.** Review preapproval, proof of funds, down payment, loan type, appraisal gap language, and lender reputation. → *Expect:* you understand the risk that financing fails.
4. **Review contingencies and deadlines.** Inspect inspection, financing, appraisal, sale-of-home, HOA, title, and due diligence terms. → *Expect:* you know how and when the buyer can cancel or renegotiate.
5. **Evaluate timing and possession.** Compare closing date, rent-back, occupancy, repair deadlines, and moving needs. → *Expect:* timing either works or requires a counteroffer.
6. **Compare competing offers consistently.** Use the same net sheet and risk categories for each offer, not just headline price. → *Expect:* offers are ranked by value and certainty.
7. **Choose accept, counter, or reject.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* accepting a purchase contract can bind you to sell, so confirm price, contingencies, deadlines, and exclusions before signing. → *Expect:* your response is written and sent before the offer expiration.

## Decision points

- Highest price has weak financing → weigh net value against failure risk.
- Buyer requests many seller concessions → compare net proceeds, not purchase price alone.
- Offer includes rent-back or possession change → verify insurance, deposit, and local legal rules.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Headline price mistake:** detect accepting the highest price with worse net proceeds → rebuild net sheets and counter if still possible.
- **F2 Hidden contingency risk:** detect broad cancellation rights after acceptance → negotiate tighter deadlines or stronger earnest money.
- **F3 Expiration missed:** detect offer expired before response → ask buyer to renew in writing.
- **F4 Ambiguous included items:** detect unclear appliances, fixtures, or personal property → clarify in a counteroffer.

## Verification

You have a signed acceptance, counteroffer, or rejection based on written net proceeds, financing risk, contingencies, timing, and legal review where needed.

## Variations

- `us`: purchase contract forms, attorney review, earnest money customs, and disclosure obligations vary by state.
- Multiple-offer situation: local rules may govern whether you can disclose competing terms or request best-and-final offers.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because accepting an offer creates legal obligations and exposes financial information. Do not sign under pressure, disclose private payoff details unnecessarily, or ignore attorney review rights.
