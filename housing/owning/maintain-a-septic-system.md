## Goal

You keep a septic system working safely by conserving water, protecting the drainfield, scheduling inspections and pumping, and responding early to warning signs.

## Preconditions

- The property uses a septic tank and drainfield or another onsite wastewater system.
- You know or can locate the tank, inspection ports, and drainfield area.
- Local rules for inspection, pumping, permits, and contractor licensing.

## Steps

1. **Create a septic file.** Gather permits, as-built drawings, pump records, inspection reports, and contractor contacts. → *Expect:* you know the system type, age, tank size, and drainfield location.
2. **Mark protected areas.** Identify the tank lids and drainfield; keep vehicles, sheds, pools, and heavy landscaping off them. → *Expect:* household members know where not to drive or build.
3. **Use water steadily, not in surges.** Fix leaks, spread laundry loads across days, and install efficient fixtures where practical. → *Expect:* the system receives manageable daily flow.
4. **Control what enters drains.** Flush only human waste and toilet paper; keep grease, wipes, paint, solvents, medications, and food waste out. → *Expect:* fewer clogs and less tank disruption.
5. **Schedule routine inspection.** Have a qualified septic professional inspect baffles, sludge/scum levels, lids, alarms, pumps, and drainfield condition. → *Expect:* you receive a written maintenance recommendation.
6. **Pump when inspection or schedule indicates.** Typical intervals depend on tank size, household size, and solids load; do not wait for backups. → *Expect:* sludge is removed before it reaches the drainfield.
7. **Watch for warning signs.** Slow drains, sewage odor, lush wet grass over the drainfield, gurgling, or alarm lights need prompt action. → *Expect:* problems are caught before sewage backs into the home.
8. **Respond to sewage exposure safely.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* sewage contains pathogens; avoid contact, keep children and pets away, and call a septic professional or local health authority for backups or surface discharge. → *Expect:* exposure is limited and repairs are handled professionally.

## Decision points

- Alarm sounds on an aerobic or pump system → reduce water use and call service promptly.
- Selling or buying a home → follow required transfer inspection rules before closing.
- Adding bedrooms, occupants, or major fixtures → check whether system capacity and permits need review.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sewage backup indoors:** detect wastewater in tubs, toilets, or floor drains → stop water use, avoid contact, call septic service, and sanitize affected areas.
- **F2 Drainfield failure:** detect persistent wet, smelly ground → stop irrigating or driving there and get professional evaluation.
- **F3 Tank access unsafe:** detect damaged lids or open holes → keep people away and replace secure riser covers immediately.
- **F4 Additives promise no pumping:** detect reliance on additives instead of service → resume inspections; additives do not replace solids removal.

## Verification

You have a current septic map, protected drainfield, documented inspection or pumping schedule, and no unresolved alarms, odors, backups, or surfacing wastewater.

## Variations

- `advanced-treatment-system`: may require service contracts, alarms, filters, disinfection, or sampling.
- `seasonal-home`: long vacant periods and sudden occupancy surges may require adjusted maintenance and water-use habits.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from pathogens, groundwater contamination, and expensive property damage. Do not enter a septic tank, open unsafe lids, or ignore sewage surfacing; keep maintenance records private but available for contractors and real-estate transactions.
