## Goal

You drain sediment from a tank-style water heater so it heats efficiently and is less likely to clog, rumble, or fail early.

## Preconditions

- A tank-style water heater, not a tankless unit.
- A garden hose long enough to reach a floor drain, utility sink, or outside discharge point.
- Work gloves, eye protection, and access to the water heater shutoff, gas control or breaker, and drain valve.

## Steps

1. **Read the data plate and manual if available.** Confirm the unit is tank-style and note whether it is gas or electric. → *Expect:* you know which power or fuel control must be shut off.
2. **Turn off the heat source.** [BRANCH: electric, switch off the dedicated breaker | gas, set the gas control to pilot or vacation mode if the manual allows it] ⚠️ *Hazard:* never run an electric element in an empty or partially drained tank; confirm the breaker is off first. → *Expect:* the burner or elements cannot heat during draining.
3. **Close the cold-water supply valve.** Turn the valve on the cold inlet above the heater clockwise or perpendicular to the pipe. → *Expect:* new water stops entering the tank.
4. **Open a nearby hot-water faucet.** Use a sink or tub on the same floor if possible. → *Expect:* air can enter the plumbing so the tank drains smoothly.
5. **Attach the hose to the drain valve.** Route the hose downhill to a safe drain point that can handle hot water. ⚠️ *Hazard:* water may be scalding; keep children, pets, and bare skin away from the discharge. → *Expect:* the hose is secure and has no kinks.
6. **Open the drain valve slowly.** Use a flat screwdriver or valve handle; stop if the valve leaks around the stem or cannot be controlled. → *Expect:* water flows through the hose with visible sediment at first.
7. **Pulse the cold-water supply to stir sediment.** Briefly open the cold-water valve for 10-20 seconds, then close it; repeat until discharge runs mostly clear. → *Expect:* bursts of cloudy water reduce to clearer flow.
8. **Close the drain valve and refill the tank.** Remove the hose only after the valve is closed, then open the cold-water supply fully while the hot faucet remains open. → *Expect:* air sputters from the faucet, then a steady stream of water appears.
9. **Restore heat only after the tank is full.** Close the hot faucet after steady flow, then restore the breaker or gas setting. ⚠️ *Hazard:* confirm the tank is full before energizing an electric heater. → *Expect:* the heater resumes normal operation without dry-firing.
10. **Check for leaks.** Inspect the drain valve, inlet valve, and nearby floor over the next hour. → *Expect:* all fittings stay dry and hot water returns after recovery time.

## Decision points

- Drain valve is plastic, brittle, or leaking → stop and call a plumber before forcing it.
- Water will discharge outside → route it where hot water and sediment will not damage plants, siding, or neighbors' property.
- Heater has not been flushed in years → expect heavier sediment; a full flush may take longer or expose a failing drain valve.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No water drains:** closed hot faucet, blocked valve, or vacuum lock → open a hot faucet fully and briefly open the cold supply; if still blocked, stop and call a plumber.
- **F2 Drain valve will not close:** sediment is caught in the seat → pulse the cold supply briefly, close again, and attach a hose cap temporarily if needed until repair.
- **F3 Rusty water continues:** tank may be corroding internally → document the discharge and schedule inspection or replacement planning.
- **F4 Pilot will not relight:** gas control or thermocouple issue → follow the printed lighting instructions; if gas smell is present, leave the area and call the gas utility.

## Verification

The tank is refilled, heat is restored only after steady hot-side water flow, discharge has run mostly clear, and the drain valve and surrounding floor remain dry.

## Variations

- `tankless`: do not use this procedure; tankless heaters are descaled with a pump, isolation valves, and manufacturer-approved solution.
- `hard-water-area`: flush more often, commonly every 6-12 months, if sediment builds quickly.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from scalding water, electricity, gas controls, and leaks. Explicitly confirm the heat source is off before draining and the tank is full before restoring heat.
