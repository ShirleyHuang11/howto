## Goal

End a lease early with the least avoidable cost by using lease terms, legal rights, landlord agreement, or replacement tenant options.

## Preconditions

- You have the signed lease, move-in date, lease end date, rent amount, deposit amount, early termination clause, notice address, and local tenant law.
- You know the reason for leaving, desired move-out date, and whether domestic violence, military service, habitability, disability accommodation, or landlord breach rules may apply.
- You can document payments, condition, key return, and landlord communications.
- You can afford possible rent, fees, advertising, cleaning, or legal advice.

## Steps

1. **Read the lease termination terms.** Identify notice period, early termination fee, reletting fee, sublet/assignment rules, mitigation language, and required delivery method. → *Expect:* you know the contractual cost and procedure.
2. **Check legal rights and exceptions.** Review local rules for military orders, domestic violence, unsafe housing, landlord harassment, disability accommodation, or failure to repair. → *Expect:* you know whether a legal early-exit path may apply.
3. **Calculate realistic cost.** Estimate rent through move-out, fees, deposit risk, utilities, advertising, and possible rent until replacement tenant starts. → *Expect:* you know the maximum exposure and negotiation target.
4. **Choose an exit strategy.** [BRANCH: pay lease break fee | negotiate surrender | find replacement tenant | assert legal right | sublet/assign] Pick the path with evidence and lowest risk. → *Expect:* the plan matches the lease and local law.
5. **Send written notice.** State move-out date, reason if helpful, forwarding address, and requested confirmation; attach proof only when needed. → *Expect:* the landlord has documented notice by the required method.
6. **Get any agreement in writing.** Confirm release date, fees, deposit treatment, utility responsibilities, showing access, and whether future rent is waived. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* signing a lease-break agreement may waive rights or admit fees, so read every clause before signing. → *Expect:* both sides have a signed or clearly accepted written agreement.
7. **Help mitigate if required.** Provide access for showings, propose qualified replacement tenants, and keep records of listings and landlord responses. → *Expect:* you can show reasonable efforts to reduce unpaid rent.
8. **Move out cleanly.** Document condition, return keys with proof, transfer utilities, and request deposit accounting. → *Expect:* possession ends on a documented date.
9. **Resolve final accounting.** Review charges for rent, fees, damages, utilities, and deposit deductions; dispute unsupported amounts promptly. → *Expect:* the account is paid, settled, or formally disputed.

## Decision points

- You have a protected legal reason to leave → use the statute's exact notice and documentation requirements.
- Landlord agrees verbally → do not rely on it until written confirmation states release terms.
- Replacement tenant is available → verify application approval and written release from liability.
- Landlord fails to repair serious conditions → document notices and consider code enforcement or legal advice before leaving.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Landlord refuses release:** detect written refusal → recover by using contractual fee, legal exception, or replacement-tenant path.
- **F2 Continued rent demand:** detect invoices after move-out → recover by requesting proof of mitigation, vacancy dates, and new lease start date.
- **F3 Replacement rejected:** detect rejection without reason → recover by asking for screening criteria and proposing another qualified applicant.
- **F4 Deposit consumed by lease break:** detect final accounting → recover by separating lawful fees from unsupported damage or cleaning charges.
- **F5 Credit/collections threat:** detect collection notice → recover by disputing in writing with lease, notices, and payment records.

## Verification

You have written confirmation of release, replacement tenancy, lawful termination, settlement, or a documented dispute of any claimed balance.

## Variations

- `us`: military, domestic-violence, repair, mitigation, notice, and fee rules vary by state and city.
- `fixed-term`: liability usually differs from month-to-month notice termination.
- `corporate-landlord`: portals may not capture legal notices; send notice to the lease address too.
- `roommate-lease`: all tenants may remain jointly liable unless the landlord releases the departing tenant.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from rent liability, collections, credit damage, and safety issues. Do not abandon the unit without notice and proof; seek local help for violence, harassment, or unsafe housing.
