## Goal

Report a serious housing code problem to the correct agency with evidence, while preserving repair notices and tenant protections.

## Preconditions

- The issue involves health, safety, habitability, occupancy, pests, heat, water, sewage, electrical, structural, fire, mold/moisture, or illegal unit conditions.
- You have the address, landlord/property manager contact, lease if any, photos/videos, dates, repair requests, and witness notes.
- You know whether the issue is urgent or life-threatening.
- You can receive inspector calls and provide access if required.

## Steps

1. **Handle emergencies first.** For fire, gas smell, carbon monoxide, active flooding near electricity, collapse, or violence, call emergency services or the utility emergency line. → *Expect:* immediate danger is routed to emergency responders.
2. **Document the condition.** Photograph and video the defect, date, location, affected rooms, temperature readings, leaks, pests, notices, and damaged belongings. → *Expect:* evidence shows severity and timeline.
3. **Notify the landlord in writing.** Send a repair request describing the issue, access availability, and urgency unless doing so would create danger. → *Expect:* the landlord has a timestamped chance to repair.
4. **Find the enforcement agency.** Identify city/county housing, building, code enforcement, health department, fire marshal, or tenant hotline. → *Expect:* you know who inspects this type of violation.
5. **Submit the complaint.** [BRANCH: web | phone | 311 | in-person] Provide address, unit, issue, dates, landlord response, photos if accepted, and access details. → *Expect:* you receive a complaint, service request, or inspection number.
6. **Prepare for inspection.** Keep the condition visible if safe, collect documents, and arrange access with other occupants as needed. → *Expect:* the inspector can verify the violation.
7. **Attend or follow the inspection.** Show the issue, answer factual questions, and ask how to get the inspection report or violation notice. → *Expect:* you know whether a violation was found and what happens next.
8. **Track orders and repairs.** Monitor deadlines for landlord correction, reinspection, fines, or emergency orders. → *Expect:* repair status and enforcement dates are documented.
9. **Escalate retaliation or noncompliance.** If repairs are not made or retaliation occurs, contact tenant legal aid, housing agency, or court process before withholding rent or moving. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* rent withholding or lease termination can risk eviction if done incorrectly, so confirm local rules first. → *Expect:* any escalation follows a documented legal path.

## Decision points

- Problem threatens life or safety now → use emergency services, not ordinary code complaint timing.
- You fear retaliation → ask the agency about confidentiality and tenant protection rules before filing.
- Multiple tenants affected → submit coordinated complaints with separate unit evidence.
- Issue is inside common areas → document access doors, hallways, stairs, heat, pests, trash, or fire exits.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong agency:** detect referral or closed complaint → recover by filing with the named housing/building/health/fire office.
- **F2 Inspector cannot access:** detect missed inspection → recover by rescheduling and confirming entry instructions.
- **F3 Landlord makes cosmetic fix:** detect recurring or hidden problem → recover by documenting recurrence and requesting reinspection.
- **F4 Retaliation:** detect eviction threat, rent increase, lockout, or service reduction after complaint → recover by contacting legal aid or tenant agency immediately.
- **F5 No violation found:** detect inspection report says compliant → recover by asking for report, documenting continuing issue, or seeking a second route such as health department.

## Verification

You have a complaint number and later an inspection result, violation notice, repair order, closure notice, or documented escalation for noncompliance.

## Variations

- `us`: habitability standards, heat seasons, rent withholding, repair-and-deduct, and retaliation protections vary by state and city.
- `subsidized-housing`: report to the landlord and the housing authority or program administrator if required.
- `mold`: many agencies enforce moisture sources rather than mold species testing.
- `fire-safety`: blocked exits, alarms, sprinklers, and overcrowding may go to the fire marshal.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from retaliation, unsafe conditions, and private home photos. Keep records outside the unit if lockout is possible, avoid unsafe inspection attempts, and get local advice before withholding rent.
