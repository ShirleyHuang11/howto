## Goal

File a renters insurance claim for covered property loss, liability, or additional living expense and track it to payment or denial.

## Preconditions

- The event is safe to document and urgent hazards have been addressed.
- You have the policy number, insurer contact, date of loss, cause, photos/videos, receipts, inventory, police/fire report if applicable, and landlord communications.
- You know deductible, coverage limits, exclusions, and claim deadlines.
- You can preserve damaged property until the insurer says disposal is allowed.

## Steps

1. **Protect people and property first.** Stop further damage if safe, call emergency services for fire/crime/injury, and notify the landlord about building issues. → *Expect:* immediate hazards and required notices are handled.
2. **Review your policy basics.** Check covered perils, deductible, personal property limit, loss-of-use coverage, liability coverage, and claim reporting deadline. → *Expect:* you know what part of the policy may apply.
3. **Document the loss.** Photograph rooms, damaged items, serial numbers, packaging, receipts, temporary housing costs, and any theft or police details. → *Expect:* evidence shows what was damaged, when, and approximate value.
4. **Prevent additional loss.** Move undamaged items, dry wet property, board up if allowed, and keep receipts for emergency supplies or temporary lodging. → *Expect:* mitigation actions and costs are documented.
5. **Start the claim.** [BRANCH: insurer app | website | phone] Provide policy number, date, location, cause, damage summary, and contact details. → *Expect:* you receive a claim number and adjuster or next-step instructions.
6. **Submit inventory and proof.** List each item with age, cost, replacement cost, receipts, photos, and repair estimates; upload police/fire reports if required. → *Expect:* the claim file shows documents received.
7. **Cooperate with inspection.** Schedule adjuster calls, video walkthroughs, sworn statements, or item inspections as requested. → *Expect:* the insurer has enough information to evaluate coverage and amount.
8. **Review settlement or denial.** Check deductible, depreciation, replacement-cost holdback, coverage limits, exclusions, and additional living expense payments. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* accepting a final settlement or release may close the claim, so understand what remains unpaid before signing. → *Expect:* you know the payment, denial reason, or documents still needed.
9. **Close and keep records.** Save claim number, payments, receipts for replacements, denial letters, and landlord correspondence. → *Expect:* the claim outcome is documented for renewal, taxes, or disputes.

## Decision points

- Loss is below or near deductible → consider whether filing is worth potential premium impact.
- Theft, vandalism, or burglary → file a police report because the insurer may require a report number.
- Unit is uninhabitable → ask about loss-of-use/additional-living-expense approval before long hotel stays.
- Landlord's building insurance applies → your renters policy usually covers your belongings and living expenses, not the building.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Claim denied for exclusion:** detect denial letter citing policy language → recover by reading the cited exclusion and appealing with facts if the cause is misstated.
- **F2 Inventory too vague:** detect adjuster requests more detail → recover with photos, bank/card records, manuals, serial numbers, and replacement links.
- **F3 Damaged items discarded:** detect insurer asks to inspect missing property → recover with photos and disposal permission records if available.
- **F4 Payment too low:** detect depreciation, missing items, or wrong limits → recover by submitting supplement evidence or replacement receipts.
- **F5 Landlord delay worsens loss:** detect continuing leak or unsafe condition → recover by sending written repair notices and preserving mitigation receipts.

## Verification

You have a claim number and either payment, written denial with reasons, or an active adjuster request with the next deadline recorded.

## Variations

- `replacement-cost`: full reimbursement may require buying replacements and submitting receipts.
- `actual-cash-value`: payment subtracts depreciation and may be lower than replacement price.
- `liability-claim`: do not admit fault; forward demand letters to the insurer.
- `catastrophe`: deadlines and adjuster availability may be extended after widespread disasters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from financial loss and sensitive home photos. Do not enter unsafe areas for documentation, do not exaggerate values, and remove unrelated private documents from photos before uploading.
