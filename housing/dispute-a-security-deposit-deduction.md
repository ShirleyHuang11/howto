## Goal

Dispute an unsupported security deposit deduction with evidence and preserve the deadline for settlement, agency complaint, or court.

## Preconditions

- You have the lease, deposit amount, move-in condition report, move-out photos/videos, key return proof, forwarding address, and landlord's itemized deduction notice.
- You know the local deadline for deposit return and disputes.
- You have receipts for cleaning, repairs, rent, utilities, and any agreed deductions.
- Communications can be sent in writing by email, portal, certified mail, or tracked delivery.

## Steps

1. **Read the deduction notice.** Identify each deduction, amount, claimed reason, invoice/receipt, date, and whether the landlord met the legal deadline. → *Expect:* disputed and undisputed amounts are separated.
2. **Compare against lease and condition evidence.** Match each charge to move-in inventory, move-out photos, walkthrough notes, repair requests, and ordinary wear. → *Expect:* you know which charges lack support.
3. **Calculate the refund requested.** Add unsupported charges, duplicate fees, late-return penalties, and any statutory interest or damages if applicable. → *Expect:* you have a specific dollar demand.
4. **Draft a written dispute.** State the tenancy dates, deposit amount, deductions disputed, evidence, and requested refund deadline. → *Expect:* the letter is factual and organized by deduction.
5. **Attach evidence.** Include photos, videos stills, inventories, receipts, emails, key return proof, and excerpts from the lease or law. → *Expect:* each claim has supporting proof.
6. **Send the dispute.** [BRANCH: email | portal | certified mail | deposit scheme] Use the lease or legal notice address and keep delivery proof. → *Expect:* you have a sent timestamp, tracking receipt, or portal confirmation.
7. **Negotiate in writing.** Accept only amounts you understand and request invoices or photos for vague charges. → *Expect:* the dispute narrows to documented issues.
8. **Escalate if needed.** File with the deposit scheme, housing agency, small claims court, or mediation before the deadline. ⚠️ *Irreversible:* filing fees and settlement agreements can affect your rights, so confirm the amount and forum before filing or signing. → *Expect:* you receive a case number, hearing date, or settlement confirmation.
9. **Close the record.** Save refund proof, settlement, judgment, or final denial with all evidence. → *Expect:* the outcome is documented for taxes, references, or future housing disputes.

## Decision points

- Deduction is for unpaid rent or utilities you truly owe → separate it from damage or cleaning disputes.
- Landlord missed the statutory deadline → demand full return and any local penalties if allowed.
- Deposit is held in a formal protection scheme → use that scheme's dispute path first.
- Amount is small → compare filing time and fees against likely recovery.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 No itemization:** detect lump-sum or vague deductions → recover by requesting itemized amounts, invoices, photos, and legal basis.
- **F2 Missing move-in evidence:** detect weak baseline proof → recover with emails, maintenance tickets, inventory, witness statements, and age/wear arguments.
- **F3 Landlord ignores dispute:** detect no response after your deadline → recover by filing with the required agency, scheme, or small claims court.
- **F4 Settlement pressure:** detect demand to sign broad release → recover by reading whether it waives other claims before accepting.
- **F5 Wrong notice address:** detect returned mail or no delivery → recover by sending to every lease, statutory, portal, and business address available.

## Verification

You have a written refund, settlement, agency case, court filing, or final decision for the disputed deposit amount.

## Variations

- `us`: itemization deadlines, penalties, interest, and small-claims limits vary by state and city.
- `uk`: protected deposits usually use the scheme's alternative dispute resolution process.
- `room-rental`: shared-area deductions may require proof that the charge is fairly allocated.
- `furnished`: inventory detail and depreciation matter more for furniture and appliances.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from money loss and tenant-history disputes. Keep communication factual, redact account numbers in evidence, and do not threaten claims you are not prepared to file.
