## Goal

You build a sustainable fat-loss plan that combines a moderate calorie deficit, strength training, daily movement, protein, and recovery.

## Preconditions

- Current body weight trend and waist measurement if available.
- Access to basic foods you can prepare consistently.
- A training option such as gym, dumbbells, bands, walking routes, or bodyweight exercises.

## Steps

1. **Set a realistic rate of loss.** Aim for about 0.5-1% of body weight per week, slower if already lean or performance matters. → *Expect:* the target is measurable without requiring crash dieting.
2. **Create a moderate calorie deficit.** Use `fitness/planning/count-calories-for-a-goal` or portion control to reduce intake by about 300-500 calories per day. → *Expect:* the plan has a clear intake strategy.
3. **Set protein and fiber anchors.** Use `fitness/planning/calculate-your-daily-protein-target` and include vegetables, fruit, legumes, or whole grains daily. → *Expect:* meals support satiety and muscle retention.
4. **Lift 2-4 days per week.** Prioritize full-body or upper/lower strength sessions with controlled reps and progressive overload. → *Expect:* training tells your body to keep muscle while fat is lost.
5. **Add low-impact movement.** Walk, cycle easy, or use steps as a baseline before adding hard cardio. → *Expect:* calorie expenditure rises without excessive joint stress.
6. **Plan diet breaks or maintenance days when needed.** Use them for adherence, high stress, or long dieting phases, not as uncontrolled binges. → *Expect:* recovery improves while the weekly average remains aligned with the goal.
7. **Review every 2 weeks.** Compare weight trend, waist, workout performance, hunger, and sleep. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop aggressive loss if you have dizziness, chest pain, fainting, or severe fatigue. → *Expect:* adjustments are based on several signals.

## Decision points

- Hunger is unmanageable → increase protein, fiber, food volume, or calories slightly.
- Strength is dropping quickly → reduce deficit or training volume.
- Weight trend stalls for 3 weeks → tighten logging, add steps, or reduce calories modestly.
- Stress or sleep is poor → hold the deficit rather than pushing harder.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much deficit:** detect poor sleep, irritability, and weak workouts → raise calories and reduce cardio.
- **F2 Cardio replacing strength:** detect weight loss with obvious strength decline → restore 2-4 strength sessions weekly.
- **F3 Weekend rebound:** detect weekday deficit erased by weekend overeating → plan higher-calorie meals inside the weekly target.
- **F4 Scale-only judgment:** detect frustration despite waist or photos improving → review non-scale metrics.

## Verification

After 2-4 weeks, your weekly weight trend is moving down at roughly 0.5-1% per week, waist or photos are improving, and you complete planned strength sessions with stable technique.

## Variations

- `easier`: begin with walking, protein at each meal, and reducing liquid calories before tracking calories.
- `harder`: use macro targets and planned diet breaks for longer cuts.
- `equipment`: gym machines, dumbbells, bands, or bodyweight circuits can all preserve muscle if progressed.
- `at-home`: pair 3 full-body sessions with daily walks and simple meal prep.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when moderate. Avoid severe restriction, dehydration, stimulant misuse, or training through sharp pain. People who are pregnant, underweight, medically complex, or have an eating-disorder history need professional guidance. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
