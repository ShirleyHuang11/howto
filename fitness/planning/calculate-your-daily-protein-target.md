## Goal

You calculate a practical daily protein target that supports training, muscle retention, and satiety without relying on guesswork.

## Preconditions

- Your current body weight or a reasonable goal body weight.
- A calculator or nutrition-tracking app.
- Awareness of any kidney disease, eating-disorder history, or medical diet restrictions.

## Steps

1. **Choose the body weight to use.** Use current body weight if you are near a healthy range; use goal body weight if you have significant fat to lose. → *Expect:* the calculation is tied to a realistic training body size.
2. **Select a protein range.** Aim for about 1.6-2.2 g/kg per day for active adults, or about 0.7-1.0 g/lb. → *Expect:* a target range instead of one fragile exact number.
3. **Pick the lower end first if adherence is hard.** Start near 1.6 g/kg and increase only if hunger, recovery, or muscle gain calls for it. → *Expect:* the target feels achievable with normal meals.
4. **Divide protein across meals.** Split the total into 3-5 servings with roughly 25-45 g each, depending on body size. → *Expect:* each meal contains a clear protein source.
5. **Build a food list.** Add lean meat, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu, tempeh, beans, lentils, or protein powder if useful. → *Expect:* the target can be met from foods you actually eat.
6. **Check the full diet context.** Make sure protein does not crowd out fruits, vegetables, carbs for training, or fats. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not use very high protein intakes to justify severe calorie restriction. → *Expect:* meals remain balanced and sustainable.

## Decision points

- You miss the target most days → lower it slightly or add one reliable protein serving.
- You feel overly full → spread intake across more meals or choose leaner sources.
- Training recovery is poor while calories are low → review total calories and carbs, not protein alone.
- You have kidney disease or prescribed diet limits → ask a clinician before changing intake.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Using pounds as kilograms:** detect a target that is roughly twice as high as expected → recalculate with correct units.
- **F2 Counting raw and cooked weights inconsistently:** detect confusing app entries → use the same food state each time.
- **F3 Protein crowds out fiber:** detect constipation or low vegetable intake → add plant foods and fluids.
- **F4 All-or-nothing tracking:** detect missed days after one imperfect meal → resume at the next meal.

## Verification

You have a daily protein range in grams, a chosen target inside that range, and a meal plan showing 3-5 protein servings that add up within about 10% of the target.

## Variations

- `easier`: use goal body weight in pounds and aim for about 0.7 g/lb.
- `harder`: periodize intake higher during fat loss and lower during maintenance if appetite and training support it.
- `equipment`: a food scale improves accuracy but measuring cups and label servings can work.
- `at-home`: keep shelf-stable options like canned fish, beans, lentils, Greek yogurt, tofu, and protein powder.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most healthy adults, but people with kidney disease, pregnancy, medical nutrition therapy, or eating-disorder history should get individualized advice. Nutrition apps may store sensitive health data. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or registered dietitian if you have a relevant condition.
