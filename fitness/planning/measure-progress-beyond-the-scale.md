## Goal

You track fitness progress with performance, body measurements, habits, and recovery markers so a single body-weight reading does not control your decisions.

## Preconditions

- Tape measure, phone camera, workout log, and optional scale.
- Consistent lighting and clothing for photos.
- At least one repeatable exercise or cardio benchmark.

## Steps

1. **Pick three categories to track.** Use one body metric, one performance metric, and one behavior metric such as workouts completed. → *Expect:* progress has multiple measurable signals.
2. **Measure waist and another relevant circumference.** Measure at the same anatomical point each time, with the tape level and snug but not compressing skin. → *Expect:* numbers are repeatable within about 0.5 in or 1 cm.
3. **Take standardized photos.** Stand relaxed from front, side, and back in the same light and distance every 2-4 weeks. → *Expect:* photos are comparable rather than posed differently each time.
4. **Choose a performance benchmark.** Use a safe repeatable test such as max clean push-ups, a 12-minute walk distance, or a familiar lift for 5 controlled reps. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not test maximal lifts without experience and proper setup. → *Expect:* the benchmark reflects fitness, not reckless effort.
5. **Track habits weekly.** Count completed workouts, daily steps, protein target hits, sleep average, or mobility sessions. → *Expect:* behavior trends explain body and performance changes.
6. **Review trends every 2-4 weeks.** Compare averages and photos, not single-day readings. → *Expect:* decisions are based on direction over time.

## Decision points

- Scale unchanged but waist smaller and strength up → keep the plan.
- Weight down but workouts feel worse and sleep is poor → reduce deficit or recovery stress.
- Measurements vary wildly → improve consistency before changing the program.
- No metric improves for 4 weeks → adjust training, nutrition, or recovery one variable at a time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Measuring different spots:** detect sudden jumps that do not match photos → mark the anatomical landmark and repeat.
- **F2 Over-testing:** detect soreness or anxiety from frequent benchmarks → test monthly, not daily.
- **F3 Ignoring performance:** detect weight loss paired with large strength drops → add food, rest, or lower training volume.
- **F4 Photo distortion:** detect different camera angles or lighting → use the same distance, lens, and room setup.

## Verification

You have at least 4 weeks of weekly habit data, two comparable body-measurement entries, two photo sets, and one repeated performance benchmark reviewed as trends rather than isolated numbers.

## Variations

- `easier`: track only workouts completed, waist measurement, and one walking benchmark.
- `harder`: add resting heart rate, heart-rate recovery, estimated training volume, and sleep averages.
- `equipment`: use a string and ruler if no tape measure is available.
- `at-home`: benchmark a timed plank, chair squat reps, or brisk walk distance.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Avoid extreme dieting or compulsive measuring, and stop any benchmark that causes sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, or unusual shortness of breath. Store photos and health data securely. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
