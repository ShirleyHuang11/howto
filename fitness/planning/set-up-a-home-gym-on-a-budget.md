## Goal

You create a safe, useful home-gym setup for strength, cardio, and mobility without buying equipment that does not match your training.

## Preconditions

- A clear budget and a training goal.
- Floor space with enough clearance for squats, hinges, pressing, and lying down.
- Permission for noise, flooring, and wall or ceiling attachments if you rent.

## Steps

1. **Measure the available space.** Check floor area, ceiling height, door swing, ventilation, and storage. → *Expect:* you know which exercises and equipment physically fit.
2. **Prioritize movement patterns.** Cover squat or lunge, hip hinge, push, pull, carry, core, and conditioning before buying specialty gear. → *Expect:* each purchase solves a training need.
3. **Buy the first essentials.** Start with a mat, resistance bands, adjustable dumbbells or kettlebells, and a sturdy bench only if your program needs one. → *Expect:* the setup supports full-body training.
4. **Protect the floor and neighbors.** Use rubber mats, avoid dropping weights, and test noise at realistic training times. → *Expect:* equipment stays stable and the space remains usable.
5. **Check equipment integrity.** Inspect collars, handles, band wear, bench stability, and weight adjustment locks before use. ⚠️ *Safety:* discard cracked bands, loose collars, or unstable benches. → *Expect:* no component shifts under load.
6. **Create a storage routine.** Store weights low, bands away from sharp edges, and walking paths clear. → *Expect:* the gym does not become a trip hazard.
7. **Add only after 4 weeks of use.** Upgrade based on bottlenecks such as needing heavier pulls, safer pressing, or cardio options. → *Expect:* money goes toward equipment you will actually use.

## Decision points

- Space is tiny → choose bands, adjustable dumbbells, a door anchor rated for exercise, and bodyweight progressions.
- Training requires heavy barbell work → budget for rack, safeties, bar, plates, and flooring instead of a partial setup.
- Noise is a constraint → avoid plyometrics and dropped weights; use controlled tempo and machines if available.
- Motivation drops at home → keep equipment visible but orderly and schedule sessions like appointments.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Buying before programming:** detect unused equipment → write the workouts first, then match tools.
- **F2 Unsafe anchoring:** detect bands attached to furniture or weak doors → use rated anchors and pull in safe directions.
- **F3 Unstable surface:** detect wobbling bench or sliding mats → replace or reposition before loading.
- **F4 Underloading legs:** detect upper-body progress but legs too easy → add unilateral work, heavier dumbbells, or gym access.

## Verification

The home gym supports a complete full-body session with clear floor space, stable equipment, safe storage, and no slipping, wobbling, or worn bands during loaded movements.

## Variations

- `easier`: start with a mat, long loop band, mini-band, jump rope if safe, and one adjustable dumbbell.
- `harder`: add a rack with safeties, barbell, plates, pulley, and cardio machine after confirming use.
- `equipment`: used dumbbells and plates are often good value if inspected for damage.
- `at-home`: prioritize compact gear that stores low and does not require permanent mounting.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk if equipment is stable and loads are controlled. Avoid attaching bands to weak furniture, lifting alone without safeties for heavy barbell work, or leaving weights where people can trip. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
