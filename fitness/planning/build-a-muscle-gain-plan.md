## Goal

You build a muscle-gain plan with enough training stimulus, food, protein, and recovery to gain lean mass without unnecessary fat gain.

## Preconditions

- Access to resistance training equipment or scalable bodyweight exercises.
- A workout log for loads, reps, and effort.
- Current body weight trend and a realistic schedule for 3-5 training days per week.

## Steps

1. **Choose a small calorie surplus.** Add about 150-300 calories above maintenance to start. → *Expect:* body weight rises slowly rather than jumping from excess calories.
2. **Set a protein target.** Use `fitness/planning/calculate-your-daily-protein-target` and spread protein across 3-5 meals. → *Expect:* muscle repair has consistent daily support.
3. **Select a repeatable training split.** Pick full-body, upper/lower, or push/pull/legs based on days available. → *Expect:* each major muscle is trained about 2 times per week.
4. **Program enough hard sets.** Start with roughly 8-12 challenging sets per muscle per week and add volume only if recovery is good. → *Expect:* sets are hard but technique stays controlled.
5. **Progress one variable at a time.** Add reps within a range before increasing load, and keep 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets. → *Expect:* performance improves without constant maximal grinding.
6. **Sleep and recover deliberately.** Aim for consistent sleep, rest days, and deloads when fatigue accumulates. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not keep adding volume if joints hurt or form breaks under fatigue. → *Expect:* soreness settles and performance trends upward.
7. **Review body-weight gain monthly.** A common target is about 0.25-0.5% body weight per week, slower for experienced lifters. → *Expect:* the surplus matches visible and performance changes.

## Decision points

- Weight is not rising for 3 weeks → add 100-200 calories per day.
- Waist increases quickly while lifts barely improve → reduce surplus or improve food quality.
- Soreness lasts into every session → reduce weekly sets or add rest.
- Technique worsens under heavier loads → repeat or reduce load until clean reps return.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Dirty bulk drift:** detect rapid fat gain and sluggish training → reduce calories and anchor meals around protein and whole foods.
- **F2 Program hopping:** detect changing exercises weekly without progression → keep key lifts stable for at least 6-8 weeks.
- **F3 Junk volume:** detect many easy sets with no close-to-failure effort → make fewer sets more focused.
- **F4 Recovery debt:** detect declining reps across multiple workouts → deload and improve sleep.

## Verification

Over 4-8 weeks, body weight trends up slowly, key lifts or reps improve with full range of motion, and each major muscle receives planned weekly hard sets without persistent joint pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: train full-body 3 days per week with 5-6 basic movement patterns.
- `harder`: use specialization blocks for lagging muscles after a base program is consistent.
- `equipment`: machines, barbells, dumbbells, cables, bands, and weighted bodyweight exercises all work if progressively overloaded.
- `at-home`: use adjustable dumbbells, bands, tempo, pauses, unilateral work, and higher reps.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when loads and volume progress gradually. Stop for sharp joint pain, numbness, dizziness, or form you cannot control. Supplements are optional and should not replace food or sleep. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
