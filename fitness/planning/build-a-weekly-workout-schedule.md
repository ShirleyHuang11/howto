## Goal

You build a weekly workout schedule that supports your goal, fits real life, and balances training stress with recovery.

## Preconditions

- A realistic goal from `fitness/planning/set-a-realistic-fitness-goal`.
- A calendar showing work, school, family, commute, sleep, and fixed commitments.
- Recovery guidance from `fitness/recovery/schedule-rest-days`.

## Steps

1. **Choose weekly training frequency.** Pick a number of sessions you can complete on a normal busy week, not an ideal week. → *Expect:* the target frequency feels repeatable.
2. **Place the highest-priority workouts first.** Schedule the sessions most tied to your goal when energy and time are best. → *Expect:* key workouts have protected calendar slots.
3. **Separate hard days.** Avoid placing heavy lower-body work, intervals, long runs, or intense sport on consecutive days when possible. → *Expect:* hard stressors have recovery space.
4. **Add easier support sessions.** Fill remaining slots with mobility, easy cardio, technique, or accessories that do not compromise key workouts. → *Expect:* support work helps rather than drains the week.
5. **Create a fallback version.** Decide the minimum plan for a disrupted week, such as two full-body sessions or three 20-minute walks. → *Expect:* missed time has a planned response.
6. **Review after one week.** Compare planned sessions, completed sessions, soreness, sleep, and performance. → *Expect:* the next week is adjusted based on evidence.

## Decision points

- You complete less than 80% of sessions → reduce frequency or session length.
- Soreness or fatigue carries into key workouts → add rest or move hard days apart.
- Schedule feels easy for 2-3 weeks → add a small amount of volume or intensity.
- Pain appears repeatedly in one activity → substitute a lower-irritation option and seek help if needed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Ideal-week planning:** detect sessions that only fit when life is perfect → schedule fewer, more reliable workouts.
- **F2 Hard days stacked together:** detect poor performance or joint aches → separate high-intensity and high-impact days.
- **F3 No fallback plan:** detect one missed session leading to a missed week → use the minimum viable schedule.
- **F4 Ignoring warmups and transitions:** detect workouts squeezed too tightly → calendar the warmup, cooldown, shower, and travel time.

## Verification

Your calendar has a full week of workouts with key sessions prioritized, at least one rest day, hard days separated when possible, and a fallback plan that preserves the habit during disruptions.

## Variations

- `easier`: schedule 2-3 full-body or walking sessions and one optional mobility day.
- `harder`: periodize the week with dedicated strength, conditioning, skill, and recovery days.
- `equipment`: use recurring calendar events or a training app with reminders.
- `at-home`: build sessions around bodyweight work, bands, stairs, walking routes, or household equipment.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as planning. A schedule should adapt to pain, illness, sleep loss, and life stress. Stop exercising for sharp pain, chest pain, fainting, fever, or worsening symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or qualified coach before starting if you have a relevant condition, recent injury, or uncertainty about safe exercise intensity.
