## Goal

You attach exercise to a repeatable cue, make the first action obvious, and grow the habit without depending on motivation.

## Preconditions

- A small exercise behavior you can do safely.
- A daily cue such as after coffee, lunch, work, or brushing teeth.
- Comfortable clothing and enough space for the chosen movement.

## Steps

1. **Choose a tiny starter behavior.** Pick something like 5 minutes walking, 5 bodyweight squats, or one mobility drill. → *Expect:* the habit is too small to feel intimidating.
2. **Attach it to a reliable cue.** Use "after I do X, I will do Y" with a cue that already happens most days. → *Expect:* the trigger is specific and repeatable.
3. **Make the start frictionless.** Place shoes, mat, bands, or clothes where the cue happens. → *Expect:* you can start without searching or deciding.
4. **Perform the habit with good mechanics.** Move in a pain-free range, breathe normally, and keep reps controlled. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if the movement causes sharp pain, dizziness, or joint instability. → *Expect:* the habit reinforces quality, not sloppy repetition.
5. **Celebrate completion with a simple mark.** Check a box, log the date, or place a tally immediately after. → *Expect:* your brain gets a clear "done" signal.
6. **Scale only after two easy weeks.** Add time, reps, resistance, or a second exercise once the tiny version is automatic. → *Expect:* the habit grows from consistency.
7. **Protect the identity of the habit.** On bad days, do the tiny version instead of skipping. → *Expect:* you remain a person who exercises even when the session is short.

## Decision points

- You forget the habit → choose a stronger cue or visible equipment.
- It feels too hard → shrink it further.
- It causes discomfort → change the movement.
- You do it easily for 2 weeks → add one small progression.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Habit too large:** detect repeated postponing → reduce to a 2-minute version.
- **F2 Weak cue:** detect forgetting until bedtime → attach it to a more reliable daily event.
- **F3 Reward depends on results:** detect discouragement from slow progress → reward completion, not body changes.
- **F4 Poor movement quality:** detect rushed reps or breath-holding → slow down and use easier variations.

## Verification

You complete the chosen exercise habit at least 10 times in 14 days, always after the same cue, with pain-free movement and a visible completion mark.

## Variations

- `easier`: one stretch, one walk around the block, or one set of sit-to-stands.
- `harder`: stack a second habit after the first becomes automatic.
- `equipment`: use no-equipment movements until the habit is stable.
- `at-home`: pair the habit with a household cue such as starting coffee or ending work.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Even tiny habits should be pain-free and controlled. Do not use habit streaks to train through illness, injury, chest pain, faintness, or severe fatigue. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
