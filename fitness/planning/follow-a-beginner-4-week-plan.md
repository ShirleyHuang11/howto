## Goal

You complete a 4-week beginner training plan that builds consistency, basic strength, aerobic capacity, and mobility without overwhelming recovery.

## Preconditions

- Medical clearance if you have heart, metabolic, orthopedic, or other relevant health concerns.
- Shoes for walking and access to simple resistance exercises.
- A calendar and workout log.

## Steps

1. **Schedule three strength days and two walking days.** Put them on the calendar before the week starts, with rest or easy mobility between harder days. → *Expect:* the plan fits your actual week.
2. **Warm up before every session.** Spend 5-8 minutes on easy cardio and dynamic joint movement before strength work. → *Expect:* breathing is slightly elevated and joints feel ready.
3. **Use a full-body strength template.** Do 2-3 sets each of squat or sit-to-stand, hip hinge, push, pull, carry or core, using easy variations first. → *Expect:* all major movement patterns are practiced.
4. **Keep reps clean.** Stop each set with 2-4 reps in reserve and no joint pain. ⚠️ *Safety:* end the set if your back rounds, knees collapse inward, shoulders pinch, or balance fails. → *Expect:* every rep looks controlled from start to finish.
5. **Walk at conversational pace.** Start with 15-25 minutes and add 5 minutes per week if recovery is good. → *Expect:* you can speak in short sentences without gasping.
6. **Progress weekly, not daily.** Add one set, a few reps, a small load, or a longer walk only after the prior week felt manageable. → *Expect:* effort rises gradually.
7. **Review at the end of week 4.** Compare completed sessions, soreness, energy, and basic performance. → *Expect:* you know whether to repeat, progress, or choose a new split.

## Decision points

- You miss more than two sessions in a week → reduce to two strength days and two walks.
- Soreness lasts more than 48-72 hours → reduce sets or range of motion.
- Walking feels too easy → increase duration before intensity.
- Exercises cause pain → switch to a pain-free regression.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Starting too hard:** detect severe soreness after week 1 → cut sets in half and rebuild.
- **F2 Skipping warmups:** detect stiff, awkward first sets → make warmup part of the session, not optional.
- **F3 Poor squat control:** detect knees caving or heels lifting → use a box squat and slower tempo.
- **F4 No logging:** detect uncertainty about progress → record exercises, sets, reps, and walk duration.

## Verification

Across 28 days, you complete at least 10 strength sessions and 6 walking sessions, with controlled reps, no sharp pain, and improved confidence in the basic movement patterns.

## Variations

- `easier`: start with two strength days and three short walks.
- `harder`: add a fourth full-body day only if recovery is consistently good.
- `equipment`: bodyweight, bands, dumbbells, machines, or kettlebells can all fit the template.
- `at-home`: use chair squats, wall or incline push-ups, band rows, glute bridges, suitcase carries, and planks.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when intensity is modest. Stop for chest pain, faintness, sharp joint pain, numbness, or unusual shortness of breath. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
