## Goal

You turn a vague fitness wish into a specific, measurable goal that matches your current baseline, schedule, and recovery capacity.

## Preconditions

- A notebook, calendar, or fitness app.
- Honest baseline information: current workouts, step count, strength numbers, pace, mobility, or body measurements if relevant.
- No medical restriction that makes the goal unsafe without professional guidance.

## Steps

1. **Name the outcome.** Choose one primary goal, such as walking 30 minutes, deadlifting a target weight, running 5k, reducing pain during daily tasks, or training 3 days per week. → *Expect:* the goal is written in one sentence.
2. **Measure the baseline.** Record what you can do now with clean form and normal recovery. → *Expect:* the starting point is objective, not guessed.
3. **Set a time-bound target.** Use a realistic window such as 28d, 56d, or 90d depending on the goal size. → *Expect:* the deadline is challenging but plausible.
4. **Define process actions.** List weekly behaviors that drive the outcome, such as sessions, steps, sleep, protein, or mobility work. → *Expect:* the plan includes controllable actions, not only the result.
5. **Add a review rule.** Decide when to progress, hold, or regress based on pain, adherence, and performance. → *Expect:* the goal can adapt without being abandoned.

## Decision points

- Goal requires daily perfection → reduce it to a sustainable minimum.
- Baseline is unknown → test gently before setting the target.
- Pain or medical condition affects the goal → get professional input.
- You miss two planned weeks → revise the goal instead of trying to cram.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vague goal:** detect "get fit" with no metric → choose one measurable outcome and one weekly behavior.
- **F2 Unrealistic timeline:** detect a target based on wishful thinking → extend the date or reduce the magnitude.
- **F3 Too many goals:** detect competing strength, endurance, fat loss, and skill priorities → pick one primary goal and one secondary habit.
- **F4 No recovery plan:** detect progress stalling despite effort → include sleep, rest days, and fueling.

## Verification

You have one written goal with a baseline metric, target metric, deadline, weekly process actions, and a review rule for progressing or adjusting safely.

## Variations

- `easier`: set a 14d consistency goal, such as two workouts per week.
- `harder`: set performance milestones every 14-28d and review trend data.
- `equipment`: use a notes app, spreadsheet, or training app to track baseline and progress.
- `at-home`: choose goals based on bodyweight exercises, walking, stairs, or mobility.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as planning. Avoid goals that reward pain, dehydration, extreme restriction, or training through illness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant medical condition, recent injury, pregnancy-related concern, eating-disorder history, or symptoms during exercise.
