## Goal

You choose a weekly workout split that matches your schedule, recovery, experience, and training goal.

## Preconditions

- Honest count of training days you can repeat most weeks.
- A main goal such as strength, muscle gain, general fitness, fat loss, or sport support.
- Access to exercises that train all major movement patterns.

## Steps

1. **Start with available days.** Count only days you can train consistently, not your ideal week. → *Expect:* the split fits real life.
2. **Match frequency to experience.** Beginners usually do well with 2-3 full-body days; intermediate trainees may use upper/lower or push/pull/legs. → *Expect:* each muscle or movement gets practiced often enough.
3. **Place hard days away from conflicts.** Avoid heavy legs before a sport match or long run unless that is intentional. → *Expect:* important sessions are not ruined by poor ordering.
4. **Balance movement patterns.** Include squat or lunge, hinge, push, pull, core, and conditioning across the week. → *Expect:* the program does not overtrain one pattern while neglecting another.
5. **Set recovery spacing.** Leave about 48 hours before hard training of the same muscle group when possible. → *Expect:* soreness does not block quality reps.
6. **Test the split for 2 weeks.** Log performance, soreness, missed sessions, and energy. ⚠️ *Safety:* reduce volume if fatigue changes technique or joint pain appears. → *Expect:* the split is sustainable, not just attractive on paper.

## Decision points

- You have 2-3 days → choose full-body.
- You have 4 days → choose upper/lower or full-body with varied emphasis.
- You have 5-6 days → choose push/pull/legs, upper/lower plus accessories, or sport-specific split.
- You miss sessions often → reduce days before adding volume.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Split too advanced:** detect missed workouts and lingering soreness → use fewer days with full-body sessions.
- **F2 Imbalanced push and pull:** detect shoulder irritation or posture fatigue → add rows, pulldowns, and rear-delt work.
- **F3 Leg day avoidance:** detect skipped lower-body training → spread leg work across full-body days.
- **F4 No progression:** detect random exercises every session → keep main lifts stable long enough to improve.

## Verification

You complete 2 consecutive weeks of the chosen split, train all major movement patterns, recover before repeating hard muscle work, and miss no more than one planned session.

## Variations

- `easier`: 3 full-body sessions on nonconsecutive days.
- `harder`: 5-day split with planned volume targets and deloads.
- `equipment`: machine-based splits work well when equipment is consistent; home splits need more variation planning.
- `at-home`: use full-body or upper/lower because equipment changes are easier to manage.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. A split is only useful if it lets you recover and keep good technique. Stop exercises that cause sharp pain, and do not add training days to compensate for poor sleep or injury. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
