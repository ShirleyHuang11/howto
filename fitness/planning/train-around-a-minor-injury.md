## Goal

You modify training around a minor ache or strain while preserving fitness and avoiding the common mistake of turning a small problem into a larger one.

## Preconditions

- The issue is minor, improving or stable, and not associated with trauma, deformity, numbness, severe swelling, fever, or unexplained symptoms.
- Access to pain-free exercise alternatives.
- Willingness to stop or seek care if symptoms worsen.

## Steps

1. **Screen for red flags first.** Check for severe pain, inability to bear weight, major swelling, numbness, weakness, chest pain, or symptoms after a fall. ⚠️ *Safety:* seek medical care instead of training if any red flag is present. → *Expect:* only minor, low-concern symptoms are handled with training modification.
2. **Identify painful movements.** Test range of motion, load, speed, and positions gently to find what triggers symptoms. → *Expect:* you know which movements to avoid or regress.
3. **Keep pain during training low.** Use a 0-10 scale and stay around 0-2/10 discomfort that does not worsen during or after the session. → *Expect:* symptoms remain stable or improve by the next day.
4. **Train unaffected areas normally.** Continue pain-free upper body, lower body, core, or cardio work as appropriate. → *Expect:* overall fitness is maintained.
5. **Regress the irritated pattern.** Reduce load, range, speed, impact, or complexity; use machines, bands, tempo, or isometrics if tolerated. → *Expect:* the movement feels controlled and does not provoke sharp pain.
6. **Rebuild gradually.** Add range, reps, load, or impact one step at a time after symptoms settle for several sessions. → *Expect:* capacity returns without flare-ups.
7. **Log symptoms and response.** Record exercise, pain during, pain after, and next-day status. → *Expect:* progression decisions are based on patterns.

## Decision points

- Pain worsens during the session → stop that exercise and switch to a pain-free option.
- Pain is worse the next day → reduce the previous session's load, range, or volume.
- Symptoms do not improve over 1-2 weeks → see a qualified clinician.
- You cannot find a pain-free variation → avoid that pattern temporarily and train other qualities.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Testing too hard:** detect repeated flare-ups after "checking" the injury → stop provocative testing and use graded exposure.
- **F2 Ignoring next-day pain:** detect symptoms that spike after training → use next-day response as the progression limit.
- **F3 Total rest too long:** detect deconditioning while the injury is minor → train unaffected areas and pain-free patterns.
- **F4 Compensation patterns:** detect limping, twisting, or shifting under load → reduce load or choose a more stable variation.

## Verification

You complete a modified session that trains unaffected areas and pain-free variations, keeps symptoms at 0-2/10, causes no next-day worsening, and includes a written progression or referral decision.

## Variations

- `easier`: replace the irritated movement entirely for 1 week while keeping gentle pain-free activity.
- `harder`: use clinician-guided return-to-load progressions for sport or heavy lifting.
- `equipment`: machines and cables can reduce balance demands while symptoms settle.
- `at-home`: use isometrics, bands, shorter ranges, and walking if tolerated.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because misjudging an injury can worsen it. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, numbness, weakness, instability, fever, chest pain, or symptoms that worsen after training. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor, physical therapist, or other qualified professional for persistent, severe, or unclear symptoms.
