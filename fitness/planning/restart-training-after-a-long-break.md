## Goal

You restart exercise after a long break with enough restraint to rebuild capacity, avoid excessive soreness, and make the second week easier than the first.

## Preconditions

- Medical clearance if the break involved illness, surgery, pregnancy, cardiac symptoms, or a significant injury.
- Previous training history written down if available.
- A conservative plan for walking, mobility, and resistance training.

## Steps

1. **Reset expectations.** Start at about 50-70% of what you think you can do, especially for loads, intervals, and total sets. → *Expect:* the first sessions feel almost too easy.
2. **Choose simple full-body sessions.** Use 4-6 familiar movements with controlled tempo and no maximal attempts. → *Expect:* movement patterns return without heavy fatigue.
3. **Warm up longer than before.** Spend 8-12 minutes on easy cardio, joint motion, and rehearsal sets. → *Expect:* joints feel smoother before working sets.
4. **Leave reps in reserve.** Stop each set with 3-5 good reps left and stable breathing. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for sharp pain, chest pain, dizziness, or form breakdown. → *Expect:* reps are clean and repeatable.
5. **Limit first-week volume.** Do 1-2 fewer sets per exercise than your old program and avoid hard finishers. → *Expect:* soreness is mild to moderate, not disabling.
6. **Use next-day feedback.** Progress only if soreness, energy, sleep, and joint comfort are acceptable the following day. → *Expect:* the plan adapts to current capacity.
7. **Add gradually in week 2.** Increase one variable, such as one set, small load, or longer walk, not everything at once. → *Expect:* training momentum builds without a crash.

## Decision points

- Severe soreness lasts more than 72 hours → reduce volume and intensity.
- Cardio feels unusually hard → use shorter easy intervals and longer recovery.
- Old injury symptoms return → regress or seek professional guidance.
- Week 1 feels easy and next-day recovery is good → progress slightly in week 2.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Old numbers trap:** detect trying to match previous personal records → base loads on current clean reps.
- **F2 DOMS overload:** detect stairs or daily tasks becoming painful → cut sets and avoid eccentric-heavy work temporarily.
- **F3 Skipped warmup:** detect stiff first reps or awkward bracing → add ramp-up sets and easy cardio.
- **F4 Too many sessions:** detect fatigue accumulating by day 4-5 → insert rest days and walking only.

## Verification

Over 14 days, you complete 3-5 conservative sessions with clean technique, no sharp pain, soreness that resolves between sessions, and one small planned progression based on next-day recovery.

## Variations

- `easier`: begin with walking and mobility for 1 week before resistance training.
- `harder`: experienced lifters can rebuild with low-volume technique work on familiar lifts.
- `equipment`: machines and dumbbells are useful because loads can be adjusted precisely.
- `at-home`: use bodyweight, bands, and slow tempo before adding external load.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk when returning after illness, injury, or long inactivity because previous capacity may not reflect current tolerance. Stop for chest pain, fainting, sharp pain, neurological symptoms, or unusual breathlessness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before restarting if you have a relevant condition.
