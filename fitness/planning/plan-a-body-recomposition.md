## Goal

You plan a body recomposition phase that aims to gain or retain muscle while gradually reducing fat, using training performance and measurements as the main feedback.

## Preconditions

- Willingness to progress strength training consistently for at least 8-12 weeks.
- Protein target from `fitness/planning/calculate-your-daily-protein-target`.
- Baseline photos, measurements, body-weight trend, and workout log.

## Steps

1. **Confirm recomposition is the right goal.** Choose it if you are a beginner, returning after time off, detrained, or carrying some fat while strength training seriously. → *Expect:* the goal matches a realistic physiology window.
2. **Set calories near maintenance.** Eat at maintenance or a small deficit of about 100-250 calories if fat loss is the priority. → *Expect:* energy is high enough to train hard.
3. **Prioritize progressive resistance training.** Train each major muscle 2 times per week with stable exercises and hard sets near technical failure. → *Expect:* strength or reps gradually improve.
4. **Hit protein daily.** Spread high-quality protein across meals and pair it with enough carbs to fuel training. → *Expect:* recovery supports muscle gain or retention.
5. **Keep cardio supportive.** Use walking or moderate cardio for health, but avoid so much high-intensity work that lifting performance drops. → *Expect:* conditioning improves without stealing recovery.
6. **Measure monthly, not daily.** Use waist, photos, gym performance, and weight averages. → *Expect:* a stable scale can still come with better body composition.
7. **Decide after 8-12 weeks.** Continue if strength is up and waist/photos improve; switch to a cut or gain phase if signals conflict. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop testing maxes or hard intervals if fatigue changes your lifting mechanics. → *Expect:* the next phase is chosen from evidence.

## Decision points

- Strength up and waist down → continue recomposition.
- Strength flat, weight flat, waist flat → improve training quality or choose a clearer calorie direction.
- Weight falling fast and lifts dropping → eat more.
- Waist rising quickly with no strength gain → reduce calories slightly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Expecting fast scale changes:** detect frustration despite better photos → judge recomposition by multiple metrics.
- **F2 Maintenance miscalculation:** detect steady weight gain or loss outside the plan → adjust calories by 100-200.
- **F3 Training too easy:** detect no rep or load progress → bring sets closer to technical failure.
- **F4 Cardio overload:** detect heavy legs and declining lifts → reduce high-intensity cardio first.

## Verification

After 8-12 weeks, at least two key lifts or rep targets improved, waist or photos improved, and body weight stayed within a controlled range or moved slowly toward the intended direction.

## Variations

- `easier`: use full-body strength training 3 days per week and daily walking.
- `harder`: use alternating 4-6 week mini-surplus and mini-deficit blocks.
- `equipment`: machines and dumbbells are often easier to progress precisely than improvised loads.
- `at-home`: track harder variations, slower tempo, pauses, and unilateral reps.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Recomposition requires patience; avoid extreme deficits, maximal lifting without skill, or ignoring pain. Photos, weight, and nutrition logs are sensitive health data. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
