## Goal

You organize training into planned phases so volume, intensity, skill practice, and recovery support progress instead of accumulating random fatigue.

## Preconditions

- At least 4 weeks of workout logs.
- A primary goal such as strength, hypertrophy, endurance, sport performance, or general fitness.
- Current weekly schedule and known constraints.

## Steps

1. **Choose the training outcome.** Define one priority for the next block, such as adding muscle, improving a lift, building aerobic base, or preparing for an event. → *Expect:* the plan has a clear performance target.
2. **Set a block length.** Use 4-8 weeks for most general training blocks, with shorter blocks if fatigue or schedule is unpredictable. → *Expect:* the time frame is long enough to adapt and short enough to review.
3. **Assign volume and intensity themes.** Start with moderate volume and technique quality, build workload gradually, then reduce fatigue before testing or changing goals. → *Expect:* hard weeks and easier weeks have a reason.
4. **Progress conservatively.** Increase weekly sets, load, reps, distance, or pace by small amounts while keeping form and recovery stable. → *Expect:* performance rises without sudden spikes.
5. **Schedule a deload or easier week.** Reduce volume by about 30-50% or keep movement easy when soreness, motivation, and performance decline together. → *Expect:* fatigue drops while movement quality returns.
6. **Track leading indicators.** Watch reps in reserve, bar speed, pace at a given effort, sleep, soreness, and mood. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not push intensity when technique degrades or pain changes movement. → *Expect:* adjustments happen before a setback.
7. **Review and repeat.** At the end of the block, compare the goal metric and decide whether to build, maintain, deload, or change focus. → *Expect:* the next block follows from evidence.

## Decision points

- Performance improves and recovery is good → continue or progress slightly.
- Performance drops for more than a week → deload or reduce volume.
- Pain appears in a repeated pattern → change exercise selection and reduce load.
- Event date is fixed → plan backward from the date with taper or recovery time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Random overload:** detect adding load, sets, and cardio all at once → change only one stressor at a time.
- **F2 No easy weeks:** detect motivation and performance sliding together → schedule a deload before testing.
- **F3 Goal conflict:** detect trying to peak strength while aggressively dieting and increasing endurance → prioritize one outcome.
- **F4 Ignoring logs:** detect decisions based on memory → use actual session data before changing the block.

## Verification

You have a written 4-8 week block with one priority goal, weekly volume or intensity targets, progression rules, an easier week or deload trigger, and a review metric that can be checked from the workout log.

## Variations

- `easier`: use 3 weeks gradually harder plus 1 easier week.
- `harder`: use separate accumulation, intensification, peak, and deload phases.
- `equipment`: machine, dumbbell, barbell, or cardio plans can all be periodized by volume and intensity.
- `at-home`: progress reps, tempo, range of motion, unilateral work, or density when load options are limited.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when progression is gradual. Periodization does not justify training through sharp pain, illness, dizziness, or technique breakdown. Training logs can reveal health patterns, so share them selectively. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
