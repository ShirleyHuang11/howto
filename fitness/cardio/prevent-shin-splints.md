## Goal

Reduce the risk of shin pain from running, walking, jumping, or sudden increases in training load.

## Preconditions

- Shoes appropriate for your activity and surface.
- A current training log or rough estimate of recent weekly volume.
- Pain-free walking before returning to impact training.

## Steps

1. **Check current symptoms.** Press along the shin and walk briskly to see whether pain is present before training. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not run through focal bone pain, swelling, limping, or pain at rest. → *Expect:* mild tightness may be monitored, but sharp or localized pain changes the plan.
2. **Warm up lower legs.** Walk 5 minutes, then do ankle circles, calf raises, toe raises, and gentle skips if pain-free. → *Expect:* ankles and calves feel springy, not stiff.
3. **Limit volume jumps.** Increase weekly running or impact volume gradually and avoid adding speed, hills, and distance in the same week. → *Expect:* training load changes are small enough that soreness settles within 24-48 hours.
4. **Use quiet foot strikes.** Keep cadence comfortable, avoid long braking strides, and land with the foot under the body. → *Expect:* impact sounds quieter and shins feel less jarred.
5. **Strengthen calves and tibialis.** Do controlled calf raises and toe raises 2-3 times per week. → *Expect:* lower-leg muscles fatigue evenly without shin pain.
6. **Recover between impact days.** Alternate hard or long impact sessions with easy days, cross-training, or rest. → *Expect:* shins feel normal at the start of the next session.

## Decision points

- Pain warms up and disappears → reduce intensity and monitor closely.
- Pain worsens during the session → stop impact work and switch to low-impact cardio.
- Pain is focal on the bone → seek evaluation before continuing.
- Shoes are worn or uncomfortable → replace or refit before increasing volume.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much too soon:** detect shin soreness after sudden mileage or hill increases → cut volume and rebuild gradually.
- **F2 Overstriding:** detect loud impacts and foot landing far ahead → shorten stride and keep cadence quick.
- **F3 Weak lower legs:** detect calf fatigue or foot slap → add calf raises, toe raises, and foot strength work.
- **F4 Ignoring warning pain:** detect limping or pain at rest → stop impact and get assessed.

## Verification

You complete two weeks of planned cardio with no increasing shin pain, no limping, quiet foot strikes, and lower-leg strength work completed 2-3 times per week.

## Variations

- Easier: replace one impact day with cycling, swimming, rowing, or elliptical.
- Harder: add hills or speed only after several pain-free weeks.
- Equipment: cushioned shoes, track, treadmill, resistance band, or slant board.
- At-home: toe raises against a wall and slow calf raises on a step.

## Safety & privacy

Persistent shin pain can indicate bone stress injury, especially if focal, worsening, or present at rest. Stop impact exercise and seek professional evaluation for those signs. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
