## Goal

Use heart-rate zones to control cardio intensity and separate easy aerobic work from harder training.

## Preconditions

- Heart-rate monitor, watch, bike computer, treadmill sensor, or manual pulse check.
- A known or estimated maximum heart rate, resting heart rate, or tested threshold if available.
- A safe cardio mode and a warmup completed before hard efforts.

## Steps

1. **Choose a zone method.** [BRANCH: beginner, use simple percent of max heart rate | experienced, use tested lactate threshold or heart-rate reserve] → *Expect:* your zones are based on one consistent method.
2. **Set easy aerobic target.** Aim most base training around zone 2, where breathing is steady and speech is possible. → *Expect:* heart rate stabilizes and effort feels sustainable.
3. **Warm up before judging the numbers.** Move easily for 10-15 minutes because heart rate lags behind effort. → *Expect:* heart rate rises gradually instead of spiking.
4. **Hold the planned zone.** Adjust pace, resistance, or incline every few minutes rather than chasing every second-by-second fluctuation. → *Expect:* the average heart rate stays inside the intended zone.
5. **Account for drift.** In heat, fatigue, dehydration, or long sessions, slow down if heart rate climbs at the same pace. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for chest pain, faintness, confusion, or unusual breathlessness even if the watch looks normal. → *Expect:* effort and heart rate both make sense together.
6. **Review after the workout.** Compare average heart rate, perceived effort, pace, and recovery. → *Expect:* the data helps set the next session rather than becoming the goal itself.

## Decision points

- Heart rate is unusually high for easy effort → slow down, hydrate, or take a recovery day.
- Heart rate will not rise despite hard effort → consider fatigue, poor sensor contact, or illness.
- Zones feel unrealistic → retest or use perceived effort until you have better data.
- Training goal is endurance base → keep most sessions easy, not threshold-hard.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Chasing lagging heart rate:** detect starting too fast while waiting for the number to rise → pace by feel for the first 10 minutes.
- **F2 Bad sensor reading:** detect sudden impossible spikes or drops → tighten strap, wet electrodes, or use a chest strap.
- **F3 Easy days too hard:** detect zone 3 creeping into every session → slow down or use flatter terrain.
- **F4 Ignoring symptoms:** detect dizziness, chest pain, or severe breathlessness → stop and seek medical advice as appropriate.

## Verification

You completed a planned cardio session with warmup, at least 20 minutes mostly in the target zone, matching perceived effort, and no concerning symptoms.

## Variations

- Easier: use talk test only: full sentences for easy, short phrases for moderate.
- Harder: add structured intervals in higher zones after a base period.
- Equipment: chest strap, optical watch, bike computer, treadmill hand sensors, or manual pulse.
- At-home: use a stationary bike, step platform, or treadmill with the same zone targets.

## Safety & privacy

Heart-rate data can be useful but is not a diagnosis. Stop for chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, or unusual symptoms. Fitness apps may store location and health data; review privacy settings. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor if you have a relevant condition.
