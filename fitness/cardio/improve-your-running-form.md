## Goal

You make your running more efficient and comfortable by improving posture, stride placement, cadence awareness, and relaxed arm action.

## Preconditions

- A flat, safe 50-100 meter stretch, track, or treadmill.
- Shoes you already tolerate well.
- A phone or mirror is optional for video feedback.
- Warm up with 5-10 minutes of easy jogging or brisk walking.

## Steps

1. **Stand tall before you move.** Stack ears, shoulders, ribs, hips, and ankles, then lean slightly from the ankles rather than bending at the waist. → *Expect:* you feel tall with the chest open and eyes forward.
2. **Run with feet landing under you.** Shorten the stride until each foot contacts close to beneath the hips. → *Expect:* braking force feels lower and footfalls become quieter.
3. **Let cadence rise naturally.** Aim for quick, light steps without forcing a specific number. → *Expect:* hips stay level and you do not reach forward with the lower leg.
4. **Relax the upper body.** Keep elbows bent around 80-100 degrees, hands unclenched, and arms swinging forward-back without crossing the midline. → *Expect:* shoulders stay low and breathing feels less restricted.
5. **Use short form strides.** Run 4-6 relaxed accelerations of 15-20 seconds with full easy recovery. → *Expect:* speed increases while posture remains tall and smooth.
6. **Check video from the side.** Record a few seconds at easy pace and look for overstriding, waist bending, or excessive bouncing. → *Expect:* the foot lands near the body and the head travels mostly forward.
7. **Apply one cue at a time.** Choose the biggest fault and practice it for 5 minutes during easy runs. → *Expect:* the cue improves comfort without making you stiff.

## Decision points

- You feel rigid while using form cues → reduce focus and return to relaxed easy running.
- You see the foot landing far ahead of the knee → shorten stride and slightly quicken turnover.
- Pain appears after changing form → return to the prior comfortable stride and change more gradually.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overstriding:** detect the heel landing far ahead with loud braking → shorten the step and think quiet feet.
- **F2 Waist bend:** detect leaning by folding at the hips → stand tall and lean subtly from the ankles.
- **F3 Arm crossover:** detect hands crossing the zipper line → drive elbows back and keep hands relaxed.
- **F4 Forced cadence:** detect choppy, tense steps → let cadence increase only enough to reduce reaching.

## Verification

During a 10-minute easy run, your posture stays tall, arms swing forward-back, feet land close under the hips, footfalls are quiet, and breathing remains controlled.

## Variations

- `easier`: practice form cues during brisk walking before jogging.
- `harder`: add relaxed strides after easy runs while preserving the same mechanics.
- `equipment`: use slow-motion phone video from the side and rear for feedback.
- `at-home`: practice posture, arm swing, and marching drills in place before outdoor running.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk, but sudden large form changes can irritate calves, Achilles tendons, shins, or hips. Stop if pain is sharp or worsens as you run. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or qualified clinician if you have a relevant condition or persistent pain.
