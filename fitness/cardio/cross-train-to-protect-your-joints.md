## Goal

Use cross-training to maintain cardio fitness while reducing repeated impact on the same joints and tissues.

## Preconditions

- Access to a low-impact option such as cycling, elliptical, rowing, swimming, walking, or hiking.
- Current main-sport schedule and any pain points identified.
- Equipment adjusted to fit your body.

## Steps

1. **Identify the stress you need to reduce.** Note whether running, jumping, hills, or volume is irritating feet, shins, knees, hips, or back. → *Expect:* you know what cross-training is replacing.
2. **Choose a compatible mode.** [BRANCH: lower-leg impact pain, use cycling, elliptical, or swimming | back-sensitive, avoid rowing if it aggravates symptoms] → *Expect:* the option raises heart rate without reproducing pain.
3. **Match effort, not mechanics.** Use time and perceived effort to replace an easy or moderate cardio session. → *Expect:* breathing matches the intended workout while joints feel better.
4. **Set up equipment correctly.** Adjust saddle, straps, handles, or resistance so motion is smooth and symmetrical. → *Expect:* knees, hips, and shoulders track comfortably.
5. **Keep technique controlled.** Move through a repeatable range without bouncing, twisting, or gripping too hard. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if cross-training causes sharp pain, numbness, dizziness, or altered movement. → *Expect:* muscles work, but irritated joints do not flare.
6. **Blend it into the week.** Replace one or two impact sessions first, especially after hard days. → *Expect:* weekly cardio continues while soreness and joint irritation decrease.

## Decision points

- Pain disappears with cross-training → keep it while gradually rebuilding impact.
- Pain appears in the new mode → change setup, reduce intensity, or choose another mode.
- Fitness goal is running-specific → keep some easy running if pain-free.
- Recovery worsens → reduce total workload, not just impact.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Treating cross-training as extra:** detect adding it on top of full workload → replace a session instead.
- **F2 Poor bike fit:** detect knee pain or rocking hips → adjust saddle height and fore-aft position.
- **F3 Rowing with rounded back:** detect low-back strain → hinge from hips, brace, or choose another mode.
- **F4 Going too hard:** detect heavy fatigue after a supposed easy day → lower resistance and use conversational effort.

## Verification

You replaced at least one impact cardio session with 25-45 minutes of low-impact work at the planned effort, with smooth mechanics and no increase in joint pain.

## Variations

- Easier: easy cycling, walking, or elliptical at conversational effort.
- Harder: structured intervals on a bike or elliptical after pain is controlled.
- Equipment: stationary bike, outdoor bike, elliptical, rower, pool, ski erg, or hiking poles.
- At-home: stationary bike, step-free marching, or low-impact video workout.

## Safety & privacy

Low-impact does not mean no-risk. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, chest pain, dizziness, or symptoms that worsen during exercise. Pools and fitness apps may involve location, membership, or health data; review privacy settings. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
