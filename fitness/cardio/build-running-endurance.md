## Goal

You increase how long and how often you can run by progressing volume gradually while keeping most training easy.

## Preconditions

- You can walk briskly for 30 minutes or already run short distances.
- Safe routes, treadmill access, or a measured loop.
- Shoes that do not cause pain or blisters.
- Willingness to repeat easy weeks instead of forcing mileage.

## Steps

1. **Establish your current baseline.** Record how many minutes you can run or run-walk comfortably three times in one week. → *Expect:* the starting point feels repeatable, not maximal.
2. **Run mostly easy.** Keep effort conversational for most sessions. → *Expect:* you can finish runs without gasping or needing a long recovery.
3. **Add time before speed.** Increase total weekly running time by a small amount, often 5-10 minutes, when the prior week felt good. → *Expect:* volume rises without new pain.
4. **Use one longer run.** Make one weekly session slightly longer than the others while keeping it easy. → *Expect:* endurance improves without racing the long run.
5. **Keep recovery days real.** Alternate runs with rest, walking, cycling, mobility, or strength that does not make the next run worse. → *Expect:* legs feel ready enough to keep form.
6. **Repeat or reduce when needed.** [BRANCH: fatigue or soreness rising, repeat the week or cut volume | feeling strong, add a small amount next week] → *Expect:* training adapts instead of accumulating strain.
7. **Check form under fatigue.** Shorten stride, keep posture tall, and stop if pain changes your gait. → *Expect:* late-run mechanics remain close to early-run mechanics.

## Decision points

- You are sore for more than 48 hours → reduce the next week.
- You can finish every run easily for two weeks → add time to one or two runs.
- You want to get faster → first build consistent endurance, then add small quality work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mileage spike:** detect new shin, knee, or foot pain → return to the last comfortable week.
- **F2 Easy pace too hard:** detect inability to talk → slow down or add walk breaks.
- **F3 No consistency:** detect random hard runs followed by long gaps → schedule smaller repeatable sessions.
- **F4 Ignoring sleep and food:** detect poor recovery and heavy legs → prioritize recovery before adding volume.

## Verification

After several weeks, you complete 3-4 weekly runs or run-walk sessions with increased total time, conversational effort on easy days, stable form, and no sharp or worsening pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: start with run-walk intervals and progress running minutes gradually.
- `harder`: add a fourth weekly run only after three runs feel routine.
- `equipment`: use a watch or phone timer to track minutes rather than pace.
- `at-home`: use treadmill running or incline walking when outdoor running is not practical.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when progression is gradual. Stop for chest pain, faintness, sharp pain, swelling, or gait changes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have relevant medical conditions.
