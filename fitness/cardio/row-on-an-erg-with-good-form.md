## Goal

You row on an indoor erg with an efficient leg-body-arm sequence that trains cardio without overloading the lower back.

## Preconditions

- A rowing erg with foot straps, handle, seat, and damper working properly.
- Shoes secured in the footplates with straps over the widest part of the foot.
- Enough space behind the rail.
- Warm up with easy rowing and hip hinges before harder strokes.

## Steps

1. **Set the foot straps and damper.** Strap feet securely and choose a moderate damper, often around 3-5 on a Concept2-style machine. → *Expect:* the handle feels connected without yanking your arms.
2. **Find the catch position.** Sit tall with shins near vertical, arms straight, shoulders in front of hips, and spine neutral. → *Expect:* you feel compressed at hips and knees without rounding the low back.
3. **Drive with the legs first.** Push the floor away while arms stay straight and torso angle stays mostly fixed. → *Expect:* the seat and handle move together at the start.
4. **Swing the body, then pull the arms.** After legs extend, hinge the torso slightly back and draw the handle to the lower ribs. → *Expect:* power flows legs, body, arms in that order.
5. **Recover in reverse.** Send arms away, hinge body forward, then bend knees and slide to the catch. → *Expect:* the handle clears the knees before the knees rise.
6. **Keep the stroke rate controlled.** Row easy at 18-24 strokes per minute with a strong drive and patient recovery. → *Expect:* the recovery takes longer than the drive.
7. **Stop if the back rounds under fatigue.** ⚠️ *Safety:* end the interval or reduce intensity if you cannot keep a neutral spine. → *Expect:* fatigue stays in legs and lungs, not sharp low-back pain.

## Decision points

- Handle hits your knees → arms and body are returning too late; sequence arms-body-legs on recovery.
- Low back feels strained → lower intensity, shorten the slide, and reset posture.
- You cannot sustain rhythm → reduce stroke rate and focus on smooth sequencing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Early arm pull:** detect bent elbows before leg drive finishes → keep arms long until legs are nearly down.
- **F2 Rushed recovery:** detect sliding forward too fast → count one beat drive, two beats recovery.
- **F3 Rounded catch:** detect pelvis tucked and back curved → sit tall and limit reach.
- **F4 Damper too high:** detect heavy, jerky strokes → lower damper and row smoothly.

## Verification

You row 10-20 minutes or planned intervals with legs-body-arms drive, arms-body-legs recovery, neutral spine, controlled stroke rate, and no sharp back, rib, or joint pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: row 5-minute easy blocks with full rest and low stroke rate.
- `harder`: add intervals such as 6 x 2 minutes while preserving sequence.
- `equipment`: adjust footplate height so straps sit across the widest part of the foot.
- `at-home`: use any indoor rower, but keep resistance moderate and movement smooth.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk with moderate resistance and good posture, but poor form can irritate the back, ribs, hips, or knees. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, or inability to maintain posture. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or clinician if you have a relevant condition.
