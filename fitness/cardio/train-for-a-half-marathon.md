## Goal

You prepare to complete 21.1 km with enough aerobic capacity, musculoskeletal tolerance, pacing discipline, and fueling practice.

## Preconditions

- You can run 10 km continuously and tolerate 3-4 runs per week.
- Shoes, socks, and clothing tested on long runs before event day.
- Safe routes for runs lasting 75-150 minutes.
- Warm up before harder workouts and start long runs very easy.

## Steps

1. **Plan 10-12 weeks of gradual loading.** Schedule easy runs, one moderate workout, one long run, and one lower-volume recovery week every few weeks. → *Expect:* weekly volume rises predictably without constant fatigue.
2. **Extend the long run toward 18-20 km.** Add distance gradually and keep long-run effort conversational. → *Expect:* you finish tired but coordinated, without limping.
3. **Keep easy days truly easy.** Use relaxed breathing, short stride, and upright posture. → *Expect:* easy runs help you recover instead of draining the next workout.
4. **Add threshold or goal-pace work.** Include blocks such as 3 x 8 minutes comfortably hard or 20-40 minutes around planned half-marathon effort. → *Expect:* effort is firm but sustainable, not a maximal interval session.
5. **Practice fueling during long runs.** Take carbohydrates every 30-45 minutes once runs exceed about 75 minutes, and drink to thirst. → *Expect:* energy and stomach comfort remain stable late in the run.
6. **Taper before the event.** Reduce volume for 7-14 days while keeping a few short, relaxed pickups. → *Expect:* legs feel fresher without feeling flat.
7. **Execute conservative pacing.** Start slightly slower than goal pace, settle through the middle, and increase only after 16 km if you still feel controlled. → *Expect:* the final kilometers are hard but not a survival shuffle.

## Decision points

- Long-run recovery takes more than two days → cut the next long run or remove the workout that week.
- Fuel causes stomach distress → test smaller doses, more water, or a different carbohydrate source during training.
- Goal pace feels unsustainable in training → adjust the race target before event day.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much intensity:** detect dead legs on easy days → remove speed work until normal easy pace returns.
- **F2 Underfueling:** detect chills, dizziness, or sudden energy drop late → slow down, take carbohydrates, and practice fueling earlier next time.
- **F3 Chafing or blisters:** detect skin irritation after long runs → change socks, use lubricant, and test clothing before race day.
- **F4 Ignoring niggles:** detect localized pain that warms up then returns worse → rest and seek professional guidance if it persists.

## Verification

You complete a half marathon or supported 21.1 km run with even pacing, practiced fueling, relaxed upper body, stable foot placement under the hips, and no injury-signaling limp.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a run-walk strategy from the start, such as 9 minutes running and 1 minute walking.
- `harder`: add controlled goal-pace segments to the second half of long runs.
- `equipment`: use a watch or marked route for pacing, plus carried fluids or planned water stops.
- `at-home`: use treadmill long runs only with hydration, cooling, and mental breaks planned.

## Safety & privacy

Low to medium cumulative-load risk depending on mileage history. Stop for chest pain, dizziness, sharp pain, heat illness symptoms, or gait changes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before training if you have a relevant medical condition.
