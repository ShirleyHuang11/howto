## Goal

Run hill repeats with strong mechanics, controlled effort, and safe recovery between uphill efforts.

## Preconditions

- Ability to run continuously for 25-30 minutes.
- A moderate hill with safe footing, low traffic, and room to recover.
- No current calf, Achilles, hamstring, or knee pain.
- Warmup completed before the first repeat.

## Steps

1. **Choose the right hill.** Use a 4-8% grade that takes 20-60 seconds to climb, not a steep scramble. → *Expect:* you can run tall without bending at the waist.
2. **Warm up thoroughly.** Jog 10-15 minutes, then do leg swings, skips, and two relaxed strides on flat ground. → *Expect:* calves and hips feel ready for uphill force.
3. **Run the first repeat controlled.** Lean slightly from the ankles, drive elbows back, keep steps short, and lift knees only as much as the grade requires. → *Expect:* effort is hard but not a sprint.
4. **Walk or jog down carefully.** Keep steps short and avoid pounding downhill. → *Expect:* breathing recovers before the next uphill.
5. **Repeat for quality.** Do 4-8 repeats, ending the set when posture, foot strike, or breathing falls apart. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for Achilles pain, calf grabbing, chest pain, dizziness, or slipping. → *Expect:* each repeat is technically similar even if pace changes.
6. **Cool down on flat ground.** Jog or walk 10 minutes and avoid fast downhill running when tired. → *Expect:* legs settle without calf or knee pain increasing.

## Decision points

- Hill is too steep → find a gentler grade or shorten repeats.
- Downhill hurts knees → walk down slower or choose a treadmill incline.
- Calves tighten early → stop repeats and cool down.
- You complete all reps easily → add one repeat next time or slightly lengthen the hill.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bending at the waist:** detect chest collapsing toward the ground → lean from ankles and keep ribs stacked.
- **F2 Sprinting uphill:** detect form breaking before halfway → reduce speed to strong but controlled.
- **F3 Pounding downhill:** detect heavy braking steps → walk down with short steps.
- **F4 Achilles overload:** detect tendon pain or morning stiffness after hills → reduce hill work and seek guidance if persistent.

## Verification

You completed 4-8 uphill repeats of 20-60 seconds with short steps, tall posture, controlled descents, and no sharp calf, Achilles, knee, or chest symptoms.

## Variations

- Easier: 4 short repeats with full walking recovery.
- Harder: 8-10 repeats or 60-90 second climbs after several successful sessions.
- Equipment: treadmill incline, marked hill, watch timer, or trail shoes.
- At-home: treadmill incline repeats or step-up intervals if running hills are unavailable.

## Safety & privacy

Hill repeats load calves, Achilles tendons, hamstrings, and lungs more than easy running. Stop for sharp pain, tendon pain, chest pain, dizziness, or unsafe footing. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
