## Goal

You adjust route, pacing, hydration, and stop conditions so a hot-weather run stays within safe limits.

## Preconditions

- Check temperature, humidity, heat index, air quality, and sun exposure before leaving.
- Choose a shaded route, treadmill, or shorter loop with water access.
- Wear light, breathable clothing and sunscreen if outdoors.
- Avoid hot runs when sick, sleep-deprived, dehydrated, or not acclimated.

## Steps

1. **Decide whether to run outside.** Compare heat, humidity, air quality, and your current health. → *Expect:* you choose treadmill, shade, early morning, or rest if conditions are unsafe.
2. **Lower the goal pace.** Plan by effort, not normal cool-weather pace. → *Expect:* the run begins slower and breathing stays controlled.
3. **Carry or plan fluids.** Drink to thirst and use water stops or loops rather than gambling on a long exposed route. → *Expect:* you can hydrate before thirst becomes urgent.
4. **Start easy and monitor drift.** Run the first 10 minutes very easy and check whether breathing, heart rate, or perceived effort climbs unusually fast. → *Expect:* you are willing to slow or shorten the route early.
5. **Use shade and cooling.** Choose shade, wet your hat or shirt if available, and avoid long sun-exposed climbs. → *Expect:* skin temperature and effort feel more manageable.
6. **Stop for heat illness signs.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop immediately for dizziness, confusion, chills, goosebumps in heat, nausea, cessation of sweating with overheating, clumsiness, or chest pain. → *Expect:* warning signs trigger stopping, cooling, and help, not pushing through.
7. **Cool down and rehydrate.** Walk in shade or indoors, replace fluids, and cool the body gradually. → *Expect:* breathing, temperature, and alertness normalize.

## Decision points

- Heat index or air quality is poor → run indoors, shorten the run, or rest.
- Effort feels high at a very slow pace → switch to walking or stop.
- You are not heat-acclimated → reduce duration and intensity for 1-2 weeks.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pace denial:** detect trying to hold normal pace in heat → slow by effort immediately.
- **F2 Exposed route:** detect no shade or water options → turn back early and choose loops next time.
- **F3 Ignoring early symptoms:** detect chills, dizziness, nausea, or confusion → stop, cool, hydrate, and seek help if severe.
- **F4 Overdressing:** detect trapped heat from dark or heavy clothing → choose light breathable layers next time.

## Verification

You complete a shortened or adjusted hot-weather run at controlled effort, with hydration access, stable coordination, no dizziness or confusion, and a cool-down that returns body temperature and breathing toward normal.

## Variations

- `easier`: replace the run with shaded walking or treadmill Zone 2 cardio.
- `harder`: add distance only after 1-2 weeks of successful heat acclimation.
- `equipment`: use a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, handheld bottle, hydration vest, or treadmill.
- `at-home`: run indoors on a treadmill with a fan when outdoor heat or air quality is unsafe.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because heat illness can become serious quickly. Stop for dizziness, confusion, chills, nausea, clumsiness, chest pain, or severe weakness, and seek urgent help for severe or persistent symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before hot-weather running if you have a relevant condition or take medications that affect heat tolerance.
