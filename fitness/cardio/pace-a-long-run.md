## Goal

You pace a long run so the aerobic work is sustainable, form stays organized, and the final miles do not become a forced shuffle.

## Preconditions

- A planned route, duration, or distance appropriate for your current training.
- Water or a route with access to fluids for longer runs.
- Fuel for runs longer than about 75 minutes if needed.
- Warm up by starting the first 10 minutes very easy.

## Steps

1. **Define the purpose.** Decide whether the long run is easy endurance, progression, or race-specific practice. → *Expect:* the pace target matches the workout, not ego.
2. **Start conservatively.** Run the first 10-20 minutes slower than your expected average pace. → *Expect:* breathing is relaxed and legs feel almost too fresh.
3. **Use effort before pace.** Keep easy long runs conversational and adjust for hills, heat, wind, and fatigue. → *Expect:* effort stays steady even when pace changes.
4. **Check form every few miles.** Relax shoulders, shorten stride if tired, and keep feet landing under the hips. → *Expect:* cadence and posture stay consistent.
5. **Fuel before you fade.** For long runs over 75 minutes, take carbohydrates on a schedule you have practiced. → *Expect:* energy stays even instead of dropping suddenly.
6. **Decide on the finish.** [BRANCH: easy long run, keep the final miles easy | progression run, gently increase only if breathing and form are controlled] → *Expect:* you finish tired but not wrecked.
7. **Stop for injury signs.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if pain changes your gait, dizziness appears, or heat/cold symptoms become concerning. → *Expect:* normal fatigue does not override safety.

## Decision points

- Pace feels hard before halfway → slow immediately and protect the purpose.
- Heart rate or breathing drifts upward in heat → reduce pace and hydrate.
- You finish with good form for several weeks → extend distance or add a small progression segment.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Fast first mile:** detect early excitement and heavy breathing → cap pace for the first 10-20 minutes.
- **F2 Watch fixation:** detect forcing pace uphill or in heat → switch to effort-based running.
- **F3 Late fueling:** detect sudden weakness or irritability → fuel earlier on the next run.
- **F4 Form collapse:** detect shuffling, overstriding, or limping → slow, walk, or end the run.

## Verification

You complete the planned long run with an easy or planned progression effort, steady breathing, practiced fueling if needed, relaxed shoulders, feet landing under the hips, and no gait-changing pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: use planned walk breaks from the start.
- `harder`: finish the final 15-30 minutes at steady moderate effort after several successful easy long runs.
- `equipment`: use a watch for time and reminders, but let effort override pace.
- `at-home`: use a treadmill with fluids, fan, and small incline changes to reduce monotony.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when distance matches current training, but long duration increases dehydration, heat, cold, and overuse risks. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, confusion, chest pain, or a limp. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor if you have relevant conditions.
