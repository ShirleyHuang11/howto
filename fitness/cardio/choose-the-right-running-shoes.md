## Goal

Choose running shoes that fit your feet, match your running surface, and feel comfortable during real movement.

## Preconditions

- Socks similar to what you will run in.
- Access to a running store, treadmill test, returnable online purchase, or safe indoor try-on space.
- Knowledge of your typical running surface and weekly distance.

## Steps

1. **Measure both feet late in the day.** Feet often swell, so check length and width when they are closer to running size. → *Expect:* shoe size is based on the larger foot, not habit.
2. **Leave toe room.** Stand and confirm about a thumb-width of space in front of the longest toe. → *Expect:* toes can spread and do not hit the front when walking downhill or jogging.
3. **Check midfoot and heel hold.** Lace the shoe snugly without crushing the arch, then walk and jog. → *Expect:* heel stays secure and the midfoot does not slide side to side.
4. **Match the shoe to the surface.** [BRANCH: road, choose smoother outsole and cushioning | trail, choose traction, protection, and secure upper] → *Expect:* the sole grips the surface you actually use.
5. **Test running mechanics briefly.** Jog 2-5 minutes and notice comfort, noise, pressure, and stability. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not buy shoes that cause numbness, sharp pressure, or immediate joint pain during a short test. → *Expect:* the shoe disappears under you rather than forcing your stride.
6. **Use return policy wisely.** Keep the first run short and on a clean surface if returns require it. → *Expect:* after one easy run, you know whether the fit stays comfortable.

## Decision points

- Toes rub or hit the front → size up or choose a wider toe box.
- Heel slips → try different lacing, smaller size, or different heel shape.
- Arch or forefoot pressure appears quickly → try a different last, width, or cushioning.
- Shoe feels unstable on trails → choose better traction and lateral hold.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Buying by looks:** detect discomfort ignored because the shoe looks good → prioritize fit and return it.
- **F2 Too-small fit:** detect black toenails, numb toes, or front rubbing → increase length or width.
- **F3 Wrong surface shoe:** detect slipping on trails or bulky tread on roads → match outsole to terrain.
- **F4 Changing too much at once:** detect calf or foot soreness in a very different shoe → transition gradually.

## Verification

You can jog 10-20 easy minutes in the shoes with secure heel hold, toe room, no numbness, no rubbing, and no sharp joint pain.

## Variations

- Easier: get fitted at a specialty running store.
- Harder: maintain separate shoes for road, trail, and faster workouts.
- Equipment: alternate lacing patterns, insoles if prescribed or already tolerated, and moisture-wicking socks.
- At-home: test on a treadmill or hallway with a clean sole before committing.

## Safety & privacy

Shoes cannot diagnose or fix all pain. Stop running in shoes that cause numbness, blistering, sharp pain, or altered gait. Online fit tools may collect foot and gait data; review privacy settings. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or clinician for persistent pain.
