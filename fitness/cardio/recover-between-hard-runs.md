## Goal

Recover between hard runs so fitness improves without stacking fatigue, soreness, or injury risk.

## Preconditions

- A completed hard run such as intervals, hills, tempo, race, or long run.
- Food, fluids, and time for sleep.
- Awareness of any pain that changed your stride during the run.

## Steps

1. **Cool down immediately.** Jog or walk 5-15 minutes at very easy effort after the hard portion. → *Expect:* breathing settles and legs stop feeling abrupt or locked up.
2. **Refuel within a practical window.** Eat a normal meal or snack with carbohydrates, protein, and fluids. → *Expect:* thirst and hunger decrease and energy begins to normalize.
3. **Check pain versus soreness.** Notice whether discomfort is symmetrical muscle fatigue or sharp, localized, or gait-changing pain. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not run hard again with sharp pain, swelling, limping, or pain at rest. → *Expect:* ordinary soreness feels broad and improves with gentle movement.
4. **Make the next day easy.** [BRANCH: mild soreness, easy run or cross-train | heavy soreness or poor sleep, rest or walk] → *Expect:* the recovery session leaves you looser, not more depleted.
5. **Space hard sessions.** Leave at least 48 hours between demanding runs for most beginners and recreational runners. → *Expect:* your next hard workout starts with normal coordination and motivation.
6. **Review the pattern.** Track sleep, soreness, mood, resting heart rate if used, and workout quality. → *Expect:* recovery decisions come from evidence, not guilt.

## Decision points

- Soreness improves after warmup → keep the day easy.
- Pain worsens as you move → stop and switch to rest or assessment.
- Performance drops for multiple sessions → reduce intensity for a week.
- You race or run unusually long → extend recovery beyond normal.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Hard-easy becomes hard-medium:** detect pushing pace on recovery days → slow to conversational effort.
- **F2 Under-fueling:** detect cravings, fatigue, or poor sleep after hard runs → add carbohydrates, protein, and fluids.
- **F3 Ignoring localized pain:** detect one-sided shin, foot, knee, or tendon pain → stop impact and seek guidance if persistent.
- **F4 Too many quality days:** detect repeated intervals, hills, and tempos in one week → keep only one or two hard runs.

## Verification

You waited at least 48 hours before the next hard run, completed only easy recovery work between hard sessions, and started the next quality workout without limping, sharp pain, or unusual fatigue.

## Variations

- Easier: take full rest or gentle walking after every hard run.
- Harder: advanced runners may use short easy doubles only when already adapted.
- Equipment: foam roller, heart-rate monitor, training log, or compression socks if comfortable.
- At-home: mobility, easy cycling, or a short walk instead of running.

## Safety & privacy

Recovery is part of training. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, chest pain, dizziness, or symptoms that do not improve with rest. Fitness trackers may collect health data; review privacy settings. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
