## Goal

You progress from a comfortable 5 km runner to completing 10 km continuously with controlled pacing and durable form.

## Preconditions

- You can run 5 km continuously at easy effort.
- Running shoes with enough life left for several weeks of training.
- A route or treadmill where you can add distance without long traffic stops.
- Warm up before each run with 5-10 minutes of easy jogging or brisk walking.

## Steps

1. **Set a three-run weekly rhythm.** Do one easy run, one short quality run, and one gradually longer run. → *Expect:* each run has a clear purpose and at least one recovery day follows harder work.
2. **Build the long run first.** Add 0.5-1 km to the long run most weeks, backing off every third or fourth week. → *Expect:* distance increases without soreness that lasts more than 48 hours.
3. **Keep most running easy.** Run at a conversational pace, with breathing steady enough to speak in phrases. → *Expect:* you finish easy days feeling like you could continue.
4. **Add light quality once per week.** Use 4-6 short pickups of 30-60 seconds or a steady 10-minute tempo within a short run. → *Expect:* legs turn over faster while posture stays tall and relaxed.
5. **Practice fueling and hydration for longer efforts.** Drink to thirst and consider a small carbohydrate snack before runs longer than 60 minutes. → *Expect:* energy stays stable instead of fading suddenly late in the run.
6. **Rehearse race-day pacing.** Start your final long workouts slower than goal pace and increase only if breathing remains controlled. → *Expect:* the second half feels as smooth as the first.
7. **Complete the 10 km.** Run the first 3 km deliberately easy, settle through the middle, and avoid sprinting until the finish is close. → *Expect:* cadence, arm swing, and foot placement stay consistent to the end.

## Decision points

- Long run feels like a race every week → slow it down and pause distance increases.
- Soreness changes your gait → replace the next run with walking or cycling and resume at lower volume.
- You finish 8-9 km comfortably → schedule the 10 km attempt after an easy week.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Weekly mileage jump:** detect new aches after a large distance increase → reduce the next week by 20-30%.
- **F2 Racing easy runs:** detect heavy breathing on normal days → use the talk test and slow down.
- **F3 Late-run form collapse:** detect slumped posture and shuffling → shorten stride, lift the chest, and walk briefly if needed.
- **F4 Poor route planning:** detect repeated stops or unsafe crossings → choose a loop, track, or treadmill for the 10 km attempt.

## Verification

You run 10 km continuously with steady effort, no limp, shoulders relaxed, feet landing under the body, and pacing that does not collapse in the final 2 km.

## Variations

- `easier`: use planned 9-minute run and 1-minute walk intervals for the full 10 km.
- `harder`: run the final 2 km slightly faster while keeping breathing controlled.
- `equipment`: use a GPS watch, phone, treadmill display, or measured loop to track distance.
- `at-home`: use a treadmill session if outdoor weather or traffic makes the route unsafe.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when mileage progresses gradually. Stop for sharp pain, chest symptoms, dizziness, heat illness signs, or a limp. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant medical condition.
