## Goal

Build enough easy aerobic volume and tissue tolerance before adding hard speed workouts.

## Preconditions

- A cardio mode you can do comfortably, usually easy running, walking, cycling, or elliptical.
- Current weekly training volume known roughly.
- No unresolved sharp pain from recent training.

## Steps

1. **Set your starting baseline.** Record your current weekly sessions, minutes, and any pain. → *Expect:* you know the workload your body already tolerates.
2. **Keep most training easy.** Use conversational effort for nearly all sessions during the base phase. → *Expect:* you finish workouts feeling steady rather than drained.
3. **Increase only one variable.** Add a little duration or frequency, not speed and hills at the same time. → *Expect:* weekly volume rises gradually without soreness accumulating.
4. **Include strides only if pain-free.** [BRANCH: no pain and running regularly, add 4-6 relaxed 15-20 second strides once weekly | any pain or new runner, skip strides] → *Expect:* strides feel smooth and fast, not forced.
5. **Recover deliberately.** Place rest or low-impact days after longer sessions. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop progression if pain changes gait, worsens during activity, or persists at rest. → *Expect:* legs feel normal by the next easy session.
6. **Earn speed work with consistency.** Maintain 4-6 weeks of steady base training before structured intervals. → *Expect:* easy pace, breathing, and recovery become more predictable.

## Decision points

- You miss several sessions → resume baseline instead of jumping ahead.
- Easy pace gets faster at the same effort → base is improving.
- Pain or fatigue rises → reduce volume for a week.
- You want intervals → add them only after consistent pain-free base work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Adding intensity too soon:** detect soreness and heavy legs after fast workouts → return to easy base work.
- **F2 Ignoring recovery:** detect poor sleep, irritability, or declining easy pace → take extra rest.
- **F3 Volume spike:** detect a large weekly jump → cut back to the last tolerated week.
- **F4 Strides become sprints:** detect straining or tight hamstrings → keep strides relaxed or remove them.

## Verification

You completed 4-6 weeks of consistent easy cardio, increased weekly volume gradually, recovered between sessions, and had no gait-changing pain before starting speed work.

## Variations

- Easier: use walk-run sessions or low-impact machines for part of the volume.
- Harder: add one short tempo or interval session only after the base block.
- Equipment: training log, watch, heart-rate monitor, or perceived-effort notes.
- At-home: stationary bike, treadmill walking, or step-ups for easy aerobic volume.

## Safety & privacy

Base building should reduce injury risk, but repetitive cardio can still overload tendons and bones. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, limping, chest pain, or dizziness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
