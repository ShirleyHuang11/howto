## Goal

You perform a short high-intensity interval session that raises cardiovascular demand while keeping technique and recovery under control.

## Preconditions

- A low-risk modality such as bike, rower, hill walk, running only if conditioned, or bodyweight movements you can perform well.
- Space free of slipping or tripping hazards.
- You are not acutely ill, dizzy, or carrying an unresolved injury.
- Warm up for 8-10 minutes before the first hard interval.

## Steps

1. **Choose simple movements.** Pick one cardio tool or 2-4 bodyweight drills you can do cleanly under fatigue. → *Expect:* coordination stays reliable before intensity rises.
2. **Warm up thoroughly.** Gradually increase effort and include two short practice pickups. → *Expect:* breathing is elevated and joints feel ready.
3. **Set the interval structure.** Use 6-10 rounds of 20-60 seconds hard with 60-120 seconds easy recovery. → *Expect:* the work period is hard but repeatable.
4. **Push effort without losing mechanics.** Move fast enough to reach heavy breathing while keeping knees tracking, trunk braced, and landings quiet if jumping. → *Expect:* intensity comes from output, not sloppy movement.
5. **Recover honestly between rounds.** Move easily or rest until breathing drops enough to repeat good form. → *Expect:* the next round starts controlled, not panicked.
6. **Stop before technique fails.** ⚠️ *Safety:* end the set if you feel chest pain, faintness, sharp joint pain, or repeated form breakdown. → *Expect:* fatigue is high but movement remains safe.
7. **Cool down.** Walk, pedal, or row easily for 5 minutes. → *Expect:* breathing slows and you can speak normally again.

## Decision points

- Form breaks before the interval ends → shorten the work period or choose a simpler modality.
- You cannot recover between rounds → lengthen rest or reduce rounds.
- You are new to exercise → build Zone 2 cardio first before HIIT.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Going maximal too soon:** detect the first round ruining all later rounds → cap effort at about 8-9 out of 10.
- **F2 Sloppy landings:** detect knees caving or loud impacts → remove jumps and use bike, rower, or step drills.
- **F3 Insufficient warmup:** detect joint stiffness during round one → stop, warm up longer, and restart easier.
- **F4 Nausea or dizziness:** detect symptoms beyond normal hard breathing → stop, sit or walk gently, and do not resume that day.

## Verification

You complete the planned intervals with hard breathing, repeatable output, stable posture, knees tracking over toes, no sharp pain, and a full cool-down.

## Variations

- `easier`: use 6 rounds of 20 seconds hard and 100 seconds easy on a stationary bike.
- `harder`: use 8-10 rounds of 45-60 seconds hard only after consistent recovery and clean form.
- `equipment`: bike, rower, ski erg, treadmill incline, or jump rope can all work.
- `at-home`: use fast marching, low step-ups, shadow boxing, or non-jumping bodyweight drills.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because high intensity can expose cardiovascular issues and form breakdown. Stop for chest pain, faintness, wheezing that does not settle, sharp pain, or confusion. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before HIIT if you have a relevant condition or are returning after illness.
