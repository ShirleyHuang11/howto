## Goal

You reduce a common exercise-related side stitch during running or cardio and adjust habits that make it more likely.

## Preconditions

- You are experiencing a familiar sharp or cramping side pain during exercise, not severe unexplained abdominal or chest pain.
- A safe place to slow down or stop.
- You can breathe and walk normally.
- No recent trauma, fever, or severe illness symptoms.

## Steps

1. **Slow immediately.** Drop to an easy jog or walk instead of pushing through. → *Expect:* pain intensity stops climbing.
2. **Exhale deeply and rhythmically.** Use long exhales through pursed lips while keeping shoulders relaxed. → *Expect:* the cramp begins to ease within a few minutes.
3. **Apply gentle pressure.** Press fingers into the sore area while exhaling, then release slightly on inhale. → *Expect:* discomfort feels more manageable, not sharper.
4. **Reset posture.** Stand tall, avoid hunching, and keep ribs stacked over hips. → *Expect:* breathing feels less restricted.
5. **Resume gradually.** [BRANCH: pain remains, keep walking or end the session | pain resolves, return to easy pace before increasing] → *Expect:* the stitch does not immediately return.
6. **Review triggers afterward.** Note recent meals, sugary drinks, dehydration, pace, and breathing pattern. → *Expect:* you identify one change to test next time.
7. **Escalate unusual pain.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop and seek medical help for severe, persistent, spreading, chest-like, fever-related, or trauma-related pain. → *Expect:* only a typical exercise stitch is handled with self-management.

## Decision points

- Pain improves when you slow and exhale → continue easy and resume gradually.
- Pain worsens or feels unlike prior stitches → stop the workout.
- Stitches happen often after eating → allow more digestion time and avoid large meals before running.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pushing through:** detect worsening stabbing pain → slow to a walk until it settles.
- **F2 Shallow breathing:** detect tight chest and quick panting → lengthen exhales and relax shoulders.
- **F3 Hunched posture:** detect folded ribs and tight abdomen → stand tall and reduce pace.
- **F4 Ignoring unusual symptoms:** detect severe or persistent pain → stop and seek medical guidance.

## Verification

The side stitch decreases enough that you can walk or jog easily with relaxed breathing, upright posture, and no severe, spreading, or persistent pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: stop completely, breathe slowly, and resume only after symptoms resolve.
- `harder`: prevent recurrence by practicing steady pacing and rhythmic breathing during faster runs.
- `equipment`: use a watch note or training log to track food and timing triggers.
- `at-home`: practice long exhales and trunk mobility before future runs.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when symptoms match a familiar side stitch. Do not self-treat severe, unexplained, persistent, chest-like, or illness-related pain as a stitch. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor for recurring or concerning pain.
