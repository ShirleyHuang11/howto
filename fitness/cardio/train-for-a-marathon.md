## Goal

You prepare for 42.2 km by developing endurance, pacing control, fueling tolerance, and resilience to sustained running volume.

## Preconditions

- You can run a half marathon or 90-minute long run comfortably.
- You have time for 4-5 weekly runs and consistent recovery.
- Shoes, socks, fuel, and hydration options tested before race day.
- Warm up before workouts and keep long-run starts deliberately easy.

## Steps

1. **Build a realistic 16-18 week plan.** Include mostly easy mileage, one quality workout, one long run, strength or mobility as tolerated, and periodic down weeks. → *Expect:* the plan fits your life without stacking hard sessions back to back.
2. **Progress long runs carefully.** Extend long runs toward 30-34 km or 2.5-3.5 hours, whichever is appropriate for your level. → *Expect:* you finish long runs fatigued but able to walk normally afterward.
3. **Use marathon-pace segments sparingly.** Add controlled blocks inside medium or long runs after you have a mileage base. → *Expect:* marathon pace feels steady and repeatable, not like 10 km effort.
4. **Practice race fueling every long run.** Take carbohydrates about every 30-40 minutes and rehearse the fluid and sodium strategy you will use. → *Expect:* stomach, energy, and concentration stay stable deep into the run.
5. **Protect recovery.** Sleep, eat enough, rotate shoes if useful, and reduce volume when soreness changes your gait. → *Expect:* normal fatigue resolves rather than accumulating into pain.
6. **Taper for 2-3 weeks.** Reduce mileage while keeping a few short pickups and marathon-pace touches. → *Expect:* legs become fresher while rhythm remains familiar.
7. **Run the race with restraint.** Hold back for the first 10 km, settle through 30 km, and use trained fueling even if you feel good early. → *Expect:* the final 10 km are difficult but your mechanics remain organized.
8. **Respect serious warning signs.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop or seek medical help for chest pain, faintness, confusion, severe cramping with chills, or sharp pain that forces a limp. → *Expect:* you distinguish normal marathon discomfort from symptoms that require stopping.

## Decision points

- Weekly volume disrupts sleep, mood, or normal life → reduce the plan rather than forcing missed recovery.
- Long-run fueling fails repeatedly → slow the long run and test different carbohydrate forms before race day.
- Pain is localized and worsening → stop speed work and consult a qualified clinician if it persists.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Chasing an unrealistic goal pace:** detect repeated failed pace workouts → reset goal pace using recent race or long-run data.
- **F2 Long runs too fast:** detect extended soreness after every long run → slow to conversational effort and remove fast finishes.
- **F3 Fueling too late:** detect sudden energy collapse → start carbohydrates early and schedule reminders.
- **F4 Training through injury:** detect a limp, sharp pain, or pain at rest → stop running and seek professional evaluation.
- **F5 Over-taper panic:** detect adding extra miles in the final week → trust the taper and keep runs short.

## Verification

You complete 42.2 km with planned fueling, controlled early pacing, upright posture, relaxed arms, consistent foot placement, and no collapse, confusion, or injury-related limp.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a run-walk plan from the beginning and choose a generous cutoff event.
- `harder`: add marathon-pace blocks only after consistent, healthy mileage.
- `equipment`: use a watch, tested shoes, anti-chafe product, and carried or course-provided fueling.
- `at-home`: use treadmills for some easy runs, but practice long-run fueling and pacing in event-like conditions.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because marathon training involves high repetitive load and prolonged exertion. Stop for severe symptoms, heat illness signs, chest pain, fainting, or gait-changing pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before beginning if you have a relevant condition.
