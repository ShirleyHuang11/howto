## Goal

Run outdoors in cold weather while keeping traction, temperature, breathing, and effort under control.

## Preconditions

- Weather checked for temperature, wind chill, precipitation, and daylight.
- Shoes with reliable tread and clothing layers that can vent sweat.
- A route with safe footing, low traffic exposure, and an option to shorten the run.
- Warm up first with easy walking, leg swings, and light jogging.

## Steps

1. **Dress in thin, removable layers.** Use a moisture-wicking base layer, insulating middle layer if needed, wind shell, gloves, and a hat or headband. → *Expect:* you feel slightly cool standing still, not warm and sweaty before starting.
2. **Check footing before committing to pace.** Walk the first minute and scan for ice, slush, uneven snow, and low-visibility crossings. → *Expect:* you know which parts of the route require shorter steps or a detour.
3. **Start slower than normal.** Jog easily for 8-10 minutes with a short stride and feet landing under your hips. → *Expect:* breathing stays conversational and your legs warm gradually.
4. **Use compact mechanics.** Keep elbows close, torso tall, cadence slightly quick, and avoid overstriding on slick surfaces. → *Expect:* each foot contacts quietly under your center of mass without skidding.
5. **Control intensity in cold air.** Breathe through the nose or a face covering when practical, and keep hard efforts brief until fully warm. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop or go indoors if chest tightness, wheezing, dizziness, numb skin, or shivering appears. → *Expect:* breathing is steady and your face, fingers, and toes remain functional.
6. **Finish with a sheltered cooldown.** Slow to a walk near home or indoors and remove wet layers promptly. → *Expect:* heart rate comes down and you are dry or changing before you chill.

## Decision points

- Ground is icy or visibility is poor → switch to a treadmill, indoor track, or brisk walk.
- Hands or feet stay numb after 10 minutes → turn back and add warmer gear next time.
- Breathing becomes sharp or wheezy → slow down, cover the mouth, and stop if it does not settle.
- You are sweating heavily early → unzip or remove a layer before sweat makes you cold.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Overstriding on slick ground:** detect heel striking far ahead and slipping backward → shorten stride, increase cadence slightly, and keep feet under hips.
- **F2 Dressing too warm:** detect sweat soaking base layers early → vent layers and choose lighter insulation next run.
- **F3 Cold hands or ears:** detect numbness or burning skin → add mittens, hat, or wind protection and shorten the route.
- **F4 Running hard before warm:** detect tight calves or ragged breathing in the first mile → extend the easy warmup and delay faster work.

## Verification

You completed 20-40 minutes outdoors with steady breathing, controlled footing, no numbness that persisted after warming, and no slips or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: walk-run intervals, shorter route loops, or treadmill when footing is uncertain.
- Harder: add short relaxed pickups only after 15 minutes of easy running and safe traction.
- Equipment: trail shoes, traction devices, reflective vest, headlamp, face covering, and mittens.
- At-home: substitute a treadmill run or low-impact cardio circuit when weather is unsafe.

## Safety & privacy

Cold, ice, traffic, and low visibility increase risk. Stop for chest pain, wheezing, dizziness, confusion, frostbite signs, or sharp joint pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have asthma, heart disease, circulation problems, or another relevant condition.
