## Goal

You coordinate breathing with running effort so you stay relaxed, avoid early panic, and match pace to aerobic capacity.

## Preconditions

- A safe, easy running route or treadmill.
- No current respiratory illness causing severe wheeze or shortness of breath.
- You can walk briskly without symptoms.
- Warm up with 5 minutes of walking or easy jogging.

## Steps

1. **Start slower than usual.** Run at a pace where nasal or mixed nose-mouth breathing feels possible at first. → *Expect:* breathing rises gradually instead of spiking.
2. **Use relaxed belly and rib expansion.** Let the abdomen and lower ribs move rather than shrugging the shoulders. → *Expect:* shoulders and jaw stay loose.
3. **Match rhythm to effort.** Try a 3-step inhale and 3-step exhale at easy pace, or 2-step inhale and 2-step exhale when effort rises. → *Expect:* rhythm feels steady but not forced.
4. **Exhale fully enough.** Focus on a smooth exhale instead of gulping bigger inhales. → *Expect:* the next inhale arrives naturally.
5. **Use the talk test.** Adjust pace so you can speak in phrases during easy runs. → *Expect:* if speech disappears, you know to slow down.
6. **Practice short pickups.** Run 4 x 20 seconds slightly faster, then return to easy breathing. → *Expect:* breathing increases during pickups and settles within a minute.
7. **Stop for abnormal breathlessness.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if breathlessness is severe, paired with chest pain, wheezing that does not settle, blue lips, dizziness, or faintness. → *Expect:* normal training breathlessness improves when you slow or walk.

## Decision points

- You gasp early → slow down and lengthen the warmup.
- Side stitch appears → slow, deepen exhale, and relax the torso.
- Breathing never settles at easy pace → use run-walk intervals and consider medical evaluation if unusual.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Shoulder breathing:** detect raised shoulders and neck tension → relax hands and expand lower ribs.
- **F2 Breath holding:** detect irregular gulping → count steps and exhale continuously.
- **F3 Pace mismatch:** detect inability to speak on an easy run → slow to a jog or walk.
- **F4 Overthinking rhythm:** detect anxiety from counting → drop the count and use conversational effort.

## Verification

During a 20-minute easy run or run-walk session, breathing stays rhythmic, shoulders remain relaxed, speech in phrases is possible, and breathlessness settles quickly when pace decreases.

## Variations

- `easier`: practice rhythmic breathing while brisk walking.
- `harder`: apply a faster 2:2 rhythm during controlled tempo intervals.
- `equipment`: use a treadmill for stable pace while practicing.
- `at-home`: rehearse diaphragmatic breathing lying down or standing before running.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for normal breath-control practice, but severe or unusual shortness of breath needs attention. Stop for chest pain, faintness, severe wheezing, or symptoms that do not improve with rest. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor if you have asthma, heart, lung, or other relevant conditions.
