## Goal

You build enough aerobic endurance to run 5 km continuously at an easy, controlled effort.

## Preconditions

- Running shoes that feel stable and do not create hot spots.
- A flat route, track, or treadmill where you can alternate running and walking safely.
- You can walk briskly for 30 minutes without chest pain, dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath.
- Warm up first with 5-10 minutes of easy walking and leg swings.

## Steps

1. **Start with run-walk intervals.** Run 60 seconds at a pace where you could speak in short sentences, then walk 90 seconds; repeat for 20-25 minutes. → *Expect:* breathing rises but settles during each walk break.
2. **Keep the stride quiet and short.** Land under your hips with a soft midfoot or whole-foot contact, arms bent and swinging forward-back. → *Expect:* footfalls sound light and your shoulders stay relaxed.
3. **Add running time gradually.** Each week, lengthen the run portions or shorten the walk portions while keeping total session time near 25-35 minutes. → *Expect:* the last interval feels controlled, not like a sprint finish.
4. **Run three days per week with rest between sessions.** Use easy walks, mobility, or complete rest on other days. → *Expect:* legs feel recovered enough that form is not worse at the next run.
5. **Convert intervals into continuous easy running.** [BRANCH: still struggling, use 4 minutes run and 1 minute walk | ready, run continuously for 20, then 25, then 30 minutes] → *Expect:* you finish with steady breathing and no sharp joint pain.
6. **Extend one session to 5 km.** Choose a flat route and run slower than you think you need for the first 2 km. → *Expect:* you can keep the same relaxed cadence through the final kilometer.
7. **Stop for warning symptoms.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you feel chest pain, faintness, sharp bone or joint pain, or a limp that changes your gait. → *Expect:* normal muscle fatigue is acceptable; pain that changes movement is not.

## Decision points

- Breathing is out of control in the first 5 minutes → slow down or return to shorter run-walk intervals.
- Knees, shins, or hips ache more each session → reduce volume for a week and check shoes, surface, and stride length.
- You can run 30 minutes comfortably → measure or map 5 km and complete it at the same easy effort.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Starting too fast:** detect gasping early → begin each run slower than walking pride allows, then settle in.
- **F2 Overstriding:** detect loud heel strikes far in front of the body → shorten stride and let cadence rise naturally.
- **F3 Shin soreness:** detect tenderness along the shin that worsens while running → reduce impact, avoid hills, and resume with walk-run sessions.
- **F4 Skipping recovery:** detect heavy legs and worsening form every session → add a rest day between runs.

## Verification

You run 5 km continuously with even pacing, relaxed shoulders, quiet footfalls under the hips, and no limping or sharp pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: complete the 5 km with planned 4:1 run-walk intervals before attempting continuous running.
- `harder`: after the first continuous 5 km, repeat it on a slightly rolling route while keeping effort easy.
- `equipment`: use a treadmill set to 0.5-1% incline if outdoor routes are unsafe.
- `at-home`: substitute brisk stair-free walking intervals only if you cannot run safely outside.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most healthy beginners, but stop for chest pain, fainting, sharp joint pain, or a new limp. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a heart, lung, bone, joint, pregnancy-related, or other relevant medical condition.
