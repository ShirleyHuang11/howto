## Goal

Use informal speed changes during a run to improve pace control, leg turnover, and aerobic fitness.

## Preconditions

- Ability to run continuously for at least 20 minutes.
- Safe route with good footing and low traffic interruptions.
- Warmup completed before faster running.

## Steps

1. **Warm up easily.** Jog 10-15 minutes with relaxed shoulders and short, quiet steps. → *Expect:* breathing is steady and legs feel loose before any surge.
2. **Pick simple landmarks or times.** Choose trees, blocks, light poles, or 30-90 second intervals for faster segments. → *Expect:* the workout has structure without requiring exact splits.
3. **Run the first surge controlled.** Increase cadence and forward drive without sprinting or straining. → *Expect:* effort rises to about 7 out of 10 while form stays smooth.
4. **Recover with easy jogging.** Slow down until breathing settles before the next surge. → *Expect:* you can start the next faster segment without gasping.
5. **Repeat 6-10 surges.** Keep every fast segment technically clean: tall torso, feet under hips, arms driving back. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the fast work if you feel sharp pain, hamstring grabbing, chest pain, or dizziness. → *Expect:* later surges are slower if needed but still controlled.
6. **Cool down fully.** Jog or walk 5-10 minutes and let the legs downshift. → *Expect:* breathing returns toward normal and muscles do not tighten abruptly.

## Decision points

- You cannot recover between surges → shorten or slow the fast parts.
- Form gets sloppy → end the speed work and cool down.
- Route becomes crowded or slippery → keep everything easy.
- Workout feels too easy → add one or two surges next time, not all at once.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Sprinting the first rep:** detect heavy breathing and fading immediately → start future surges at controlled fast pace.
- **F2 Recovery too fast:** detect heart rate staying high between reps → jog slower or walk briefly.
- **F3 Overstriding:** detect loud foot strikes during speed → increase cadence and land under hips.
- **F4 Tight hamstring or calf:** detect grabbing or sharp pull → stop fast running and walk home.

## Verification

You completed 6-10 controlled faster segments after a warmup, recovered between them, maintained upright mechanics, and finished without sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: 6 rounds of 30 seconds brisk, 90 seconds easy.
- Harder: 10 rounds of 1 minute fast, 1 minute easy, or longer landmark surges.
- Equipment: watch timer, marked path, track, or treadmill.
- At-home: use treadmill speed changes with the same easy recovery rule.

## Safety & privacy

Speed changes increase muscle and tendon load. Warm up thoroughly and stop for sharp pain, chest pain, dizziness, or sudden tightness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
