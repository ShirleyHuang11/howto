## Goal

You swim laps for aerobic fitness with safe pool habits, controlled breathing, and efficient basic stroke mechanics.

## Preconditions

- You can swim one pool length and are comfortable in the lane depth.
- A lifeguarded pool or supervised swimming environment.
- Goggles, swim cap if useful, and awareness of lane etiquette.
- Do not swim alone if you are unsure of your ability.

## Steps

1. **Choose the right lane.** Pick a lane matching your speed and ask to share if needed. → *Expect:* you can swim without blocking or being overtaken constantly.
2. **Warm up easily.** Swim 4-8 relaxed lengths with generous rest. → *Expect:* breathing settles and shoulders feel loose.
3. **Keep the body long and level.** Look down, keep hips near the surface, and avoid lifting the head to breathe. → *Expect:* legs do not sink dramatically.
4. **Breathe with a small rotation.** Turn the head with the body roll, exhale in the water, and inhale quickly without craning the neck. → *Expect:* one goggle may stay near the water as you inhale.
5. **Use repeatable intervals.** Swim sets such as 8 x 25 m or 6 x 50 m with rest that lets technique recover. → *Expect:* later lengths look similar to early lengths.
6. **Push off safely and stay aware.** Push from the wall under control and sight before passing or changing strokes. → *Expect:* lane traffic stays predictable.
7. **Stop if safety changes.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop and signal for help if you feel panicked, dizzy, cramped, breathless beyond control, or unable to reach the wall calmly. → *Expect:* fatigue remains manageable and you always have a recovery option.

## Decision points

- You gasp every length → shorten repeats, rest longer, or use drills with a kickboard.
- Shoulder pain appears → reduce volume, avoid forceful pulling, and check technique.
- Lane is too crowded or fast → move lanes or wait rather than fighting traffic.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Head lifting:** detect hips sinking during breaths → rotate to breathe and keep one eye near the water.
- **F2 Holding breath:** detect panic or gasping → exhale bubbles continuously underwater.
- **F3 Overkicking:** detect legs burning early → use a smaller, relaxed flutter kick.
- **F4 Swimming past skill:** detect inability to finish a length calmly → stop, rest, and use shorter repeats with supervision.

## Verification

You complete 20-40 minutes of lap swimming or planned intervals in a supervised pool, with controlled breathing, level body position, predictable lane etiquette, and no panic, dizziness, or shoulder pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: alternate 25 m swims with full rest or use a kickboard for some lengths.
- `harder`: build toward longer continuous swims or structured intervals with shorter rest.
- `equipment`: use goggles, pull buoy, kickboard, or fins sparingly for drills.
- `at-home`: no true at-home substitute; use supervised pool swimming for water safety.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because swimming involves drowning risk and breath-control stress. Swim where help is available, do not hyperventilate, and stop for panic, dizziness, chest pain, severe cramps, or shoulder pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
