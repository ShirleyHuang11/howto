## Goal

You run on a treadmill with safe setup, stable pacing, and form that transfers well to outdoor running.

## Preconditions

- A treadmill with working belt, speed controls, incline controls, and emergency stop.
- Shoes tied securely and no loose clothing near the belt.
- Water nearby and enough space to step off safely.
- Warm up with 5 minutes of easy walking before running.

## Steps

1. **Start straddling the belt.** Stand on the side rails, start the belt slowly, then step onto it when speed is predictable. → *Expect:* you feel balanced before increasing pace.
2. **Use the safety clip when appropriate.** Attach the emergency stop clip to clothing, especially during faster running. → *Expect:* the belt will stop if you drift too far back.
3. **Build speed gradually.** Increase pace in small increments until you reach an easy jog or planned workout speed. → *Expect:* cadence and breathing settle without grabbing the rails.
4. **Set a slight incline for steady runs.** Use 0.5-1% incline if it feels natural and does not strain the calves. → *Expect:* effort feels similar to flat outdoor running.
5. **Run centered on the belt.** Keep eyes forward, shoulders relaxed, arms swinging naturally, and feet landing under the hips. → *Expect:* you stay away from the console and do not drift backward.
6. **Avoid holding the handrails while running.** [BRANCH: balance concern, slow to a walk and hold briefly | stable, release rails and run naturally] → *Expect:* posture stays upright and stride is not pulled forward.
7. **Cool down before stepping off.** Decrease speed to a walk for 3-5 minutes, then stop the belt fully. → *Expect:* breathing lowers and footing is steady before you exit.

## Decision points

- You keep drifting backward → slow down or shorten the workout until balance improves.
- Calves tighten at incline → reduce incline to 0% and progress more slowly.
- You need rails to run → the speed is too high; slow to a pace you can control hands-free.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Console crowding:** detect feet landing too far forward near the motor cover → move back to the center and shorten stride.
- **F2 Rail dependence:** detect leaning on the handles → slow down and use arms normally.
- **F3 Belt surprise:** detect stumbling when speed changes quickly → adjust in small increments and use the safety clip.
- **F4 Overheating indoors:** detect dizziness, chills, or unusual fatigue → stop, cool down, and hydrate.

## Verification

You complete the planned treadmill run centered on the belt, without holding the rails, with quiet footfalls, relaxed shoulders, controlled breathing, and a 3-5 minute walking cool-down.

## Variations

- `easier`: use brisk walking intervals before jogging.
- `harder`: add controlled incline or speed intervals after you can run steadily.
- `equipment`: use the safety clip, treadmill fan, and a towel placed safely off the belt.
- `at-home`: keep children, pets, cords, and loose objects away from the moving belt.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when speed is controlled. Use the emergency stop, avoid stepping onto a fast belt, and stop for dizziness, chest pain, or sharp pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
