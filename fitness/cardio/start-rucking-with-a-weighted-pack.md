## Goal

Start rucking with a weighted pack while protecting the feet, knees, hips, and back from excessive load.

## Preconditions

- Supportive shoes, socks that reduce friction, and a backpack with snug straps.
- A light starting load, usually 5-10% of body weight or less for beginners.
- Flat, familiar walking route and water for longer outings.

## Steps

1. **Pack the weight high and stable.** Place weight close to the upper back, pad hard edges, and tighten straps so the pack does not bounce. → *Expect:* the load feels close to your body, not pulling backward.
2. **Warm up unloaded.** Walk 5 minutes, then add ankle circles, calf raises, and hip hinges. → *Expect:* calves, hips, and back feel ready before load is added.
3. **Start with a conservative load.** [BRANCH: new to rucking, use 5-10 lb | already walking regularly, use up to 10% body weight] → *Expect:* you can stand tall without leaning forward aggressively.
4. **Walk with short, steady steps.** Keep ribs stacked over pelvis, eyes forward, and feet landing under the hips. → *Expect:* your pack stays quiet and your breathing is controlled.
5. **Monitor hot spots and posture.** Check feet, shoulders, and low back every 5-10 minutes. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you feel sharp pain, numbness, altered gait, or back pain that increases while walking. → *Expect:* pressure is tolerable and gait remains symmetrical.
6. **Finish before form breaks.** End the first session at 15-30 minutes, remove the pack, and walk easy for 3-5 minutes. → *Expect:* legs feel trained but you could repeat the route with the same form.

## Decision points

- Pack bounces or rubs → tighten straps, repack higher, or reduce load.
- Feet develop hot spots → stop early and adjust socks, shoes, or distance.
- Low back feels compressed → reduce weight and keep the torso taller.
- Session feels easy for two weeks → add 5 minutes or 2-5 lb, not both at once.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Load too heavy:** detect forward lean, stomping, or back tightness → reduce weight immediately.
- **F2 Weight packed low:** detect the pack pulling the shoulders backward → move weight higher and closer to the spine.
- **F3 Long stride under load:** detect knee or shin discomfort → shorten steps and maintain a quiet cadence.
- **F4 Ignoring foot friction:** detect burning skin or blisters → stop, treat the hot spot, and shorten future sessions.

## Verification

You completed 15-30 minutes with a stable pack, upright posture, even steps, no blisters, and no increasing joint or back pain.

## Variations

- Easier: use an empty pack or very light load on flat ground.
- Harder: add small load increases, longer duration, or gentle hills after several pain-free sessions.
- Equipment: ruck plate, books wrapped in a towel, hiking poles, or blister-resistant socks.
- At-home: walk stairs or a hallway only with very light load and secure footing.

## Safety & privacy

Rucking adds repetitive load to the spine, hips, knees, ankles, and feet. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, limping, chest pain, or dizziness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have back, joint, cardiovascular, or balance concerns.
