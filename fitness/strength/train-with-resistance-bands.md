## Goal

You build a full-body resistance-band session with secure anchors, controlled tension, and joint-friendly movement.

## Preconditions

- Bands without cracks, tears, powdery spots, or damaged handles.
- A secure anchor point if anchoring is needed.
- Clear space so a snapped or released band will not hit your face.
- Warmup movements for shoulders, hips, and trunk.

## Steps

1. **Inspect every band.** Stretch it lightly and check for damage along the full length. → *Expect:* no visible weak points or fraying before loading.
2. **Set the anchor safely.** Attach the band to a closed door hinge side, rated anchor, or stable heavy post. ⚠️ *Safety:* never anchor to loose furniture or anything that can tip. → *Expect:* the anchor does not shift when tension increases.
3. **Choose the line of pull.** Stand so the band pulls in the same direction the target muscle should resist. → *Expect:* tension feels smooth through the full range.
4. **Train lower body.** Use band squats, good mornings, lateral walks, or split squats with knees and hips aligned. → *Expect:* hips and thighs work without knees collapsing inward.
5. **Train push and pull.** Pair band chest presses or overhead presses with rows or pull-aparts. → *Expect:* shoulder blades move smoothly and wrists stay neutral.
6. **Control both directions.** Move out against tension and return slowly instead of snapping back. → *Expect:* the band stays under control at the start and end of each rep.
7. **Adjust tension by stance.** Step farther from the anchor or use a thicker band only when reps stay clean. → *Expect:* difficulty changes without losing range of motion.

## Decision points

- Band tension is hardest only at lockout → use slower tempo and avoid forcing the final joint position.
- Anchor height changes the exercise → align it with chest, hip, or floor height as the movement requires.
- Band rubs skin or joints → change angle or use handles.
- Movement is too easy → increase stretch, double the band, or choose a thicker band.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Anchor failure:** detect movement of the door, post, or furniture → stop and choose a rated anchor.
- **F2 Snapping reps:** detect noisy or fast returns → slow the eccentric to 2-3 seconds.
- **F3 Poor alignment:** detect knees caving or wrists bending → reduce tension and reset joint position.
- **F4 Uneven stretch:** detect one side pulling harder → center the band and match hand or foot placement.

## Verification

You complete a full-body band session with secure anchoring, controlled returns, stable joint alignment, and no band slippage or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: use thinner bands, shorter sets, and seated or supported positions.
- Harder: use thicker bands, longer pauses at peak tension, or unilateral versions.
- Equipment: loop bands, tube bands, and therapy bands can all work if inspected and anchored safely.
- At-home: use door anchors on the hinge side with the door closed toward you when possible.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Damaged bands can snap; keep them away from eyes and face, stop for sharp pain or numbness, and consult a professional if you have medical or injury concerns.
