## Goal

You select a first working weight that is heavy enough to train but light enough to keep clean technique and room to progress.

## Preconditions

- The exercise technique is understood at bodyweight or with an empty implement.
- Warmup space and the intended equipment are available.
- No sharp pain during the movement unloaded.
- A target set and rep range from your program.

## Steps

1. **Practice the movement unloaded.** Perform 5-10 slow reps with full range and stable joints. → *Expect:* the movement path feels controlled before any meaningful load.
2. **Add a very light load.** Use an empty bar, light dumbbells, or the lowest machine setting. → *Expect:* the load does not change your posture or balance.
3. **Build in small jumps.** Add weight gradually for sets of 3-5 reps, resting enough to assess form. → *Expect:* effort rises without sudden technique changes.
4. **Stop before form breaks.** Choose the last load where you could do 2-4 more clean reps than required. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not test a maximum on the first day. → *Expect:* the working weight feels like practice under load, not survival.
5. **Check the target set.** Perform one work set at the chosen load and compare it to the planned reps. → *Expect:* you finish within the rep target with stable range, breathing, and joint alignment.
6. **Record the result.** Note load, reps, and how many reps you had in reserve. → *Expect:* next session has a clear starting point.

## Decision points

- You exceed the top of the rep range by more than 4 reps → increase the load slightly next set.
- You cannot reach the low end with good form → reduce the load.
- Balance or coordination is the limiter → use a simpler variation before adding weight.
- Pain appears during warmups → stop that exercise and choose a pain-free alternative.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Starting too heavy:** detect grinding, shaking, or shortened range on set one → reduce weight 10-20%.
- **F2 Confusing discomfort with pain:** detect burning muscles but stable joints → continue cautiously; sharp joint pain means stop.
- **F3 Poor setup:** detect different stance or grip each set → standardize foot, hand, and brace positions.
- **F4 No record:** detect guessing every session → write down load, reps, and effort immediately after each set.

## Verification

You complete the planned first work set at a load that leaves 2-4 reps in reserve, uses full intended range of motion, and causes no sharp pain or visible form breakdown.

## Variations

- Easier: start with bodyweight, a dowel, or machines that guide the path.
- Harder: experienced lifters can estimate from recent rep maxes, then confirm with submaximal warmups.
- Equipment: use the smallest plate, dumbbell, or band jump available.
- At-home: use household loads only when they can be held securely and do not change the movement path.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Avoid max attempts when learning, stop for sharp pain or dizziness, and consult a qualified professional if you have injuries, medical conditions, or uncertainty about safe loading.
