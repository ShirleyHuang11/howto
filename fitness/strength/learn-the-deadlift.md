## Goal

You deadlift a barbell from the floor by hinging at the hips, bracing the trunk, and keeping the bar close without rounding your back.

## Preconditions

- A barbell with plates that place the bar around mid-shin height, plus collars.
- Flat, stable shoes or deadlift slippers; avoid soft running shoes.
- You can perform `fitness/strength/master-the-hip-hinge` with a neutral spine.
- Warm up with hip hinges, glute bridges, and several light deadlift sets.

## Steps

1. **Place the bar over midfoot.** Stand with feet about hip width and the bar roughly over your shoelaces, 1-2 inches from the shins. → *Expect:* when viewed from the side, the bar is above the middle of the foot.
2. **Hinge down to grip.** Push hips back, bend knees only enough to reach the bar, and grip just outside the legs. → *Expect:* shins touch or nearly touch the bar after you set the grip.
3. **Set your back and lats.** Pull your chest up, brace the abdomen, squeeze armpits toward your pockets, and take slack out of the bar. → *Expect:* the bar clicks lightly against the plates and your spine feels locked in neutral.
4. **Push the floor away.** Drive through the whole foot and keep the bar sliding close to the legs. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop immediately if your back rounds under load or you feel sharp pain. → *Expect:* hips and shoulders rise together and the bar path stays vertical.
5. **Lock out with glutes.** Stand tall by squeezing the glutes and bringing hips through, without leaning back. → *Expect:* knees and hips are straight, ribs stay down, and the bar rests against the thighs.
6. **Lower with the same hinge.** Push hips back first, keep the bar close, then bend knees after the bar passes them. → *Expect:* the bar returns to the floor over midfoot without drifting forward.
7. **Reset each rep.** Let the plates settle, rebuild brace and lat tension, then lift again. → *Expect:* every rep starts from the same tight position.

## Decision points

- Back rounds before the bar leaves the floor → reduce load, elevate the bar, or return to hinge drills.
- Bar drifts away from the legs → engage lats and pull the bar back toward you.
- Hips shoot up first → start with hips slightly higher or lighten the bar and push through the floor.
- Grip fails before the target muscles → use chalk if allowed, mixed grip, hook grip, or straps for non-competition work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rounded lumbar spine:** detect a visible low-back curve under load → stop the set, lower weight, and practice braced hinges.
- **F2 Squatting the deadlift:** detect knees far forward and hips too low → move hips back and keep shoulders slightly in front of the bar.
- **F3 Overextended lockout:** detect leaning back at the top → finish by squeezing glutes, not by arching the spine.
- **F4 Bar scrape or swing:** detect the bar banging forward around the knees → lower by hinging first and keep lats tight.
- **F5 Tugging loose:** detect yanking before you are braced → pull slack out before the bar leaves the floor.

## Verification

Complete 3 sets of 5 deadlifts from a consistent start position with the bar over midfoot, neutral spine, close bar path, hips and shoulders rising together, and a controlled return to the floor.

## Variations

- `easier`: elevate the bar on blocks or use kettlebell deadlifts from between the feet.
- `harder`: add load gradually, use paused deadlifts, or slow eccentrics with lighter weight.
- `equipment`: use trap bar deadlifts for a more upright torso if available.
- `at-home`: practice hip hinges and loaded backpack or kettlebell deadlifts.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because heavy deadlifts load the spine, hips, and grip. Do not lift through sharp back pain, numbness, dizziness, or uncontrolled rounding, and avoid maximal attempts without competent supervision. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist if you have back injury history or medical restrictions.
