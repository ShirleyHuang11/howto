## Goal

You complete a balanced dumbbell strength session at home using safe setup, full range of motion, and progressive loading.

## Preconditions

- One or two dumbbells you can hold securely.
- Clear floor space and a sturdy bench, chair, or step if needed.
- Shoes or a stable non-slip surface.
- Starting loads chosen conservatively: `fitness/strength/choose-a-starting-weight`.

## Steps

1. **Clear and stage the space.** Move trip hazards and place dumbbells where you can pick them up without twisting. → *Expect:* you can step, hinge, press, and lower weights without obstruction.
2. **Warm up with bodyweight patterns.** Do squats, hinges, push-ups to a surface, rows without load, and shoulder circles. → *Expect:* joints feel warm and movement feels coordinated.
3. **Train a squat pattern.** Do goblet squats or split squats with knees tracking over toes and torso controlled. → *Expect:* feet stay planted and depth is repeatable.
4. **Train a hinge pattern.** Do Romanian deadlifts with hips moving back, soft knees, and a neutral spine. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if your lower back rounds or you feel sharp back pain. → *Expect:* hamstrings and glutes work more than the low back.
5. **Train a push pattern.** Do floor presses, overhead presses, or incline push-ups with ribs down and wrists stacked. → *Expect:* shoulders stay controlled and elbows track naturally.
6. **Train a pull pattern.** Do one-arm rows with a supported torso and shoulder blade moving around the rib cage. → *Expect:* the weight reaches your side without torso rotation.
7. **Finish with carry or core work.** Use suitcase carries, dead bugs, or planks with level hips. → *Expect:* trunk stays steady while breathing continues.
8. **Log sets and effort.** Record weight, reps, and which exercises need more or less load next time. → *Expect:* the next session has clear progression targets.

## Decision points

- Only one dumbbell is available → use unilateral exercises and switch sides evenly.
- Grip fails before target muscles → reduce reps or use straps only after grip training goals are considered.
- Movement feels too easy → slow tempo, add reps, or use one-leg and one-arm variations.
- Joint pain appears → reduce range or swap to a pain-free pattern.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Swinging weights:** detect momentum on curls, rows, or raises → pause at the hard position and reduce load.
- **F2 Back-dominant hinges:** detect low-back fatigue before hamstrings → push hips back and keep dumbbells close to legs.
- **F3 Uneven sides:** detect one side doing extra reps or range → start with the weaker side and match it.
- **F4 Cluttered setup:** detect stepping over weights between sets → keep a dedicated lifting zone.

## Verification

You complete one session with squat, hinge, push, pull, and core or carry work, logging all sets while maintaining stable joints, controlled tempo, and no sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: do fewer sets and use supported split squats, incline push-ups, and chest-supported rows.
- Harder: use slower eccentrics, pauses, unilateral work, or shorter rest periods.
- Equipment: adjustable dumbbells, bands, or a backpack can substitute when held securely.
- At-home: floor press replaces bench press, goblet squat replaces barbell squat, and suitcase carry replaces loaded machines.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Keep weights away from children and pets, stop for sharp pain or dizziness, and consult a professional before training if medical conditions or injuries affect exercise tolerance.
