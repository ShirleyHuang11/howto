## Goal

You structure hypertrophy training so muscles receive enough hard, recoverable work to grow over repeated weeks.

## Preconditions

- A stable weekly schedule with 2-5 lifting days.
- Exercises you can perform through a consistent, pain-free range of motion.
- A logbook and progression method: `fitness/strength/apply-progressive-overload`.
- Nutrition and sleep adequate enough to support training.

## Steps

1. **Pick stable exercises.** Choose movements that load the target muscle well and do not irritate joints. → *Expect:* you can repeat them weekly and compare performance.
2. **Use moderate weekly volume.** Start around 8-12 hard sets per muscle per week. → *Expect:* muscles feel trained but recover before the next session.
3. **Train close to failure.** Most sets should end with 0-3 reps in reserve, especially on safer accessories. → *Expect:* the target muscle, not joints or balance, limits the set.
4. **Use full useful range.** Control the stretched position and finish with the target muscle contracted without bouncing. → *Expect:* reps look the same from first to last.
5. **Rest long enough.** Use 1-3 minutes for accessories and 2-4 minutes for demanding compound lifts. → *Expect:* the next set is limited by the muscle, not breathlessness alone.
6. **Progress gradually.** Add reps within a range, then load, then volume only if recovery is good. → *Expect:* logbook numbers improve without chronic soreness.
7. **Adjust after feedback.** ⚠️ *Safety:* reduce or replace exercises that create sharp joint pain, numbness, or tendon pain that worsens across sessions. → *Expect:* hard training remains localized to working muscles.

## Decision points

- No pump, soreness, or progress for a muscle → add 2 weekly sets or choose a more stable exercise.
- Soreness lasts more than 3-4 days → reduce volume or avoid excessive lengthened-position overload.
- Strength drops across all exercises → deload or improve recovery.
- Joint pain appears before muscle fatigue → change grip, range, machine, or exercise.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Junk volume:** detect many easy sets with little effort → bring sets closer to failure before adding more.
- **F2 Load chasing:** detect shortened reps as weight rises → reduce load and restore range.
- **F3 Poor muscle targeting:** detect another muscle always limiting first → alter setup or use a more isolated variation.
- **F4 Recovery mismatch:** detect sleep disruption and constant soreness → cut volume 20-30% for one week.

## Verification

Over 6-8 weeks, target muscles receive 8-20 hard weekly sets, most sets finish within 0-3 reps in reserve, performance trends upward, and joints remain pain-free.

## Variations

- Easier: train full body 2-3 days per week with fewer exercises.
- Harder: specialize one lagging muscle by adding volume while reducing work elsewhere.
- Equipment: machines, cables, dumbbells, barbells, and bands all work if they load the target muscle well.
- At-home: use dumbbells, bands, push-ups, split squats, hip thrusts, and tempo work.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, numbness, or symptoms that worsen under training, and consult a clinician before starting if you have relevant health conditions.
