## Goal

You build a simple full-body strength routine that trains major movement patterns 2-3 days per week with recoverable volume and clear progression.

## Preconditions

- Access to basic equipment such as dumbbells, machines, bands, or bodyweight options.
- A notebook or app for logging exercises, sets, reps, and load.
- At least one rest day between full-body strength sessions.
- Medical clearance if you have a condition that affects exercise tolerance.

## Steps

1. **Pick a weekly schedule.** Choose 2 or 3 nonconsecutive training days, such as Monday and Thursday or Monday, Wednesday, Friday. → *Expect:* each strength day has at least 24-48 hours before the next similar session.
2. **Choose one squat pattern.** Use `fitness/strength/do-a-proper-bodyweight-squat`, goblet squat, leg press, or box squat. → *Expect:* the movement trains the thighs and hips with controllable knee tracking.
3. **Choose one hinge pattern.** Use `fitness/strength/master-the-hip-hinge`, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, or light deadlift variation. → *Expect:* the movement trains glutes and hamstrings without back rounding.
4. **Choose one push and one pull.** Pair push-ups, dumbbell press, or machine press with rows, pulldowns, or assisted pull-ups. → *Expect:* upper-body pushing and pulling both appear in the plan.
5. **Add a core or carry option.** Use planks, dead bugs, suitcase carries, or farmer carries. → *Expect:* trunk training supports posture and bracing without excessive fatigue.
6. **Set beginner volume.** Start with 2-3 sets of 6-12 reps per exercise, leaving 2-3 reps in reserve. → *Expect:* sets feel challenging but do not require grinding or form breakdown.
7. **Plan progression.** When all sets hit the top of the rep range with good form, add a small amount of weight or a harder variation next time. → *Expect:* the log shows one clear target for the next session.
8. **Review recovery.** ⚠️ *Safety:* reduce volume if soreness, joint pain, or fatigue worsens across sessions. → *Expect:* you can train consistently without pain accumulating.

## Decision points

- You are very sore for more than 72 hours → reduce sets or load.
- Technique breaks before the target reps → keep the same weight or regress the exercise.
- You have limited equipment → use bodyweight, bands, and unilateral movements.
- Progress stalls for 2-3 weeks → add rest, improve sleep, or adjust one variable at a time.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much volume:** detect soreness and fatigue increasing each week → cut each exercise to 1-2 sets temporarily.
- **F2 Random exercise swapping:** detect no repeated lifts to measure → keep the same main patterns for 4-8 weeks.
- **F3 Training to failure every set:** detect grinding and form breakdown → stop sets with 1-3 good reps left.
- **F4 Missing pulls:** detect only presses and squats → add rows or pulldowns every session.
- **F5 No log:** detect guessing loads each workout → record exercise, load, reps, and notes immediately.

## Verification

You have a written 2- or 3-day full-body routine with squat, hinge, push, pull, and core or carry work, plus sets, reps, rest days, and a progression rule.

## Variations

- `easier`: start with 2 days per week, 1-2 sets per exercise, and mostly machines or bodyweight.
- `harder`: use 3 days per week, add one accessory per session, or increase load gradually.
- `equipment`: build versions for gym machines, dumbbells, bands, or bodyweight.
- `at-home`: use squat-to-chair, glute bridges, incline push-ups, band rows, and planks.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when volume and load progress gradually. Stop exercises that cause sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, or unusual shortness of breath, and avoid copying advanced routines before technique is reliable. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have relevant medical conditions or have been inactive for a long time.
