## Goal

You hold a plank that trains trunk stiffness without sagging the lower back, shrugging the shoulders, or holding your breath.

## Preconditions

- Floor space and an exercise mat or comfortable surface for forearms.
- Shoulders and wrists tolerate supporting body weight.
- Warm up with easy breathing drills, shoulder circles, and glute bridges.

## Steps

1. **Set elbows under shoulders.** Place forearms parallel or hands lightly clasped, with elbows directly below shoulders. → *Expect:* shoulders feel supported rather than jammed forward.
2. **Step back into a straight line.** Extend legs and place feet hip width or slightly wider. [BRANCH: easier, keep knees on the floor | standard, support from toes] → *Expect:* head, ribs, hips, and heels form one long line.
3. **Brace and squeeze glutes.** Tighten the abdomen, lightly tuck the pelvis, and squeeze glutes without lifting hips high. → *Expect:* lower back feels neutral and abs work harder than the spine.
4. **Push the floor away.** Spread shoulder blades slightly and keep the neck long. → *Expect:* chest does not sink between the shoulders.
5. **Breathe while holding.** Take small steady breaths behind the brace instead of holding your breath. → *Expect:* you can breathe without losing body position.
6. **End before form fails.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop when hips sag, sharp pain appears, or you cannot breathe steadily. → *Expect:* the set ends with the same alignment it started with.

## Decision points

- Lower back feels strained → shorten the hold or use a knee plank.
- Shoulders fatigue first → push the floor away and check elbow position.
- Hips rise too high → lower hips until ribs and pelvis stack.
- You can hold 45-60 seconds cleanly → progress by adding limb movement or harder plank variations.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Hip sag:** detect low back arching toward the floor → squeeze glutes and shorten the set.
- **F2 Pike position:** detect hips high like an inverted V → lower hips and rebrace.
- **F3 Shoulder collapse:** detect chest sinking between arms → press forearms into the floor.
- **F4 Breath holding:** detect face tension or air hunger → use shorter holds with steady breaths.

## Verification

Hold a 30-second forearm plank with elbows under shoulders, straight body line, glutes engaged, steady breathing, and no lower-back sag.

## Variations

- `easier`: knee plank, elevated plank on a bench, or shorter 10-second holds.
- `harder`: long-lever plank, shoulder taps, side plank, or weighted plank after strong baseline control.
- `equipment`: use a mat or folded towel under the elbows.
- `at-home`: use a carpeted area or yoga mat and a timer.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when alignment is maintained. Stop for sharp shoulder, elbow, wrist, neck, or back pain, and avoid long holds that turn into sagging endurance tests. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a professional if planks aggravate a condition.
