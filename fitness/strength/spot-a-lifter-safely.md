## Goal

You assist another lifter during heavy sets without taking over good reps, missing failed reps, or creating extra risk.

## Preconditions

- The lifter has asked for a spot and explained the lift, rep target, and help signal.
- Rack height, safeties, collars, and bench position are checked.
- You are strong enough and positioned well enough to help with the expected load.
- The lifter has warmed up and knows how to bail if needed.

## Steps

1. **Agree on commands.** Confirm whether the lifter wants a lift-off, how many reps, and what words mean help. → *Expect:* both people know when you will touch the bar.
2. **Set safeties first.** Use pins or arms for bench and squat whenever possible. → *Expect:* a missed rep has a mechanical backup.
3. **Position for the lift.** For bench, stand behind the head with hands near but not on the bar; for dumbbells, support near wrists only if needed. → *Expect:* you can assist quickly without blocking the bar path.
4. **Give a clean lift-off if requested.** Help move the bar from hooks to start position, then release evenly. → *Expect:* the lifter begins with shoulders set and bar stable.
5. **Track the bar without touching good reps.** Keep hands close and eyes on bar speed, symmetry, and the lifter's face. → *Expect:* the lifter does the work unless help is clearly needed.
6. **Assist only enough to finish or re-rack.** Apply upward force evenly and guide the bar back to hooks. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not yank one side, curl the bar from far away, or let the lifter struggle after asking for help. → *Expect:* the bar returns under control.
7. **Debrief briefly.** State whether you touched the bar and where the rep slowed. → *Expect:* the lifter can log the set honestly.

## Decision points

- Load is beyond what one spotter can help → use safeties or multiple trained spotters.
- Squatting heavy → prioritize properly set safeties; only experienced spotters should body-spot squats.
- Deadlifting → do not spot the bar; clear the area and coach setup instead.
- Lifter wants forced reps repeatedly → limit assistance because fatigue raises risk.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Touching too early:** detect hands on the bar during clean reps → keep hands close but off until speed stalls or help is requested.
- **F2 Uneven assistance:** detect the bar tilting during help → use both hands symmetrically and guide to the rack.
- **F3 No agreed signal:** detect confusion mid-set → stop the set early and agree on commands before continuing.
- **F4 Unsafe station:** detect missing safeties on near-max attempts → set them or decline to spot.

## Verification

The lifter completes or safely misses the planned set with agreed commands, no surprise contact, controlled re-racking, and an honest log of whether assistance occurred.

## Variations

- Easier: spot only light bench sets while learning.
- Harder: coordinate side spotters for heavy squats, with one person calling the commands.
- Equipment: machines often need no spotter but may need help setting pins or handles.
- At-home: avoid max barbell benching alone; use dumbbells, push-ups, or safeties.

## Safety & privacy

Spotting heavy lifts carries medium risk for both people. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Decline a spot you cannot perform safely, stop for pain or loss of control, and use qualified coaching for maximal lifting.
