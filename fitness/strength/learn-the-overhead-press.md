## Goal

You press a barbell overhead with a braced trunk, stacked wrists and elbows, and a stable lockout over the midfoot.

## Preconditions

- A rack, barbell, plates, and collars, or an empty bar for practice.
- Enough overhead clearance and no loose objects above you.
- You can brace your trunk in `fitness/strength/hold-a-proper-plank` without low-back sagging.
- Warm up wrists, shoulders, upper back, and several light press sets.

## Steps

1. **Set the rack height.** Place the bar at upper chest height so you can unrack without calf raising or shrugging. → *Expect:* the bar starts near the collarbone with elbows slightly in front of it.
2. **Grip and brace.** Grip just outside shoulder width, wrap thumbs, squeeze glutes, and brace the abdomen with ribs down. → *Expect:* wrists are stacked, forearms are nearly vertical, and your lower back is not arched.
3. **Unrack and step back.** Stand tall with feet about hip width and the bar resting on the upper chest or front delts. → *Expect:* the bar is balanced over midfoot before the press starts.
4. **Press close to the face.** Move your head slightly back, press the bar upward in a close vertical path, then bring your head through once it clears the forehead. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you must lean back sharply to pass the bar. → *Expect:* the bar travels near the nose and chin without hitting them.
5. **Lock out overhead.** Finish with elbows straight, biceps near ears, traps shrugged slightly, and bar over midfoot. → *Expect:* wrist, elbow, shoulder, hip, knee, and midfoot line up from the side.
6. **Lower under control.** Move the head back, lower the bar to the upper chest, and keep the brace until the rep is over. → *Expect:* the bar returns to the same start position without crashing down.

## Decision points

- Low back arches during the press → squeeze glutes, brace harder, and reduce load.
- Bar drifts forward → press closer to the face and get the head through sooner.
- Shoulder pain overhead → stop and use pain-free dumbbell or landmine pressing.
- Lockout is unstable → lower the weight and practice controlled holds overhead.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Layback press:** detect turning the lift into a standing incline press → reduce weight and keep ribs stacked over pelvis.
- **F2 Forward bar path:** detect the bar finishing in front of the body → cue head through and bar over midfoot.
- **F3 Wrist collapse:** detect wrists bent back hard → grip lower in the palm and stack knuckles over forearms.
- **F4 Soft brace:** detect rib flare or low-back discomfort → reset with glutes and abs tight before each rep.
- **F5 Uneven press:** detect one side rising faster → use lighter sets and verify even grip width.

## Verification

Complete 3 sets of 5 overhead presses with a close bar path, ribs down, glutes tight, stable overhead lockout over midfoot, and no sharp shoulder or back pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: use dumbbell shoulder press, half-kneeling single-arm press, or an empty bar.
- `harder`: add small load increases, tempo lowers, or strict pause presses.
- `equipment`: use a landmine press if full overhead motion is uncomfortable.
- `at-home`: use resistance bands or dumbbells while keeping the same bracing cues.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because overhead loading stresses the shoulders, spine, and balance. Keep the area clear, do not press through sharp pain or major back arching, and avoid heavy attempts without solid technique. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a professional if you have shoulder, neck, back, or blood-pressure concerns.
