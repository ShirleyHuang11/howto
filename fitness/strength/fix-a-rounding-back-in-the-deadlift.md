## Goal

You identify why your back rounds during deadlifts and correct setup, bracing, and load so the pull stays controlled.

## Preconditions

- A barbell, plates, and clear platform, or a lighter hinge variation.
- Side-view video or a coach for feedback.
- Basic bracing practice: `fitness/strength/brace-your-core-under-load`.
- No acute back pain requiring medical care.

## Steps

1. **Film a light set from the side.** Use 40-60% of normal working weight for 3-5 reps. → *Expect:* you can see whether rounding starts before the pull or during the lift.
2. **Set the bar over midfoot.** Stand with the bar close, about over shoelaces, before gripping. → *Expect:* the bar can travel vertically without drifting forward.
3. **Hinge to the bar.** Push hips back, bend knees enough to reach the bar, and keep shins close. → *Expect:* shoulders are slightly in front of the bar and hips are not squatted too low.
4. **Brace and pull slack out.** Inhale low, brace, squeeze the bar, and create tension before the plates leave the floor. → *Expect:* the bar clicks or tightens without your hips shooting up.
5. **Push the floor away.** Keep the bar close and extend knees and hips together. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the set if rounding increases under load or pain appears. → *Expect:* back angle changes gradually, not suddenly.
6. **Use corrective variations.** Try block pulls, Romanian deadlifts, paused deadlifts below knee, or trap-bar pulls at lighter loads. → *Expect:* the target position can be held under easier conditions.
7. **Reload gradually.** Add weight only when video shows the same spine position across reps. → *Expect:* heavier sets look like lighter sets.

## Decision points

- Rounding happens before the bar leaves the floor → fix setup height, mobility, and brace before loading.
- Rounding happens only near heavy loads → reduce load and build strength in the weak position.
- Hips shoot up first → bring the bar closer and create more pre-pull tension.
- Back pain is sharp or radiating → stop deadlifting and consult a clinician.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bar too far forward:** detect bar drifting away from legs → start over midfoot and drag close without scraping aggressively.
- **F2 Squatting the deadlift:** detect hips too low and knees blocking the bar → raise hips until shoulders sit slightly over the bar.
- **F3 Soft brace:** detect spine changing as plates break floor → reset breath and brace before each rep.
- **F4 Mobility forcing position:** detect inability to reach the bar without rounding → use blocks or trap bar while improving hinge range.

## Verification

You complete 3 sets of 3-5 deadlift or deadlift-variant reps on video with the bar over midfoot, lats tight, neutral controlled spine, hips and shoulders rising together, and no sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: use block pulls, kettlebell deadlifts, trap-bar deadlifts, or Romanian deadlifts.
- Harder: add paused deadlifts or tempo eccentrics only after position is stable.
- Equipment: lifting straps can reduce grip distraction but do not fix bracing.
- At-home: practice hinges with a dowel along head, upper back, and pelvis.

## Safety & privacy

Deadlifts under load carry medium risk when spinal position is uncontrolled. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp, radiating, or worsening back pain, and seek coaching or clinical care when symptoms or technique faults persist.
