## Goal

You set up, load, lift, and re-rack a barbell with basic safety habits that reduce preventable injuries and equipment mistakes.

## Preconditions

- A stable barbell, plates, collars, rack, bench, or platform appropriate to the lift.
- Clear floor space with no loose items underfoot.
- A known starting weight: `fitness/strength/choose-a-starting-weight`.
- Bracing understood: `fitness/strength/brace-your-core-under-load`.

## Steps

1. **Inspect the station.** Check rack height, J-hooks, safeties, bench position, sleeve spin, and floor clearance. → *Expect:* the bar path is clear and safeties can catch a missed rep.
2. **Load both sides evenly.** Add plates in matched pairs and use collars when plates could slide. → *Expect:* the bar stays level when unracked.
3. **Use a consistent grip.** Center your hands using the knurl marks and squeeze the bar before lifting. → *Expect:* the bar feels locked to your body or hands rather than drifting.
4. **Brace before unracking.** Set your ribs over pelvis, inhale into your trunk, and create tension through feet and hands. → *Expect:* the bar leaves the rack without wobbling or collapsing posture.
5. **Perform controlled reps.** Move through the intended range with the bar over the correct midline for the lift. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the set if your spine position, shoulder control, or balance fails under load. → *Expect:* each rep follows a repeatable path.
6. **Re-rack deliberately.** Finish the rep, walk or guide the bar into both hooks, and confirm contact before releasing tension. → *Expect:* both sides settle securely before your hands leave the bar.
7. **Unload evenly.** Remove plates in alternating pairs, especially on a racked bar. → *Expect:* the bar never tips from one side being stripped first.

## Decision points

- Training alone on squat or bench → set safeties correctly and avoid true max attempts.
- Bar path drifts or twists → reduce load and check stance, grip, and bracing.
- Plates slide or rattle → use collars and inspect sleeve loading.
- You are unsure how to fail a lift → practice with an empty bar or ask a qualified coach before heavy sets.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Uneven loading:** detect a tilted bar or mismatched plates → unload and reload both sides from the inside out.
- **F2 Unsafe rack height:** detect shrugging to unrack or tiptoeing to re-rack → adjust hooks before the next set.
- **F3 Loose brace:** detect rib flare, lower-back arch collapse, or bar wobble → reset breathing and reduce load.
- **F4 Failed rep panic:** detect not knowing where the bar will go → set pins and rehearse the bailout path unloaded.

## Verification

You complete all planned barbell sets with matched plates, collars when needed, correct safeties, stable bracing, controlled bar path, and secure re-racking on every set.

## Variations

- Easier: use dumbbells, machines, or an empty bar while learning.
- Harder: use heavier barbell work only with planned warmups, safeties, and conservative attempts.
- Equipment: specialty bars may change grip or balance; learn the specific setup before loading.
- At-home: avoid improvised racks for heavy barbell lifts unless the equipment is rated and stable.

## Safety & privacy

Barbell lifting carries medium risk, especially near maximal loads or without safeties. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, or loss of control, and get coaching or medical clearance when appropriate.
