## Goal

You reduce training stress for one week so fatigue drops while skill, routine, and momentum stay intact.

## Preconditions

- At least several weeks of consistent training or clear signs of accumulated fatigue.
- A training log showing recent loads, sets, reps, and effort.
- No acute injury that requires medical evaluation instead of a deload.
- Normal sleep and nutrition as much as possible.

## Steps

1. **Identify why you are deloading.** Look for stalled lifts, persistent soreness, poor sleep, low motivation, or planned recovery after hard training. → *Expect:* the deload has a clear purpose.
2. **Reduce volume first.** Cut hard sets by about 30-50% while keeping movement patterns familiar. → *Expect:* workouts feel easier and end sooner.
3. **Reduce load if needed.** Use about 80-90% of usual load for strength lifts or leave more reps in reserve. → *Expect:* every rep moves cleanly and quickly.
4. **Avoid failure and novelty.** Do not add new exercises, max tests, or high-soreness finishers. → *Expect:* muscles feel practiced, not beaten up.
5. **Keep light movement.** Walk, do mobility, and practice technique at low effort. → *Expect:* stiffness decreases without adding fatigue.
6. **Monitor pain and readiness.** ⚠️ *Safety:* pain that worsens during an easier week is not normal training fatigue. → *Expect:* soreness and joint irritation trend down by the end of the week.
7. **Return gradually.** Resume normal training at the previous plan or slightly below it. → *Expect:* the first regular week feels sharper than the week before the deload.

## Decision points

- Fatigue is mostly muscular soreness → cut sets more than load.
- Fatigue is joint irritation → reduce load, range, or exercise selection.
- Performance improves midweek → still finish the deload instead of testing.
- Pain persists after deload → seek assessment before pushing hard again.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Deload becomes no training:** detect skipped movement and loss of routine → do short easy sessions on normal training days.
- **F2 Deload too hard:** detect grinding or soreness from new work → remove intensity techniques and reduce sets.
- **F3 Returning too aggressively:** detect immediate failed reps after deload → restart at 90-95% of prior loads.
- **F4 Ignoring injury signs:** detect sharp, localized, or worsening pain → stop the aggravating lift and consult a professional.

## Verification

You complete a 7-day deload with 30-50% less hard volume, no sets to failure, reduced soreness or fatigue, and a clear plan for the next normal training week.

## Variations

- Easier: take extra rest days and do only technique work plus walking.
- Harder: keep intensity moderately high but perform very few crisp singles or doubles.
- Equipment: deloads can use the same equipment or lighter alternatives.
- At-home: replace gym sessions with mobility, easy band work, and unloaded pattern practice.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. A deload is not treatment for injury; stop for sharp pain, swelling, numbness, or systemic symptoms, and consult a clinician when symptoms persist or worsen.
