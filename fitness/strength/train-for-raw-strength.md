## Goal

You train maximal strength by practicing heavy, technically consistent lifts while managing fatigue and risk.

## Preconditions

- Competent technique in the main lifts and safe barbell setup.
- A rack, safeties, collars, and spotter when appropriate.
- A training max or conservative estimate for programming.
- A logbook with recent sets, reps, and effort.

## Steps

1. **Choose the competition or main lifts.** Center the plan on squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, or close variants. → *Expect:* the lifts match the strength you want to express.
2. **Base loads on a training max.** Use a conservative max you could hit on a normal day, not an all-time best. → *Expect:* prescribed percentages feel challenging but realistic.
3. **Practice heavy low reps.** Use sets of 1-5 reps with long rests and precise setup. → *Expect:* each rep starts from the same brace and follows the same path.
4. **Add supportive volume.** Use lighter back-off sets and accessories for weak positions and muscle mass. → *Expect:* assistance work supports the lift without exhausting recovery.
5. **Warm up thoroughly.** Use ramp sets before heavy work: `fitness/strength/warm-up-for-a-heavy-lift`. → *Expect:* heavy attempts feel prepared rather than abrupt.
6. **Keep failure rare.** Stop heavy sets when bar speed collapses or form changes. ⚠️ *Safety:* missed maximal reps under poor position raise injury risk; use safeties and do not grind through pain. → *Expect:* most heavy reps are difficult but controlled.
7. **Plan deloads and tests.** Deload every 4-8 hard weeks or before a true max test. → *Expect:* testing happens when fatigue is low and technique is sharp.

## Decision points

- Heavy singles are slow every week → lower training max or reduce volume.
- Technique breaks only near max loads → add paused or tempo variants at lower intensity.
- Accessories interfere with main lifts → reduce accessory volume first.
- Pain changes setup or bar path → stop heavy loading and assess the movement.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Maxing too often:** detect weekly failed attempts → replace maxes with heavy singles at 7-8 effort.
- **F2 Technique drift:** detect different depth, touch point, or lockout under load → use lighter practice sets and video review.
- **F3 Inadequate rest:** detect breathlessness limiting heavy triples → rest 3-5 minutes for primary sets.
- **F4 Ignoring recovery:** detect falling numbers across all lifts → take a deload week and reset the training max.

## Verification

After a training block, your tested or estimated max improves while heavy sets are completed with consistent depth or range, stable bracing, controlled bar path, and no pain-driven compensation.

## Variations

- Easier: use submaximal 3-5 rep work and avoid true one-rep maxes.
- Harder: peak for a meet or test with planned singles and reduced accessory fatigue.
- Equipment: specialty bars, blocks, pins, and pauses can target weak points.
- At-home: raw strength work is limited without safe heavy equipment; use unilateral and tempo work instead.

## Safety & privacy

Heavy strength training carries medium risk. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Use safeties and spotters, stop for sharp pain or neurological symptoms, and seek professional guidance for maximal lifting or medical concerns.
