## Goal

You create trunk stiffness under load so force transfers through your hips and shoulders without uncontrolled spinal motion.

## Preconditions

- A light load, empty bar, or bodyweight movement to practice with.
- No pain with normal breathing or unloaded trunk movement.
- Space to stand, squat, hinge, and carry.
- Optional belt for advanced heavy sets, not for learning the basic brace.

## Steps

1. **Stack ribs over pelvis.** Stand tall without flaring ribs or tucking hard under. → *Expect:* ears, ribs, and pelvis line up over midfoot.
2. **Inhale into the trunk.** Breathe low and wide into belly, sides, and back rather than lifting shoulders. → *Expect:* your waist expands in all directions.
3. **Create pressure.** Tighten as if preparing to be bumped while keeping the breath held briefly or controlled. → *Expect:* the trunk feels firm without clenching the neck.
4. **Practice with low-load movement.** Squat, hinge, press, or carry while maintaining the brace through the hard portion. → *Expect:* spine position stays stable as limbs move.
5. **Exhale after the sticking point.** Release air gradually when the rep is past its hardest phase. → *Expect:* you do not lose posture at the bottom or during the pull.
6. **Reset between reps when needed.** For heavy reps, breathe and brace again before the next rep. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if bracing causes dizziness, sharp pain, or pressure symptoms. → *Expect:* every rep starts with the same trunk tension.
7. **Use a belt correctly if worn.** Push your trunk into the belt evenly; do not use it to replace breathing and tension. → *Expect:* the belt gives feedback while your torso stays active.

## Decision points

- Lower back arches hard during lifts → bring ribs down and reduce load.
- You cannot breathe between reps → use smaller sets or lighter weight while practicing.
- Brace disappears at the bottom of a squat → pause lighter reps and reset pressure before descending.
- Dizziness occurs → stop, sit down, and avoid prolonged breath holds.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Chest-only breathing:** detect shoulders rising on inhale → place hands on sides and breathe into them.
- **F2 Over-tucking pelvis:** detect rounded lower back before lifting → find a neutral pelvis instead of squeezing glutes constantly.
- **F3 Relaxing under load:** detect torso collapse at the sticking point → reset brace before each rep and lower weight.
- **F4 Belt dependence:** detect poor control without a belt → practice unloaded bracing and carries beltless.

## Verification

You perform 5 controlled loaded reps or a 30-second carry with ribs stacked, waist pressure maintained, neutral spine, steady balance, and no dizziness or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: practice dead bugs, planks, and suitcase carries with light loads.
- Harder: apply the brace to heavy squats, deadlifts, presses, and carries.
- Equipment: belts provide feedback but do not replace trunk control.
- At-home: use a backpack or suitcase carry to practice bracing while walking.

## Safety & privacy

Loaded bracing can involve breath holding and medium risk during heavy lifts. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for dizziness, chest pain, pelvic pressure, or sharp back pain, and consult a professional if you have cardiovascular, pelvic-floor, or spine concerns.
