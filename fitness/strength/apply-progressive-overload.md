## Goal

You make training gradually harder so strength or muscle can improve without turning every workout into a max test.

## Preconditions

- A written program with exercises, sets, reps, and rest periods.
- A way to record load, reps, and perceived effort: `fitness/strength/log-your-lifts`.
- Technique that stays consistent across sets.
- Warmup sets completed before work sets.

## Steps

1. **Define the target range.** Pick a rep range such as 3 sets of 8-12 for each lift. → *Expect:* you know what counts as the low and high end before loading the exercise.
2. **Start below failure.** Choose a load that leaves 1-3 reps in reserve on the first week. → *Expect:* the last reps require focus but do not grind or change shape.
3. **Add reps before load.** Keep the same weight until all sets reach the top of the rep range cleanly. → *Expect:* progress appears as more total reps at the same form standard.
4. **Increase load modestly.** Add the smallest practical jump once the top range is achieved. → *Expect:* reps drop back toward the low end but still meet the target.
5. **Hold technique constant.** Compare depth, range of motion, tempo, and rest periods before counting a personal record. → *Expect:* the new performance is not created by shorter reps or longer rest.
6. **Use recovery feedback.** Track soreness, sleep, appetite, and motivation. ⚠️ *Safety:* reduce load or volume if pain changes your movement. → *Expect:* hard weeks are challenging but do not degrade normal movement.
7. **Deload when progress degrades.** After several hard weeks or repeated missed targets, reduce volume or load for a week. → *Expect:* the next normal week feels sharper, not heavier.

## Decision points

- All sets hit the top of the range with clean form → add a small load next time.
- Form breaks before the low end of the range → reduce load or use an easier variation.
- Same lift stalls for 2-3 weeks → check sleep, calories, and total weekly volume.
- Joint pain increases as load rises → pause progression and choose a friendlier variation.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Ego jumps:** detect large load increases followed by missed reps → return to the last clean load and use smaller jumps.
- **F2 Hidden cheating:** detect shortened range or bouncing → slow the eccentric and film one set from the side.
- **F3 Too many variables:** detect changes in load, reps, sets, and rest all at once → progress only one variable for 2-4 weeks.
- **F4 No recovery:** detect worse performance across several exercises → take a deload week and reduce non-training stress where possible.

## Verification

Across 4-6 weeks, at least one key lift improves by load or reps while range of motion, tempo, rest periods, and pain-free form remain consistent.

## Variations

- Easier: use double progression, adding reps first and load only after reaching the top of the range.
- Harder: use planned top sets plus back-off sets for advanced strength work.
- Equipment: machines and dumbbells may need larger jumps, so add reps or tempo before weight.
- At-home: progress with slower tempo, pauses, range of motion, unilateral work, or added sets.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp pain, faintness, chest pain, or neurological symptoms, and consult a clinician before starting or progressing training if you have relevant health conditions.
