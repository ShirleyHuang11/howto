## Goal

You perform a bent-over row with a stable hip hinge, neutral spine, and shoulder blades moving without yanking the weight.

## Preconditions

- A barbell, dumbbells, or kettlebells with a load you can control.
- Flat floor and enough space to hinge without hitting equipment.
- You can hold the hinge pattern from `fitness/strength/master-the-hip-hinge`.
- Warm up with light hinges, band pull-aparts, and light rows.

## Steps

1. **Set your stance and load.** Stand hip width with the weight in front of you or in your hands. → *Expect:* feet feel balanced and the load is not pulling you onto your toes.
2. **Hinge into position.** Push hips back, soften knees, brace the trunk, and keep the spine neutral. → *Expect:* torso is angled forward with hamstrings lightly loaded and back flat.
3. **Let arms hang under shoulders.** Keep neck long and shoulders away from ears before the pull. → *Expect:* the weight hangs still without swinging.
4. **Row toward the lower ribs.** Pull elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades toward each other, and keep the torso quiet. → *Expect:* the weight touches or approaches the lower ribs while your spine position stays unchanged.
5. **Lower with control.** Let the shoulder blades spread naturally and extend the arms without losing the hinge. → *Expect:* the weight returns under the shoulders without a bounce.
6. **Reset tension each rep.** Rebrace and keep the hinge before starting the next pull. → *Expect:* every rep looks the same and does not become a deadlift or shrug.

## Decision points

- Lower back fatigues before the upper back → reduce load or use chest-supported rows.
- Weight swings → pause at the bottom and use a lighter weight.
- Neck strains → look at the floor a few feet ahead and keep the chin tucked.
- You cannot keep a hinge → practice `fitness/strength/master-the-hip-hinge` or use one-arm supported rows.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Torso heave:** detect the chest jerking up to move the weight → lower the load and pause each rep.
- **F2 Rounded back:** detect spine flexing in the hinge → stop, rebrace, and reduce range or load.
- **F3 Shrugging:** detect shoulders rising toward ears → pull elbows toward hips and keep neck long.
- **F4 Elbow flare:** detect elbows wide and shoulders pinching → row slightly lower toward the ribs.

## Verification

Complete 3 sets of 8 rows with a neutral spine, steady hip hinge, controlled pull to the lower ribs, and no torso swinging.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a chest-supported machine row or one-arm dumbbell row with a hand on a bench.
- `harder`: use a paused top position, slower lowering, or heavier load only if hinge stays stable.
- `equipment`: use dumbbells, kettlebells, barbell, cable, or resistance bands.
- `at-home`: row a backpack or resistance band anchored securely at foot level.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when the load is controlled, but poor bracing can irritate the lower back. Stop for sharp back, shoulder, or elbow pain, and avoid jerking heavy weights from a rounded position. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a professional for persistent pain or relevant medical conditions.
