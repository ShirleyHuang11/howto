## Goal

You set up, unrack, squat, and rerack a barbell back squat with a stable brace, consistent depth, and safe bar path.

## Preconditions

- A squat rack with safety pins or arms set just below your bottom squat position.
- A straight barbell, collars, and plates appropriate for your current strength.
- You can already perform `fitness/strength/do-a-proper-bodyweight-squat` without pain.
- Warm up with unloaded squats, hip hinges, and several lighter barbell sets before work sets.

## Steps

1. **Set the safeties and bar height.** Place safeties just below your lowest controlled depth and set the bar around upper chest to shoulder height. → *Expect:* you can squat to depth without touching safeties, but they would catch a failed rep.
2. **Create your shelf.** Duck under the bar, pull shoulder blades together, grip evenly, and place the bar on the upper traps for high-bar or rear delts for low-bar. → *Expect:* the bar rests on muscle, not the neck or spine.
3. **Brace and unrack cleanly.** Stand the bar up with both feet under it, then take 1-2 small steps back into your squat stance. → *Expect:* the bar feels centered over the midfoot and the walkout is short and controlled.
4. **Descend with tension.** Inhale, brace hard, unlock hips and knees together, and keep the bar over the midfoot. → *Expect:* knees track over toes and the bar path stays nearly vertical.
5. **Hit honest depth.** Squat until the hip crease is at least near knee level if mobility and comfort allow. [BRANCH: new lifter, use a controlled depth you can own | experienced lifter, reach competition-depth or training target depth] ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the set if the lower back rounds under load or pain appears. → *Expect:* depth is consistent and the torso angle does not suddenly change.
6. **Drive up without losing position.** Push the floor away, keep your brace, and let hips and chest rise together. → *Expect:* knees do not cave and the bar does not drift forward.
7. **Rerack deliberately.** Step forward until both uprights contact the bar, then bend knees slightly to set it into the hooks. → *Expect:* both sides of the bar settle securely before you let go.

## Decision points

- Bar rolls or digs into the neck → adjust hand width, squeeze upper back harder, and reposition the bar on muscle.
- Knees cave or hips shoot up → reduce the load and practice tempo squats.
- You cannot control depth → raise the safeties, use lighter weight, or return to goblet squats.
- You miss a rep → stay braced, lower to the safeties, and move out from under the bar calmly.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Forward bar drift:** detect pressure shifting to the toes → brace harder, keep the bar over midfoot, and lower the weight.
- **F2 Knee cave:** detect knees collapsing inward on the ascent → cue knees out and strengthen with lighter controlled reps.
- **F3 Good-morning squat:** detect hips rising faster than shoulders → reduce load and drive the upper back into the bar.
- **F4 Unsafe walkout:** detect extra steps or wobbling before each set → use fewer steps and reset before squatting.
- **F5 Depth collapse:** detect relaxing into the bottom → maintain tension and stop at the deepest stable position.

## Verification

Complete 3 sets of 5 back squats with safeties set, stable walkout, bar over midfoot, knees tracking over toes, consistent depth, and no lower-back rounding under load.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a goblet squat, box squat, or empty bar squat.
- `harder`: add load gradually, use pause squats, or progress from high-bar to low-bar only if coached.
- `equipment`: use squat shoes for ankle support or flat shoes if they suit your mechanics.
- `at-home`: practice bodyweight and goblet squats until rack access is available.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because a loaded barbell can injure the back, knees, hips, or shoulders if control is lost. Always use safeties or spotters, avoid ego-lifting, and stop for sharp pain, numbness, dizziness, or repeated form breakdown. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a qualified professional if you have relevant injuries or medical restrictions.
