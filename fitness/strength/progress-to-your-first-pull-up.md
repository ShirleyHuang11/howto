## Goal

You build the grip, shoulder control, and pulling strength needed to perform your first strict pull-up.

## Preconditions

- A secure pull-up bar that can hold your body weight.
- Optional resistance band, box, or assisted pull-up machine.
- Shoulders tolerate hanging without sharp pain.
- Warm up with arm circles, scapular pulls, light rows, and wrist movement.

## Steps

1. **Check the bar and grip.** Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width with thumbs wrapped or securely opposed. → *Expect:* the bar does not move and your hands feel secure.
2. **Practice an active hang.** Hang with straight elbows, pull shoulder blades slightly down, and keep ribs from flaring. → *Expect:* shoulders feel engaged rather than jammed by your ears.
3. **Train scapular pulls.** From an active hang, move only the shoulder blades down and slightly together, then relax back to active hang. → *Expect:* your body rises a small amount without bending elbows.
4. **Use assisted full reps.** [BRANCH: band, place one foot or knee in the band | machine, set assistance that lets you move smoothly | box, use the feet lightly] Pull chest toward the bar by driving elbows down. → *Expect:* chin reaches bar height without kicking or craning the neck.
5. **Lower slowly.** Take 2-5 seconds to descend from top to active hang. → *Expect:* elbows extend under control and shoulders stay engaged.
6. **Add negatives when ready.** Step or jump to the top, hold briefly, then lower as slowly as possible. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if shoulder pain is sharp or you lose control of the descent. → *Expect:* descent lasts at least 3 seconds without dropping.
7. **Test a strict single.** Start from active hang, brace, drive elbows down, and pull until the chin clears the bar. → *Expect:* no kipping, leg swing, or partial range is needed.

## Decision points

- You cannot hang comfortably → start with feet-assisted hangs and rows.
- Assisted reps are jerky → increase assistance and slow the tempo.
- Elbows or shoulders hurt → reduce volume, use neutral-grip pulls, or stop and seek guidance.
- You can do 3 clean singles → begin sets of low reps with full rest.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Passive shoulder hang:** detect shoulders shrugged into ears → practice active hangs before pulling.
- **F2 Kipping:** detect leg swing or hip snap → brace legs slightly forward and use more assistance.
- **F3 Half reps:** detect chin never clearing or elbows not extending → reduce difficulty and use full range.
- **F4 Grip failure:** detect hands opening before back fatigues → add dead hangs and use chalk if allowed.
- **F5 Neck reach:** detect chin jutting upward → pull chest up instead of reaching the face to the bar.

## Verification

Perform 1 strict pull-up from active hang to chin over bar, then lower under control with no kick, swing, shoulder pain, or partial range.

## Variations

- `easier`: inverted rows, band-assisted pull-ups, machine-assisted pull-ups, or feet-assisted pull-ups.
- `harder`: multiple strict reps, tempo negatives, pauses at the top, or weighted pull-ups after a strong base.
- `equipment`: use a neutral-grip bar if straight-bar grip irritates shoulders or elbows.
- `at-home`: use a doorway pull-up bar only if it is installed according to the manufacturer instructions.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk with a secure bar and controlled reps, but falls and shoulder irritation are possible. Confirm equipment stability, avoid jumping to a bar you cannot control, and stop for sharp shoulder, elbow, wrist, or neck pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a professional for persistent pain or prior shoulder injury.
