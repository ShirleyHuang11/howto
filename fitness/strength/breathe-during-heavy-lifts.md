## Goal

You coordinate breathing and bracing during heavy lifts so your trunk stays stable without unnecessary breath-holding risk.

## Preconditions

- Basic bracing skill: `fitness/strength/brace-your-core-under-load`.
- A lift you can perform with light and moderate loads.
- No medical restriction against breath holding or heavy resistance training.
- Safety pins, spotter, or controlled implement choice for heavy attempts.

## Steps

1. **Set posture before breathing.** Stack ribs over pelvis and place feet or hands for the lift. → *Expect:* you are balanced before taking air.
2. **Take a low, wide inhale.** Breathe into belly, sides, and back rather than into the upper chest. → *Expect:* your trunk expands evenly.
3. **Brace around the air.** Tighten the abdominal wall and back muscles without shrugging or clenching your jaw. → *Expect:* torso feels rigid but controlled.
4. **Hold through the hardest portion.** For heavy single reps, maintain pressure through descent and the sticking point. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not use long breath holds if you become dizzy, see spots, or have medical contraindications. → *Expect:* posture does not buckle when force peaks.
5. **Exhale after control is regained.** Let air out gradually near lockout or once the implement is secure. → *Expect:* the bar or weight stays on path while air releases.
6. **Reset for repeated heavy reps.** Take a new breath and brace at the top or safe start point before each rep. → *Expect:* later reps start as organized as the first.
7. **Use continuous breathing for lighter sets.** Inhale during the easier phase and exhale through effort when loads are not maximal. → *Expect:* you stay stable without excessive pressure.

## Decision points

- You get lightheaded → stop the set, sit or stand supported, and use shorter breath holds or lighter loads.
- High-rep set feels panicked → switch to steady inhale-exhale rhythm and reduce load.
- Brace leaks at the bottom → reset breathing before descending and avoid rushing.
- Medical condition affects blood pressure or eyes → ask a clinician before heavy breath-hold lifting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Exhaling too early:** detect torso softening before the sticking point → hold pressure longer and reduce load while practicing.
- **F2 Holding too long:** detect dizziness after multi-rep sets → breathe at the top between reps.
- **F3 Neck tension:** detect jaw clenching or headaches → relax face and create pressure in the trunk instead.
- **F4 Chest breathing:** detect shoulders rising and ribs flaring → practice low 360-degree breathing.

## Verification

You complete 3-5 heavy but submaximal reps with a fresh breath and brace before each rep, stable trunk position through the sticking point, controlled exhale, and no dizziness or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: practice with goblet squats, carries, or empty-bar lifts.
- Harder: apply precise breath resets to heavy singles, doubles, and triples.
- Equipment: belts can improve feedback for 360-degree pressure.
- At-home: use dumbbell or backpack squats and hinges to practice timing before heavier gym lifts.

## Safety & privacy

Heavy-lift breathing carries medium risk for people sensitive to pressure changes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for dizziness, chest pain, severe headache, vision changes, or faintness, and consult a clinician if you have cardiovascular, eye, or pelvic-floor concerns.
