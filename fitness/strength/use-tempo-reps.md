## Goal

You use planned lifting tempo to improve control, consistency, and training stimulus without needing heavier loads.

## Preconditions

- An exercise you can perform safely with normal tempo.
- A load light enough to control through the full range.
- A timer, metronome, or simple count.
- Warmup sets completed.

## Steps

1. **Choose the tempo purpose.** Use slower lowering for control, pauses for position, or faster intent for power with light loads. → *Expect:* the tempo has a clear training reason.
2. **Write the tempo.** Use a simple count such as 3 seconds down, 1-second pause, controlled up. → *Expect:* every rep has the same timing target.
3. **Reduce the load.** Start 10-30% lighter than usual because time under tension is higher. → *Expect:* the first set feels controlled, not unexpectedly maximal.
4. **Control the eccentric.** Lower with the target muscle loaded and joints tracking cleanly. → *Expect:* no dropping, bouncing, or position loss.
5. **Pause without relaxing.** Hold the chosen position while maintaining tension and breath control. → *Expect:* the weight stays still without sinking deeper or wobbling.
6. **Lift with intent.** Drive up smoothly while keeping the same path. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if slow reps force joint pain or spinal position loss. → *Expect:* the rep finishes in control, even if speed is not fast.
7. **Stop before tempo breaks.** End the set when you can no longer match the count. → *Expect:* all counted reps are comparable.

## Decision points

- Tempo breaks before target reps → lower the weight or shorten the set.
- Pauses create joint discomfort → pause slightly above the painful position or choose another variation.
- Goal is maximal strength → use tempo as assistance, not for all heavy work.
- Goal is hypertrophy at home → tempo can replace some load when equipment is limited.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Fake counting:** detect rushed seconds near the end → count out loud or use a metronome.
- **F2 Relaxed pauses:** detect bouncing out of the bottom → pause with active tension and lighter load.
- **F3 Too much soreness:** detect prolonged soreness after slow eccentrics → reduce sets next session.
- **F4 Technique distortion:** detect changing range to satisfy the count → prioritize full range over more reps.

## Verification

You complete the planned sets with the assigned tempo on every rep, full intended range of motion, controlled pauses, and no joint pain or bouncing.

## Variations

- Easier: use a 2-second lower with no pause.
- Harder: use 3-5 second eccentrics or 1-3 second pauses in weak positions.
- Equipment: tempo works with bodyweight, dumbbells, machines, barbells, and bands.
- At-home: slow split squats, push-ups, rows, and hip hinges can be challenging with little load.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Slow reps increase fatigue and soreness; stop for sharp pain, numbness, dizziness, or form collapse, and consult a professional if symptoms persist.
