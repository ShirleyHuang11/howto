## Goal

You record strength training clearly enough to repeat good work, apply progressive overload, and spot recovery problems early.

## Preconditions

- A notebook, spreadsheet, or workout app.
- A program or exercise list with sets and reps.
- Honest willingness to record effort and form quality, not just best numbers.
- Basic knowledge of the units used by your equipment.

## Steps

1. **Create a simple template.** Include date, exercise, load, reps, sets, rest, effort, and notes. → *Expect:* every useful training variable has a place.
2. **Record work sets immediately.** Write each set after it happens, not after the workout. → *Expect:* numbers are accurate instead of reconstructed from memory.
3. **Note effort honestly.** Use reps in reserve or a 1-10 effort rating. → *Expect:* a hard clean set and an ugly max are not treated the same.
4. **Track form checkpoints.** Add short notes like depth good, grip slipped, back rounded, or pain-free. → *Expect:* future progress reflects technique quality.
5. **Record context.** Note sleep, soreness, bodyweight, or stress when they clearly affected performance. → *Expect:* bad sessions have clues, not mystery.
6. **Review before the next session.** Choose the next load or rep goal from the previous clean performance. → *Expect:* progression decisions are evidence-based.
7. **Protect privacy.** If using an app, limit public sharing of location, schedule, and body metrics. → *Expect:* your training data helps you without exposing more than intended.

## Decision points

- You hit the top of the rep range cleanly → increase load next time.
- You missed the target with good effort → repeat the load or reduce slightly.
- Pain or form breakdown appears → mark it and change the next session.
- App entry is too slow → use a simpler note format you will actually maintain.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Incomplete sets:** detect missing reps or loads → simplify the template to the few fields you need.
- **F2 Inflated numbers:** detect logging assisted or partial reps as normal → label assistance and range honestly.
- **F3 No review habit:** detect opening the log only after workouts → check the last entry before warming up.
- **F4 Oversharing:** detect public routes, gym times, or body data exposed → change privacy settings.

## Verification

You can open your log and see the last two weeks of workouts with exercise, load, sets, reps, effort, key form notes, and a clear next-session target for each main lift.

## Variations

- Easier: use paper with one line per exercise.
- Harder: track estimated one-rep max, weekly volume, and fatigue trends.
- Equipment: note machine model or cable station because resistance can vary.
- At-home: record improvised load details such as backpack contents or band thickness.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Logs can reveal health, location, and schedule patterns; keep sensitive data private, and stop or seek professional help when logged symptoms show persistent pain or decline.
