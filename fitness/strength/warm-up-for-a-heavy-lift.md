## Goal

You prepare joints, tissues, technique, and nervous system for a heavy barbell or dumbbell lift without tiring yourself out before the work sets.

## Preconditions

- The main lift and work weight are planned.
- The rack, bench, plates, collars, and safety equipment are set correctly.
- You can perform the movement unloaded without pain.
- Enough time is available for gradual warmup sets.

## Steps

1. **Raise general temperature.** Do 5-8 minutes of easy cardio or dynamic movement that uses the target joints. → *Expect:* breathing is slightly elevated and the first unloaded reps feel smoother.
2. **Mobilize the exact positions.** Use controlled bodyweight reps, hinges, squats, band pull-aparts, or shoulder circles specific to the lift. → *Expect:* the bottom and lockout positions feel accessible without forcing range.
3. **Start with the empty implement.** Perform 8-10 precise reps using the same stance, grip, brace, and bar path planned for working sets. → *Expect:* the pattern is crisp and symmetrical.
4. **Ramp with low-rep sets.** Add weight in 3-5 jumps, reducing reps as load rises, such as 5, 3, 2, 1 before work sets. → *Expect:* each warmup feels heavier but not fatiguing.
5. **Rest as the load gets heavy.** Use short rests early and 2-4 minutes before the final warmup. → *Expect:* breathing and focus return before the next set.
6. **Run a readiness check.** Confirm bar speed, balance, brace, and joint comfort on the final warmup. ⚠️ *Safety:* if the final warmup causes pain or major form loss, lower the planned work weight. → *Expect:* the work weight feels like a natural next step, not a surprise.
7. **Begin work sets with the same setup.** Do not change stance, grip, rack height, or tempo after the warmup unless correcting a clear issue. → *Expect:* the first work set resembles the best warmup rep.

## Decision points

- Final warmup moves slowly but cleanly → reduce the first work set 5-10% or keep more reps in reserve.
- Joint pain appears only under load → stop that lift and use a pain-free variation.
- Warmups feel unstable → add one lighter technical set instead of jumping heavier.
- Work sets are high-rep and moderate load → use fewer ramp sets to avoid fatigue.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much volume:** detect tired legs or arms before work sets → cut warmup reps and keep only ramp singles or doubles near working weight.
- **F2 Random technique:** detect different foot or hand placement each set → mark the setup and repeat it exactly.
- **F3 Skipping safety setup:** detect pins, spotters, or collars missing → set them before adding meaningful weight.
- **F4 Ignoring warning pain:** detect sharp or escalating pain in warmups → stop loading and substitute a pain-free movement.

## Verification

You reach the first heavy work set after 4-7 warmup sets, with stable setup, controlled bar path, normal joint sensation, and no fatigue that reduces planned performance.

## Variations

- Easier: add an extra light technique set before loading.
- Harder: advanced lifters can use heavier singles near working weight, but keep them fast.
- Equipment: machines need fewer safety checks but still need gradual load increases.
- At-home: warm up with bodyweight versions, light dumbbells, and slower tempo before heavy household or dumbbell loads.

## Safety & privacy

Heavy lifting carries medium risk. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, numbness, or loss of control, and consult a qualified coach or clinician if you have injury history or medical concerns.
