## Goal

You organize strength training into upper-body and lower-body days so each muscle group gets enough work and recovery.

## Preconditions

- Four training slots per week, ideally with at least one rest day after two sessions.
- Access to basic strength equipment or suitable home substitutions.
- Starting loads selected conservatively: `fitness/strength/choose-a-starting-weight`.
- A logbook or app ready: `fitness/strength/log-your-lifts`.

## Steps

1. **Set the weekly order.** Use upper, lower, rest, upper, lower, then two flexible recovery days. → *Expect:* each muscle group has about 48-72 hours before being trained hard again.
2. **Choose one main lift pattern per day.** Put a press and row on upper days; put a squat or hinge pattern first on lower days. → *Expect:* the hardest movement happens while you are fresh.
3. **Add balanced accessory work.** Pair horizontal push with horizontal pull, vertical push with vertical pull, knee-dominant work with hip-dominant work, and core work with loaded carries or anti-rotation. → *Expect:* the session trains opposing patterns without one joint taking all the stress.
4. **Use workable volume.** Start with 2-4 hard sets per exercise and 8-14 total hard sets per major muscle group per week. → *Expect:* you finish tired but could repeat the plan next week.
5. **Keep reps honest.** Stop most sets with 1-3 reps in reserve unless testing is planned. → *Expect:* speed slows slightly near the end, but technique does not collapse.
6. **Progress one variable at a time.** Add a rep, a small amount of load, or one set only when the target reps are clean. → *Expect:* progress is visible in the log without sudden jumps in soreness or joint pain.
7. **Recover between sessions.** Sleep, eat enough protein, and keep light movement on rest days. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop or reduce volume if joint pain worsens during warmups. → *Expect:* you begin most workouts feeling ready after the first few warmup sets.

## Decision points

- Workouts take longer than 75 minutes → cut accessory sets before cutting the main lift.
- Lower back stays sore between lower days → reduce hinge volume and check bracing.
- Pressing stalls but rows progress → add triceps or shoulder work, not more chest volume automatically.
- Missing two sessions in a week → resume the next planned workout instead of cramming all sessions together.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much overlap:** detect sore shoulders on every upper day → reduce pressing sets and add pulling balance.
- **F2 Lower-body fatigue spillover:** detect slow warmups and heavy legs every session → place a rest day between lower and upper if needed.
- **F3 Program hopping:** detect changing exercises weekly with no comparable log entries → keep main lifts stable for 6-8 weeks.
- **F4 Poor effort control:** detect failed reps every session → leave 1-3 reps in reserve and progress slower.

## Verification

You complete four upper/lower sessions in one week, log every working set, train each major pattern twice, and finish all main lifts with stable posture and no sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: run three days per week by alternating upper, lower, upper one week and lower, upper, lower the next.
- Harder: add a fifth light technique or conditioning day if recovery is consistently good.
- Equipment: use dumbbells, bands, machines, or bodyweight patterns when barbells are unavailable.
- At-home: use split squats, push-ups, rows under a sturdy table, hip thrusts, and loaded carries.

## Safety & privacy

General fitness guidance, not medical advice. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, numbness, or worsening joint symptoms, and consult a qualified professional if you have a medical condition, injury history, or are unsure whether strength training is appropriate.
