## Goal

You improve your mile time by combining pacing, interval work, easy running, and recovery instead of racing every run.

## Preconditions

- Ability to run continuously for at least 10 minutes without pain.
- Running shoes, a track or measured route, and a watch or timer.
- A warmup before faster running, such as easy jogging and dynamic leg swings.

## Steps

1. **Record a baseline mile.** Warm up, then run one hard but controlled mile and note splits. → *Expect:* you have a current time and know whether you faded or started too fast.
2. **Set a realistic target pace.** Choose a goal 5 to 10 percent faster than baseline for the first training block. → *Expect:* each lap or quarter-mile split has a target time.
3. **Run easy most days.** Do 2 to 3 easy runs per week at a conversational pace. → *Expect:* breathing is controlled and legs recover by the next day.
4. **Add one interval session weekly.** Run 6 to 8 repeats of 200 to 400 meters at goal mile pace with full easy recovery. → *Expect:* the final repeat is hard but form stays tall.
5. **Add one tempo or hill session weekly.** [BRANCH: tempo, run 10 to 15 minutes comfortably hard | hills, run 6 to 8 short uphill efforts with walk-down recovery] → *Expect:* effort is strong but not a sprint.
6. **Practice efficient form.** Keep posture tall, arms driving back, foot landing under the body, and cadence quick but relaxed. → *Expect:* you do not overstride or bounce excessively.
7. **Recover deliberately.** Leave at least one easy or rest day after hard sessions. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop speed work if sharp pain, limping, chest pain, or dizziness appears. → *Expect:* soreness settles within 24 to 48 hours.
8. **Retest after four weeks.** Warm up the same way and run another measured mile with even or slightly negative splits. → *Expect:* the last quarter is difficult but controlled, not a collapse.

## Decision points

- You fade badly after the first lap → start 3 to 5 seconds slower per quarter next test.
- Legs ache every run → reduce interval volume and add rest.
- Target pace feels easy for all repeats → slightly reduce recovery or add one repeat before increasing speed.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Racing workouts:** detect every session becoming all-out → cap hard days at two per week.
- **F2 Overstriding:** detect heel striking far ahead and braking → shorten stride and increase cadence slightly.
- **F3 Skipping warmups:** detect tight calves or hamstrings in the first repeat → jog 10 minutes and add drills before speed.
- **F4 No progression:** detect identical times after several weeks → check sleep, easy-day effort, and interval consistency.

## Verification

After 28 days, you run a measured mile faster than baseline or complete goal-pace quarter-mile splits with upright posture, steady breathing rhythm, and no pain or limping.

## Variations

- `easier`: train for a steady continuous mile before adding interval workouts.
- `harder`: add race-pace 600-meter repeats for advanced pacing tolerance.
- `equipment`: use a GPS watch, track, or measured road loop for reliable splits.
- `at-home`: use treadmill intervals with a 1 percent incline if outdoor running is unavailable.

## Safety & privacy

Running intensity can aggravate tendons, bones, joints, or the cardiovascular system. Stop for sharp pain, limping, chest pain, faintness, or unusual shortness of breath, and seek professional care for persistent symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
