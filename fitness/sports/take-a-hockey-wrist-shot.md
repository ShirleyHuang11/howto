## Goal

You take a hockey wrist shot with weight transfer, blade control, and follow-through so the puck leaves quickly and accurately.

## Preconditions

- A stick matched to your height, a puck or training puck, and a safe shooting surface.
- A net, shooting pad, or clear target with no people in the shooting lane.
- Skates or athletic shoes appropriate to the surface.

## Steps

1. **Set an athletic stance.** Stand with knees bent, chest tall, hands away from the body, and puck slightly behind the lead foot. → *Expect:* you feel balanced and ready to shift weight.
2. **Load the puck on the blade.** Cup the puck near the heel or mid-blade with the top hand out and bottom hand lower on the shaft. → *Expect:* the puck stays attached to the blade as you begin the pull.
3. **Shift weight to the back leg.** Let hips and shoulders coil slightly without collapsing the chest. → *Expect:* pressure builds through the back skate or foot.
4. **Pull and press through the shaft.** Drag the puck forward while the bottom hand pushes down enough to flex the stick. → *Expect:* the stick loads and the puck stays flat.
5. **Transfer weight forward.** Drive from the back leg to the front leg as the blade sweeps through the puck. → *Expect:* your hips move toward the target instead of spinning in place.
6. **Release off the toe.** Roll the wrists so the puck leaves near the toe of the blade. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if wrist or shoulder pain appears. → *Expect:* the puck comes off with spin and a crisp snap.
7. **Follow through to aim.** Point the blade face where you want the puck to go, high follow-through for higher shots and lower for lower shots. → *Expect:* the puck travels along the line of the blade finish.
8. **Reset before the next shot.** Return to stance and repeat deliberately rather than rapid-firing poor reps. → *Expect:* each shot has the same load, transfer, and finish.

## Decision points

- Puck flutters → keep the blade closed longer and release off the toe.
- Shot lacks power → add weight transfer before trying to swing harder.
- Shot sails too high → lower the follow-through and avoid opening the blade early.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Arm-only shot:** detect no hip or leg movement → rehearse back-leg to front-leg weight shifts without shooting.
- **F2 Open blade:** detect pucks rising unpredictably → keep the top hand forward and blade cupped through release.
- **F3 Puck too far forward:** detect weak pushes instead of snaps → start with the puck slightly behind the lead foot.
- **F4 Over-rotating shoulders:** detect shots pulled across the body → finish the blade toward the target.

## Verification

You take 20 wrist shots with a clear weight shift, puck released off the toe, balanced finish, and at least 12 shots hitting or passing within one stick length of the target.

## Variations

- `easier`: shoot from a stationary stance on a dryland shooting pad.
- `harder`: receive a pass, settle the puck, and shoot within two seconds.
- `equipment`: use a lighter training puck for mechanics or a regulation puck for power practice.
- `at-home`: use a shooting pad, net, and backstop in a garage or driveway.

## Safety & privacy

Keep the shooting lane clear, use eye protection when appropriate, and stop for sharp wrist, elbow, shoulder, hip, or back pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
