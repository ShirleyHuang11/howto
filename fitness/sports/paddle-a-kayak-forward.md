## Goal

You paddle a kayak forward efficiently using torso rotation, stable posture, and clean blade entry rather than tiring your arms quickly.

## Preconditions

- A kayak, properly sized paddle, personal flotation device, and calm supervised water.
- Ability to enter, exit, and recover near shore or with instruction.
- Weather, current, and boat traffic checked before launching.

## Steps

1. **Fit the boat and safety gear.** Wear the personal flotation device, set foot braces so knees are slightly bent, and keep essential gear secured. → *Expect:* you sit stable with feet supported.
2. **Hold the paddle correctly.** Grip slightly wider than shoulder width with knuckles aligned and blades oriented properly. → *Expect:* wrists stay mostly straight.
3. **Sit tall with relaxed shoulders.** Stack ribs over hips and brace lightly through feet and legs. → *Expect:* the boat feels stable without slumping.
4. **Reach from the torso.** Rotate the chest and place one blade in the water near your toes. → *Expect:* the blade enters cleanly with little splash.
5. **Drive past the blade.** Press through the opposite foot and unwind the torso as the blade anchors in the water. → *Expect:* the kayak moves past the paddle instead of the paddle dragging behind.
6. **Exit at the hip.** Slice the blade out when your hand reaches the hip, then set up the other side. → *Expect:* the stroke stays short, powerful, and quiet.
7. **Keep the boat tracking.** Alternate even strokes and look where you want to go. → *Expect:* the kayak travels forward with minimal zigzag.
8. **Practice near shore first.** ⚠️ *Safety:* return to shore if wind, current, fatigue, cold, numbness, or fear makes control uncertain. → *Expect:* you can stop, turn, and land before getting tired.

## Decision points

- Arms fatigue quickly → shorten the stroke and rotate the torso more.
- Kayak zigzags → match stroke length and pressure on both sides.
- Conditions worsen → head to shore early, not after exhaustion.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Arm paddling:** detect burning biceps and shoulders → drive with torso rotation and foot pressure.
- **F2 Late blade exit:** detect water lifting behind the hip → remove the blade at the hip.
- **F3 Slumped posture:** detect low-back discomfort → sit tall and adjust foot braces.
- **F4 Overreaching:** detect rocking or unstable entries → place the blade closer and keep the head centered.

## Verification

You paddle 200 meters in calm supervised water with a fitted PFD, tall posture, clean blade entries near the toes, exits at the hip, and steady forward tracking without shoulder pain or loss of control.

## Variations

- `easier`: practice on a calm pond near shore with short intervals.
- `harder`: maintain pace into light wind while preserving technique.
- `equipment`: use a lighter paddle or feather angle that keeps wrists neutral.
- `at-home`: practice seated torso rotation and paddle path with a broom handle, away from fragile objects.

## Safety & privacy

Kayaking carries drowning, cold-water, weather, and boat-traffic risks. Wear a personal flotation device, avoid paddling alone or in unsafe conditions, and stop for numbness, cold stress, dizziness, shoulder pain, or loss of boat control. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
