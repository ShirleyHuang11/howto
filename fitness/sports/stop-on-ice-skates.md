## Goal

You stop on ice skates using a controlled snowplow or hockey-stop progression without twisting the knees or falling into other skaters.

## Preconditions

- Properly fitted skates, gloves, and a maintained rink with open practice space.
- Helmet recommended, especially for beginners.
- Ability to skate forward slowly and glide in balance.

## Steps

1. **Choose an open lane.** Practice away from traffic and near the boards at first. → *Expect:* you have room to slow down without crossing another skater's path.
2. **Start with a slow glide.** Skate forward gently with knees bent and hands forward. → *Expect:* speed is low enough that you can coast safely.
3. **Make a snowplow shape.** Turn toes slightly inward and heels outward while keeping knees bent. → *Expect:* inner blade edges begin scraping the ice.
4. **Press edges gradually.** Apply light outward pressure through both heels without locking the knees. → *Expect:* shaved ice appears and speed drops smoothly.
5. **Keep the torso centered.** Stay tall through the chest but low through the legs, with weight between both skates. → *Expect:* you slow without pitching forward or backward.
6. **Progress to one-foot emphasis.** [BRANCH: easier, keep both skates in snowplow | harder, rotate hips slightly and use one skate more like a hockey-stop entry] → *Expect:* edge pressure increases while the knees stay aligned.
7. **Reset before repeating.** Skate away only after regaining balance. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the drill if the knee twists, speed is too high, or you fall and hit your head. → *Expect:* each attempt ends upright or safely near the boards.

## Decision points

- Skates chatter instead of scrape → reduce speed and apply edge pressure more gradually.
- Knees twist inward painfully → stop and return to slow snowplow practice.
- You cannot slow before the boards → start farther away and lower speed.
- Hockey-stop attempts cause falls → keep practicing snowplow until edge control improves.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Locked knees:** detect bouncing or inability to steer edges → bend knees and ankles more.
- **F2 Toes too far inward:** detect knee strain → use a smaller wedge and pressure through heels.
- **F3 Leaning backward:** detect feet shooting forward → bring hands and chest slightly forward.
- **F4 Practicing in traffic:** detect skaters passing close → move to open ice or wait.

## Verification

You completed 10 controlled stops from slow skating with bent knees, gradual edge pressure, visible ice scrape, no knee twisting, and enough control to stop before a chosen marker.

## Variations

- easier: practice snowplow stops at walking speed while near boards.
- harder: progress to one-foot snowplow, then coached hockey-stop mechanics.
- equipment: helmet and gloves reduce consequences of beginner falls.
- at-home: rehearse wedge shape and knee alignment off ice without sliding.

## Safety & privacy

Stopping on ice has medium fall and knee-twist risk. Keep speed low, practice away from traffic, stop after head impact or sharp joint pain, and get instruction if edge control is inconsistent. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
