## Goal

You use agility ladder drills to improve foot rhythm, coordination, and body control without turning the drill into sloppy speed work.

## Preconditions

- An agility ladder or taped floor grid on a flat, non-slip surface.
- Athletic shoes and clear space beyond both ends of the ladder.
- A light warmup with marching, skipping, side shuffles, and ankle mobility.

## Steps

1. **Set posture first.** Stand tall with knees soft, hips under ribs, and arms bent at about 90 degrees. → *Expect:* you feel ready to move in any direction.
2. **Start with one foot per square.** Jog through placing one foot in each box, staying light on the balls of the feet. → *Expect:* feet land quietly and do not touch the ladder.
3. **Add two feet per square.** Step right-left into each box, then repeat left-right on the next pass. → *Expect:* rhythm stays even on both leads.
4. **Use the arms naturally.** Drive elbows back and avoid letting arms cross the body. → *Expect:* upper body stays quiet while feet move quickly.
5. **Progress to lateral steps.** Move sideways with both feet touching each square before advancing. → *Expect:* hips stay level and toes point mostly forward.
6. **Keep eyes forward.** Glance down only as needed while maintaining posture. → *Expect:* you know where the ladder is without folding at the waist.
7. **Increase speed only after clean reps.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop or slow down if you trip, roll an ankle, or cannot control foot placement. → *Expect:* faster reps still avoid the ladder and preserve posture.
8. **Rest enough for quality.** Work 10 to 20 seconds, then rest 30 to 60 seconds between passes. → *Expect:* each pass stays crisp.

## Decision points

- Feet keep hitting rungs → slow down and use fewer patterns.
- Breathing becomes the limiter → lengthen rest; this is a coordination drill, not conditioning first.
- One direction feels worse → add an extra slow practice pass on that side.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Looking straight down:** detect rounded posture → lift chest and use peripheral vision.
- **F2 Heavy landings:** detect loud stomping → reduce speed and land under the hips.
- **F3 Crossed arms:** detect torso twisting → drive elbows straight back.
- **F4 Pattern overload:** detect confusion after two drills → master one pattern before adding another.

## Verification

You complete 6 clean ladder passes using at least two patterns, with quiet feet, upright posture, no rung contact, and controlled deceleration after each pass.

## Variations

- `easier`: use marching speed and wider tape squares.
- `harder`: add in-in-out-out, icky shuffle, or sport-specific acceleration after the ladder.
- `equipment`: tape lines on the floor if no ladder is available.
- `at-home`: use painter's tape on a hallway floor with furniture cleared away.

## Safety & privacy

Use a flat non-slip surface and stop for ankle rolls, knee pain, dizziness, or repeated trips. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
