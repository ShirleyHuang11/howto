## Goal

You stay afloat in deep water using calm breathing, efficient leg action, and small hand sculls without exhausting yourself.

## Preconditions

- A supervised pool or calm shallow-to-deep practice area with a lifeguard or competent spotter.
- Ability to enter and exit the water safely.
- No practice alone, in open water, or when fatigued.

## Steps

1. **Start near support.** Begin beside a wall or lane rope where you can touch immediately if needed. → *Expect:* you can stop the drill without panic.
2. **Set a vertical body line.** Keep head above water, chest tall, ribs stacked over hips, and eyes forward. → *Expect:* your mouth clears the surface without craning the neck.
3. **Use gentle hand sculls.** Sweep hands outward and inward at chest level with palms angled to press water. → *Expect:* you feel continuous pressure on the palms.
4. **Add a stable kick.** [BRANCH: eggbeater, circle each lower leg alternately like pedaling outward | flutter or breaststroke kick, use small relaxed kicks if eggbeater is not available] → *Expect:* your body rises without big splashing.
5. **Breathe slowly.** Inhale through the mouth and exhale steadily, keeping shoulders low. → *Expect:* breathing stays controlled and you can speak a short sentence.
6. **Reduce effort.** Make smaller, smoother motions once you are floating. → *Expect:* you stay up while using less energy.
7. **Practice timed holds.** Tread for 15 to 30 seconds, touch the wall, then repeat. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop immediately if you swallow water, feel panicked, cramp, dizzy, or unable to keep your mouth clear. → *Expect:* each interval ends before form breaks.
8. **Build duration gradually.** Add 10 to 15 seconds only when the previous interval felt controlled. → *Expect:* endurance increases without frantic kicking.

## Decision points

- Mouth dips below water → return to the wall and use larger leg circles before extending time.
- Calves or hips cramp → stop, hold support, and leave the water if it does not settle quickly.
- You feel panic → stop the drill and practice supported floating first.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bicycle legs without pressure:** detect lots of motion but sinking → turn the shins and feet to press water outward.
- **F2 Hands pushing down only:** detect bobbing up and down → scull side to side with angled palms.
- **F3 Raised shoulders:** detect neck tension and fast breathing → lower shoulders and shorten the interval.
- **F4 Fatigue creep:** detect kicks getting frantic → stop at the wall before exhaustion.

## Verification

You tread water for 60 seconds in a supervised pool with mouth continuously above the surface, calm breathing, steady hand sculls, and no frantic splashing or wall contact.

## Variations

- `easier`: wear a flotation belt and practice the eggbeater pattern near the wall.
- `harder`: tread for longer intervals with hands out of the water only after mastering normal treading.
- `equipment`: use goggles for comfort, but do not rely on fins for basic survival skill.
- `at-home`: practice only in a supervised pool; do not practice deep-water treading alone.

## Safety & privacy

Water practice carries drowning risk. Use lifeguarded water, never train alone, avoid breath-holding contests, and stop for panic, dizziness, cramps, chest pain, or repeated water inhalation. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
