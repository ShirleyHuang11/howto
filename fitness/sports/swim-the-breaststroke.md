## Goal

You swim breaststroke with a legal pull, efficient whip kick, timed breath, and streamlined glide.

## Preconditions

- A supervised pool lane and basic comfort floating and swimming.
- Goggles and enough space to avoid other swimmers.
- Healthy knees, hips, and shoulders for the frog-like kick and pull.

## Steps

1. **Start streamlined.** Push off with arms straight, hands stacked, head between arms, and legs together. → *Expect:* you glide forward with minimal splash.
2. **Sweep the hands outward.** Turn palms slightly out and press water wider than shoulder width without pulling past the ribs. → *Expect:* forearms feel pressure while elbows stay near the surface.
3. **Breathe during the in-sweep.** Bend elbows, bring hands toward the upper chest, and lift the mouth just enough to inhale. → *Expect:* the breath happens as the chest rises, not from craning the neck.
4. **Recover the arms forward.** Shoot hands forward together as the face returns to the water. → *Expect:* arms finish long before the kick snaps.
5. **Load the kick.** Draw heels toward the hips with knees no wider than comfortable hip to shoulder width. → *Expect:* shins fold under the body without knees splaying excessively.
6. **Whip and squeeze.** Turn feet out, sweep them around and back, then snap legs together. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you feel sharp knee or groin pain. → *Expect:* propulsion comes from the feet pressing water, followed by legs touching.
7. **Glide after the kick.** Hold a long body line for a brief glide before starting the next pull. → *Expect:* the body moves forward quietly after the legs close.
8. **Keep the timing pull-breathe-kick-glide.** Repeat the sequence without rushing the next stroke. → *Expect:* each cycle has a visible glide phase.

## Decision points

- You feel knee strain → narrow the knees, reduce kick force, or switch strokes.
- You stop moving between strokes → shorten the glide slightly but keep streamline.
- Hips drop → return face to the water earlier and keep the head neutral.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Scissor kick:** detect one leg kicking differently from the other → practice kicks on your back or with a board.
- **F2 Pull too far back:** detect hands finishing near hips → stop the pull at the chest and recover forward sooner.
- **F3 Head held high:** detect low hips and neck tension → look down during the glide.
- **F4 Rushed timing:** detect no glide → say "pull, breathe, kick, glide" through each cycle.

## Verification

You swim 25 meters of breaststroke in a supervised pool with symmetrical kicks, hands recovering forward together, one breath per stroke cycle, and a clear glide after each kick.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a kickboard for breaststroke kick practice or swim short repeats with rest.
- `harder`: swim 4 x 25 meters with consistent stroke count and legal timing.
- `equipment`: use a pull buoy only for arm timing drills, not full-stroke practice.
- `at-home`: practice the arm path standing on deck; do kick mechanics only in water.

## Safety & privacy

Breaststroke can irritate knees, hips, groin, neck, or low back if forced. Swim in supervised water, stop for sharp pain or breathing distress, and avoid underwater breath-holding games. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
