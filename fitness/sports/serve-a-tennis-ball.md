## Goal

You serve a tennis ball with a repeatable stance, controlled toss, upward swing path, and balanced finish into the service box.

## Preconditions

- A tennis court, tennis racket, balls, and enough space behind the baseline.
- Warm shoulders, wrists, trunk, hips, and calves.
- Basic overhead range of motion without pain.

## Steps

1. **Set the stance.** Stand sideways behind the baseline with front foot angled toward the net post and back foot roughly parallel to the baseline. → *Expect:* shoulders and hips start turned, not square to the net.
2. **Choose a consistent grip.** Use a continental grip for a standard serve. → *Expect:* the racket edge can lead upward like throwing a ball.
3. **Make a calm toss.** Lift the ball with a straight tossing arm and release around eye height so it rises slightly in front of the lead shoulder. → *Expect:* the ball peaks where you can reach up without chasing it.
4. **Load the legs and shoulders.** Bend knees lightly while the racket drops behind you and the tossing arm stays up. → *Expect:* you feel stretch through the trunk and shoulder without arching the low back aggressively.
5. **Swing up to contact.** Drive upward, extend the hitting arm, and contact the ball high. [BRANCH: easier, serve from the service line | harder, add spin and full baseline distance] → *Expect:* contact feels above the head and slightly in front, not beside the ear.
6. **Pronate and follow through.** Let the forearm rotate naturally and the racket finish across the body. → *Expect:* the ball travels forward with controlled pace and the arm decelerates smoothly.
7. **Land balanced.** Step into the court after contact without falling sideways. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for sharp shoulder, elbow, or back pain. → *Expect:* you can recover for the next ball.

## Decision points

- Toss drifts behind you → practice toss-only reps until it lands just in front of the lead foot.
- Serve goes into the net → reach higher and swing upward before forward.
- Serve sails long → reduce force and aim higher spin or a smoother follow-through.
- Shoulder feels pinchy → stop overhead serving and assess mobility or technique.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pancake grip:** detect flat pushing contact → reset to continental grip and rehearse edge-leading swings.
- **F2 Chasing the toss:** detect stepping randomly before contact → toss lower and more consistently.
- **F3 Collapsing toss arm:** detect early shoulder drop → keep the non-hitting arm up until the swing begins.
- **F4 Overarching the back:** detect low-back compression → use more knee bend and trunk rotation, less lumbar extension.

## Verification

You served 20 balls with a continental grip, consistent toss, high contact point, smooth follow-through, balanced landing, and at least several balls landing in the correct service box.

## Variations

- easier: start at the service line or use a half-speed motion.
- harder: practice slice, kick, or target serves after basic contact is reliable.
- equipment: use lower-compression balls for beginners.
- at-home: rehearse toss and shadow swings only where ceiling height and space are safe.

## Safety & privacy

Serving can irritate the shoulder, elbow, wrist, or low back if volume and force increase too quickly. Stop for sharp pain and get coaching or medical help for persistent symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
