## Goal

You improve vertical jump height by training jump mechanics, lower-body strength, tendon stiffness, and recovery with controlled volume.

## Preconditions

- Pain-free ankles, knees, hips, and back.
- A safe floor, wall or jump-measure tool, and room to land.
- A warmup with light jogging, mobility, squats, and low pogo jumps.

## Steps

1. **Measure your baseline.** Mark standing reach, then perform three countermovement jumps and record the best touch. → *Expect:* you have a starting jump height to compare later.
2. **Practice the countermovement.** Dip quickly to a quarter squat with hips back, knees over toes, chest tall, and arms behind you. → *Expect:* the dip feels springy, not deep and slow.
3. **Jump with full extension.** Drive arms up and extend ankles, knees, and hips together. → *Expect:* you leave the floor tall through the toes.
4. **Land like you jumped.** Absorb with bent ankles, knees, and hips while keeping knees aligned over toes. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop plyometrics if landings become loud, knees cave, or sharp joint pain appears. → *Expect:* landing is quiet and balanced.
5. **Train strength twice weekly.** Do squats, split squats, hip hinges, calf raises, and core bracing at controlled loads. → *Expect:* legs feel challenged without grinding reps.
6. **Train low-volume plyometrics twice weekly.** Do 3 to 5 sets of 3 to 5 maximal jumps or pogo hops with full rest. → *Expect:* jump height stays high across sets.
7. **Rest between power sessions.** Leave at least 48 hours between hard jump sessions. → *Expect:* tendons and joints feel recovered before jumping hard again.
8. **Retest after four weeks.** Repeat the same reach and three-jump test after an easy day. → *Expect:* the test conditions match baseline and the best jump is higher or more consistent.

## Decision points

- Jump height drops during a session → stop power work and move to mobility or easy strength.
- Knees cave on landing → reduce intensity and strengthen hips with split squats and lateral walks.
- No improvement after four weeks → check sleep, protein intake, strength progression, and plyometric quality.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much volume:** detect sore tendons or flat jumps → cut jump contacts in half for a week.
- **F2 Deep squat dip:** detect slow takeoff → practice quick quarter-squat jumps.
- **F3 Incomplete hip extension:** detect bent hips at takeoff → cue tall finish and arms up.
- **F4 Stiff-legged landing:** detect loud impact → rehearse snap-downs and soft athletic landings.

## Verification

After 28 days, your best measured vertical jump improves or your three attempts cluster within 1 inch while showing full triple extension, quiet landings, and no knee cave or pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: use low pogo hops, calf raises, and bodyweight squats before maximal jumps.
- `harder`: add loaded trap-bar jumps or Olympic-lift derivatives only with qualified coaching.
- `equipment`: use chalk on a wall, a jump mat, or a phone app for measurement.
- `at-home`: do low-volume jumps on a firm non-slip surface with enough overhead clearance.

## Safety & privacy

Jump training stresses joints and tendons. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, Achilles or patellar tendon pain, unstable landings, or unusual fatigue, and seek professional help for persistent symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
