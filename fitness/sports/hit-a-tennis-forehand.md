## Goal

You hit a tennis forehand with early preparation, body rotation, clean contact in front, and a controlled follow-through.

## Preconditions

- A tennis racket, balls, court or practice wall, and safe space around the swing.
- Warm wrists, elbows, shoulders, trunk, hips, and ankles.
- Basic ability to track an incoming ball.

## Steps

1. **Start in ready position.** Hold the racket in front, knees soft, weight on the balls of the feet. → *Expect:* you can split step or move without delay.
2. **Turn early.** As the ball comes to the forehand side, rotate shoulders and hips while taking the racket back. → *Expect:* the racket is prepared before the ball bounces near you.
3. **Set the hitting stance.** Step or load with space between your body and the ball. → *Expect:* contact will happen comfortably in front and to the side, not jammed near the hip.
4. **Swing low to high.** Drop the racket below the ball, rotate the trunk, and brush forward and upward through contact. → *Expect:* the ball clears the net with topspin or controlled arc.
5. **Contact in front.** Keep the head steady and meet the ball slightly ahead of the lead hip. → *Expect:* the strings feel solid and the ball does not twist the racket.
6. **Finish across the body.** Let the racket wrap naturally over the opposite shoulder or around the torso. [BRANCH: easier, mini-tennis from the service line | harder, rally from the baseline] → *Expect:* the swing decelerates without wrist flipping.
7. **Recover to ready.** Return the racket to neutral and move back into balance. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if elbow or shoulder pain appears during repeated swings. → *Expect:* you are ready for the next ball.

## Decision points

- Ball flies long → add topspin, reduce swing speed, or close the racket face slightly.
- Ball hits the net → prepare earlier and swing through a higher finish.
- Contact feels cramped → move feet sooner and create more spacing.
- Elbow discomfort builds → reduce volume and check grip tension.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Late preparation:** detect rushed contact beside the body → turn shoulders as soon as you read forehand.
- **F2 All-arm swing:** detect no hip or trunk turn → initiate from legs and torso before the arm comes through.
- **F3 Wrist flip:** detect unpredictable direction → keep wrist firm through contact and finish with the whole arm.
- **F4 Overgripping:** detect forearm fatigue → hold the racket firmly enough to control it, not squeeze it.

## Verification

You hit 20 forehands with early shoulder turn, clean spacing, contact in front, low-to-high swing path, balanced finish, and no sharp arm pain.

## Variations

- easier: drop-feed yourself or hit mini-tennis inside the service box.
- harder: rally cross-court, add targets, or practice on the run.
- equipment: lower-compression balls give beginners more time.
- at-home: shadow swing with no ball in a clear area.

## Safety & privacy

Forehand practice can aggravate the wrist, elbow, or shoulder if technique is tense or volume spikes. Stop for sharp pain and seek coaching for repeated discomfort. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
