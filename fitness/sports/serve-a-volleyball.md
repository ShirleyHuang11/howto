## Goal

You serve a volleyball over the net with consistent toss or hold, clean hand contact, and balanced follow-through.

## Preconditions

- A volleyball court or open net area and a volleyball.
- Clear space behind the service line.
- Warm shoulders, elbows, wrists, trunk, hips, and calves.

## Steps

1. **Choose a serve type.** [BRANCH: underhand, hold the ball in the non-hitting hand | overhand, toss the ball slightly in front of the hitting shoulder] → *Expect:* the setup matches your current skill and strength.
2. **Set your stance.** Put the non-dominant foot forward for most right-handed serves and square shoulders toward the target area. → *Expect:* balance stays steady before contact.
3. **Prepare the hitting arm.** For underhand, swing the arm back like a pendulum; for overhand, draw the elbow back with the hand high. → *Expect:* the arm path feels repeatable.
4. **Contact a firm ball.** Strike with the heel of the hand or firm palm; contact the lower back of the ball for underhand or center-back for overhand. → *Expect:* the ball leaves cleanly without spinning off the fingers.
5. **Step through the serve.** Shift weight from back foot to front foot as the arm swings. → *Expect:* power comes from the body, not just the shoulder.
6. **Aim over the net with margin.** Pick a deep court target and serve at controlled speed. → *Expect:* the ball clears the net with enough height to avoid clipping it.
7. **Finish balanced and enter the court.** Follow through toward the target, then move into defensive position. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for sharp shoulder, elbow, wrist, or back pain. → *Expect:* you can recover without falling forward.

## Decision points

- Ball falls short → step through more and contact more solidly before swinging harder.
- Ball sails long → reduce arm speed and aim with a calmer follow-through.
- Overhand toss is inconsistent → practice toss-only reps or use underhand temporarily.
- Shoulder pain appears → stop overhead serving and reduce volume.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Loose hand contact:** detect weak spinny serves → firm the hand and contact the center of the ball.
- **F2 No weight transfer:** detect short serves despite effort → step through as the arm strikes.
- **F3 Toss behind the head:** detect arching or reaching back → toss slightly forward and lower.
- **F4 Crossing the line early:** detect foot fault → start farther behind the line and step after contact.

## Verification

You served 20 balls with legal foot position, balanced stance, firm hand contact, follow-through toward target, and at least 10 serves clearing the net into the court.

## Variations

- easier: use underhand serves from closer practice distance.
- harder: serve overhand to deep zones or add float-serve contact.
- equipment: use a lighter training ball for new players.
- at-home: practice tosses and arm path outdoors with safe clearance.

## Safety & privacy

Serving is usually low risk but can irritate the shoulder, elbow, wrist, or back with too much volume. Stop for sharp pain and keep the serving lane clear. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
