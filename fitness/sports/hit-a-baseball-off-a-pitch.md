## Goal

You hit a pitched baseball with a safe batting setup, early timing, efficient rotation, and controlled contact through the hitting zone.

## Preconditions

- A batting helmet, bat, baseballs, and a safe cage, field, or supervised hitting area.
- A pitcher, machine, or coach throwing at an appropriate speed.
- Warm wrists, shoulders, trunk, hips, knees, and ankles.

## Steps

1. **Check the hitting area.** Confirm no one is near the batter, catcher, or ball flight path. → *Expect:* the swing zone and field of play are clear.
2. **Set your stance.** Stand balanced with feet slightly wider than shoulders, knees soft, hands near the back shoulder, and eyes level. → *Expect:* you can stride or load without drifting.
3. **Track the pitcher.** Focus on the release window and pick up the ball early. → *Expect:* you see the ball out of the hand, not only near the plate.
4. **Load on time.** Shift pressure slightly into the back hip as the pitcher begins delivery, keeping the head steady. → *Expect:* you feel coiled, not lunging forward.
5. **Stride and rotate.** Take a small controlled stride, plant the front foot, then rotate hips and torso before the hands fire. → *Expect:* the swing starts from the ground and stays balanced.
6. **Swing through the zone.** Keep the barrel on plane with the pitch and contact the ball out front for an inside pitch or slightly deeper for outside. [BRANCH: easier, use front toss or slower machine speed | harder, add live pitching and pitch recognition] → *Expect:* contact feels solid and the bat continues through the ball.
7. **Finish and hold balance.** Let the back foot rotate and finish without stepping across the plate. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop immediately after any head impact, hand injury, dizziness, or uncontrolled pitches near the body. → *Expect:* you can stay upright and locate the ball after contact.

## Decision points

- You are late repeatedly → start the load earlier or reduce pitch speed.
- You lunge at the ball → shorten stride and keep the head between the feet.
- You miss under the ball → adjust barrel path and avoid dropping the back shoulder.
- Pitching is wild or too fast → stop and change the drill.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pulling the head:** detect eyes leaving contact early → keep the head down through the hitting zone.
- **F2 Casting the hands:** detect weak contact off the end of the bat → keep hands closer to the body as hips start.
- **F3 Lunging forward:** detect weight falling over the front foot → load into the back hip and stride smaller.
- **F4 Unsafe bat handling:** detect swinging near others or releasing the bat → reset spacing and grip before continuing.

## Verification

You took 20 controlled swings against appropriate pitches with a helmet on, clear surroundings, early ball tracking, balanced load, hip-led rotation, and no unsafe near-misses or pain.

## Variations

- easier: use tee work, soft toss, or front toss before live pitching.
- harder: add mixed pitch locations or game-speed pitching only with proper protection.
- equipment: use a bat length and weight you can accelerate without losing control.
- at-home: use dry swings or a net only where space and property safety are assured.

## Safety & privacy

Batting has medium risk from pitched balls, swung bats, and batted balls. Wear a helmet, keep bystanders clear, stop after head impact or sharp pain, and use appropriate supervision. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
