## Goal

You dribble a basketball under control with either hand while keeping your eyes up and body balanced.

## Preconditions

- A properly inflated basketball and flat court or driveway space.
- Shoes with traction and no loose objects nearby.
- Warm wrists, shoulders, hips, and ankles before higher-speed drills.

## Steps

1. **Start in an athletic stance.** Bend knees and hips, chest up, feet slightly wider than hips. → *Expect:* you can move in any direction without standing tall first.
2. **Use finger pads, not the palm.** Push the ball down with spread fingers and a relaxed wrist. → *Expect:* the ball rebounds predictably without slapping sounds.
3. **Keep the bounce beside your body.** Dribble near the outside of the lead foot, below waist height. → *Expect:* the ball stays protected and does not hit your toes.
4. **Look up between bounces.** Pick a point ahead and use peripheral vision for the ball. → *Expect:* you can see the court while maintaining rhythm.
5. **Switch hands in front.** Push the ball across low from right to left, then left to right, shifting weight with the ball. [BRANCH: easier, pause between switches | harder, keep the crossover below the knees] → *Expect:* the ball crosses cleanly without rising high.
6. **Dribble while walking.** Step forward slowly while keeping the ball on the outside of the body. → *Expect:* your steps and bounces stay coordinated.
7. **Protect the ball.** Turn the non-dribbling shoulder and forearm slightly between the ball and an imaginary defender. ⚠️ *Safety:* avoid reaching into crowded games where fingers can be jammed. → *Expect:* the dribble stays legal and shielded.

## Decision points

- Ball bounces too high → lower your stance and soften the wrist.
- You lose the ball when looking up → slow down and alternate one glance up with one glance down.
- Weak hand feels clumsy → practice it alone before adding movement.
- Wrist or finger pain appears → stop and reduce volume next session.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Palm slapping:** detect loud, flat contacts → spread fingers and push with the pads.
- **F2 Standing upright:** detect slow reaction and high dribble → bend hips and knees before moving.
- **F3 Ball in front of feet:** detect kicks or trips → move the bounce to the side pocket.
- **F4 Staring down:** detect missed visual cues → set a target on the wall and keep eyes there.

## Verification

You dribbled 60 seconds with each hand and 20 controlled crossovers while staying in an athletic stance, using finger pads, keeping the ball below waist height, and looking up regularly.

## Variations

- easier: stationary pound dribbles and slow hand switches.
- harder: add retreat dribbles, speed changes, or defender pressure.
- equipment: use a smaller ball for young athletes or hand-size limitations.
- at-home: dribble outdoors or in a space where noise and floor marks are acceptable.

## Safety & privacy

Dribbling carries low injury risk but can cause finger jams, ankle rolls, or falls on slippery surfaces. Stop for sharp pain and keep the area clear. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
