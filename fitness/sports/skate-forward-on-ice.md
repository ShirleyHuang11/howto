## Goal

You skate forward on ice with a stable stance, controlled weight transfer, and safe glide recovery.

## Preconditions

- Properly fitted ice skates, gloves, and access to a maintained rink.
- Helmet recommended for beginners or crowded sessions.
- Ability to stand and balance on skates near the boards; warm ankles, knees, hips, and wrists.

## Steps

1. **Check skate fit and blade guards.** Tighten skates snugly around the ankle and remove guards before stepping on ice. → *Expect:* feet feel supported and blades contact ice directly.
2. **Start at the boards.** Hold the boards lightly, bend knees, hips back, chest up, and feet under hips. → *Expect:* you feel centered over the middle of the blades.
3. **Practice marching.** Lift one skate slightly, set it down, then alternate while staying low. → *Expect:* balance improves without the feet sliding apart.
4. **Make small pushes.** Turn one toe slightly outward, push to the side and back, then glide on the other skate. → *Expect:* the glide foot tracks forward while the push foot returns under the body.
5. **Transfer weight fully.** Shift the belly button over the gliding skate before pushing with the other side. → *Expect:* each push creates a short, stable glide.
6. **Keep arms quiet and eyes up.** Hold arms slightly out for balance and look where you want to go. [BRANCH: easier, stay near the boards | harder, link longer glides away from boards] → *Expect:* posture stays low and direction improves.
7. **Slow down before stopping.** Coast near the boards or use a snowplow if trained. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop practicing if you cannot control speed, if the rink is crowded, or after any head impact. → *Expect:* you finish upright and controlled.

## Decision points

- Feet slide too far apart → bend knees more and shorten pushes.
- You fall backward → bring hands forward, chest over knees, and hips behind heels.
- Speed feels uncontrolled → return to the boards and practice marching.
- Ankles collapse inward → check skate fit and reduce time on ice.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Standing tall:** detect stiff legs and backward falls → bend knees and keep chest slightly forward.
- **F2 Pushing straight back:** detect toe-pick scraping or no glide → push diagonally to the side.
- **F3 Incomplete weight transfer:** detect choppy steps → move body weight over the gliding skate.
- **F4 Looking at feet:** detect wandering path → look ahead and use peripheral vision.

## Verification

You skated forward for 10-15 meters repeatedly with bent knees, side pushes, full weight transfer, short controlled glides, eyes up, and no uncontrolled falls or collisions.

## Variations

- easier: practice marching and two-foot glides while holding the boards.
- harder: extend glide time or add gentle turns after forward skating is stable.
- equipment: wear a helmet, gloves, and well-fitted skates.
- at-home: practice balance and knee bend off ice in shoes, not on slippery floors.

## Safety & privacy

Ice skating has medium risk from falls, blade contact, and collisions. Wear protective gear, stay within your speed control, stop after head impact, and seek help for persistent pain or dizziness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
