## Goal

You climb a hill on a road bike with efficient gearing, steady cadence, controlled breathing, and safe line choice.

## Preconditions

- A road bike in good working order, helmet, water, and a climb with safe traffic conditions.
- Tires inflated, brakes working, and drivetrain shifting smoothly.
- Basic bike handling and comfort riding on roads; warm up with 10 minutes of easy riding.

## Steps

1. **Inspect the bike before the climb.** Check tire pressure, brakes, chain, and quick releases or thru-axles. → *Expect:* the bike responds normally before the grade starts.
2. **Approach in an easy gear.** Shift before the steepest section while pedaling lightly. → *Expect:* cadence stays smooth instead of bogging down.
3. **Settle into seated climbing.** Keep hands relaxed on the hoods or tops, chest open, and hips stable on the saddle. → *Expect:* breathing is steady and the front wheel tracks straight.
4. **Pedal at sustainable cadence.** Aim for a cadence you can maintain without grinding, often around 70-90 rpm for many riders. → *Expect:* legs work continuously without knee strain from huge gears.
5. **Use standing sparingly.** Stand for short steep sections by shifting weight over the pedals and keeping the bike stable. [BRANCH: easier, stay seated and use the lowest gear | harder, add short standing surges] → *Expect:* speed changes smoothly without swerving.
6. **Hold a safe road position.** Ride predictably, scan ahead, and avoid weaving across the lane. → *Expect:* drivers and other riders can anticipate your line.
7. **Crest and recover.** Keep pedaling over the top, then shift harder gradually before descending. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for chest pain, dizziness, traffic danger, or loss of bike control. → *Expect:* you clear the climb with enough awareness to descend safely.

## Decision points

- Cadence drops below control → shift easier before grinding harder.
- Front wheel wanders → relax the upper body and keep eyes up the road.
- Knee pain appears → reduce gear load and stop if pain persists.
- Traffic feels unsafe → end the attempt and choose a quieter hill.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Grinding too hard:** detect slow cadence and knee pressure → shift easier and spin.
- **F2 Upper-body tension:** detect locked elbows or shoulder shrugging → soften elbows and grip.
- **F3 Weaving:** detect drifting across the lane → reduce effort and hold a straighter line.
- **F4 Poor crest transition:** detect sudden heavy gear or unstable descent → keep pedaling and shift gradually.

## Verification

You climbed a selected hill while wearing a helmet, maintaining a predictable line, using gears to keep a sustainable cadence, avoiding knee pain, and recovering safely at the top.

## Variations

- easier: choose a shorter or gentler hill and use the lowest gear early.
- harder: repeat the climb or add structured intervals after basic climbing is comfortable.
- equipment: compact chainrings or a wider cassette help steep climbs.
- at-home: practice cadence control on an indoor trainer with resistance.

## Safety & privacy

Road climbs have medium risk from traffic, falls, overexertion, and descending after fatigue. Wear a helmet, follow traffic laws, stop for chest pain or dizziness, and choose safe routes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
