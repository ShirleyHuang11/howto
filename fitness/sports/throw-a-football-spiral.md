## Goal

You throw a football with a stable base, overhand mechanics, fingertip release, and a tight spiral toward a target.

## Preconditions

- A football sized appropriately for your hand and age.
- Open space with no people directly in the throwing lane.
- Warm shoulders, elbows, wrists, trunk, and hips before repeated throws.

## Steps

1. **Grip the laces.** Place fingertips across or near the laces with a small gap between palm and ball. → *Expect:* the ball feels controlled by the fingers, not squeezed in the palm.
2. **Set sideways to the target.** Put the non-throwing shoulder toward the target and feet about shoulder-width. → *Expect:* hips and shoulders are turned before the throw.
3. **Bring the ball back high.** Keep the elbow below or near shoulder height and the ball by the ear, not dropped behind the waist. → *Expect:* the throwing path feels compact and overhand.
4. **Step toward the target.** Step with the opposite foot and rotate hips, then shoulders. → *Expect:* force starts from the ground instead of only the arm.
5. **Release off the fingertips.** Snap the wrist down and let the index finger finish last along the outside of the ball. → *Expect:* the ball spins tightly around its long axis.
6. **Follow through across the body.** Let the throwing hand finish toward the opposite pocket while the back foot comes through naturally. [BRANCH: easier, throw shorter with less speed | harder, add moving targets] → *Expect:* your body decelerates smoothly without shoulder strain.
7. **Reset between throws.** Take enough time to keep mechanics consistent. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if shoulder or elbow pain becomes sharp or throwing speed suddenly drops. → *Expect:* accuracy and spiral quality stay steady.

## Decision points

- Ball wobbles → adjust grip and make sure the wrist finishes through the throw.
- Throw sails high → release slightly earlier and keep the front shoulder from flying open.
- Throw dives low → step through and finish fully instead of pushing downward.
- Arm fatigue appears → stop the session before mechanics break down.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Palm-heavy grip:** detect wobble and poor spin → create a small air gap under the palm and use the laces.
- **F2 Sidearm collapse:** detect elbow dropping below the ball → shorten distance and rehearse overhand throws.
- **F3 No lower-body drive:** detect all effort in the shoulder → step and rotate before releasing.
- **F4 Overthrowing:** detect pain or wild throws → reduce distance and speed.

## Verification

You completed 20 throws to a partner or target with a sideways setup, step toward target, fingertip release, visible spiral, and no sharp shoulder or elbow pain.

## Variations

- easier: use a smaller ball or shorten the throw to 5-10 yards.
- harder: throw on the move or to a receiver breaking across the field.
- equipment: use a youth-size football if hand size prevents a clean grip.
- at-home: practice grip and dry throwing mechanics without releasing the ball indoors.

## Safety & privacy

Throwing volume can irritate the shoulder or elbow if you throw hard while fatigued. Keep the throwing lane clear, stop for sharp pain, and seek professional guidance for recurring symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
