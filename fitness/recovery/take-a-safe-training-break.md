## Goal

You pause or reduce training without losing the habit entirely, then return with less injury risk.

## Preconditions

- A reason for the break, such as fatigue, travel, stress, illness recovery, pain, or schedule overload.
- A calendar or training log.
- Medical guidance if the break is due to significant injury or illness.

## Steps

1. **Define the purpose.** Decide whether the break is full rest, active recovery, deload, travel maintenance, or injury management. → *Expect:* the break has a clear job.
2. **Set a time window.** Choose a review date, such as 3 days, 7 days, or 14 days, rather than leaving the break undefined. → *Expect:* you know when to reassess.
3. **Keep non-aggravating movement optional.** Use walking, easy mobility, or light technique only if it helps symptoms and mood. → *Expect:* movement feels restorative, not like hidden hard training.
4. **Protect recovery basics.** Prioritize sleep, meals, hydration, and stress reduction. → *Expect:* fatigue and soreness trend down.
5. **Return below previous load.** Resume at reduced volume, intensity, or frequency, then progress over several sessions. → *Expect:* the first sessions back feel controlled and leave no symptom rebound.

## Decision points

- Break is for normal fatigue → use a deload or several rest days.
- Break is for pain → avoid aggravating movements and seek assessment if pain persists.
- Break is for illness → follow `fitness/recovery/return-to-exercise-after-illness`.
- Motivation is low but body feels fine → keep a tiny minimum habit, such as a walk or mobility session.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Undefined break:** detect weeks passing without reassessment → set a calendar review date today.
- **F2 Secret intensity:** detect turning recovery days into hard workouts → cap effort at easy conversational pace.
- **F3 Full return too soon:** detect soreness or pain spike after first session back → cut volume and rebuild gradually.
- **F4 Ignoring the cause:** detect the same fatigue or pain returning → change the plan, not just the calendar.

## Verification

You complete the planned break or deload through a set review date, symptoms or fatigue improve, and your first return sessions are reduced enough that pain, illness symptoms, or excessive soreness do not rebound.

## Variations

- `easier`: take 3 full rest days, then reassess with a short walk or mobility session.
- `harder`: plan a structured deload with 40-60% lower volume while keeping technique practice.
- `equipment`: use a calendar reminder to mark both the break start and review date.
- `at-home`: keep a 5-minute mobility habit if stopping completely makes restarting difficult.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when breaks are intentional. Stop and seek care for sharp pain, swelling, fever, chest pain, fainting, or worsening illness symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if the break is due to injury, illness, surgery, or a relevant medical condition.
