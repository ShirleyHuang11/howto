## Goal

You build and practice a fueling plan for a long run, ride, swim, hike, or race so energy, hydration, and stomach comfort hold through the event.

## Preconditions

- Event duration, expected intensity, weather, and aid-station access.
- Familiar carbohydrate sources such as gels, chews, sports drink, bananas, bars, rice cakes, or sandwiches.
- Hydration plan from `fitness/recovery/hydrate-properly-for-exercise`.

## Steps

1. **Estimate the event demand.** Decide whether the event is under 90 minutes, 90 minutes to 3 hours, or longer than 3 hours. → *Expect:* a fueling category that matches duration and intensity.
2. **Set an hourly carbohydrate target.** Use roughly 30-60 g carbohydrate per hour for many endurance events, and only move toward 60-90 g per hour after practicing gut tolerance. → *Expect:* a specific hourly gram target.
3. **Choose portable fuel you tolerate.** Combine drink mix, gels, chews, or real food based on terrain and access. → *Expect:* you can carry or collect enough fuel without relying on guesswork.
4. **Schedule early intake.** Start fueling within the first 30-45 minutes instead of waiting until you feel depleted. → *Expect:* energy stays steadier and late-event cravings are reduced.
5. **Practice in training.** Rehearse the exact products, timing, and fluid amounts during long sessions. → *Expect:* no surprise nausea, diarrhea, or bonking at event pace.
6. **Plan backups.** Carry one extra serving and know what aid-station options you can safely use. → *Expect:* a missed bottle or dropped gel does not end the event plan.

## Decision points

- Event under 60-90 minutes → water and normal pre-workout fueling may be enough.
- Stomach distress during practice → lower hourly carbohydrate, slow intake, and try different textures or drink concentration.
- Energy crashes late → begin earlier or increase carbohydrate modestly in training.
- Hot conditions → separate hydration needs from carbohydrate needs and add sodium as appropriate.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Waiting too long to eat:** detect sudden heavy legs, irritability, or pace collapse → start intake earlier next time and take a small carbohydrate serving now if tolerated.
- **F2 Overconcentrated drink:** detect nausea or sticky mouth → dilute with water and reduce powder concentration.
- **F3 Unpracticed race fuel:** detect gastrointestinal upset from aid-station food → use familiar carried fuel for key events.
- **F4 No sodium plan:** detect headache, salt stains, or repeated cramps in heat → add electrolytes and reassess fluid rate.

## Verification

During a long practice session, you take the planned carbohydrate amount each hour, hydrate appropriately, maintain steady effort, and finish without major stomach distress or a late energy crash.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a timer that reminds you to take a small serving every 20-30 minutes.
- `harder`: train gut tolerance gradually toward higher carbohydrate targets for events over 3 hours.
- `equipment`: use bottles, soft flasks, bento boxes, belts, or vest pockets to make fuel reachable.
- `at-home`: lay out all event fuel by hour before packing so gaps are visible.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when practiced gradually. Do not force food or fluid through severe nausea, confusion, chest pain, faintness, or heat illness symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or registered dietitian before starting if you have diabetes, gastrointestinal disease, kidney disease, eating-disorder history, or a prescribed diet.
