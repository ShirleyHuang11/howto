## Goal

You place rest days into a training week so fitness improves without accumulating avoidable fatigue or overuse pain.

## Preconditions

- A list of your planned workouts, including intensity and muscle groups.
- A calendar, notebook, or training app.
- Honest notes on sleep, soreness, stress, and recent performance.

## Steps

1. **List hard sessions first.** Mark heavy lifting, intervals, long endurance sessions, sport matches, and high-impact work. → *Expect:* the stressful sessions are visible on the calendar.
2. **Separate similar stressors.** Avoid stacking the same joints or muscle groups hard on consecutive days when possible. → *Expect:* legs, back, shoulders, and high-impact sessions have recovery space.
3. **Add at least one full rest day.** Keep it free of structured training; walking and easy mobility are fine if they feel restorative. → *Expect:* one day is clearly labeled rest.
4. **Use active recovery strategically.** Place easy cycling, walking, swimming, or mobility after harder sessions only if it leaves you fresher. → *Expect:* recovery work feels easy enough to hold a conversation.
5. **Review fatigue weekly.** Adjust the next week based on soreness, sleep, motivation, and performance. → *Expect:* the schedule changes before fatigue becomes an injury.

## Decision points

- Performance drops for several sessions → add rest or reduce volume.
- Soreness changes your movement pattern → rest the affected area and return gradually.
- Sleep or life stress is high → reduce intensity before adding more workouts.
- You feel fresh and progress is steady → keep rest days where they are.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rest day becomes hard cardio:** detect breathing above easy conversational pace → cap it as active recovery or make it full rest.
- **F2 Same joint loaded daily:** detect knees, shoulders, or low back aching across sessions → alternate movement patterns and impact.
- **F3 Skipping deloads:** detect enthusiasm masking poor performance → schedule a lighter week every few hard training blocks.
- **F4 Guilt-driven training:** detect training only to avoid feeling lazy → use the calendar as the plan and respect rest as part of adaptation.

## Verification

Your weekly schedule includes at least one full rest day, hard sessions are separated by stress type when possible, and you complete the week without worsening soreness, joint pain, or repeated performance drops.

## Variations

- `easier`: start with 3 training days and 4 rest or easy days.
- `harder`: use heart-rate variability, resting heart rate, or readiness notes to fine-tune rest.
- `equipment`: use a calendar app with recurring rest-day blocks.
- `at-home`: label rest days with non-training recovery habits such as sleep, meal prep, and light walking.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. Rest is a training variable, not a sign of failure. Stop training through sharp pain, swelling, dizziness, chest pain, or illness symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or qualified coach before starting if you have a relevant condition, recent injury, or unusually persistent fatigue.
