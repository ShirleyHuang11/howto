## Goal

You identify early warning signs of excessive training stress and adjust workload before performance, mood, sleep, or injury risk worsens.

## Preconditions

- A recent training log or honest memory of workouts over the last 2-4 weeks.
- Notes on sleep, appetite, mood, resting heart rate, soreness, and performance.
- Willingness to reduce training when recovery signals are poor.

## Steps

1. **Compare training load to recovery.** Look for sudden increases in volume, intensity, frequency, or life stress. → *Expect:* you can name what changed before symptoms appeared.
2. **Check performance trend.** Notice repeated failure to hit normal paces, loads, reps, or coordination despite effort. → *Expect:* performance data confirms whether fatigue is accumulating.
3. **Screen body signals.** Look for persistent soreness, heavy legs, nagging joint pain, frequent illness, elevated resting heart rate, or poor sleep. → *Expect:* warning signs are listed plainly.
4. **Screen behavior signals.** Note irritability, low motivation, anxiety about missing workouts, appetite changes, or loss of enjoyment. → *Expect:* mental and physical recovery are both considered.
5. **Reduce load early.** Cut volume or intensity for several days, keep easy movement optional, and prioritize sleep and food. → *Expect:* symptoms begin improving rather than escalating.

## Decision points

- One bad workout only → monitor, but do not panic.
- Several bad workouts plus poor sleep or mood → deload or rest.
- Pain changes mechanics → stop the aggravating movement and seek assessment if it persists.
- Symptoms continue despite 1-2 weeks of reduced load → consult a clinician or qualified coach.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Treating fatigue as laziness:** detect forcing intensity despite declining output → reduce training stress and review recovery basics.
- **F2 Only tracking workouts:** detect no sleep, food, or mood notes → add simple daily recovery markers.
- **F3 Replacing hard training with hard cross-training:** detect total stress staying high → make easy days genuinely easy.
- **F4 Ignoring illness:** detect training hard while feverish or systemically sick → stop and recover before resuming.

## Verification

You identify at least two recovery markers, reduce training load when multiple warning signs appear, and see sleep, soreness, motivation, or performance stabilize before returning to normal training.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a 1-5 daily readiness score for sleep, soreness, mood, and energy.
- `harder`: track weekly volume, intensity distribution, resting heart rate, and subjective readiness together.
- `equipment`: use a wearable only as supporting data, not as the sole decision-maker.
- `at-home`: replace a hard session with walking, mobility, or complete rest when warning signs cluster.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as a monitoring habit, but overtraining-like symptoms can overlap with illness, anemia, hormonal issues, depression, or other medical conditions. Stop hard training for fever, chest pain, faintness, unexplained weight loss, or worsening pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting or continuing if symptoms are persistent or severe.
