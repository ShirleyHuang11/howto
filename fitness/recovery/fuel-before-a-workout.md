## Goal

You choose a pre-workout meal or snack that supports energy without causing stomach discomfort.

## Preconditions

- A planned workout time, duration, and intensity.
- Familiar foods you tolerate well.
- Water available before training.

## Steps

1. **Match food size to timing.** [BRANCH: 2-4 hours before, eat a normal meal with carbohydrate and protein | 30-90 minutes before, choose a smaller carbohydrate-focused snack] → *Expect:* you start with steady energy and a settled stomach.
2. **Prioritize easy carbohydrates.** Use options like oats, rice, toast, fruit, potatoes, yogurt, or a sports drink for harder sessions. → *Expect:* your legs and concentration feel fueled during warmup.
3. **Keep fat and fiber moderate close to training.** Avoid large amounts of fried food, heavy cream, beans, or very high-fiber meals right before intense work. → *Expect:* less reflux, bloating, or urgent bathroom need.
4. **Add protein when there is enough time.** Include a modest serving such as eggs, yogurt, tofu, fish, poultry, or whey/soy protein if eating a full meal. → *Expect:* you feel satisfied but not heavy.
5. **Test and record what works.** Note the food, timing, workout quality, and stomach response. → *Expect:* you identify 2-3 reliable pre-workout options.

## Decision points

- Workout is low intensity and under 45 minutes → a normal meal schedule may be enough.
- Workout is hard, long, or early morning → add a small carbohydrate snack or drink.
- Nausea or reflux occurs → reduce portion size, eat earlier, and choose lower-fat foods.
- Diabetes or glucose-sensitive condition → follow clinician guidance for timing and carbohydrate amount.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Training underfueled:** detect early fatigue, shakiness, or poor focus → add a small carbohydrate snack before similar workouts.
- **F2 Eating too much too late:** detect nausea, cramps, or reflux → move the meal earlier and use a smaller snack close to training.
- **F3 New foods on important days:** detect unexpected stomach upset → practice fueling during normal sessions first.
- **F4 Only caffeine, no food:** detect jitters with low energy → pair caffeine with carbohydrate or reduce caffeine.

## Verification

You complete a planned workout with stable energy, no significant stomach distress, and a repeatable food-and-timing plan written down for that workout type.

## Variations

- `easier`: eat a banana, toast, applesauce, or yogurt 30-60 minutes before training.
- `harder`: periodize carbohydrate intake so the hardest sessions get the most pre-workout fuel.
- `equipment`: use a tracking app or notes app to record food timing and workout quality.
- `at-home`: keep shelf-stable options such as oats, rice cakes, cereal, or sports drink mix available.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most people. Stop exercising if you feel faint, confused, have chest pain, or cannot keep fluids down. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or registered dietitian before starting if you have diabetes, gastrointestinal disease, pregnancy-related concerns, eating-disorder history, or a prescribed diet.
