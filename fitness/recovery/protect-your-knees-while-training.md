## Goal

You train lower-body movements with knee-friendly alignment, load management, and pain rules that reduce avoidable irritation.

## Preconditions

- Shoes and surface appropriate for the activity.
- Warm up first: `fitness/recovery/warm-up-to-prevent-injury`.
- No acute knee injury, major swelling, locking, or instability unless cleared by a clinician.

## Steps

1. **Warm the hips, ankles, and quads.** Use easy cardio plus squats, lunges, step-downs, or leg swings. → *Expect:* knees feel warmer and movement feels smoother.
2. **Align knees with toes.** During squats, lunges, landings, and step-ups, let the knee track over the middle toes instead of collapsing inward. → *Expect:* kneecap points generally the same direction as the foot.
3. **Control depth and tempo.** Use a range you can own without bouncing, twisting, or pain. → *Expect:* reps look smooth on the way down and up.
4. **Load gradually.** Increase weight, speed, hills, jumping, or weekly mileage in small steps. → *Expect:* knees tolerate training without next-day swelling or worsening pain.
5. **Stop for red flags.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if the knee gives way, locks, swells quickly, or sharp pain changes your mechanics. → *Expect:* questionable symptoms are not trained through.

## Decision points

- Mild muscle effort around the knee → continue if form stays controlled.
- Sharp joint pain or swelling → stop and reduce load; seek care if persistent.
- Knees cave inward under fatigue → regress the movement or reduce speed/load.
- Downhill running or jumping causes pain → reduce impact and build strength first.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Knee valgus collapse:** detect knees diving inward on squats or landings → slow down, reduce load, and cue knees over middle toes.
- **F2 Too much impact too soon:** detect aching after jumps or mileage jumps → replace with low-impact conditioning and rebuild gradually.
- **F3 Ignoring ankle or hip limits:** detect heels lifting or hips shifting → add mobility and use a reduced range temporarily.
- **F4 Pain masked by warmup:** detect pain returning worse after training → reduce the aggravating work and get assessed if it repeats.

## Verification

You complete the planned lower-body session with knees tracking over toes, controlled tempo, no giving way or locking, and no increase in knee swelling or sharp pain during or after training.

## Variations

- `easier`: use box squats, step-ups to a low step, or supported split squats.
- `harder`: add load or impact only after alignment stays stable under fatigue.
- `equipment`: use a mirror, video, or light resistance band for feedback, not to force alignment.
- `at-home`: practice sit-to-stand reps and low step-downs with slow control.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as a protective habit. Stop for sharp pain, swelling, locking, giving way, numbness, or pain that changes your gait. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if you have a recent knee injury, surgery, persistent swelling, or diagnosed joint condition.
