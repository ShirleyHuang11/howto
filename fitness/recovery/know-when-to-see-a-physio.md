## Goal

You decide when a training ache can be self-managed briefly and when it deserves assessment by a physical therapist or clinician.

## Preconditions

- You can describe the pain location, onset, aggravating movements, and training changes.
- You know whether symptoms are improving, stable, or worsening.
- Emergency symptoms are absent; urgent red flags require medical care, not routine physio scheduling.

## Steps

1. **Identify how it started.** Note whether pain appeared suddenly during a rep, after a fall, or gradually with training load. → *Expect:* the onset pattern is clear.
2. **Check function.** Test walking, stairs, reaching, squatting, gripping, or sport-specific movement at easy effort. → *Expect:* you know whether pain changes normal mechanics.
3. **Look for red flags.** ⚠️ *Safety:* seek urgent care for fracture suspicion, major swelling, deformity, chest pain, fainting, neurological symptoms, fever, or bowel/bladder changes. → *Expect:* serious symptoms are handled immediately.
4. **Use the 7-14 day rule.** If mild symptoms do not improve with reduced load, sleep, and sensible movement within 1-2 weeks, schedule assessment. → *Expect:* persistent problems are not ignored.
5. **Prepare useful details.** Bring training logs, videos, shoes/equipment notes, and what worsens or eases symptoms. → *Expect:* the physio can connect symptoms to real movement demands.

## Decision points

- Pain is mild, diffuse, and improving → monitor while reducing aggravating load.
- Pain is sharp, focal, worsening, or changes movement → book a physio or clinician.
- Numbness, weakness, radiating symptoms, or night pain → seek medical evaluation promptly.
- Recurrent issue keeps returning with the same exercise → get assessment even if it settles temporarily.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Waiting for months:** detect repeated flare-ups after every return → schedule assessment and modify training.
- **F2 Only resting:** detect pain disappears at rest but returns immediately → address load tolerance and mechanics with help.
- **F3 Ignoring neurological signs:** detect numbness, tingling, weakness, or radiating pain → seek medical care promptly.
- **F4 Poor appointment detail:** detect vague symptom history → write a timeline and bring exercise videos.

## Verification

You either have a clear self-management plan for mild improving symptoms or you have scheduled professional assessment for pain that is sharp, worsening, recurrent, function-limiting, neurological, or not improving after 7-14 days.

## Variations

- `easier`: write a three-line symptom log: when it started, what worsens it, what helps.
- `harder`: bring training volume, load, sleep, and recovery data to the appointment.
- `equipment`: record a short video of the painful movement from the side and front if safe.
- `at-home`: use telehealth if in-person access is limited and symptoms are not urgent.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as a decision process, but some symptoms need urgent care. Do not delay medical attention for severe pain, deformity, suspected fracture, infection signs, chest pain, fainting, progressive weakness, saddle numbness, or bowel/bladder changes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist for diagnosis and individualized treatment.
