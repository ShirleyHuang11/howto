## Goal

You prepare joints, muscles, and nervous system for training with a warmup that matches the session instead of wasting energy.

## Preconditions

- The planned workout or sport is known.
- Enough space for easy movement and dynamic drills.
- No acute injury or illness symptoms that make training unsafe.

## Steps

1. **Raise body temperature.** Do 3-5 minutes of easy cardio such as brisk walking, cycling, rowing, jump rope, or light jogging. → *Expect:* breathing increases slightly and muscles feel warmer.
2. **Move the joints you will use.** Use dynamic mobility for ankles, hips, spine, shoulders, or wrists based on the workout. → *Expect:* range of motion improves without long passive holds.
3. **Activate key muscles.** Add low-effort drills such as glute bridges, band rows, dead bugs, calf raises, or scapular push-ups. → *Expect:* target muscles contract clearly without fatigue.
4. **Rehearse the movement pattern.** Perform lighter sets or slower versions of the main exercise. → *Expect:* coordination sharpens and the first work set does not feel abrupt.
5. **Ramp intensity gradually.** Increase speed, load, or complexity in small steps before full effort. → *Expect:* you reach training intensity with stable form and no surprise pain.

## Decision points

- You still feel stiff after 10 minutes → add targeted mobility or reduce first-set intensity.
- Sharp pain appears → stop the planned movement and assess before continuing.
- Workout is short and easy → keep the warmup brief but still specific.
- Max effort, sprinting, heavy lifting, or sport play → use a longer ramp-up.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Static stretching only:** detect feeling relaxed but not ready to move fast → add dynamic movement and rehearsal sets.
- **F2 Warmup becomes workout:** detect fatigue before the main session → reduce volume and keep drills submaximal.
- **F3 Ignoring specific joints:** detect shoulders, hips, or ankles feeling unprepared → add drills for the actual movement demands.
- **F4 Skipping ramp-up sets:** detect first heavy set feeling awkward → add lighter progressive sets.

## Verification

You begin the main workout warm, coordinated, and pain-free, with at least one easy general movement, one specific mobility or activation drill, and one rehearsal or ramp-up set completed.

## Variations

- `easier`: use 5 minutes of brisk walking plus bodyweight versions of the main exercise.
- `harder`: build a sport-specific warmup with acceleration, deceleration, cutting, or jumping progressions.
- `equipment`: use bands, light dumbbells, a bike, rower, or jump rope.
- `at-home`: use stairs, marching, bodyweight squats, arm circles, and hip hinges.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk. A warmup reduces but does not eliminate injury risk. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, or illness symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if you have a recent injury, heart/lung condition, or movement restriction requiring individualized care.
