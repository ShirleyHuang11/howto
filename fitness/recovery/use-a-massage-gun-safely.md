## Goal

You use a massage gun to reduce muscle tension without irritating joints, nerves, bruises, or injured tissue.

## Preconditions

- A working massage gun with a soft or ball attachment.
- A relaxed seated or lying position where the target muscle is supported.
- No acute injury, unexplained swelling, blood clot concern, open wound, or numb area in the target region.

## Steps

1. **Choose the lowest useful setting.** Start with low speed and light pressure. → *Expect:* vibration feels tolerable and does not cause guarding.
2. **Target muscle belly, not joints or bone.** Place the head on large soft tissue such as quads, calves, glutes, hamstrings, lats, or upper traps. → *Expect:* the device contacts muscle, not kneecap, spine, shin bone, ribs, throat, or joint line.
3. **Glide slowly.** Move about 1-2 inches per second and pause briefly on tight spots without digging. → *Expect:* tissue warms and relaxes without sharp pain.
4. **Limit each area.** Spend about 30-90 seconds per muscle group. → *Expect:* the area feels looser, not numb, bruised, or irritated.
5. **Recheck movement.** After use, gently move the joint through its normal range. → *Expect:* motion feels the same or easier, with no new pain.

## Decision points

- Pain is sharp, electric, or radiating → stop immediately and avoid that area.
- Area is bruised, swollen, hot, or recently injured → do not use the massage gun there.
- Tingling or numbness appears → stop and move away from possible nerve pressure.
- Soreness worsens later → reduce speed, pressure, and time next session.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Pressing too hard:** detect skin blanching, guarding, or bruising → use lighter contact and let vibration do the work.
- **F2 Hitting bony landmarks:** detect rattling discomfort over shin, kneecap, spine, or elbow → move to adjacent muscle belly.
- **F3 Staying too long:** detect numbness or lingering tenderness → cap time to 30-90 seconds per area.
- **F4 Treating an injury like soreness:** detect swelling, instability, or sharp pain → stop and seek professional evaluation.

## Verification

You treat each target muscle for 30-90 seconds with light pressure, no contact on bone or joints, no sharp or radiating pain, and equal or improved comfortable range of motion afterward.

## Variations

- `easier`: use the softest attachment and keep the gun moving continuously.
- `harder`: pair brief massage with active mobility drills for the same muscle group.
- `equipment`: use a foam roller or lacrosse ball if vibration feels too intense.
- `at-home`: sit or lie with pillows supporting the limb so muscles stay relaxed.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when used on healthy muscle tissue. Avoid the front of the neck, spine, head, abdomen, genitals, varicose veins, wounds, bruises, suspected clots, fractures, or acutely injured areas. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if you have nerve symptoms, circulation problems, blood-thinner use, pregnancy concerns, or recent surgery.
