## Goal

You choose ice, heat, or neither for soreness based on symptom type, timing, and warning signs.

## Preconditions

- A sore area after exercise or daily activity.
- Access to a cold pack, warm shower, heating pad, or towel barrier if using temperature therapy.
- No open wounds, impaired sensation, or circulation problems in the area unless cleared by a clinician.

## Steps

1. **Identify the sensation.** Decide whether the area feels dull and stiff, hot and swollen, bruised, sharp, or numb. → *Expect:* you can describe the symptom rather than treating blindly.
2. **Check timing and cause.** Note whether soreness followed a normal workout, a twist, fall, collision, or sudden pop. → *Expect:* traumatic or sudden symptoms are separated from normal muscle soreness.
3. **Choose heat for stiffness.** Use gentle warmth for broad muscle tightness or delayed soreness without swelling. → *Expect:* the area relaxes and movement feels easier.
4. **Choose ice for acute irritation.** Use cold for recent bumps, swelling, or hot irritated tissue, with a towel barrier. → *Expect:* discomfort or swelling sensation decreases without skin pain.
5. **Use short exposures.** Apply heat or ice for 10 to 20 minutes, then remove it and reassess skin and symptoms. → *Expect:* skin remains normal colored and sensation returns quickly.
6. **Skip both if symptoms worsen.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop temperature therapy if skin burns, becomes numb, mottled, very red, or pain increases. → *Expect:* treatment never causes new symptoms.
7. **Combine with gentle movement.** Move the area through comfortable range after heat or once acute pain settles. → *Expect:* motion improves without forcing.
8. **Escalate warning signs.** Seek professional advice for severe swelling, deformity, inability to bear weight, chest pain, fever, spreading redness, numbness, or pain that persists. → *Expect:* concerning symptoms are not self-treated as simple soreness.

## Decision points

- Dull muscle stiffness with no swelling → heat is usually reasonable.
- New swelling, bruising, or hot irritated tissue → ice is usually reasonable.
- Numbness, impaired circulation, open wound, or uncertain injury → avoid heat and ice until medically advised.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Ice burn:** detect painful cold, numb skin, or color change → remove the pack and use a towel barrier next time.
- **F2 Heating swelling:** detect throbbing or increased swelling → stop heat and consider cold or medical evaluation.
- **F3 Treating a serious injury as soreness:** detect pop, deformity, or inability to use the limb → seek care.
- **F4 Too-long exposure:** detect skin irritation after 20 minutes → shorten sessions and reassess.

## Verification

You choose heat, ice, or no temperature therapy based on symptom pattern, use any exposure for 10 to 20 minutes with skin protected, and finish with no increased pain, numbness, swelling, or skin irritation.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a warm shower for general stiffness instead of a heating pad.
- `harder`: contrast therapy only if you already tolerate both temperatures and have no vascular concerns.
- `equipment`: wrap cold packs or heat packs in a towel.
- `at-home`: use household ice wrapped in a towel or a warm shower.

## Safety & privacy

Avoid heat or ice over numb skin, poor circulation, open wounds, or areas where you cannot monitor skin response. Seek medical care for severe, worsening, traumatic, or systemic symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
