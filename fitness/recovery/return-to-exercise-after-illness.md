## Goal

You restart exercise after an illness with a conservative progression that respects symptoms, breathing, heart rate, and fatigue.

## Preconditions

- Fever, vomiting, diarrhea, chest pain, and significant shortness of breath have resolved.
- You can complete normal daily activities without unusual exhaustion.
- Medical clearance if the illness involved the heart, lungs, hospitalization, fainting, severe dehydration, or concerning symptoms.

## Steps

1. **Wait for symptom stability.** Be fever-free and improving for at least 24 hours without relying on fever-reducing medication. → *Expect:* daily tasks feel normal or clearly improving.
2. **Start with easy movement.** Walk, cycle easily, or do gentle mobility for 10-20 minutes. → *Expect:* breathing stays comfortable and you could speak in full sentences.
3. **Check the next-day response.** Monitor fatigue, cough, chest symptoms, dizziness, and resting heart rate the next morning. → *Expect:* symptoms are unchanged or better, not flared.
4. **Progress one variable at a time.** Increase duration before intensity, using short easy sessions for several days. → *Expect:* you add work without a rebound of illness symptoms.
5. **Resume intensity last.** Add intervals, heavy lifting, or sport only after easy sessions feel normal. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop immediately for chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness, palpitations, or severe weakness. → *Expect:* hard efforts return gradually without red-flag symptoms.

## Decision points

- Symptoms are only above the neck and mild → easy movement may be acceptable if energy is normal.
- Fever, body aches, chest congestion, vomiting, or diarrhea occurred → wait longer and restart gently.
- Symptoms worsen after activity → return to rest and retry at a lower dose after improvement.
- COVID, flu, pneumonia, mono, or heart symptoms → seek medical guidance before intensity.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Returning at previous volume:** detect heavy fatigue or symptom rebound → cut back to short easy sessions.
- **F2 Ignoring chest symptoms:** detect tightness, palpitations, or unusual breathlessness → stop and get medical advice.
- **F3 Progressing intensity first:** detect high heart rate for easy work → build duration at low intensity before speed or load.
- **F4 Treating fatigue as deconditioning only:** detect exhaustion out of proportion to effort → add rest days and consider clinical evaluation.

## Verification

You complete 3-5 easy sessions over several days with normal breathing, no fever or symptom rebound, and no chest pain, fainting, palpitations, or unusual next-day fatigue before resuming harder training.

## Variations

- `easier`: begin with 5-10 minutes of walking and stop while you still feel good.
- `harder`: use a staged return plan from a clinician or coach for competitive sport.
- `equipment`: use heart rate as a guardrail, keeping early sessions in an easy conversational zone.
- `at-home`: use gentle mobility and short walks around the house before outdoor or gym sessions.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because illness can affect hydration, lungs, heart rhythm, and recovery capacity. Do not train with fever, chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, uncontrolled asthma symptoms, or worsening illness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you had a serious infection, COVID complications, mono, pneumonia, hospitalization, heart symptoms, or any relevant medical condition.
