## Goal

You improve muscle recovery by making sleep duration, timing, and environment more consistent after training.

## Preconditions

- A normal sleeping space you can adjust for light, noise, temperature, and phone use.
- A rough training schedule and bedtime target.
- No untreated severe sleep disorder symptoms requiring immediate medical advice.

## Steps

1. **Set a consistent sleep window.** Choose a bedtime and wake time that allow 7 to 9 hours in bed most nights. → *Expect:* your schedule has a clear target instead of shifting randomly.
2. **Place hard workouts earlier when possible.** Avoid intense training in the last 2 to 3 hours before bed if it keeps you alert. → *Expect:* heart rate and body temperature settle before sleep.
3. **Eat and hydrate normally.** Have enough protein, carbohydrates, and fluids earlier in the evening without going to bed overly full. → *Expect:* hunger and thirst do not wake you.
4. **Dim light and reduce stimulation.** Lower bright screens and work stress for the last 30 to 60 minutes. → *Expect:* sleepiness builds gradually.
5. **Make the room recovery-friendly.** Keep the room cool, dark, and quiet, using eye mask, earplugs, or white noise if needed. → *Expect:* fewer awakenings from the environment.
6. **Use a simple wind-down routine.** Read, stretch gently, breathe slowly, or take a warm shower. → *Expect:* the routine signals that training and work are done.
7. **Track sleep and soreness briefly.** Use a notebook or app to note bedtime, wake time, sleep quality, soreness, and performance. → *Expect:* patterns become visible without obsessing over a single night.
8. **Protect sleep after hard sessions.** ⚠️ *Safety:* do not use alcohol, sedatives, or excessive supplements as a recovery shortcut; seek medical advice for persistent insomnia, loud snoring with choking, or severe daytime sleepiness. → *Expect:* recovery relies on sustainable habits.

## Decision points

- You wake often hungry → add a balanced evening snack.
- You cannot fall asleep after late workouts → move intensity earlier or extend the cool-down.
- Sleep is poor for weeks despite habits → talk with a healthcare professional.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Inconsistent wake time:** detect weekend swings that disrupt weekdays → anchor wake time within about an hour.
- **F2 Late caffeine:** detect alertness at bedtime → move caffeine earlier or reduce dose.
- **F3 Overtracking:** detect anxiety from sleep scores → track trends, not nightly perfection.
- **F4 Hot room:** detect sweating or restless sleep → cool the room and use lighter bedding.

## Verification

Over 7 days, you keep a mostly consistent sleep window, spend 7 to 9 hours in bed on most nights, reduce late stimulation, and see stable or improved soreness, energy, or training readiness.

## Variations

- `easier`: set only a fixed wake time and 30-minute wind-down routine.
- `harder`: coordinate training intensity, meal timing, and bedtime across the week.
- `equipment`: use blackout curtains, eye mask, earplugs, fan, or a basic sleep log.
- `at-home`: adjust light, temperature, phone location, and bedtime routine in your own room.

## Safety & privacy

Sleep problems can reflect medical or mental-health conditions. Seek professional help for persistent insomnia, breathing pauses, loud snoring with choking, severe daytime sleepiness, chest pain, or reliance on alcohol or sedatives for sleep. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
