## Goal

You lower running-injury risk by managing training load, strengthening key tissues, warming up, and responding early to warning signs.

## Preconditions

- Running shoes appropriate for your comfort and surface.
- A simple log for mileage, intensity, soreness, and sleep.
- A safe route or treadmill and enough time for warmup and recovery.

## Steps

1. **Record your current load.** Note weekly mileage, run frequency, hard sessions, and longest run. → *Expect:* you know your actual starting point.
2. **Increase gradually.** Add distance or intensity in small steps, not both aggressively in the same week. → *Expect:* weekly load changes feel manageable.
3. **Keep easy runs easy.** Run most miles at conversational effort. → *Expect:* you can speak in full sentences on easy days.
4. **Warm up before intensity.** Jog easily, then add dynamic drills before faster running or hills. → *Expect:* the first hard rep does not feel abrupt.
5. **Strength train twice weekly.** Include calf raises, squats or split squats, hip hinges, side planks, and foot or balance work. → *Expect:* lower legs, hips, and trunk tolerate running better.
6. **Rotate stress.** Alternate hard and easy days and avoid stacking speed, hills, and long runs without recovery. → *Expect:* soreness does not accumulate each day.
7. **Monitor warning signs.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop or reduce running for sharp pain, limping, swelling, pain that worsens during a run, or pain that changes your stride. → *Expect:* small issues are addressed before becoming major.
8. **Schedule cutback weeks.** Every few weeks, reduce volume by 15 to 30 percent if fatigue is building. → *Expect:* legs rebound and motivation improves.

## Decision points

- Pain warms up and disappears quickly → monitor, reduce intensity, and check the next morning.
- Pain worsens as you run → stop the run and switch to rest or low-impact cross-training.
- You are adding speed work → keep total weekly mileage stable while adapting.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Mileage spike:** detect a sudden jump in weekly distance → return to the last comfortable week.
- **F2 Too many hard days:** detect heavy legs and poor sleep → replace one hard session with easy running.
- **F3 Ignored calf or shin pain:** detect localized bone or tendon tenderness → stop impact and seek assessment if persistent.
- **F4 Weak hips and calves:** detect knee cave, hip drop, or calf tightness → prioritize strength work before adding volume.

## Verification

Across 28 days, you log each run, keep most runs conversational, complete two strength sessions per week, avoid sudden load spikes, and finish runs without limping, swelling, or pain that worsens during activity.

## Variations

- `easier`: run-walk with rest days between sessions.
- `harder`: add one structured speed session only after four stable weeks.
- `equipment`: use a watch or simple notes app to track load.
- `at-home`: do strength and mobility sessions indoors on non-running days.

## Safety & privacy

Stop for sharp pain, limping, swelling, chest pain, faintness, stress-fracture-like focal bone pain, or symptoms that worsen as you run. Seek professional help for persistent or recurring pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
