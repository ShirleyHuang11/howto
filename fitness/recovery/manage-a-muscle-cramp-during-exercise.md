## Goal

You stop a common exercise-related muscle cramp safely, reduce recurrence during the session, and know when symptoms are not a simple cramp.

## Preconditions

- A safe place to stop moving without traffic, weights, or other hazards.
- Water or electrolyte access if the session is long, hot, or sweaty.
- No major trauma, swelling, or suspected tear in the cramped area.

## Steps

1. **Stop the activity safely.** Put weights down, step off the field, or move to the side of the path. → *Expect:* you are stable and not fighting the cramp while under load.
2. **Gently lengthen the cramped muscle.** Move into a mild stretch opposite the cramp without bouncing. → *Expect:* the muscle tension begins to ease within seconds to a few minutes.
3. **Breathe and relax the area.** Keep the stretch light and avoid clenching nearby muscles. → *Expect:* pain decreases rather than spikes.
4. **Rehydrate if appropriate.** Sip water and include sodium if heat, long duration, or heavy sweating is involved. → *Expect:* thirst and overheating begin to settle.
5. **Restart below previous intensity.** Resume with easier effort, shorter stride, lighter load, or reduced pace. → *Expect:* the muscle works without immediately cramping again.

## Decision points

- Cramp resolves fully → restart gently and monitor.
- Cramp returns repeatedly → end the hard portion of the workout.
- Sharp tearing pain, bruising, swelling, or weakness → stop and treat as possible injury.
- Confusion, collapse, heat illness signs, or whole-body cramping → seek urgent help.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stretching aggressively:** detect sharper pain or guarding → back off to a mild stretch and breathe slowly.
- **F2 Continuing at the same pace:** detect immediate re-cramp → reduce intensity or stop the session.
- **F3 Blaming only hydration:** detect cramps during high-fatigue efforts despite fluids → reduce workload and improve pacing or conditioning.
- **F4 Missing injury signs:** detect bruising, swelling, or loss of strength → stop exercise and seek medical evaluation.

## Verification

The cramp releases, you can move the limb through normal range without sharp pain, and any resumed activity stays easy enough that the cramp does not recur for at least several minutes.

## Variations

- `easier`: stop the workout after the first cramp and walk home or cool down.
- `harder`: analyze pacing, heat exposure, strength endurance, and fueling after repeated cramps.
- `equipment`: use a bottle with electrolytes for long hot sessions.
- `at-home`: use a wall or towel for gentle calf, hamstring, or quad stretching.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary cramps, but cramps can resemble injury or heat illness. Stop for sharp tearing pain, swelling, bruising, weakness, confusion, faintness, chest pain, or severe heat symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if cramps are frequent, unexplained, medication-related, or associated with neurological or circulation symptoms.
