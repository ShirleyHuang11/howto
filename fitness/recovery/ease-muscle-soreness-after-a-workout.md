## Goal

You reduce normal post-workout muscle soreness with gentle movement, hydration, food, and sleep while recognizing signs that are not normal soreness.

## Preconditions

- Soreness began after exercise and feels broad or muscular, not sharp or traumatic.
- Access to water, a light meal or snack, and room for easy movement.
- No severe swelling, dark urine, fever, or major loss of function.

## Steps

1. **Screen the soreness.** Identify where it is, when it started, and whether it is symmetrical and muscle-like. → *Expect:* normal soreness feels dull, tender, and worse with the first few movements.
2. **Move gently for 5 to 10 minutes.** Walk, cycle easily, or do light mobility through comfortable ranges. → *Expect:* stiffness decreases as tissues warm.
3. **Use light self-massage if helpful.** Roll or massage around sore muscles with mild pressure, avoiding joints and bruised areas. → *Expect:* pressure feels tolerable and does not cause guarding.
4. **Hydrate and eat protein with carbohydrates.** Drink to thirst and include a normal recovery meal or snack. → *Expect:* energy and hydration improve over the next few hours.
5. **Use heat for general stiffness.** Apply warm shower or gentle heat for 10 to 20 minutes if it feels soothing. → *Expect:* muscles relax without skin irritation.
6. **Keep the next workout easy.** Train different muscles or reduce load and volume until soreness fades. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop exercise and seek care if pain is sharp, swelling is severe, urine is dark, weakness is unusual, or symptoms worsen rapidly. → *Expect:* movement remains comfortable and controlled.
7. **Prioritize sleep.** Set up a full night's sleep and avoid late hard training if possible. → *Expect:* soreness is improved by the next day or two.
8. **Track what caused it.** Note new exercises, high eccentric work, or volume jumps. → *Expect:* you can adjust the next session to prevent repeat excessive soreness.

## Decision points

- Soreness improves with light movement → continue gentle activity.
- Pain is localized near a tendon or joint → avoid loading it and monitor closely.
- Soreness lasts more than several days or limits normal function → reduce training and consider professional advice.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stretching aggressively:** detect pain or guarding → use gentle range of motion instead.
- **F2 Training hard through soreness:** detect degraded form → reduce load, range, or volume.
- **F3 Dehydration and under-eating:** detect headache, fatigue, or poor appetite management → drink to thirst and eat a balanced meal.
- **F4 Missing injury signs:** detect bruising, swelling, sharp pain, or weakness → stop and seek evaluation.

## Verification

Within 20 minutes, soreness feels the same or better after easy movement, you can move through daily ranges without sharp pain, and you have a hydration, meal, sleep, and next-workout adjustment plan.

## Variations

- `easier`: take a short walk and warm shower only.
- `harder`: add a low-intensity recovery bike ride if soreness is mild.
- `equipment`: use a foam roller, massage ball, or heating pad gently.
- `at-home`: do walking, light mobility, shower heat, and a normal recovery meal.

## Safety & privacy

Normal soreness should not include severe swelling, dark urine, faintness, fever, numbness, sharp pain, or major weakness. Seek medical care for those signs or symptoms that do not improve. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
