## Goal

You start exercise normally hydrated, replace fluid during longer sessions, and avoid both dehydration and overdrinking.

## Preconditions

- Water access before, during, and after training.
- Optional electrolyte drink for long, hot, or very sweaty sessions.
- Awareness of heat, humidity, session length, and your usual sweat rate.

## Steps

1. **Check baseline hydration.** Look for pale-yellow urine, normal thirst, and stable body weight before training. → *Expect:* you begin without dry mouth, dizziness, or dark urine.
2. **Drink ahead, not all at once.** Sip fluid over the 2-4 hours before exercise instead of chugging immediately before. → *Expect:* you feel comfortable, not sloshy, at the start.
3. **Match fluid to session length.** [BRANCH: under 60 minutes, drink to thirst | over 60 minutes or hot conditions, plan regular sips and consider electrolytes] → *Expect:* thirst stays manageable and performance does not drop from fluid loss.
4. **Use sodium when sweat loss is high.** For long endurance work, heat, or visible salt marks on clothing, include electrolytes or salty foods. → *Expect:* cramping risk, headache, and heavy thirst are reduced without stomach upset.
5. **Rehydrate after training.** Drink gradually with a meal or snack, especially if you lost weight during the session. → *Expect:* urine color and thirst return toward normal within a few hours.

## Decision points

- Dark urine and strong thirst before training → delay intensity and drink gradually.
- Clear urine all day plus nausea or headache after forced drinking → stop overdrinking and consider electrolytes.
- Hot weather or heavy sweating → reduce pace, add shade breaks, and increase sodium and fluid access.
- Dizziness, confusion, chills, or stopped sweating in heat → stop exercise and seek urgent help.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Chugging right before exercise:** detect stomach sloshing or side stitch → sip earlier and reduce last-minute volume.
- **F2 Ignoring electrolytes:** detect salt crust, headache, or repeated cramps during long hot sessions → add sodium and practice the plan in training.
- **F3 Overhydration:** detect bloating, nausea, and very frequent clear urine → drink to thirst and avoid forcing large volumes.
- **F4 No post-session plan:** detect lingering thirst and dark urine hours later → pair water with a normal salty meal.

## Verification

You complete a workout starting without dehydration symptoms, drink appropriately for the session length and heat, and return to normal thirst and pale-yellow urine within a few hours afterward.

## Variations

- `easier`: carry a marked bottle and finish only what the session conditions justify.
- `harder`: weigh before and after key sessions to estimate personal sweat loss.
- `equipment`: use a hydration vest, handheld bottle, or bike bottles for long sessions.
- `at-home`: pre-fill bottles before workouts so fluid is available immediately.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when drinking to thirst and matching conditions. Both dehydration and excessive water intake can be dangerous. Stop if you develop dizziness, confusion, faintness, chest pain, severe headache, or heat illness symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have kidney, heart, blood-pressure, or electrolyte disorders.
