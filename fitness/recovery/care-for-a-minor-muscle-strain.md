## Goal

You manage a minor muscle strain with protection, gradual motion, and sensible return to activity while watching for signs that need medical evaluation.

## Preconditions

- Mild strain suspected after a pull or overreach, with no deformity or major loss of function.
- Ability to rest the area and avoid the painful activity.
- Access to basic first-aid supplies and professional care if symptoms are concerning.

## Steps

1. **Stop the aggravating activity.** End the set, practice, or run as soon as the strain is noticed. → *Expect:* pain is not repeatedly provoked.
2. **Check for red flags.** Look for a pop, visible deformity, major bruising, severe swelling, numbness, or inability to bear weight or use the limb. ⚠️ *Safety:* seek urgent care for these signs. → *Expect:* serious injury is ruled out before home care.
3. **Protect the area for 24 to 48 hours.** Avoid painful stretching, heavy loading, sprinting, or explosive movement. → *Expect:* symptoms settle instead of flaring.
4. **Use compression and elevation if swollen.** Apply gentle compression and elevate when practical, without cutting off circulation. → *Expect:* swelling does not increase and skin stays normal.
5. **Add comfortable motion.** Move through pain-free range several times per day. → *Expect:* motion gradually improves without sharp pain.
6. **Reintroduce light isometrics.** Gently contract the muscle at low effort for 5 to 10 seconds if pain-free. → *Expect:* contraction feels tolerable and does not worsen later.
7. **Progress loading gradually.** Move from easy range of motion to light resistance, then sport-specific work over days. → *Expect:* each step is pain-free during and the next day.
8. **Return only after function returns.** Resume full activity when strength, range, and movement quality match the uninjured side. → *Expect:* no limp, guarding, or compensation appears.

## Decision points

- Pain increases the next day → return to the previous easier step.
- Bruising spreads or swelling grows → get medical evaluation.
- You cannot perform daily movements normally after several days → seek professional guidance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stretching too soon:** detect sharp pulling pain → stop stretching and use gentle range only.
- **F2 Returning on adrenaline:** detect pain hidden during sport but worse later → delay return and test controlled drills first.
- **F3 Compression too tight:** detect numbness, tingling, or color change → remove and rewrap looser.
- **F4 Ignoring compensation:** detect limping or altered mechanics → reduce load until movement is symmetrical.

## Verification

Within 7 days for a truly minor strain, daily movement is pain-free or clearly improving, range of motion is near normal, light contractions are tolerated, and no red flags such as severe swelling, deformity, numbness, or inability to use the limb are present.

## Variations

- `easier`: extend the protection phase and use only gentle range of motion.
- `harder`: add progressive resistance only after pain-free daily movement returns.
- `equipment`: use an elastic wrap for gentle compression if swelling is present.
- `at-home`: perform pain-free range-of-motion and light isometric checks without equipment.

## Safety & privacy

Muscle strains can be more serious than they first appear. Seek care for a pop, major bruising, severe swelling, deformity, numbness, weakness, inability to bear weight, or symptoms that do not improve. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
