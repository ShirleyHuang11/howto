## Goal

You foam roll the major leg muscles with controlled pressure to reduce perceived tightness and prepare for comfortable movement.

## Preconditions

- A foam roller and floor space.
- Ability to get down to and up from the floor safely.
- No acute bruising, swelling, fracture, open wound, blood clot concern, or severe unexplained pain.

## Steps

1. **Start with light pressure.** Support some body weight through your hands or opposite leg. → *Expect:* pressure feels tolerable, not sharp or breath-holding intense.
2. **Roll the calves.** Move from below the knee to above the Achilles, avoiding the back of the knee and Achilles tendon itself. → *Expect:* calf muscle pressure is broad and controlled.
3. **Roll the quads.** Lie face down and roll from upper thigh to above the kneecap, keeping the abdomen lightly braced. → *Expect:* pressure stays on the front thigh muscle, not the kneecap.
4. **Roll the hamstrings and glutes.** Sit on the roller and shift side to side to reach muscle, not bone. → *Expect:* tight spots ease without numbness or tingling.
5. **Finish with movement.** Stand, walk, squat shallowly, or perform gentle leg swings. → *Expect:* legs feel warmer or freer without new pain.

## Decision points

- Pressure causes sharp pain or tingling → stop or move away from that spot.
- You are very sore after rolling → reduce time and pressure next session.
- A joint hurts rather than muscle tissue → stop rolling that area.
- Rolling improves comfort only briefly → pair it with strength and mobility work.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rolling directly on joints:** detect pressure on kneecap, ankle bone, hip point, or back of knee → reposition to muscle belly.
- **F2 Too much pressure:** detect bruising, breath holding, or guarding → support more weight with your arms or use a softer roller.
- **F3 Fast random rolling:** detect no change in tissue feel → slow down and spend 30-60 seconds per muscle.
- **F4 Nerve irritation:** detect tingling, numbness, or electric sensations → stop and avoid compressing that area.

## Verification

You roll calves, quads, hamstrings, and glutes for about 30-60 seconds each with tolerable pressure, no joint or nerve symptoms, and equal or improved walking, squatting, or leg-swing comfort afterward.

## Variations

- `easier`: use a soft roller and keep more body weight on the floor.
- `harder`: pause on a tight muscle area for 2-3 slow breaths, then move the joint gently.
- `equipment`: use a massage stick for calves or quads if floor positions are difficult.
- `at-home`: roll beside a wall or couch so you can stand up safely afterward.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when used on healthy soft tissue. Avoid rolling over joints, bones, varicose veins, wounds, bruises, suspected clots, or acutely injured tissue. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if you have nerve symptoms, circulation problems, blood-thinner use, recent surgery, or unexplained leg pain.
