## Goal

You set a practical daily protein target and spread intake across meals so training recovery, muscle repair, and satiety are supported.

## Preconditions

- Your approximate body weight and a normal day of meals available to review.
- A way to estimate portions, such as food labels, a tracking app, or common serving sizes.
- No medical restriction requiring protein limitation unless cleared by a clinician.

## Steps

1. **Choose a daily target.** Use about 1.2-2.0 g protein per kg body weight per day for regular training, aiming toward the lower end for general fitness and the higher end during strength training or fat loss. → *Expect:* a realistic gram target you can meet with normal foods.
2. **Split the target across the day.** Plan 3-5 protein servings rather than saving most of it for one meal. → *Expect:* each meal has a meaningful protein source and you are not trying to catch up at night.
3. **Anchor meals with complete protein.** Use foods such as eggs, dairy, fish, poultry, meat, soy, or a mix of legumes and grains. → *Expect:* the main plate includes a palm-sized protein serving or a measured equivalent.
4. **Add a recovery serving after hard sessions.** Within a few hours after training, include roughly 20-40 g protein, especially if the next meal is far away. → *Expect:* hunger settles and the meal or snack contains enough protein to count.
5. **Check the weekly average.** Track or estimate 3-7 days, then adjust food choices rather than forcing one perfect day. → *Expect:* your average lands near the target without digestive discomfort.

## Decision points

- Target feels impossible → start with one added protein serving per day, then increase gradually.
- Stomach discomfort or bloating → reduce serving size, spread intake further, and use foods you tolerate.
- Kidney disease, pregnancy, or prescribed diet → ask a clinician or registered dietitian before changing intake.
- Training volume increases → reassess the target and total calories, not protein alone.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 All protein at dinner:** detect low-protein breakfast and lunch → move one serving earlier in the day.
- **F2 Under-counted plant protein:** detect meals built mostly from vegetables or starches → add tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, seitan, or a protein-rich dairy/soy option.
- **F3 Protein without enough total food:** detect fatigue, poor sleep, or persistent soreness while dieting aggressively → raise total calories and carbohydrates around training.
- **F4 Supplement overuse:** detect shakes replacing most meals → keep supplements optional and rebuild meals around whole foods first.

## Verification

For at least 5 of 7 days, you meet your chosen daily protein target within about 10-15 g, with 3 or more protein servings per day and no new digestive or medical symptoms.

## Variations

- `easier`: add one high-protein snack, such as Greek yogurt, tofu, eggs, tuna, cottage cheese, or a protein shake.
- `harder`: time 20-40 g protein after the hardest training sessions and before long gaps without food.
- `equipment`: use a food scale or tracking app for 1 week, then return to hand portions.
- `at-home`: batch-cook a protein staple twice per week so recovery meals are already available.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for most healthy adults, but high protein intake is not appropriate for everyone. Stop changes that cause persistent nausea, severe digestive symptoms, or allergic reactions. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or registered dietitian before starting if you have kidney disease, metabolic disease, pregnancy-related concerns, or a prescribed diet.
