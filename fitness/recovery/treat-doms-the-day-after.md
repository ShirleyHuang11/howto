## Goal

You manage delayed-onset muscle soreness after training while distinguishing normal soreness from possible injury.

## Preconditions

- Soreness began several hours to a day after unfamiliar or hard exercise.
- No sharp traumatic pain, major swelling, bruising, fever, dark urine, or severe weakness.
- Access to easy movement, fluids, normal meals, and sleep.

## Steps

1. **Confirm it behaves like DOMS.** Look for broad muscle tenderness and stiffness that is worse after rest but improves as you move. → *Expect:* soreness is muscular and symmetrical or explainable by the workout.
2. **Move gently.** Walk, cycle easily, swim gently, or do light mobility for 10-20 minutes. → *Expect:* stiffness decreases during movement.
3. **Use light range of motion.** Move sore joints through comfortable ranges without forcing end positions. → *Expect:* motion improves without sharp pain.
4. **Support recovery basics.** Eat enough total food and protein, hydrate, and prioritize sleep. → *Expect:* soreness trends down over 24-72 hours.
5. **Resume training at lower intensity.** Train sore muscles lightly or choose a different area until movement quality returns. → *Expect:* form stays normal and soreness does not spike.

## Decision points

- Soreness is mild and form is normal → light training is acceptable.
- Soreness changes gait or lifting mechanics → reduce load or rest that area.
- Sharp pain, bruising, swelling, or weakness → treat as possible injury.
- Dark urine, severe swelling, or extreme pain after hard exercise → seek urgent medical care.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Training hard through altered form:** detect limping or shortened range → switch to recovery movement or rest.
- **F2 Aggressive stretching:** detect sharper pain in sore tissue → use gentle range of motion instead.
- **F3 Confusing injury for soreness:** detect one specific painful spot, bruising, or sudden onset during exercise → stop and get assessed if it persists.
- **F4 Repeating the same overload:** detect DOMS every week from the same session → reduce eccentric volume and progress gradually.

## Verification

Within 24-72 hours, soreness is decreasing, daily movement is normal, and any return to training is completed with full control, normal range, and no sharp pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: take a full rest day with short walks and gentle mobility only.
- `harder`: use a planned deload or gradual exposure to reduce repeated DOMS from a new exercise.
- `equipment`: use a foam roller or massage gun lightly if it improves comfort.
- `at-home`: use warm showers, easy stairs, and bodyweight mobility rather than intense stretching.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for normal DOMS. Stop and seek care for severe swelling, dark cola-colored urine, fever, numbness, major weakness, bruising, or pain that is sharp or worsening. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if soreness is extreme, recurrent, or hard to distinguish from injury.
