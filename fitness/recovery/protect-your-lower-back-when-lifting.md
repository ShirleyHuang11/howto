## Goal

You lift with a stable trunk, effective hip hinge, and load choices that reduce avoidable lower-back strain.

## Preconditions

- Warm up first: `fitness/recovery/warm-up-to-prevent-injury`.
- A load you can lift without grinding or losing position.
- No severe back pain, leg numbness, bowel/bladder changes, or recent traumatic injury.

## Steps

1. **Set your brace before lifting.** Inhale quietly into the abdomen and sides, then tighten as if preparing for a cough. → *Expect:* ribs, pelvis, and trunk feel stacked and stable.
2. **Hinge from the hips.** Push the hips back while keeping the spine long and the load close. → *Expect:* hamstrings and glutes load more than the low back.
3. **Keep the load near your center.** Hold weights close to the body and avoid reaching far while loaded. → *Expect:* the weight feels connected to your legs and trunk, not pulling your spine forward.
4. **Move without twisting under load.** Turn with your feet instead of rotating the spine while carrying or lifting. → *Expect:* hips and feet redirect the body together.
5. **Stop when position fails.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop the set if your lower back rounds under load, pain shoots down the leg, or you cannot keep the brace. → *Expect:* every completed rep keeps a controlled trunk position.

## Decision points

- Back muscles feel like normal effort → continue if position stays stable.
- Sharp back pain or radiating leg symptoms → stop and seek medical guidance if persistent or severe.
- Form breaks only at heavier loads → reduce weight and rebuild.
- Grip fails before posture → use lighter load, shorter sets, or appropriate grip assistance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Rounding under load:** detect pelvis tucking and spine flexing as the weight moves → reduce load and hinge shallower.
- **F2 Overarching:** detect ribs flaring and low back pinching → stack ribs over pelvis and brace evenly.
- **F3 Load too far away:** detect weight drifting forward → bring it closer or change the implement.
- **F4 Twisting while carrying:** detect shoulders turning while feet stay planted → pivot the feet and move slower.

## Verification

You complete the lifting task with a braced trunk, neutral controlled spine, load kept close, no twisting under load, and no sharp back pain or radiating symptoms.

## Variations

- `easier`: practice a dowel hip hinge or bodyweight good morning before loading.
- `harder`: increase load only while video confirms the same trunk position.
- `equipment`: use kettlebells, dumbbells, trap bar handles, or raised blocks to keep the load closer.
- `at-home`: apply the same brace and hip hinge to laundry baskets, boxes, and groceries.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk as general lifting practice, but back symptoms can be serious. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, weakness, pain below the knee, saddle numbness, or bowel/bladder changes. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or physical therapist before starting if you have current back pain, a disc diagnosis, osteoporosis, recent injury, or neurological symptoms.
