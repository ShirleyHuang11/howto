## Goal

You reduce calf tightness and improve ankle motion for walking, running, squatting, and stairs without overstretching the Achilles tendon.

## Preconditions

- A wall or step and optional foam roller or ball.
- Mild muscular tightness only, not acute calf strain or Achilles pain.
- Shoes off or stable shoes on a non-slip surface.

## Steps

1. **Check ankle motion.** Face a wall and drive one knee toward it while the heel stays down. → *Expect:* you know which side feels tighter or lifts the heel sooner.
2. **Massage the calf lightly.** Roll or knead the calf muscle belly for 60 seconds, avoiding direct pressure on the Achilles tendon. → *Expect:* muscle tone decreases without bruising pain.
3. **Stretch the gastrocnemius.** Place hands on a wall, back leg straight, heel down, and toes forward. → *Expect:* stretch is felt high in the calf.
4. **Stretch the soleus.** Keep the same stance but bend the back knee while the heel stays down. → *Expect:* stretch moves lower and deeper in the calf.
5. **Add active ankle rocks.** From a split stance, drive the knee forward over the toes for 10 controlled reps. → *Expect:* the ankle moves farther while the arch stays lifted.
6. **Load gently with calf raises.** Rise on both feet, pause, and lower slowly for 8-12 reps. → *Expect:* calves work through range without cramping.
7. **Respect tendon pain.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if pain is sharp, localized at the Achilles, or worsens with each rep. → *Expect:* sensations remain muscular and settle after the set.

## Decision points

- Tightness is worse after running -> reduce volume and add gradual calf strengthening.
- Achilles feels tender -> avoid aggressive stretching and seek professional advice if persistent.
- One side is much tighter -> spend one extra round on that side without forcing.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Toe turnout:** detect the back foot angled outward -> point toes forward.
- **F2 Heel lift:** detect heel peeling up during rocks -> reduce range.
- **F3 Arch collapse:** detect foot rolling inward -> press the big toe and heel down while lifting the arch.
- **F4 Over-rolling:** detect bruised or numb sensation -> use lighter pressure and shorter duration.

## Verification

You complete 8 minutes, perform 10 knee-to-wall rocks per side with heels down and knees tracking over toes, and walk with less calf tightness and no Achilles pain.

## Variations

- easier: stretch with hands on a wall and smaller stance.
- harder: add slow single-leg calf raises after pain-free double-leg work.
- equipment: use a foam roller, massage ball, wall, or step.
- at-home: use a stair edge only if balance is secure.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for ordinary tightness. Stop for sharp calf pain, swelling, warmth, sudden popping, Achilles pain, or symptoms after injury. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
