## Goal

You improve hamstring and hip-hinge range so your fingertips move closer to your toes without rounding aggressively or yanking on your low back.

## Preconditions

- Warm up first: `fitness/do-a-dynamic-warmup`.
- A wall, step, or yoga block if your hands do not reach the floor.
- No radiating nerve pain, numbness, or recent hamstring or back injury.

## Steps

1. **Measure your starting reach.** Stand tall, feet hip-width, exhale, and fold forward gently without bouncing. → *Expect:* you know whether your hands reach thighs, shins, ankles, or toes.
2. **Find a clean hip hinge.** Put hands on hips, soften knees, push hips back, and keep your spine long as your torso tips forward. → *Expect:* you feel tension in the back of the thighs, not pinching in the low back.
3. **Use bent-knee forward folds.** Bend knees enough to rest hands on thighs, shins, blocks, or the floor, then slowly raise hips until a mild stretch appears. → *Expect:* the stretch is broad and tolerable, with steady breathing.
4. **Add active hamstring pulses.** From the folded position, bend and straighten one knee at a time through a small range. → *Expect:* each leg alternates tension and release without bouncing.
5. **Train end-range control.** Lie on your back, loop a towel behind one thigh, and straighten the knee only as far as the pelvis stays heavy. → *Expect:* the raised leg straightens more over time without your hips tucking hard.
6. **Retest without forcing.** Stand and repeat the forward fold after the drills. → *Expect:* your reach improves slightly or feels smoother at the same distance.
7. **Respect nerve symptoms.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you feel burning, tingling, electric pain, or symptoms traveling below the knee. → *Expect:* stretching stays muscular and local.

## Decision points

- Stretch feels behind the knee with tingling -> reduce range and use a bent-knee version.
- Low back feels strained -> hinge less deeply and support your hands higher.
- Progress stalls -> practice 4-6 days per week and add gentle loaded hinges.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Bouncing:** detect jerky movement at the bottom -> pause, breathe, and move slowly.
- **F2 Locked knees:** detect hard knee extension and calf pulling -> soften the knees.
- **F3 Pelvis tucks early:** detect your low back rounding immediately -> use blocks and hinge from the hips.
- **F4 Holding breath:** detect bracing or facial tension -> exhale into the stretch.

## Verification

After 10 minutes, you can fold forward to your current best reachable point with mild hamstring tension, steady breathing, soft knees, and no sharp back or nerve symptoms.

## Variations

- easier: place hands on a chair or blocks and keep knees bent.
- harder: add slow Romanian deadlift patterns with light weight after basic range is comfortable.
- equipment: use a towel, strap, blocks, or a low step.
- at-home: use a couch cushion or thick books as hand supports.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when you avoid force. Stop for sharp pain, tingling, numbness, or radiating symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
