## Goal

Stretch the hamstrings with a hip-hinge position that targets the back of the thigh without irritating the low back or sciatic nerve.

## Preconditions

- Warm legs from 3-5 minutes of walking or easy movement.
- Wall, chair, strap, or towel available if needed.
- No acute hamstring strain or nerve symptoms.

## Steps

1. **Choose a supported position.** [BRANCH: beginner, lie on your back with a strap around one foot | standing option, place heel on a low step] → *Expect:* pelvis stays level and low back feels neutral.
2. **Set a soft knee.** Keep the stretching leg slightly bent instead of locked. → *Expect:* stretch moves into the hamstring belly rather than sharp tension behind the knee.
3. **Hinge or lift from the hip.** In standing, tip the pelvis forward; lying down, raise the leg until mild-moderate tension appears. → *Expect:* back of thigh stretches while spine stays quiet.
4. **Hold and breathe.** Hold 20-45 seconds with slow exhales and no bouncing. → *Expect:* tension eases slightly during the hold.
5. **Add gentle active work.** Press the heel lightly down into the strap or surface for 5 seconds, then relax deeper only if comfortable. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for tingling, numbness, electric pain, or a sharp pull. → *Expect:* range improves without nerve-like symptoms.
6. **Repeat both sides.** Do 2-3 rounds per leg and stand up slowly. → *Expect:* forward bending or stride length feels easier.

## Decision points

- Stretch is behind the knee or calf with tingling → bend the knee more and reduce range.
- Low back rounds or aches → use the lying strap version.
- One side feels much tighter → give it one extra gentle round.
- You need flexibility for sport → follow stretching with active hamstring strength.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Locked knee:** detect hard knee extension and nerve tension → soften the knee.
- **F2 Spinal rounding:** detect low back doing the movement → hinge at hips or lie down.
- **F3 Bouncing:** detect pulsing into end range → hold still and breathe.
- **F4 Too much intensity:** detect guarding or breath-holding → back off to a 3-5 out of 10 stretch.

## Verification

You completed 2-3 controlled hamstring stretches per side, held 20-45 seconds each, kept the spine neutral, and had no tingling, numbness, or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: lie near a doorway and rest the leg on the wall.
- Harder: add gentle contract-relax holds or active straight-leg raises.
- Equipment: strap, towel, yoga block, low step, or chair.
- At-home: use a towel around the foot while lying on the floor.

## Safety & privacy

Hamstring stretches can irritate nerves when forced. Stop for tingling, numbness, electric pain, sharp pulling, or recent strain symptoms. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or clinician before starting if you have a relevant condition.
