## Goal

You reduce mild lower-back tightness by using breathing, hip mobility, and gentle spinal motion without forcing painful ranges.

## Preconditions

- A mat, carpet, or firm bed.
- Mild tightness only, not severe pain, trauma, fever, numbness, weakness, or bowel/bladder changes.
- Ability to get down to and up from the floor safely.

## Steps

1. **Start with supported breathing.** Lie on your back with calves on a chair or knees bent, and breathe into the ribs for 1 minute. → *Expect:* back muscles feel less guarded.
2. **Do pelvic tilts.** Gently flatten and release the low back against the floor without lifting the hips. → *Expect:* pelvis moves smoothly with minimal effort.
3. **Add knee rocks.** Let both knees move side to side through a comfortable range. → *Expect:* low back rotates gently without sharp pain.
4. **Mobilize hips.** Hold a figure-four stretch or single-knee hug for 30-45 seconds per side. → *Expect:* stretch is felt in the hip or glute, not as nerve symptoms.
5. **Activate glutes.** Perform 8 slow glute bridges with ribs down and pressure through the midfoot. → *Expect:* glutes work and the low back does not cramp.
6. **Stand and hinge lightly.** Practice 5 hip hinges with hands on hips, pushing hips back and keeping spine long. → *Expect:* bending feels more hip-driven and less back-dominant.
7. **Screen for red flags.** ⚠️ *Safety:* seek medical care for trauma, fever, unexplained weight loss, numbness, weakness, saddle numbness, or bowel/bladder changes. → *Expect:* only mild mechanical tightness is being treated.

## Decision points

- Symptoms travel down the leg -> stop stretching and seek professional advice if persistent.
- Bridges increase back pain -> reduce height or skip activation.
- Sitting caused tightness -> add standing breaks and hip extension during the day.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Stretching aggressively:** detect pain rising during holds -> reduce intensity to mild.
- **F2 Arching bridges:** detect low-back cramping -> tuck pelvis slightly and squeeze glutes first.
- **F3 Twisting too far:** detect sharp pain during knee rocks -> shorten the range.
- **F4 Ignoring red flags:** detect neurological or systemic symptoms -> stop self-care and get evaluated.

## Verification

You complete 10 minutes, stand up with lower perceived tightness, hinge 5 times with a long spine, and have no sharp pain or radiating symptoms.

## Variations

- easier: do only breathing, pelvic tilts, and knee rocks.
- harder: add bird dogs or side planks after symptoms calm.
- equipment: use a chair, pillow, or mat.
- at-home: perform the sequence on a firm bed if the floor is not accessible.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk for mild tightness, but back symptoms can signal serious issues. Stop for sharp pain, radiating symptoms, numbness, weakness, or red flags. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
