## Goal

You raise body temperature, move major joints through usable range, and arrive at training ready to produce force without feeling stiff.

## Preconditions

- Clear floor space where you can step, lunge, and swing your arms.
- Shoes appropriate for the session you are about to do.
- No sharp joint pain, dizziness, or illness symptoms that make exercise unsafe.

## Steps

1. **Start with easy whole-body motion.** March, brisk walk, or jog lightly for 2 minutes with relaxed shoulders and quiet foot strikes. → *Expect:* breathing is slightly faster and your body feels warmer without fatigue.
2. **Mobilize your ankles and calves.** Rock forward over one foot at a time, keeping the heel down and knee tracking over the second or third toe. → *Expect:* the shin moves forward without the arch collapsing or heel lifting early.
3. **Open the hips dynamically.** Alternate knee hugs, leg swings front-to-back, and controlled lateral leg swings while keeping the trunk tall. → *Expect:* the leg moves from the hip without twisting your low back.
4. **Practice lunge patterns.** Step into alternating reverse lunges, lightly touch the back knee toward the floor, and drive through the front midfoot to stand. → *Expect:* both knees stay aligned with the toes and balance improves with each rep.
5. **Warm the trunk and upper back.** Do bodyweight good mornings and thoracic rotations, hinging from the hips and rotating through the upper back. → *Expect:* hamstrings and upper back feel active, not strained.
6. **Prime the shoulders.** Make arm circles, scapular push-ups, or band pull-aparts with ribs down and neck relaxed. → *Expect:* shoulder blades glide smoothly without shrugging.
7. **Rehearse the session's first movement.** [BRANCH: lifting, do light sets of the first lift | running, add 3 short relaxed accelerations | sport, add low-speed versions of the skill] → *Expect:* the target movement feels coordinated before intensity rises.
8. **Stop if symptoms appear.** ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for chest pain, faintness, sharp joint pain, or worsening pain that changes your movement. → *Expect:* you either feel ready to train or you choose a safer lower-intensity session.

## Decision points

- Still stiff after 8 minutes -> repeat the tight area's drill once before increasing intensity.
- Breathing hard too early -> slow down; a warmup should prepare you, not tire you out.
- Pain changes your mechanics -> stop that drill and choose a pain-free range or skip training.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Static stretching too long:** detect relaxed, sleepy muscles before power work -> keep holds brief and emphasize moving reps.
- **F2 Rushed joints:** detect clicking with poor control or bouncing -> slow the drill and reduce range.
- **F3 Knee collapse in lunges:** detect knees drifting inward -> shorten the step and press the knee over the toes.
- **F4 Shoulder shrugging:** detect neck tension during arm work -> lower the arms and move the shoulder blades instead.

## Verification

You complete 8 minutes of continuous dynamic movement, can perform the first exercise or activity at low intensity with clean alignment, and feel warm without being tired.

## Variations

- easier: walk for longer and use smaller ranges of motion.
- harder: add skips, light jumps, or faster accelerations after basic movement feels smooth.
- equipment: use a light resistance band for shoulders and hips.
- at-home: march, lunge, hinge, rotate, and arm-circle in a hallway or living room.

## Safety & privacy

Low risk when kept controlled. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness, chest symptoms, or numbness. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition, injury, or exercise restriction.
