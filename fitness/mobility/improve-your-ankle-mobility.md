## Goal

Improve ankle dorsiflexion and control so squats, stairs, walking, and running feel smoother.

## Preconditions

- Wall, step, or sturdy support.
- Ankles warmed by walking or calf raises.
- No acute ankle sprain, swelling, or fracture concern.

## Steps

1. **Test knee-to-wall range.** Place toes a few inches from a wall and move the knee toward the wall while heel stays down. → *Expect:* you find the farthest distance where the heel stays planted and knee tracks over toes.
2. **Mobilize with knee-over-toe rocks.** In a half-kneeling or standing lunge, glide the knee forward over the second or third toe. → *Expect:* motion is felt at the ankle or calf, not as pinching in the front of the joint.
3. **Stretch the calf with a straight knee.** Lean into a wall with back heel down and knee straight. → *Expect:* stretch is in the upper calf.
4. **Stretch the soleus with a bent knee.** Keep heel down and bend the back knee slightly. → *Expect:* stretch moves lower toward the Achilles and deep calf.
5. **Add controlled calf raises.** Rise onto the balls of the feet, pause, and lower slowly through full comfortable range. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for sharp Achilles pain, ankle instability, swelling, or joint locking. → *Expect:* both ankles move evenly and heels stay aligned.
6. **Retest knee-to-wall.** Repeat the first test without forcing. → *Expect:* range is equal or slightly improved, and the movement feels smoother.

## Decision points

- Heel lifts during knee-to-wall → move closer or reduce range.
- Front ankle pinches → adjust knee angle or stop forcing dorsiflexion.
- Achilles feels painful → reduce stretching intensity and skip loaded calf raises.
- One side is limited → train both sides but add one gentle set to the tighter side.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Collapsing arch:** detect foot rolling inward during rocks → tripod the foot and track knee over toes.
- **F2 Heel lifting:** detect range coming from the heel leaving the floor → shorten the distance.
- **F3 Bouncing calf stretch:** detect pulsing at end range → hold steady and breathe.
- **F4 Loading painful tendon:** detect Achilles pain during raises → stop loaded work and seek guidance if persistent.

## Verification

You completed 10-15 minutes of ankle rocks, calf stretches, and controlled calf raises with heels down, knees tracking over toes, and no sharp pain or swelling.

## Variations

- Easier: seated ankle circles and gentle wall rocks.
- Harder: loaded knee-over-toe rocks or single-leg calf raises after pain-free basics.
- Equipment: wall, step, slant board, resistance band, or chair.
- At-home: use a wall and stair step.

## Safety & privacy

Do not force ankle range through pain, swelling, instability, or joint locking. Stop for sharp Achilles or ankle pain. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or clinician before starting if you have a relevant condition or recent sprain.
