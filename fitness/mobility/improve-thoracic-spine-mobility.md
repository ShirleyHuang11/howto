## Goal

Improve upper-back extension and rotation so overhead movement, breathing, posture, and sport skills feel smoother.

## Preconditions

- Mat, wall, foam roller, or firm towel roll.
- Gentle warmup for shoulders and spine.
- No acute spine injury, unexplained numbness, or severe pain.

## Steps

1. **Set a neutral base.** Sit, kneel, or lie with ribs stacked over pelvis and neck long. → *Expect:* low back is not arched to fake upper-back motion.
2. **Do cat-cow slowly.** Move the spine segment by segment without forcing the neck. → *Expect:* stiffness starts to ease and breathing remains calm.
3. **Practice open-book rotations.** Lie on one side with knees stacked, rotate the top arm and chest open while hips stay quiet. → *Expect:* rotation is felt in the upper back and ribs, not the low back.
4. **Use thoracic extension over support.** Place a foam roller or towel across the upper back, support the head, and extend gently over it. → *Expect:* stretch is broad across the upper back, not a pinch in the low back or neck.
5. **Add quadruped reach-throughs.** On hands and knees, thread one arm under, then rotate open toward the ceiling. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop for sharp spine pain, numbness, tingling, dizziness, or symptoms down the arm. → *Expect:* movement is controlled and symmetrical enough to repeat.
6. **Retest overhead reach or rotation.** Stand and raise arms overhead or rotate the trunk gently. → *Expect:* motion feels freer without compensation.

## Decision points

- Low back arches during extension → brace lightly and use a smaller range.
- Neck hurts → support the head and reduce range.
- Shoulder limits the drill → bend elbow or choose open-book rotation.
- Mobility improves → follow with light strength to control the new range.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Lumbar compensation:** detect ribs flaring and low-back arch → keep ribs down and shrink motion.
- **F2 Neck cranking:** detect chin jutting or headache → support head and keep gaze soft.
- **F3 Hips rotating in open books:** detect knees separating or pelvis rolling back → hold knees stacked.
- **F4 Moving too fast:** detect momentum instead of control → slow each rep and breathe out into rotation.

## Verification

You completed 10-15 minutes of thoracic flexion-extension and rotation drills with ribs controlled, neck comfortable, and no numbness, tingling, or sharp pain.

## Variations

- Easier: seated rotations with arms crossed.
- Harder: half-kneeling windmills or loaded carries after mobility work.
- Equipment: foam roller, towel roll, yoga block, wall, or mat.
- At-home: use a rolled towel instead of a foam roller.

## Safety & privacy

Upper-back mobility should not cause nerve symptoms or sharp spine pain. Stop for numbness, tingling, dizziness, radiating pain, or severe discomfort. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or clinician before starting if you have a relevant condition.
