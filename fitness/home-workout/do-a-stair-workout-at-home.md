## Goal

You complete a low-equipment stair workout that builds leg endurance and cardio fitness while keeping each step controlled and quiet.

## Preconditions

- A stable staircase with a handrail, dry steps, and no loose rugs or objects nearby.
- Supportive shoes with grip.
- Enough fitness to climb stairs without dizziness; warm up first with easy marching, ankle circles, and gentle squats.

## Steps

1. **Check the stairs and choose a work zone.** Use the bottom 1-2 flights, confirm every step is dry, and keep one hand near the rail. → *Expect:* the stairs feel stable and your path is clear.
2. **Warm up with easy stair walks.** Walk up and down for 3 minutes at a conversational pace, placing the whole foot on each step. → *Expect:* breathing rises gradually without leg heaviness.
3. **Do step-ups.** Step onto the first or second stair with the right foot, drive through the midfoot, stand tall, then step down under control; switch sides after 10 reps. → *Expect:* the working knee tracks over the middle toes and your hips stay level.
4. **Climb one flight briskly.** Walk up with short, quick steps, then descend slowly using the rail if needed. → *Expect:* you feel effort in the thighs and glutes without stumbling or pounding.
5. **Add stair push-offs.** Face the stairs, place one foot on the first step, and push up just enough to straighten the hip and knee before returning down. [BRANCH: easier, keep both hands on the rail | harder, use a higher step only if the knee stays aligned] → *Expect:* the push comes from the leg, not from pulling on the rail.
6. **Perform lateral step-ups.** Stand sideways to the first step, step up with the uphill foot, stand tall, then step down slowly; do both sides. → *Expect:* your pelvis stays level and the knee does not cave inward.
7. **Finish with a steady stair walk.** Walk continuously for 3-5 minutes at a pace you can control. ⚠️ *Safety:* stop if you feel chest pain, dizziness, sharp knee pain, or loss of footing. → *Expect:* you can keep even steps and recover within a few minutes.

## Decision points

- Breathing becomes ragged → slow the pace or extend rest between rounds.
- Knee pain on step-ups → use a lower step, reduce reps, or stop if pain persists.
- Balance feels uncertain → keep one hand on the rail and avoid lateral or faster drills.
- Workout feels easy for two sessions → add one round or increase brisk climbing time, not both at once.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Knee caves inward:** detect the kneecap drifting inside the toes → slow down, use a lower step, and press the knee gently toward the middle toes.
- **F2 Heel hangs off the step:** detect pressure only through the toes → place the full foot on the stair before driving up.
- **F3 Loud descending:** detect stomping or jarring landings → shorten the descent, hold the rail, and lower through the hip and knee.
- **F4 Too much rail pulling:** detect tired arms or leaning back → reduce the step height and make the leg do the work.

## Verification

You completed 15-20 minutes of stair walking, step-ups, push-offs, and lateral step-ups with full-foot contact, knees tracking over toes, controlled descents, and no sharp pain or dizziness.

## Variations

- easier: use only the first step and take longer rests.
- harder: add one extra round or carry light household objects only after form stays steady.
- equipment: use a handrail for balance, not propulsion.
- at-home: if stairs are unavailable, use a sturdy low step platform.

## Safety & privacy

Stairs add fall risk when tired, rushed, or distracted. Stop for sharp joint pain, dizziness, chest pain, or missed footing, and consult a qualified professional for persistent pain or balance problems. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor before starting if you have a relevant condition.
