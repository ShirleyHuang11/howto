## Goal

You complete a single-kettlebell session that trains hinging, squatting, carrying, pressing, and trunk control with safe loading.

## Preconditions

- One kettlebell you can control without straining.
- Clear floor space and shoes or surface with traction.
- Warm up hips, hamstrings, shoulders, wrists, and trunk before lifting.

## Steps

1. **Check the bell and space.** Confirm the handle is dry and the floor around you is clear. → *Expect:* you can set the bell down without hitting anything.
2. **Practice the hip hinge.** Stand over the bell, push hips back, keep spine neutral, and reach to the handle. → *Expect:* hamstrings load while shins stay mostly vertical.
3. **Do kettlebell deadlifts.** Grip the handle, brace, stand by driving hips forward, then lower with control for 3 sets of 8-12 reps. → *Expect:* bell travels close to the body and back stays neutral.
4. **Do goblet squats.** Hold the bell at chest height, squat to comfortable depth, and stand through the whole foot. → *Expect:* elbows stay near ribs and knees track over toes.
5. **Do one-arm rows.** Support one hand on thigh or stable surface, row the bell toward the hip, and lower slowly. → *Expect:* upper back works without torso twisting.
6. **Do suitcase carries.** Hold the bell at one side and walk slowly, resisting leaning. → *Expect:* trunk stays tall and shoulders remain level.
7. **Press only if appropriate.** [BRANCH: shoulder-ready, do half-kneeling or standing presses | not ready, do floor presses or skip pressing] → *Expect:* wrist stays stacked and ribs do not flare.
8. **Keep reps clean.** Rest 60-120 seconds and stop each set before grip or back position fails. ⚠️ *Safety:* do not swing the bell unless you have learned the hinge and can keep a neutral spine. → *Expect:* fatigue is muscular, not joint or back pain.

## Decision points

- Back rounds during deadlifts → raise the bell on books or use a lighter load.
- Grip is failing → end the set before the bell path changes.
- Shoulder pinches overhead → use floor presses or rows instead.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Squatting the deadlift:** detect knees driving far forward and hips not hinging → push hips back and keep bell close.
- **F2 Bell drifting away:** detect load pulling the shoulders forward → keep lats engaged and handle near mid-foot.
- **F3 Leaning on carries:** detect torso bending toward or away from bell → lighten load or shorten distance.
- **F4 Rib flare on press:** detect low-back arching overhead → use half-kneeling stance and brace.

## Verification

Complete 3 rounds of kettlebell deadlifts, goblet squats, one-arm rows, suitcase carries, and an appropriate press variation with neutral spine, stable knees, controlled bell path, and no sharp pain.

## Variations

- `easier`: use deadlifts, goblet box squats, supported rows, and short carries only.
- `harder`: add reverse lunges, cleans, or swings only after technique is coached and consistent.
- `equipment`: substitute a dumbbell for most drills if no kettlebell is available.
- `at-home`: avoid overhead moves under low ceilings and protect floors when setting the bell down.

## Safety & privacy

Loaded kettlebell work can injure the back, shoulders, feet, or hands if grip or position fails. Stop for sharp pain, numbness, dizziness, or loss of control. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice; consult a doctor or coach if you have injuries or are new to loaded hinging.
