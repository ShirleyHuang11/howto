## Goal

You write and publish a product description that helps qualified buyers decide while reducing returns, disputes, and misleading claims.

## Preconditions

- Product facts: dimensions, materials, compatibility, included items, care, warranty, restrictions, and photos.
- A known buyer use case and the main objections or questions.
- Access to edit the product page or draft content for approval.

## Steps

1. **Collect verified product facts.** Use supplier specs, measurement, testing notes, and customer-service questions rather than guesses. → *Expect:* a fact sheet with claims you can support.
2. **Define the buyer and job-to-be-done.** Identify who buys the product, what problem it solves, and what would make it a poor fit. → *Expect:* the description has a specific audience and use case.
3. **Lead with the concrete benefit.** Write an opening sentence that connects the product's main feature to the buyer's practical outcome. → *Expect:* the first line explains why the product matters.
4. **Add scannable purchase details.** Include bullets for specifications, included items, compatibility, sizing, materials, setup, and care. → *Expect:* a buyer can answer key fit questions quickly.
5. **Handle limitations honestly.** State exclusions, size constraints, color variation, maintenance, region compatibility, or use restrictions. → *Expect:* the page prevents predictable misunderstandings.
6. **Remove unsupported claims.** Delete exaggerations, medical/legal claims, fake urgency, or comparisons you cannot substantiate. → *Expect:* remaining claims are defensible and compliant.
7. **Align description with media and variants.** Confirm photos, variant names, alt text, and description all refer to the same product configuration. → *Expect:* no contradiction between text, images, and options.
8. **Publish or submit for approval.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* before saving live, confirm price-sensitive claims, warranty terms, and compliance language because customers may rely on them. → *Expect:* the product page shows the approved description.
9. **Check conversion and return signals.** Review add-to-cart rate, search queries, support questions, and return reasons after traffic arrives. → *Expect:* evidence of whether the description is reducing friction or creating confusion.

## Decision points

- Product is technical → prioritize compatibility, measurements, installation, and standards.
- Product is giftable or aesthetic → include sensory details, packaging, and care without hiding practical specs.
- Return reasons mention "not as expected" → add clearer photos, dimensions, and limitations.
- Legal or health claims are tempting → use only approved language and avoid unsupported outcomes.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Misleading benefit claim:** detect complaints that the product did not do what the copy promised → revise claim to match evidence and update support scripts.
- **F2 Missing compatibility detail:** detect pre-sale questions or returns about fit → add compatibility tables and exclusions near the top.
- **F3 Variant confusion:** detect buyers ordering the wrong size/color/package → rename variants and clarify included items.
- **F4 SEO stuffing:** detect unreadable copy or search penalties → rewrite for humans first and keep keywords natural.

## Verification

The live or approved product description includes verified benefits, specifications, limitations, and included items, with no unsupported claims or contradictions against photos and variants.

## Variations

- Marketplace listing: stay within marketplace style rules and prohibited-claims policy.
- Subscription product: clearly disclose renewal interval, cancellation, and first-shipment contents.
- Regulated product: use only compliance-approved language and required warnings.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because misleading copy can cause financial loss, disputes, or regulatory issues. Do not invent testimonials, hide material limitations, or collect customer information through unapproved links.
