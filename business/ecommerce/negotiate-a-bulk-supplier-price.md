## Goal

You negotiate a bulk supplier price or term improvement that lowers landed cost while preserving quality, delivery, and supplier reliability.

## Preconditions

- A current supplier quote, target quantity, and maximum acceptable landed cost.
- Knowledge of market alternatives or competing quotes.
- Authority to negotiate but not necessarily to commit funds without approval.

## Steps

1. **Set your walk-away economics.** Calculate target unit price, maximum landed cost, acceptable MOQ, and cash-flow limit. → *Expect:* a private negotiation range and walk-away point.
2. **Identify tradable variables.** List price, quantity, payment terms, lead time, packaging, warranty, freight, exclusivity, and reorder commitment. → *Expect:* multiple negotiation levers beyond demanding a lower price.
3. **Prepare evidence.** Gather competing quotes, historical order volume, forecasted demand, and reasons the supplier should value the account. → *Expect:* a credible basis for the ask.
4. **Open with a specific proposal.** Ask for a price tier or term tied to quantity, reorder schedule, or prompt payment. → *Expect:* the supplier receives a concrete offer they can accept or counter.
5. **Trade concessions deliberately.** [BRANCH: supplier lowers price, protect quality and delivery terms | supplier refuses, ask for freight, payment, or defect allowance improvement] → *Expect:* each concession is exchanged for something measurable.
6. **Request the revised quote in writing.** Include unit price, MOQ, validity date, lead time, payment terms, freight terms, and quality obligations. → *Expect:* the supplier sends an updated written quote.
7. **Recalculate landed cost.** Include any changed freight, payment fee, defect allowance, or inventory carrying cost. → *Expect:* the revised offer is confirmed to meet or miss the target.
8. **Accept or walk away.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* do not issue a purchase order, deposit, or payment until the written quote matches the approved terms. → *Expect:* either a documented accepted quote or a clear decision to continue sourcing.

## Decision points

- Supplier offers lower price only for excessive MOQ → compare savings against inventory carrying and obsolescence risk.
- Supplier will not move on price → negotiate payment terms, freight inclusion, quality allowance, or reorder priority.
- Competing quote is unverified → use it as context, not as the sole reason to abandon a reliable supplier.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Verbal discount not honored:** detect invoice or pro forma at old price → pause payment and request corrected written quote.
- **F2 Quality reduced to meet price:** detect changed materials or tolerances → restore original spec in writing or reject the concession.
- **F3 MOQ creates cash crunch:** detect deposit or inventory cost above budget → renegotiate smaller tier or split deliveries.
- **F4 Supplier relationship damage:** detect defensive response → reframe around forecast volume and mutual planning, not threats.

## Verification

A revised written supplier quote meets the target landed cost or documented alternative term, stays within MOQ and cash-flow limits, and is approved before any purchase order or payment is sent.

## Variations

- Long-term supplier: use forecast volume and payment reliability as leverage.
- New supplier: negotiate a pilot order first, then volume pricing after quality passes.
- International supplier: negotiate incoterms and freight consolidation as part of the price.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because negotiation may commit funds or disclose demand forecasts. Keep walk-away limits private, avoid false claims, and require explicit confirmation before issuing purchase orders or deposits.
