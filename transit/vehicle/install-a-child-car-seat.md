## Goal

You install a child restraint appropriate for the child's size and confirm it is tight, correctly angled, and used with the right belt path or anchors.

## Preconditions

- You have the child car seat manual and vehicle owner's manual.
- The seat is not expired, recalled, crashed beyond manufacturer limits, missing parts, or of unknown history.
- You know the child's age, weight, height, and developmental needs.

## Steps

1. **Choose the correct restraint mode.** Match rear-facing, forward-facing, booster, or seat belt use to the child's size and local law, following the seat manual limits. → *Expect:* the selected mode is allowed for the child and seat.
2. **Choose a seating position.** Prefer a rear seat position allowed by the vehicle and car seat manuals; avoid active front airbags for rear-facing seats. → *Expect:* the position has an approved belt or lower anchors and enough space.
3. **Set the seat angle and belt path.** Adjust recline for rear-facing seats or upright position for forward-facing seats, then identify the correct belt path. → *Expect:* the seat is routed for the chosen mode, not the wrong-facing path.
4. **Install using one primary method.** [BRANCH: lower anchors allowed for child and seat weight, use lower anchors | seat belt preferred/required, use the locked seat belt] Do not use both unless both manuals explicitly allow it. → *Expect:* one approved installation method is tightened correctly.
5. **Lock and tighten.** Press down where the child sits while pulling slack from the belt or anchor strap. → *Expect:* the seat moves less than 1 inch side-to-side or front-to-back at the belt path.
6. **Attach the top tether for forward-facing.** Connect the tether to the approved tether anchor and tighten. ⚠️ *Hazard:* top tether reduces head movement in a crash; do not attach it to cargo hooks unless the manual says they are tether anchors. → *Expect:* tether is connected to the correct anchor with slack removed.
7. **Position harness for the child.** Rear-facing harness straps usually start at or below shoulders; forward-facing usually at or above. Tighten until you cannot pinch slack at the collarbone. → *Expect:* harness height and tightness match the manual.
8. **Check chest clip and final fit.** Place the chest clip at armpit level, remove bulky coats, and verify the seat angle indicator. → *Expect:* the child is restrained snugly with the seat still tight.
9. **Get an expert check when possible.** Use a certified child passenger safety technician or official fitting station if available. → *Expect:* installation is independently confirmed or corrected.

## Decision points

- Child exceeds lower-anchor weight limit → install with the vehicle seat belt and use tether if forward-facing.
- Seat moves more than 1 inch at belt path → reinstall, try a different seating position, or seek technician help.
- Vehicle lacks tether anchor for forward-facing → consult the vehicle manual or dealer; do not improvise an anchor.
- Seat has unknown history or was in a crash → follow manufacturer replacement rules before using it.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Wrong belt path:** detect belt running through rear-facing path in forward-facing mode or vice versa → uninstall and reroute using labels/manual.
- **F2 Seat belt will not lock:** detect belt loosening after installation → learn that vehicle's locking method or use a locking clip only if allowed by the manual.
- **F3 Bulky clothing creates slack:** detect harness tight over a coat but loose without it → remove the coat and use blankets over the buckled harness.
- **F4 Incompatible seat/vehicle fit:** detect impossible angle or front seat interference → try another approved position or different restraint.

## Verification

The seat moves less than 1 inch at the belt path, the angle indicator is correct, harness routing fits the child, and the installation uses only approved anchors or belt paths from both manuals.

## Variations

- `us`: certified Child Passenger Safety Technicians can inspect installations; local fire stations may or may not offer this service.
- ISOFIX/LATCH/lower anchors: names differ by region, and anchor weight limits vary.
- Booster seat: the lap belt must lie low on hips/thighs and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck or belly.

## Safety & privacy

High risk because incorrect installation can fail in a crash. Confirm the manuals, child size limits, anchor limits, and crash/recall status before using the seat.
