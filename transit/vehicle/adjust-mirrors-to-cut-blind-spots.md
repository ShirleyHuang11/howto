## Goal

You set the driver's seat and mirrors so rear and adjacent-lane views overlap less, reducing blind spots while preserving safe rear visibility.

## Preconditions

- The vehicle is parked safely with power available for mirror adjustment.
- The driver's seat, steering wheel, and head restraint are already adjusted for the actual driver.
- Windows and mirrors are clean enough to see clearly.

## Steps

1. **Set your driving posture first.** Sit normally with pedals, wheel, and head restraint correctly positioned. → *Expect:* mirror settings will match the posture used while driving.
2. **Adjust the rearview mirror.** Center the back window in the mirror without moving your head from normal posture. → *Expect:* the rear window fills the mirror evenly.
3. **Set the left side mirror outward.** Lean your head close to the driver's window and adjust until you barely see the side of your vehicle. → *Expect:* when you sit normally, the mirror shows more adjacent lane and little or none of your own car.
4. **Set the right side mirror outward.** Lean toward the center of the vehicle and adjust until you barely see the side of your vehicle. → *Expect:* when seated normally, the right mirror covers the adjacent lane with minimal bodywork visible.
5. **Check overlap with a passing vehicle.** While parked safely or as a passenger/with help, observe a vehicle moving from rearview to side mirror to peripheral vision. → *Expect:* the vehicle transitions smoothly with minimal disappearance.
6. **Keep shoulder checks.** Use mirrors to reduce blind spots, not to replace a glance before lane changes. → *Expect:* your lane-change routine still includes signal, mirror, shoulder check, and movement.
7. **Save settings if available.** Store seat/mirror memory for the driver profile. → *Expect:* the setup can be restored after another driver changes it.

## Decision points

- You tow a trailer or carry wide cargo → adjust mirrors to see trailer sides and rear corners; add tow mirrors if needed.
- You feel disoriented by outward mirrors → transition gradually but confirm adjacent-lane coverage before highway use.
- Vehicle has blind-spot monitoring → still set mirrors properly; sensors can miss fast or unusual objects.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Too much car body visible:** detect your vehicle side taking up mirror space while seated normally → move side mirrors outward.
- **F2 No rear reference:** detect inability to judge lanes behind → ensure the rearview mirror is centered and side mirrors retain horizon/context.
- **F3 Different driver uses car:** detect mirrors no longer align with your posture → reset before driving, not while merging.
- **F4 Rain or glare obscures view:** detect poor mirror visibility → clean mirrors, use defog/heat if equipped, and increase shoulder checks.

## Verification

From normal driving posture, the rearview mirror centers the back window and each side mirror shows the adjacent lane with minimal vehicle body visible; a passing vehicle remains visible through mirror/peripheral transitions.

## Variations

- Large SUVs/vans: blind zones remain significant; use cameras and spotters when backing.
- Right-hand-drive vehicles: reverse the lean directions for left/right mirror setup.
- Convex passenger mirror: objects appear farther away; use it for coverage, not precise distance alone.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because mirror misadjustment can contribute to crashes. Confirm settings while parked and continue using shoulder checks before every lane change.
