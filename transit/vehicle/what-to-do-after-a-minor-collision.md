## Goal

You make the scene safe, document the facts, exchange required information, and start the insurance or reporting process after a minor crash.

## Preconditions

- The collision appears minor, with no obvious serious injury, fire, hazardous spill, or vehicle in immediate danger.
- You have a phone, insurance details, registration, and driver's license if legally required.
- You can move to a safe location if vehicles are drivable and local law permits.

## Steps

1. **Check for injuries and immediate danger.** Ask occupants if anyone is hurt and look for smoke, fuel, traffic exposure, or airbags deployed. → *Expect:* emergency needs are identified before paperwork.
2. **Call emergency services if needed.** [BRANCH: injury, blocked lane, impaired driver, hit-and-run, major damage, or required local reporting, call police/emergency services | truly minor and safe, proceed with documentation] → *Expect:* official help is requested when legally or practically necessary.
3. **Move out of traffic if safe and legal.** Turn on hazards and move vehicles to a shoulder, parking lot, or side street if they can be driven safely. → *Expect:* people and vehicles are no longer blocking active traffic.
4. **Exchange information calmly.** Collect name, phone, license plate, driver's license number where appropriate, insurance company, policy number, and vehicle details. → *Expect:* both drivers have enough information for a claim.
5. **Document the scene.** Photograph vehicle positions, damage, plates, VIN areas if accessible, traffic controls, road conditions, skid marks, debris, and wide-angle context. → *Expect:* photos show both close damage and the whole scene.
6. **Avoid admitting fault at the scene.** Be factual and cooperative, but do not guess, argue, or sign informal blame statements. → *Expect:* communication stays limited to facts and safety.
7. **Get witness and report details.** Record witness contact information and any police report, incident, or case number. → *Expect:* independent documentation can be found later.
8. **Notify your insurer.** Report the collision promptly through the app, website, or phone line, using facts and photos. → *Expect:* a claim or notice number is created.
9. **Arrange safe vehicle handling.** [BRANCH: car is safe to drive, proceed cautiously | leaking fluids, damaged lights, tire/wheel damage, airbag deployed, or warning lights, tow or inspect first] → *Expect:* the vehicle leaves the scene legally and safely.

## Decision points

- Someone may be injured → call emergency services even if damage looks small.
- Other driver refuses to share information → record plate, vehicle, driver description, location, and call police if appropriate.
- Crash involves a parked unattended vehicle → leave required contact information and file any required report.
- Government, rental, company, or rideshare vehicle involved → follow employer/rental/platform reporting rules in addition to insurance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Missing photos:** detect that vehicles moved before photos → take photos of final positions, damage, street signs, and write a timeline immediately.
- **F2 Other driver changes story:** detect inconsistent statements later → rely on photos, witness contacts, report number, and contemporaneous notes.
- **F3 Hidden damage:** detect steering pull, warning light, or fluid later → stop driving and arrange inspection; update the insurer.
- **F4 Claim delayed:** detect no response from insurer → use the claim number and escalate through the insurer's claims department.

## Verification

Everyone is out of immediate danger, required information and photos are saved, any police/incident number is recorded, and your insurer has issued a claim or notice confirmation.

## Variations

- `us`: police-report requirements vary by state and damage amount; some states require DMV crash reports in addition to insurance claims.
- Rental car: call the rental company's emergency or claims number before authorizing repairs.
- No-fault insurance region: medical and vehicle-damage claims may route differently; follow local policy instructions.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from legal, insurance, identity, and roadside hazards. Confirm safety before standing outside, share only required information, and do not post crash photos or other drivers' documents publicly.
