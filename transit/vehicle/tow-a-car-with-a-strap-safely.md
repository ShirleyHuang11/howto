## Goal

You decide whether strap towing is appropriate and, if it is, move a disabled vehicle only a short distance at low speed using rated recovery points.

## Preconditions

- Both drivers are licensed, sober, and can communicate clearly.
- The tow vehicle, disabled vehicle, strap, and attachment points are rated for the load.
- The disabled vehicle can steer and brake; if not, do not strap tow.

## Steps

1. **Decide if strap towing is appropriate.** Use a strap only for short, low-speed movement off a road or around private property, not highway towing. ⚠️ *Hazard:* strap towing on public roads can be illegal and dangerous. → *Expect:* the route is short, slow, legal, and controlled.
2. **Inspect the strap and hardware.** Use a rated tow/recovery strap without cuts, knots, hooks of unknown rating, or frayed stitching. → *Expect:* equipment is visibly sound and rated above the expected load.
3. **Identify rated attachment points.** Use factory tow hooks, recovery points, or hitch receivers with rated shackles; never attach to bumpers, suspension arms, steering parts, or tie-down loops unless rated for towing. → *Expect:* both ends are connected to structural rated points.
4. **Plan signals and route.** Agree on speed, stopping signal, turns, destination, and what to do if the strap slackens or traffic appears. → *Expect:* both drivers understand the same plan.
5. **Clear the area.** Keep bystanders away from the strap line and remove obstacles from the route. → *Expect:* no one is standing near a tensioned strap.
6. **Tension the strap gently.** The tow vehicle creeps forward until slack is gone; no jerking unless using a proper recovery strap for stuck-vehicle recovery and trained to do so. → *Expect:* the strap is straight and lightly tensioned.
7. **Move slowly and smoothly.** Keep speeds walking pace to very low speed, avoid sudden acceleration, and let the towed driver brake to maintain tension. → *Expect:* the strap stays controlled and both vehicles track predictably.
8. **Stop and disconnect safely.** Park both vehicles, set parking brakes, release tension, then disconnect hardware. → *Expect:* the strap is slack before anyone touches connections.

## Decision points

- Disabled car has no brakes, steering, lights, or driver → do not strap tow; call a tow truck.
- Route includes highway, busy street, steep hill, or sharp turns → do not strap tow.
- Vehicle is stuck in mud/snow → recovery forces can exceed towing forces; use proper recovery gear and keep people far away.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Strap snaps or hardware fails:** detect a bang, recoil, or dropped connection → stop immediately, keep people clear, and replace equipment; inspect vehicles for damage.
- **F2 Towed vehicle overruns strap:** detect slack bunching or near contact → slow down, use more braking in the towed vehicle, and rebrief signals.
- **F3 Attachment point bends:** detect deformation or cracking → stop and choose a rated point or abandon strap towing.
- **F4 Traffic enters route:** detect other vehicles or pedestrians nearby → stop and wait or use professional towing.

## Verification

The disabled vehicle has been moved to the intended safe location at low speed, no equipment or attachment point is damaged, and the strap is disconnected only after tension is released.

## Variations

- Tow dolly or flat tow bar: follow the equipment manual, lighting, braking, and drivetrain rules instead of strap-tow steps.
- Off-road recovery: kinetic recovery straps and winches require specific training and rated recovery points.
- Automatic/AWD vehicles: drivetrain damage can occur if wheels roll improperly; check the manual before any distance towing.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from collision, strap recoil, and mechanical failure. Confirm rated points, legal route, working brakes/steering, and clear communication before moving either vehicle.
