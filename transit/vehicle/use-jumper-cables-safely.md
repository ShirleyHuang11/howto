## Goal

You jump-start a vehicle with a discharged 12-volt battery while minimizing fire, explosion, electrical, and vehicle-damage risks.

## Preconditions

- Both vehicles use compatible 12-volt systems, or the jump pack is rated for your vehicle.
- You have undamaged jumper cables or a charged jump starter and eye protection if available.
- The dead battery is not frozen, cracked, leaking, swollen, or smelling strongly of sulfur.

## Steps

1. **Inspect before connecting.** Check battery condition, cable insulation, and owner's manual warnings for both vehicles. ⚠️ *Hazard:* do not jump a damaged, frozen, or leaking battery. → *Expect:* the batteries and cables appear safe to use.
2. **Position vehicles safely.** Park close enough for cables to reach without vehicles touching, set parking brakes, and turn off ignitions and accessories. → *Expect:* both vehicles are stable and powered down.
3. **Connect positive to dead positive.** Clamp the red cable to the dead battery's positive terminal. → *Expect:* the clamp is secure on the positive marked post or jump point.
4. **Connect positive to donor positive.** Clamp the other red end to the donor battery positive terminal or jump point. → *Expect:* both red clamps are on positive points only.
5. **Connect negative to donor negative.** Clamp the black cable to the donor negative terminal or ground point. → *Expect:* the black clamp is secure and away from moving parts.
6. **Connect final negative to dead vehicle ground.** Clamp the remaining black end to an unpainted metal engine/block ground on the dead vehicle, away from the battery and fuel lines. ⚠️ *Hazard:* make this final connection away from the dead battery to reduce spark risk near battery gas. → *Expect:* all four clamps are secure and cables avoid fans and belts.
7. **Start the donor, then the dead vehicle.** Start the donor vehicle and wait a few minutes, then try the dead vehicle for only a few seconds at a time. → *Expect:* the dead vehicle cranks and starts, or the attempt stops without overheating cables.
8. **Remove cables in reverse order.** Remove dead-vehicle ground, donor negative, donor positive, then dead positive, keeping clamps from touching. → *Expect:* cables are disconnected without sparks or contact between clamps.
9. **Let the recovered vehicle run and plan charging diagnosis.** Drive or idle as recommended by the manual, and test battery/charging system soon. → *Expect:* the vehicle stays running and a battery check is planned.

## Decision points

- Vehicle is hybrid/EV or has remote jump posts → use only the manufacturer-designated 12-volt jump points.
- Cables get hot, smoke, or spark heavily → stop, disconnect safely if possible, and call assistance.
- Vehicle does not start after several short attempts → stop to avoid starter and cable damage; call roadside assistance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Reversed polarity:** detect a clamp on the wrong terminal before starting → do not start; disconnect in reverse and reconnect correctly.
- **F2 No accessible ground point:** detect only painted or plastic surfaces → use the manual's jump-start ground; do not improvise on fuel lines or moving parts.
- **F3 Starts then dies:** detect the engine quits after disconnecting → suspect charging-system failure and arrange service or tow.
- **F4 Alarm or electronics behave oddly:** detect alarm sounding or dash errors → stop and consult the manual; some vehicles need specific procedures.

## Verification

The vehicle starts, jumper cables are removed in the correct reverse order, no warning smoke/heat occurred, and the battery or charging system is scheduled for testing if the battery was deeply discharged.

## Variations

- Portable jump starter: connect red positive first and black to approved ground, power the pack on only after clamps are secure, and follow the pack's manual.
- Side-post batteries: use proper terminal adapters or designated jump posts.
- Diesel or large engines: may require higher-capacity cables or battery packs.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from battery explosion, burns, fire, and electronic damage. Confirm polarity before each clamp, keep sparks away from the dead battery, and do not lean over batteries during starting.
