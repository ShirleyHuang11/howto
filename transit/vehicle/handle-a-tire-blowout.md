## Goal

You keep control after a tire fails suddenly, slow the vehicle without spinning or swerving, and stop in the safest available place.

## Preconditions

- You are driving and hear a bang, feel heavy vibration, or the vehicle pulls sharply.
- You have enough lane or shoulder space to stabilize before stopping if traffic permits.
- Passengers can remain belted until the vehicle is stopped.

## Steps

1. **Grip the wheel firmly and look where you want to go.** Keep both hands on the wheel and point the vehicle straight. → *Expect:* the vehicle tracks mostly forward even if it pulls to one side.
2. **Do not slam the brakes.** Ease off the accelerator and let the vehicle slow naturally. ⚠️ *Hazard:* hard braking can shift weight onto the failed tire and cause loss of control. → *Expect:* speed begins dropping without a skid.
3. **Stabilize before steering off.** Keep the vehicle in its lane until the initial pull and wobble reduce. → *Expect:* steering response feels more predictable.
4. **Signal and move toward safety.** Check mirrors, signal, and steer gradually toward the shoulder, breakdown lane, or a wide turnout. → *Expect:* nearby traffic can see your intent and you are leaving the travel lane smoothly.
5. **Brake gently below low speed.** Once the vehicle is slow and straight, use light braking to stop completely. → *Expect:* the vehicle stops without spinning or crossing lanes.
6. **Secure the stopped vehicle.** Put the transmission in park, set the parking brake, turn on hazard lights, and keep occupants away from traffic. → *Expect:* the vehicle is stationary and visible.
7. **Call for help if the location is unsafe.** Use roadside assistance or emergency services if you are on a highway shoulder, narrow bridge, tunnel, or blind curve. → *Expect:* help is requested and passengers remain in the safest available position.

## Decision points

- Blowout happens at highway speed → prioritize control over stopping immediately; slow first, move over second.
- Safe exit or parking lot is very close and reachable slowly → continue carefully only if the vehicle remains controllable and the tire is not shredding dangerously.
- You are stopped next to fast traffic → usually stay belted inside unless there is fire, smoke, or another immediate danger; follow local emergency guidance.
- Tire change would expose you to traffic → do not change it there; call roadside assistance.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Vehicle pulls hard:** detect strong drift toward another lane or barrier → keep steering straight, ease off speed, and avoid abrupt corrections.
- **F2 Driver brakes instinctively:** detect skid, fishtail, or sharper pull → release brake pressure, steer toward the lane, and regain control before braking again.
- **F3 No shoulder:** detect barriers or no breakdown lane → continue slowly with hazards to the nearest wider safe area if the car remains controllable.
- **F4 Secondary danger after stopping:** detect smoke, fire, leaking fuel, or traffic striking debris → evacuate away from traffic if safe and call emergency services.

## Verification

The vehicle is fully stopped outside the active travel lane or in the safest reachable position, hazard lights are on, occupants are protected from traffic, and repair or towing help has been contacted.

## Variations

- Front tire blowout: steering pull is usually stronger; hold the wheel firmly and avoid hard braking.
- Rear tire blowout: the rear may sway; keep steering smooth and resist overcorrecting.
- Motorcycle or heavy vehicle: procedures differ materially; use training specific to that vehicle.

## Safety & privacy

High risk from crash and roadside-strike hazards. Confirm the vehicle is stable and away from traffic before inspecting damage; never stand between your vehicle and oncoming traffic.
