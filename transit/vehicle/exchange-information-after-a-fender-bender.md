## Goal

You collect and provide the information needed for insurance and legal reporting after a low-speed vehicle collision.

## Preconditions

- The scene is safe enough to speak briefly and take photos.
- No one needs urgent medical attention; if they do, call emergency services first.
- You have your driver's license, registration, and insurance information available if required.

## Steps

1. **Move to safety first.** Turn on hazards and stand away from traffic before handling documents. → *Expect:* both parties can exchange information without standing in an active lane.
2. **Confirm whether police are needed.** Follow local law for injury, damage threshold, uninsured driver, impairment, blocked traffic, or hit-and-run. → *Expect:* a report is requested if required.
3. **Photograph vehicle and plate information.** Capture license plates, vehicle make/model/color, damage, and wide scene context. → *Expect:* vehicle identity and damage are documented even if the conversation ends early.
4. **Exchange driver identity information.** Provide and collect name, phone number, driver's license details where required, and address only if legally required or needed for the report. → *Expect:* each driver can be identified for a claim.
5. **Exchange insurance information.** Photograph or write insurer name, policy number, claims phone number, and insured name if different from the driver. → *Expect:* insurance claims can be opened without another call to the driver.
6. **Record vehicle registration details.** Capture registration card or VIN only as locally appropriate, avoiding unnecessary exposure of unrelated personal data. → *Expect:* vehicle ownership information is available for insurer or police.
7. **Write a short fact note.** Note time, exact location, lane/direction, weather, traffic controls, and what happened in neutral language. → *Expect:* you have a contemporaneous factual record.
8. **Confirm receipt before leaving.** Check that photos are readable and phone numbers are not mistyped. → *Expect:* both parties leave with usable information.

## Decision points

- Other driver wants to handle it privately → still exchange insurance and document damage; private agreements can fail.
- Driver lacks insurance or license → document what they have and call police if local law or safety requires it.
- You feel unsafe or threatened → return to your locked vehicle or public area and call emergency services.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Blurry document photo:** detect unreadable policy or license numbers → retake before leaving or ask the insurer/police to help later using plate information.
- **F2 Refusal to cooperate:** detect the other driver leaving or withholding details → record plate, vehicle, direction of travel, and witness contacts; report promptly.
- **F3 Too much personal data shared:** detect full document photos with extra data → keep them for claim use only and do not send broadly.
- **F4 Mismatched driver and insured name:** detect a borrowed or company vehicle → record both the driver and policyholder/vehicle owner information.

## Verification

You have readable photos or notes containing the other vehicle's plate, driver identity, insurer, policy number, vehicle details, damage, location, time, and any report number.

## Variations

- `us`: some states require showing license, registration, and insurance after a crash; reporting thresholds vary.
- Company vehicle: collect employer fleet or claims contact if provided.
- Parked car with no driver: leave required contact information and photograph your note in place.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from identity exposure, legal consequences, and roadside danger. Share required crash information only, keep documents out of public view, and avoid arguing about fault at the scene.
