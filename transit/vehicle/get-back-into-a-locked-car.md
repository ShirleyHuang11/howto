## Goal

You regain access to a locked vehicle legally and safely without damaging the car or endangering anyone inside.

## Preconditions

- You are the owner, authorized user, or have permission to access the vehicle.
- No child, vulnerable person, or pet is in immediate danger inside; if they are, treat it as an emergency.
- You have a phone, ID, and any roadside, insurer, or automaker account information available.

## Steps

1. **Check for immediate danger.** Look for a child, pet, medical need, extreme heat/cold, or engine running in an unsafe place. → *Expect:* you know whether this is an emergency.
2. **Call emergency services if life safety is at risk.** ⚠️ *Hazard:* heatstroke or hypothermia can develop quickly; do not wait for routine locksmith service when someone is trapped. → *Expect:* emergency responders are contacted if needed.
3. **Confirm all normal access points.** Try every door, hatch, keypad, wallet key, hidden mechanical key in the fob, and spare key location without forcing anything. → *Expect:* simple entry options are exhausted.
4. **Use remote unlock if available.** Try the manufacturer's app, roadside app, connected-services call center, or a trusted person with app access. → *Expect:* the vehicle unlocks remotely or the app reports it cannot.
5. **Call roadside assistance or a locksmith.** Provide location, vehicle make/model/year, proof of ownership if requested, and whether keys are visible inside. → *Expect:* a legitimate provider is dispatched.
6. **Verify identity and authorization.** Be ready to show driver's license, registration, insurance, or app ownership before unlocking. → *Expect:* the provider confirms you are authorized.
7. **Avoid DIY prying unless directed by professionals.** Do not wedge doors, pull weather stripping, or use coat hangers on modern vehicles unless you accept damage risk. → *Expect:* entry method is handled by trained help or approved remote unlock.
8. **Recover keys and test locks.** Once open, retrieve keys, check alarm status, and confirm no weather seal, window, or lock damage. → *Expect:* the vehicle is accessible and secure.

## Decision points

- Child, pet, or vulnerable adult inside in dangerous temperature → call emergency services immediately.
- Keys locked in trunk only → check cabin trunk releases, fold-down seats, and manufacturer guidance before forcing entry.
- Vehicle is running and locked → tell the provider; exhaust and theft risks may change urgency.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Remote app fails:** detect no signal, expired subscription, or dead vehicle battery → call roadside assistance or locksmith.
- **F2 Provider asks for proof you lack:** detect inability to prove ownership at the scene → contact a trusted person to bring registration/title or call police non-emergency for guidance.
- **F3 Alarm activates:** detect alarm after entry → use the key/fob, start procedure, or app to disarm.
- **F4 Door seal damaged:** detect wind noise or water leak after unlock → document and arrange repair; report to provider if caused during service.

## Verification

The vehicle is unlocked by an authorized method, keys are recovered or replacement access is arranged, occupants are safe, and locks/windows/seals still function.

## Variations

- Keypad vehicle: use the factory door code if known; keep codes private.
- Rental or car-share: contact the rental/share company first because they may unlock remotely and require specific reporting.
- Older vehicle: locksmith entry may be simpler, but proof of authorization still matters.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk from vehicle damage, theft suspicion, and trapped-occupant danger. Confirm authorization before attempting entry and escalate immediately for anyone at medical or temperature risk inside.
