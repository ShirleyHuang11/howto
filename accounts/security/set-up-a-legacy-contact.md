## Goal

You designate a trusted person who can help manage, memorialize, or retrieve allowed account data after your death.

## Preconditions

- You can sign in to the account.
- You have chosen a trusted adult who understands the responsibility.
- You understand what the service lets a legacy contact access and what remains private.

## Steps

1. **Open legacy or account-after-death settings.** Look for Legacy contact, Inactive account manager, Memorialization, Digital legacy, or Account ownership after death. → *Expect:* the provider explains available post-death options.
2. **Read the contact's permissions.** Check whether the contact can download data, manage a memorial profile, close the account, or see private messages. → *Expect:* you know exactly what the contact can and cannot do.
3. **Choose the trusted contact.** Enter the person's account, email address, or phone number as required. → *Expect:* the provider identifies the intended person before saving.
4. **Set timing and data choices.** Choose inactivity periods, notification timing, downloadable data categories, or memorialization preferences where offered. → *Expect:* the settings match your intended post-death plan.
5. **Save and confirm.** ⚠️ *Irreversible:* confirm the person and permissions before saving, because this may grant sensitive access after a legal death or inactivity process. → *Expect:* the legacy contact appears as active, pending, or invited.
6. **Tell the contact outside the platform.** Explain what you selected and where they can find death certificate or executor information if needed. → *Expect:* the contact understands their role and does not mistake the notification for spam.
7. **Record the setting with estate documents.** Note the account name, legacy-contact choice, and review date without writing down passwords. → *Expect:* your estate plan can point the contact to the official provider process.

## Decision points

- The service offers inactivity transfer instead of death-based access → choose an inactivity period long enough to avoid accidental triggering.
- You do not trust anyone with the data → choose deletion or no legacy contact if the service offers it.
- You are under workplace or school policy → do not assign personal legacy access to organization-owned accounts.

## Failure modes & recovery

- **F1 Contact invitation ignored:** detect pending status for weeks → remind the person or choose another trusted contact.
- **F2 Wrong person selected:** detect a similar name or old email → remove the entry and add the correct contact immediately.
- **F3 Permissions too broad:** detect data categories you do not want shared → narrow the selected data or choose memorialization only.
- **F4 Provider lacks legacy settings:** detect no account-after-death option → document the official support process with estate paperwork instead.

## Verification

The account settings show the intended legacy contact, status, and selected post-death or inactivity options, and the contact has been informed outside the platform.

## Variations

- Apple: Digital Legacy uses an access key plus death certificate process.
- Google: Inactive Account Manager uses inactivity timing and selected data sharing.
- Social networks: legacy contacts may manage memorial profiles without reading private messages.

## Safety & privacy

Medium risk because legacy settings can expose private data or affect estate administration. Confirm the person, permissions, and timing before saving, and do not share passwords as a substitute for an official legacy process.
